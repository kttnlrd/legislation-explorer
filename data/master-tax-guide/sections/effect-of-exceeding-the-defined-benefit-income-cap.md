# Eﬀect of exceeding the deﬁned beneﬁt income cap ¶14-370

The transfer balance cap regime applies to limit the amount that an individual can have in retirement phase income streams, and therefore limit the income that can be exempt current pension income. The ATO maintains a transfer balance account for each individual to monitor the amount of their retirement phase income streams. If an individual's transfer balance account exceeds the transfer balance cap in a ﬁnancial year, the excess amount must be commuted to a lump sum and the individual is liable to pay excess transfer balance tax (¶14-320 – ¶14-360).

The transfer balance cap regime is modiﬁed where an individual has capped deﬁned beneﬁt income streams (as deﬁned in ITAA97 s 294-130: see ¶14-365), by applying a "deﬁned beneﬁt income cap" to these income streams instead.

If the cap for the year is exceeded, there are additional income tax consequences for the superannuation beneﬁts received from the capped deﬁned beneﬁt income streams (see "Tax consequences if deﬁned beneﬁt income cap is exceeded" below). The reason for the diﬀerent tax treatment is because capped deﬁned beneﬁt income streams have commutation restrictions or cannot be commuted to a lump sum so as to address an excess transfer balance like other superannuation income streams.

*Refs: ITAA97 s 294-130*

balance A statutory formula is used to work out the value of an individual's superannuation interest that supports a capped deﬁned beneﬁt income stream in the individual's transfer balance account (called the "special value") (s 294-135). An individual's transfer balance account will have a "capped deﬁned beneﬁt balance", which reﬂects the credits and debits in the account in respect of capped deﬁned beneﬁt income stream(s) (s 294-140) (¶14-365). This balance represents the net amount of capital an individual has in the retirement phase in respect of capped deﬁned beneﬁt income streams.

An individual will have an "excess transfer balance" at a particular time if, at that time, the transfer balance in the individual's transfer balance account exceeds the individual's transfer balance cap and the individual's capped deﬁned beneﬁt balance (see above) at that time. The amount of the excess transfer balance is the lesser of the 2 excesses (ss 294-30, 294-140).
