# Conﬁdentiality of tax information ¶1-220

The tax administration legislation contains a general prohibition on the unauthorised disclosure by a tax oﬃcer of "protected information", with some speciﬁc exceptions (TAA sch 1 Subdiv 355-B). The general prohibition also applies to non-taxation oﬃcers who receive protected information whether lawfully or unlawfully (Subdivs 355-C and 355-D). Protected information is information disclosed or obtained under a taxation law (other than under TASA: ¶32-000) that relates to the aﬀairs of a taxpayer and identiﬁes, or is reasonably capable of identifying, a taxpayer. Tax ﬁle numbers, however, do not constitute protected information.

In relation to the secrecy provisions in ITAA36 former s 16, it was held that once the ATO comes into possession of information by whatever means, and whether due to exertions by one of its oﬃcers or not, if the information is relevant to the tax aﬀairs of any person it becomes subject to the secrecy provisions (Tang 2006 ATC 4592; [2006] SASC 252).

Disclosures to certain entities are permitted, such as to the taxpayer's registered tax agent or BAS agent, legal representative for tax aﬀairs, legal personal representative, other representative or guardian, and consolidated group or MEC group members. Other disclosures permitted include disclosures made in performing one's duties as a tax oﬃcer, disclosures of publicly available information, disclosures to certain government ministers, disclosures for certain other government purposes, and disclosures for law enforcement and related purposes. The applicant's argument in McEwan v Oﬃce of the Australian Information Commissioner 2023 ATC ¶20-877; [2023] FCAFC 137 that, under s 355-50, it was only permissible for a taxation oﬃcer to make a disclosure to another taxation oﬃcer was rejected.

Permitted disclosures to courts and tribunals are limited to those necessary for the purpose of carrying into eﬀect the provisions of a taxation law (Binqld Finances Pty Ltd (in liq) v Israel Discount Bank Ltd 2019 ATC ¶20-701; [2019] FCA 1186, Jordan, FC of T v Second Commissioner of Taxation 2019 ATC ¶20-712; [2019] FCA 1602, DPP & Ors v Kinghorn; Kinghorn v DPP 2020 ATC ¶20-737; [2020] NSWCCA 48, Cth v Kupang Resources Pty Ltd 2022 ATC ¶20-830; [2022] NSWCA 77). Neither the accusatorial principle nor the companion rule operates to limit the power of disclosure conferred by the TAA (R v Van Eps 2024 ATC ¶20-901; [2024] QCA 46, R v Kinghorn 2021 ATC ¶20-812; [2021] NSWCCA 313).

Legislation was enacted in 2019 to allow the ATO to disclose to Credit Reporting Bureaus the tax debt information of businesses, subject to certain conditions and safeguards being met (TAA sch 1 ss 355-72 and 355-215; Taxation Administration (Tax Debt Information Disclosure) Declaration 2019). The ATO commenced reporting business tax debts in August 2021. The ATO can report tax debts if a business owes at least $100,000 that has been overdue for more than 90 days. However, the ATO will not report tax debts if the business engages with the ATO (eg if the business agrees to a payment arrangement or formally disputes the debt) or, if the business has lodged a complaint with the IGTO (¶1-200), until the complaint investigation has concluded.

The unauthorised disclosure of protected information is an oﬀence punishable by a maximum penalty of 2 years' imprisonment.

Protection for whistleblowers A whistleblower protection regime facilitates the reporting of breaches of tax laws or misconduct (TAA Pt IVD: ss 14ZZT to 14ZZZE). To qualify for protection, the disclosure must be made to an "eligible recipient", ie the Commissioner or an auditor of the whistleblower entity, or a member of the ATO audit team; a person connected to the whistleblower entity who is likely to be able to investigate and take action in response to the disclosure; or a person or body prescribed by regulation. To ensure eﬀective collaboration between regulators of the tax system, a legislative instrument (F2024L01291) amended the Taxation Administration Regulations 2017 in October 2024 to prescribe the Inspector-General of Taxation, the Tax Practitioners Board and the Commissioner of the Australian Charities and Not-for-proﬁts Commission (ACNC) as eligible recipients under s 14ZZW(2)(d) of the TAA.

An individual will also be protected for a disclosure to their lawyer that is made for the purposes of seeking legal advice.

Public disclosures To improve tax transparency, the Commissioner is required to publish publicly certain tax information of corporate tax entities with a total income of $100 million or more for an income year and all entities that have any petroleum resource rent tax (¶19-003) payable in a given year (TAA ss 3C; 3E). (Prior to the 2022–23 year, Australian resident private companies that returned less than $200 million were excluded from this requirement.) The ATO released the "Corporate tax transparency report" for public and private corporate tax entities for the 2022–23 income year on 1 November 2024.

Voluntary disclosures To encourage voluntary public disclosures of tax information, the Board of Taxation (¶1-710) developed the "Tax Transparency Code" (TTC). This is a set of principles and minimum standards to guide disclosure of tax information by large and medium-sized businesses. The TTC disclosure is divided into 2 parts, being Part A (improvements to disclosures of tax information in ﬁnancial statements) and Part B (taxes paid).

Large businesses (ie with aggregated TTC Australian turnover of A$500 million or more) should adopt Part A and Part B; medium businesses (ie with aggregated TTC Australian turnover of at least A$100 million but less than A$500 million) should adopt Part A only. The ATO advises that there is no prescribed timing for the release of annual TTC reports. Once a large or medium business has made its TTC report publicly available, it should notify the ATO at TTC@ato.gov.au.

Recent reforms As part of its response to the PwC tax leaks scandal the government introduced legislation in late 2023 (ie the Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Accountability and Fairness) Act 2024), which received assent on 31 May 2024 as Act No 37 of 2024.

Schedule 4 to the Act amended the TAA and the Tax Agent Services Act 2009 to allow taxation oﬃcers and TPB oﬃcials to share protected information with Treasury about misconduct arising out of breaches or suspected breaches of conﬁdence by intermediaries engaging with the Commonwealth. It also allows taxation oﬃcers and TPB oﬃcials to share protected information with prescribed professional disciplinary bodies to enable them to perform their disciplinary functions.

Schedule 2 to the Act amended the TAA to extend whistleblower protections to eligible whistleblowers who make disclosures to the TPB, as well as disclosures to certain other entities who may support or assist the whistleblower. It also reversed the burden of proof for certain claims of protection under Pt IVD of the TAA.

The government also introduced the Treasury Laws Amendment (Better Targeted Superannuation Concessions and Other Measures) Bill 2023 in November 2023 (¶41-150), which the Senate agreed to split into 2 bills on 28 November 2024. Schedule 4 of the original Bill (to be moved into Treasury Laws Amendment (Miscellaneous Measures) Bill 2024) makes amendments to the Australian Charities and Not- for-proﬁts Commission Act 2012 to allow oﬃcers of the ACNC to disclose protected information describing an ongoing or proposed investigation if the disclosure would prevent or minimise the risk of signiﬁcant harm.

The ACNC Commissioner must compare the harm likely to be caused by the disclosure to the harm that the disclosure may prevent as a safeguard before authorising disclosure of information under the exception.
