# Superannuation lump sums and income streams ¶14-120

A superannuation beneﬁt can be a superannuation member beneﬁt or a superannuation death beneﬁt (¶14-100), and can be either a lump sum or an income stream beneﬁt.

A superannuation lump sum means a superannuation beneﬁt that is not a superannuation income stream beneﬁt (ITAA97 s 307-65). The meaning of "superannuation income stream beneﬁt" (as deﬁned in ITAA97 s 307-70(1)) is discussed in ¶14-125.

Superannuation member benefits taxation The taxation of superannuation member beneﬁts (lump sums and income streams) received from a complying superannuation plan is governed by Div 301 and is discussed in ¶14-200. Generally, superannuation beneﬁt taxation is based on the amount of the payment, whether the beneﬁt is a lump sum or income stream beneﬁt, the recipient's age and whether the payer is a taxed or untaxed source (¶14-220, ¶14-240). For the taxation of beneﬁts received from a non-complying plan, including foreign superannuation funds, see ¶14-400 and ¶14-420.

Superannuation death benefits taxation A superannuation beneﬁt is a "superannuation death beneﬁt" if it is paid to a taxpayer (eg a dependant or non-dependant or the trustee of a deceased estate) because of the death of the person originally entitled to the beneﬁt (eg a fund member) (s 307-5(1), Table column 3).

The taxation of superannuation death beneﬁts (lump sums and income streams) from a complying plan is governed by Div 302 and is discussed at ¶14-270 and ¶14-280.

Special taxation rules for certain benefit payments Special taxation rules override the tax treatment in Divs 301 and 302 for payments from complying superannuation plans that are in breach of prescribed legislative requirements or are made in particular circumstances (¶14-300, ¶14-310), or are departing Australia superannuation payments (¶14-390).

Tax offset for certain military invalidity payments A non-refundable tax oﬀset (the veterans' superannuation (invalidity pension) tax oﬀset) is available to individuals who, during an income year, receive one or more superannuation lump sums that are payments of:

- invalidity pay within the meaning of the Defence Force Retirement and Death Beneﬁts Act 1973

  (DFRDBA)

- an invalidity pension within the meaning of the Military Superannuation and Beneﬁts Act 1991

  (MSBA), or

- a pension mentioned in ITAA97 s 307-70.02(1A) as discussed in ¶14-125 (ITAA97 s 301-275).

The tax oﬀset means that veterans who would face a higher tax liability as a result of Full Federal Court decision in Douglas (2020 ATC ¶20-773; [2020] FCAFC 220: ¶14-130) will have their income tax liability reduced to the amount it would be if the beneﬁts received were treated as a superannuation income stream beneﬁt. This tax treatment also applies to an eligible spouse or child beneﬁciary receiving a pension mentioned in s 307-70.02(1A). The oﬀset applies retrospectively to income years starting on or after 1 July 2007, to prevent adverse income tax outcomes arising from the Douglas decision.

When an income stream commences and ceases The commencement and cessation of a superannuation income stream is important to determine the taxation consequences for both the superannuation fund (¶13-130, ¶13-140) and the member in relation to superannuation income stream beneﬁt paid (¶14-220).

TR 2013/5 clariﬁes when a "superannuation income stream" commences and ceases. It considers the case where a member with an accumulation interest in a taxed complying superannuation fund commences a superannuation income stream on or after 1 July 2007 that meets the conditions of the deﬁnition of superannuation income stream in ITR2021 s 307-70.02(1)(a)(ii) (ie where the income stream is taken to be a pension in accordance with SISR reg 1.06(1)). Although there are various pension types that satisfy reg 1.06(1), this Ruling applies to a pension that satisﬁes SISR reg 1.06(1) and (9A)(a) (such a product is referred to as an "account-based pension" or a superannuation income stream in the Ruling). The Ruling also applies to an account-based pension that is a transition to retirement income stream (see SISR reg 6.01(2)(b) deﬁnition of transition to retirement income stream).

When a superannuation income stream commences Before a superannuation income stream can commence, all the capital which is to support that income stream must have been paid into the relevant superannuation interest (by way of contribution or rollover) from which the income stream is to be paid.

A superannuation income stream commences on the ﬁrst day of the period to which the ﬁrst payment of the income stream relates, as determined by the terms and conditions of the income stream agreed by the trustee and member and the applicable SISR or RSAR standard (¶14-125). Once an income stream commences, there is an obligation to pay the beneﬁts under that superannuation income stream until such time as that income stream ceases (TR 2013/5 paras 9–13).

When a superannuation income stream ceases An income stream ceases when there is no longer a member, or a dependent beneﬁciary who is automatically entitled, to be paid an income stream beneﬁt from the superannuation interest supporting the income stream. It is not the eﬀect of ITR2021 s 307-200.05 to ensure that once an income stream commences it can only cease once the amount in the relevant superannuation interest is exhausted. When a superannuation income stream ceases is determined by reference to the fund's trust deed, the SISR or RSAR standards, and the particular facts and circumstances of the beneﬁt payment in each case. Examples of these circumstances include:

- when there is a failure to comply with the SISR or RSAR pension payment standard in an income

  year

- when a superannuation income stream must be cashed as a lump sum by operation of SISR reg

6.21(2B) (¶14-310). This arises where the income stream is being paid to a ﬁnancially dependent child of the deceased member who attains the age of 25 (unless the child has a relevant disability).

The income stream ceases at the earlier of the time speciﬁed, if any, in the governing rules, or on the day the child attains age 25

- when the capital supporting the income stream has been reduced to nil

- when the member's entitlements to future income stream beneﬁts have been fully commuted

  into a lump sum entitlement (a partial commutation does not result in the cessation of the income

  stream)

- when the liability to pay superannuation income stream beneﬁts is transferred without the

  consent of the member to another superannuation fund under a successor fund transfer. If the

  successor fund is required to pay superannuation income stream beneﬁts, they will arise from a

  new superannuation interest in the successor fund as a result of a new superannuation income

  stream commencing to be paid to the member from the successor fund, and

- when the member receiving the income stream dies, unless a reversionary income stream takes
