# Meaning of digital game ¶20-430

For the digital games tax oﬀset (¶20-400), eligible expenditure can only be claimed if it is incurred in developing an eligible digital game. A "digital game" is deﬁned in ITAA97 s 378-20 to mean an electronic game that can be displayed on a portable electronic device or screen that allows for the playing of an interactive game. It includes a component of a digital game in certain circumstances where a foreign entity without a permanent establishment in Australia that owns a digital game engages an Australian developer company to develop the component.

An "eligible digital game" is a digital game that is primarily developed to be made available to the general public for educational or entertainment purposes and must:

- be made available for use over the internet

- be primarily played through the internet, or

- only operate when a player is connected to the internet.

A game that has a single player "oﬄine" mode may still be an eligible game if it meets the other eligibility criteria. A game will not be precluded from being eligible if it is also physically available (eg for sale by disc through traditional retail outlets).

Certiﬁcates issued by the Arts Minister for digital games For the digital games tax oﬀset (¶20-400), a company may apply to the Arts Minister for a certiﬁcate stating the total QADE in relation to eligible activity when it completes a new digital game, conducts development activity to port an existing game, or engages in ongoing development on an existing game (ITAA97 s 378-25). When a company applies for a certiﬁcate, the Arts Minister will determine in writing, as soon as possible, the total amount of the company's QADE in respect of the completion, porting or ongoing development of a digital game. Expenditure must be incurred on or after 1 July 2022 to qualify for the digital games tax oﬀset.

Completion certificate A company may apply for a completion certiﬁcate where it develops a new game, including a component of a new game, and the game is complete. Eligible expenditure incurred in developing a new game can be incurred over multiple income years. Provided the new game is an eligible game (¶20-430), a company may apply for this certiﬁcate to access an oﬀset in respect of an income year when:

- the game is completed in that income year

- the total of the company's QADE incurred in completing the game (possibly over multiple income

  years) is at least $500,000, and

- the Arts Minister is satisﬁed that the eligibility conditions relating to the company applying for the

  certiﬁcate are met.

Porting certificate A company may apply for a porting certiﬁcate in relation to a ported game. A digital game is ported when an eligible game that has already been completed is ﬁrst made available to the general public (not merely for testing purposes) on a new gaming platform. Provided the ported digital game is eligible, once it is ported, a company may apply for a porting certiﬁcate to access the oﬀset in respect of a particular income year when:

- the game is ported in that income year

- the total of the company's QADE incurred in porting the game is at least $500,000 (possibly

  incurred over multiple income years), and

- the Arts Minister is satisﬁed that the conditions relating to the company applying for the certiﬁcate

  are met.

Ongoing development certificate A company may apply for an ongoing development certiﬁcate for an income year in relation to the ongoing development of one or more completed digital games. Ongoing development means activities undertaken to maintain, update, expand or improve a digital game that has already been completed. This can include the addition of new levels, maps, characters, vehicles, storylines, cosmetic items, addressing bugs, work required to ensure that the game can continue to operate, or porting to new platforms. The ongoing development certiﬁcate can be claimed with respect to expenditure on multiple eligible games incurred in the income year to which the certiﬁcate relates. A company may apply for the ongoing development certiﬁcate in relation to a particular income year if:

- ongoing development occurs in relation to eligible digital games in that income year

- the total of the company's QADE on the ongoing development of the games in the income year is

  at least $500,000, and

- the Arts Minister is satisﬁed that the conditions relating to the company applying for the certiﬁcate

  are met for each game in relation to which that expenditure is being claimed.

Unlike the completion and porting certiﬁcates, the ongoing development certiﬁcate allows a company to claim QADE on multiple eligible games in the same income year towards its $500,000 threshold. However, the company can only claim QADE for the income year it was incurred, unlike the completion and porting expenditure, which can be recognised in respect of multiple income years. expenditure (QADE) Qualifying Australian development expenditure (QADE) For the digital games tax oﬀset (¶20-400), QADE is a subset of a company's overall development expenditure on an eligible digital game (ITAA97 s 378-40). The digital games tax oﬀset only applies to QADE incurred on or after 1 July 2022.

Development expenditure on a digital game is expenditure that the company incurs in relation to the development of the game. Expenditure speciﬁcally included for the purposes of the oﬀset includes employee remuneration for those directly working on game development as well as ancillary activities such as R&D, prototyping, and creating underlying game technology, such as game engines and anti-cheating controls.

There are also some items of expenditure that are speciﬁcally excluded including, but not limited to, company overheads, non-residents, marketing and distribution.

Development expenditure constitutes QADE where it is incurred for, or is reasonably attributable to, goods and services provided or acquired in Australia and to the extent that the expenditure is attributable to developing the game. Integrity measures operate to exclude certain expenditure relating to further subcontracting, associates of the company and non-arm's length transactions. Additional limitations also apply in relation to expenditure incurred by prior companies in completing or porting a digital game.

The amount of expenditure for the oﬀset does not include GST. The point in time that goods and services are considered to be incurred may be diﬀerent to when they are actually paid for or received.

The Oﬃce for the Arts' Digital Games Tax Oﬀset (DGTO) Glossary contains advice on the treatment of speciﬁc types of expenditure for the purposes of the oﬀset.
