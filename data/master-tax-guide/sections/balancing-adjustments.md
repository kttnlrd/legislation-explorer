# balancing adjustments ¶20-180

Where an R&D entity is entitled to an R&D tax oﬀset that is greater than their company tax rate, there are 3 clawback events and one catch up deduction event that can further change the tax payable by the taxpayer.

1. Recoupment: Where the R&D entity receives or is entitled to receive a partial recoupment for its expenditure on R&D activities from a federal, state or territory government body. 2. Feedstock: When the R&D entity sells (or uses other than in R&D) goods or materials produced in R&D activities from acquired or produced feedstock inputs. 3. Assessable balancing adjustments: When the R&D entity disposes of assets that were at some time depreciated or partly depreciated in calculating an R&D tax oﬀset. 4. Catch up deduction for balancing adjustments: When this disposal is for less than the written value of the asset used or partly depreciated in calculating an R&D tax oﬀset.

and balancing adjustments The clawback rules for recoupments, feedstock adjustments and assessable balancing adjustments in Subdiv 355-G, and the catch up deductible balancing adjustments identify when a clawback or catch up adjustment is required and how to value these adjustments. They provide a consistent method to correct the net after tax beneﬁt for the current and each prior year where the R&D entity claimed the R&D tax oﬀset on expenditure related to these adjustment events. The objective is that net R&D expenditure provides a beneﬁt equal to the applicable tax oﬀset rate and deductions for non-R&D expenditure are at the applicable company tax rate.

The amounts subject to the clawback adjustment calculation in s 355-450 are calculated for each adjustment event in the current year for each oﬀset year that the event relates to (eg feedstock expenditure is claimed in the year it is incurred and it is adjusted in the year the feedstock outputs are sold or used). This calculation starts with calculating the speciﬁc "oﬀset reduction" for each event by determining the diﬀerences between the "starting oﬀset" and the "adjusted oﬀset" and then subtracting the nominal tax deduction that may have been otherwise made. A similar adjustment is made to "catch up" on the missed net R&D tax oﬀset not previously claimed.

Recoupment The oﬀset clawback under s 355-440 occurs when an R&D entity receives or becomes entitled to receive a government recoupment on expenditure (including notional Division 40 deductions) incurred on, or in relation to registered R&D activities as part of an Australian government program. Note, this excludes where expenditure is not at risk.

Where the recoupment covers a project that includes non-R&D activities, the net recoupment will be apportioned so it only relates to the R&D. feedstock recoveries and balancing adjustments Example New Furniture Ltd is developing and commercialising a range of new products. It is eligible for a government grant to cover aspects of its R&D eﬀorts and commercialisation of the resulting new products. To be eligible for the $100,000 grant New Furniture must spend $2 for every $1 of the grant (ie a dollar-for-dollar grant). $60,000 of the grant will partly fund the $250,000 expenditure on R&D activities and the remaining $40,000 is for commercialisation. Each stage payment is dependent on the R&D entity achieving uncertain outcomes under the grant agreement.

New Furniture has a tax rate of 25% and is able to claim $250,000 expenditure for the oﬀset. As its group aggregated turnover is less than $20 million, it is eligible for the +18.5% refundable oﬀset. This will provide an oﬀset of $108,750 (0.435 × $250,000).

- the starting oﬀset is $250,000 × (25% + 18.5%) = $108,750

- the adjusted oﬀset is therefore $250,000 – $120,000 = $130,000 × 43.5% = $56,550

- for the 2021–22 year the $120,000 allows a deduction amount of 25% = $30,000.

  The amount that needs to be clawed back is:

Starting oﬀset – adjusted oﬀset – deduction amount, ie $108,750 – $56,550 – $30,000 = $22,200.

This is divided by the R&D entity's current year's tax rate to calculate the additional assessable income to clawback the $22,200, ie $22,200 ÷ 25% = $88,800.

Feedstock Section 355-445 is used to clawback any recovery made on the sale or own use of goods or materials produced by R&D activities by the common clawback method. The clawback event is the sale of feedstock outputs produced in the R&D activities outside of the R&D entity and its related entities or their application to own use other than for further R&D. This may occur in a diﬀerent tax year from the oﬀset year. The amount to be clawed back is the lower of:

- feedstock revenue, or

- the sum of (s 355-445(2)):

  – expenditure to acquire or produce feedstock inputs used in the R&D activity

– attributable expenditure on energy directly used to transform or process the feedstock inputs in the R&D activity, and – the attributable decline in value of assets used to acquire or produce those feedstock inputs prior to the R&D activities where the feedstock inputs are goods or materials acquired or produced that are processed or transformed (ie not consumed) in R&D activities that produce tangible products. GQHC [2024] AATA 409 conﬁrmed that this can apply to expenditure on biological goods (animals) and their feed where the feed is transformed or processed to provide nutrients that become feedstock outputs (eg meat and eggs) or provide the energy for that processing or transformation.

The assessable income is recognised each time a good or material is sold or is applied to own use other than as a feedstock input by the R&D entity (or any connected or aﬃliated entity) where that good or material is made from, or partly made from, tangible products made in R&D activities. Note, sales of like goods or materials in the same income year may be combined for convenience, but these sales may be in a diﬀerent income year or income years from when the R&D activity occurred. There may be many diﬀerent sales of diﬀerent types of products with diﬀerent proportions of feedstock outputs produced by diﬀerent R&D activities. To calculate the feedstock revenue, the market value of the sold or used products must be apportioned to exclude both the value added after the last R&D activity occurred that produced tangible products and the value added by activities that are not associated with any R&D activity. However, in more complex circumstances, the calculations rely on ensuring that product tracking is able to determine how much of each aﬀected sale is comprised of goods or materials made from potentially several sets of R&D activities on many component parts of the ﬁnal sale. Where the clawback is made in a diﬀerent income year after the R&D activity was undertaken, the R&D entity is able to claim the incentive on feedstock inputs, energy and depreciation in the year it performs the R&D and only repay this amount when it sells (or uses) the output.

Where it is known that feedstock outputs were or will be sold, the R&D entity can choose to exclude registering the acquisition of feedstock input expenditure as per Speciﬁc Issue Guidance: "Can companies avoid needing to report feedstock adjustments by not registering certain activities and not claiming associated feedstock input expenditure?" The concepts of "applied to the entity's own use", "transformed", "costs of producing" and others are considered in TR 2013/3.

A distinction was made in ID 2012/89 between the construction of a depreciating asset as a prototype that was subsequently used in R&D activities and expenditure on goods or materials that are processed or transformed in R&D activities that produce tangible feedstock outputs for sale or own use. Expenditure on capital assets does not form part of R&D expenditure under s 355-205. Instead, the notional decline in value of the asset is included by s 355-305. The R&D entity is not able to "obtain under section 355-100" (s 355-445(1)(b)) a tax oﬀset for capital expenditure so there is no feedstock adjustment trigger when the capital asset is used by the R&D entity.

Assessable balancing adjustments Net income on the disposal of depreciating tangible assets must be increased where these assets are used solely (ss 355-446 and 355-448) or partly (ss 355-447 and 355-449) in R&D activities. This is adjusted for depreciation claimed in prior years as part of the R&D expenditure. feedstock recoveries and balancing adjustments The clawback is made by recognising an additional s 355-450 assessable income amount. This is based on the s 40-285 assessable amount for assets only used in R&D activities or the adjusted s 40-285 amount if the asset was only partly used for R&D. These amounts must be calculated for each year that the asset's decline in value was included in calculating the R&D tax oﬀset (or the former R&D tax concession deduction).

This amount is subtracted from the previously claimed "starting oﬀset" to calculate the "adjusted oﬀset" for each year. This diﬀerence is reduced by the adjustment multiplied by the R&D entity's corporate tax rate in the year the oﬀset was claimed, all divided by the R&D entity's current corporate tax rate.

If the R&D entity included any notional deductions for the decline in value of that disposed of tangible capital asset because it was wholly or partly used in R&D activities under s 355-305 or 355-520 or former s 73BA or 73BH ITAA36, then the s 40-285 deduction can be increased to reﬂect R&D tax oﬀset amount unclaimed. The calculation in s 355-475 works in a similar way to that in s 355-450 except the adjusted oﬀset will be higher than the starting oﬀset and the end result will be an additional deductable amount.

Example A small business has 2 entities with at least 40% common ownership, Operating Co and Development Co. During the year all the costs of the group are paid through Operating Co. Under the concession after 30 June each year when the ﬁnal accounts and income tax return are being prepared, the business would calculate the amount spent on R&D activities in the year and transfer these costs and any related revenue to Development Co. Development Co would then register its R&D activities through Department of Industry, Science and Resources (DISR) and claim the concession with the ATO.

Under the incentive this method will result in Development Co not being able to claim these costs until the year it makes constructive payment to Operating Co. If it uses the procedure above it will need to register the activities through DISR in the year the activities occurred and then again in the year Development Co paid Operating Co for doing these activities on its behalf.

Constructive payment can be by bank payment or loan. The payment from the Development Co bank account to Operating Co must be made prior to the end of the ﬁnancial year. If it is by loan, then this needs to be properly documented prior to the end of the ﬁnancial year and Development Co should be able to demonstrate it has a clear intent to repay this loan. Any Div 7A implications need to be considered (¶4-200). If the 2 entities are in a tax consolidated group, then the head company is considered to be the R&D entity and the associate payment issue does not arise (¶8-000).

Taxpayer Alert TA 2023/4 identiﬁed a review of arrangements of concern where the R&D may not be being conducted for the R&D entity where it is largely conducted by a connected entity or associate of the R&D entity.

R&D partnerships Sections 355-500 to 355-545 provide for the application of Div 355 to R&D partnerships including determining and allocating the incentive to each R&D partner. An R&D partnership is one where all partners at the time are R&D entities (¶20-165). Where the R&D is undertaken by the partnership, the incentive is claimed by each R&D entity. It does not apply to normal partnerships between individuals, nor to corporate limited partnerships as these can include entities that are not eligible R&D entities. R&D expenditures, including the decline in the value of depreciating assets, as well as any balancing adjustment assessable income on R&D only assets are allocated to the R&D entities that are R&D partners in the agreed proportions or on the basis of relative partnership interests. For the purposes of determining whether the R&D entity is eligible for the +18.5% refundable oﬀset, the aggregated turnover includes the allocated proportion of the partnership turnover. The recoupments rules apply on the basis of allocated expenditure and allocated recoupments. Partnership net income and losses are amended to exclude R&D expenditures, deductions and recoupments under Subdiv 355-J.

Application to Cooperative Research Centres Under s 355-580 an R&D entity can include its monetary contributions to Cooperative Research Centre (CRC) Program as expenditure on R&D activities where these contributions are to be spent on R&D activities that are or will be registered by the R&D entity under IR&D Act s 27A.

To prevent double counting, where any R&D entity that subsequently incurs expenditure under the CRC out of these contributions by any R&D entity or from Commonwealth funding, the entity being paid to do the R&D activities cannot notionally deduct this expenditure as R&D expenditure.
