# Transfer balance cap regime ¶14-320

A transfer balance cap applies from 1 July 2017 to limit the total amount of superannuation that an individual can have in or can transfer to the retirement phase of superannuation, where earnings from the income streams are exempt from taxation (ITAA97 Div 294 ss 294-5 to 294-250). The cap also eﬀectively limits the amount of exempt income from assets supporting the income stream accounts in the hands of the income stream provider. The meaning of "retirement phase" and "retirement phase superannuation income streams" is discussed in ¶13-140.

The ATO keeps a transfer balance account for individuals who have income streams in the retirement phase.

The operation of an individual's transfer balance account (ie when and what credits and debits are made to the account) is discussed in ¶14-340. Transfer balance events aﬀecting an individual's account must be reported to the ATO by the individual's superannuation funds or in certain circumstances by the individuals.

If an individual's transfer balance account balance (including excess transfer balance earnings) exceeds the transfer balance cap, the excess must be reduced by commuting in full or in part the superannuation income streams in the retirement phase. An individual who has more than one superannuation income stream may choose which one to commute. The excess transfer balance determination process and issue of commutation authority by the Commissioner under TAA sch 1 Div 136 are discussed in ¶14-350.

In addition, the individual will be liable to pay excess transfer balance tax imposed by the Superannuation (Excess Transfer Balance Tax) Imposition Act 2016 on the excess transfer balance earnings in the transfer balance account to neutralise the beneﬁt received from having excess capital in the tax-exempt retirement phase. The tax rate on excess transfer balance earnings is 15% for a ﬁrst breach and 30% for the second and subsequent breaches in the year In summary, the general scheme of Div 294 is to limit the amount of capital used by an individual to support their superannuation income stream(s) by: (a) forcing a commutation of any excess over the transfer balance cap; and (b) imposing a tax on retirement phase income streams that have resulted in excess transfer balance (¶14-360).

Notably, the general scheme and excess transfer balance tax only work where commutation is possible and therefore cannot apply to deﬁned beneﬁt lifetime pensions and other superannuation income streams (ie capped deﬁned beneﬁt income streams: ¶14-365) which have commutation restrictions. Instead, individuals with these income streams are subject to diﬀerent rules for the purposes of the transfer balance cap regime under which they are not subject to excess transfer balance tax but diﬀerent tax consequences will apply if the balance in their transfer balance account exceeds both their "transfer balance cap" and their "capped deﬁned beneﬁt balance" (¶14-370).

*Refs: s 294-5*

personal transfer balance cap The general transfer balance cap is $1.9 million in 2024–25. The cap is subject to an annual indexation, increasing by $100,000 increments, in line with the CPI (ss 294-35(3); 960-285).

An individual's transfer balance comprises the value of the individual's superannuation interests supporting superannuation income streams in the retirement phase as at 30 June 2017, plus the commencement value of any new superannuation income streams after that date (¶14-340).

An individual's personal transfer balance cap begins as the general transfer balance cap at the time the individual ﬁrst begins to have a transfer balance. This means that at the time an individual ﬁrst commences a retirement phase superannuation income stream, the individual's transfer balance cap will be equal to the general transfer balance cap for that ﬁnancial year. In later years when the general transfer balance is increased by indexation, an individual's personal transfer balance cap amount may also increase by proportional indexation (see below).

Personal transfer balance cap and proportional indexation The indexation of the general transfer balance cap from 1 July 2021 means that there is not a single personal transfer balance cap that applies to all individuals.

If an individual has not used the full amount of their personal transfer balance cap when the general transfer balance cap is indexed, the individual's personal transfer balance cap will be indexed based on the highest ever balance of the individual's transfer balance account. That is, proportional indexation (in line with increases in the general transfer balance cap) applies to an individual's unused cap percentage where:

- the individual had a transfer balance account in the ﬁnancial year before indexation commenced,

  and

- at any time, the transfer account balance has not reached or exceeded the individual's personal

  transfer balance cap (ie the highest ever balance in the transfer balance account at the end of any

  given day did not reach or exceed the individual's personal transfer balance cap) (ss 294-35(1), (2),

294-40).

The ATO will calculate an individual's entitlement to proportional indexation and personal transfer balance cap based on information reported to the ATO (ATO website Calculating your personal transfer balance cap).

Individual taxpayers can view their personal transfer balance cap and transfer balance account (including any excess over the cap) at ATO online services.

Modifications for child death benefit recipients The transfer balance cap that applies to child dependants in receipt of a death beneﬁt income stream from a deceased parent is subject to modiﬁcations which generally allow the child to receive their share of the deceased's retirement phase interest (¶14-330).
