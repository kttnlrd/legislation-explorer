# Concessional contributions and excess contributions ¶13-820

The rules governing concessional contributions and the tax treatment of excess concessional contributions are set out in ITAA97 Div 291.

An individual has excess concessional contributions in a ﬁnancial year if their concessional contributions for the year exceed the concessional contributions cap for the year (ITAA97 s 291-20(1)). Excess concessional contributions in a ﬁnancial year are included in assessable income for the year and are taxed at marginal tax rates, with a tax oﬀset equal to 15% of the excess contributions.

Concessional contributions are generally contributions which are included in the assessable income of the recipient superannuation funds and are subject to "contributions tax" in the fund (see below).

The concessional contributions cap amount is $30,000 in 2024–25 (see "Concessional contributions cap amount" below). Individuals who have unused cap amounts from previous years are permitted to make "catch-up" contributions as discussed below.

Eligible individuals with SG contributions from multiple employers may apply to the Commissioner to opt- out of the SG regime in respect of one or more employers so as to avoid unintentionally breaching the concessional contributions cap (¶39-030).

What are concessional contributions?

In relation to an accumulation interest in a superannuation fund, an individual's concessional contributions for a ﬁnancial year is the sum of:

- contributions made in the ﬁnancial year to a complying superannuation fund in respect of the

  individual, where the contributions are included in the fund's assessable income or, by way of a roll-

  over superannuation beneﬁt, in the assessable income of a complying superannuation fund or RSA

  provider in the circumstances mentioned in s 290-170(5) (about transfers to a successor fund) (s

291-25(2)(b)). Certain contributions are not covered (see "Assessable contributions exceptions and modiﬁcations" below), and

- an amount allocated for the individual in accordance with the conditions speciﬁed in ITR2021

  s 291-25.01 (see "Allocations treated as concessional contributions and exceptions" below) (s

291-25(3)).

For the purposes of s 291-25(2)(b), certain contributions which are otherwise not assessable contributions of the recipient fund, as below, are speciﬁcally included as assessable contributions and are concessional contributions in respect of the individual (s 291-25(4)):

- contributions received by a constitutionally protected fund

- contributions received by a superannuation fund which are transferred to an investment vehicle

  (eg a life insurance company or a PST), and

- contributions which are reduced by the application of pre-1 July 88 funding credits or anticipated

  funding credits (ITAA97 ss 295-260 to 295-270; ¶13-125).

The meaning of concessional contributions is modiﬁed by ITAA97 s 291-165 for deﬁned beneﬁt interests (see "Deﬁned beneﬁt interests — concessional contributions" below).

Assessable contributions exceptions and modifications Amounts that are included in a superannuation fund's assessable income as assessable contributions under s 291-25(2)(b) (see above) are generally employer contributions, deductible personal contributions, certain roll-over superannuation beneﬁts and other payments to the fund under ITAA97 Subdiv 295-C as discussed in ¶13-125.

However, a contribution under s 291-25(2) does not include the amounts speciﬁed in s 291-25(2)(c), (d) and (e) as below:

- an amount transferred from a foreign superannuation fund that a member has chosen to include

  as an assessable contribution of the recipient fund

- an amount mentioned in item 2 of the table in s 295-190(1), ie a rollover superannuation beneﬁt

  that a member is taken to have received to the extent that it consists of an element untaxed in the

  fund and is not an excess untaxed roll-over amount (see further ¶13-125), and

- an amount paid by a superannuation fund trustee to a member's account under SISA s 99G(6), ie

  a refund of fees to a low balance account where the total fees for the year are above the maximum

  level.

Where there are special circumstances, an individual may apply to the Commissioner to disregard or reallocate to another year all or a part of the individual's concessional contributions (or non-concessional contributions) in a year (ITAA97 s 291-465) (¶13-875).

Transfers from a family trust to a superannuation fund were held to be concessional contributions in the absence of clear evidence that they were the beneﬁciary's personal contributions (Jendahl Investments Pty Ltd (as trustee for the Jensen Superannuation Fund) 2009 ATC ¶10-115; [2009] AATA 881).

Under SISR Div 7.2, a fund that receives a contribution in a month in relation to an accumulation interest must allocate the contribution to a fund member within 28 days after the end of the month, or within such longer period as is reasonable in the circumstances (SISR reg 7.08(2)).

A contribution made in one ﬁnancial year (year 1), which is allocated under SISR Div 7.2 to an accumulation interest of a fund member with eﬀect from a date in the subsequent ﬁnancial year (year 2) is included in the member's concessional contributions for year 2 as an amount covered under s 291-25(3). In such a case, the amount is not also included in the member's concessional contributions for year 1 as a contribution covered under s 291-25(2) (TD 2013/22). This is to avoid double counting of the concessional contribution — once under s 291-25(2) when received by the fund, and a second time under s 291-25(3) when allocated to the member.

Example Harry, a member of a retail superannuation fund, makes a personal contribution of $20,000 which is received by his fund on 30 June 2024. The $20,000 amount is placed into an unallocated contributions account established in accordance with the governing rules of the fund on 30 June 2024.

Harry's contribution is covered by a valid and acknowledged notice under ITAA97 s 290-170 of his intention to deduct the amount of the contribution.

On 1 July 2024, an amount of $20,000 is allocated to Harry's member account in the fund with eﬀect from that date.

The $20,000 contribution is included in the amount of Harry's concessional contributions for the 2024–25 ﬁnancial year as an amount covered under s 291-25(3).

Grossed-up rule for certain reserve allocations If a superannuation fund allocates an amount from a reserve to a member as a substitute for an actual contribution by the employer for the member and the amount allocated is net of tax (ie contributions tax has been paid on that contribution), the allocated amount is grossed up by 1.176 so that the gross contribution amount represented by the allocation from the reserve is counted as a concessional contribution (s 291-25.01(6)).

Example An employer is required to contribute $1,000 to a superannuation fund for an employee. Instead of the employer making a contribution, the trustee of the fund allocates $850 to the member's account (ie an amount equivalent to what would have been the after-tax amount if the employer had made the contribution). The $850 is grossed up by 1.176 ($850 × 1.176 = $1,000) to work out the amount that is taken to be allocated.

of reserves by SMSFs There is no deﬁnition of "reserve" in the ITAA97 or ITR2021. The Commissioner considers that "reserve" as used in reg 291-25.01 has a wider meaning for the purposes of determining excess concessional contributions than its meaning for SISA purposes (SMSFRB 2018/1) (ID 2015/21: allocation from a fund's self-insurance reserve; ID 2015/22: allocation from a pension reserve account supporting a complying lifetime pension; APRA's Prudential Practice Guide SPG 222 on management of reserves).

SMSF Regulator's Bulletin SMSFRB 2018/1 states that any need to maintain reserves in SMSFs is distinct from the need to maintain reserves in APRA-regulated funds. SMSFs should use reserves only in limited circumstances and only for speciﬁc and legitimate purposes. The ATO will scrutinise arrangements where an SMSF purports to hold an amount in a reserve rather than allocate it to a member's account outside of these circumstances, and consider the potential application of the SISA sole purpose test and the ITAA36 Pt IVA anti-avoidance provisions.

The ATO is also concerned that SMSFs may implement strategies utilising reserves or other accounts to circumvent restrictions under the tax law. These include the intentional use of reserves to reduce:

- a member's total superannuation balance so as to make non-concessional contributions without

  breaching the non-concessional contributions' cap, or reduce the balance below $500,000 to

  access the catch-up concessional contributions arrangements, or reduce the balance below $1.6

  million to allow the SMSF to use the segregated assets method to calculate its exempt current

  pension income, and

- a member's transfer balance account below the transfer balance cap to allow the member to

  allocate a greater amount to the retirement phase and thereby have a greater amount of fund

  earnings being treated as exempt current pension income.

Defined benefit interests — concessional contributions The meaning of concessional contributions is modiﬁed for deﬁned beneﬁt interests (Subdiv 291-C: ss 291-155 to 291-175; ITR2021 Subdiv 291-C and sch 1A).

A deﬁned beneﬁt interest exists where all or part of the superannuation beneﬁts payable to a person are deﬁned by reference to the person's salary, a speciﬁed amount or speciﬁed conversion factors.

If there are 2 or more superannuation sub-funds in relation to deﬁned beneﬁt members of a superannuation fund, the modiﬁcations provided in Subdiv 291-C and sch 1A apply to the superannuation fund separately in respect of each superannuation sub-fund (ITR2021 s 291-170.01).

The concessional contributions amount for a deﬁned beneﬁt interest is the sum of the contributions covered by s 291-25(2) and (3) (see above) to the extent that they do not relate to the deﬁned beneﬁt interest and the "notional taxed contributions" for the person for the ﬁnancial year in respect of the deﬁned beneﬁt interests (ss 291-165; 291-170). The calculation of notional taxed contributions for a ﬁnancial year in various circumstances (eg for a fund with 5 or more deﬁned beneﬁt members or where notional taxed contributions are taken to be at the maximum level of the contributions cap for the year) is prescribed by regulations (ID 2008/89: non-accruing member; ID 2008/97: choice by member; ID 2008/98: superannuation salary increase; ID 2008/162: productivity notional taxed contributions to PSS) (ITR2021 ss 291-170.02 to 291-170.07, sch 1A).

Under grandfathering arrangements, members with a deﬁned beneﬁt interest on 5 September 2006 with notional taxed contributions in excess of the concessional contributions cap are taken to have the notional taxed contributions as their concessional contributions cap (ITTPA s 291-170(2)). In addition, grandfathering rules ensure that members who held a deﬁned beneﬁt interest on 12 May 2009 with notional taxed contributions exceeding the reduced cap amounts (which came into eﬀect from the 2009–10 year) are taken to have their concessional contributions for that deﬁned beneﬁt interest deemed as their concessional contributions cap (ITR2021 s 291-170.07). These transitional arrangements cease to apply if the rules of the scheme change to increase the member's beneﬁt. However, deﬁned beneﬁt schemes may amend their rules to meet other legislative requirements without their members losing access to the transitional arrangements.

defined benefit schemes Contributions and certain other amounts relating to constitutionally protected funds and unfunded deﬁned beneﬁt schemes are concessional contributions and are counted towards an individual's concessional contributions cap.

LCR 2016/11 provides guidelines on the calculation of concessional contributions and amounts allocated by superannuation providers for ﬁnancial years commencing on or after 1 July 2017.

Concessional contributions cap amount The concessional contributions cap amount is $30,000 in 2024–25 for all individuals, regardless of their age (s 291-20(2)). The cap amount is indexed annually to AWOTE, in increments of $5,000 rounded down (ITAA97 ss 960-265; 960-285; see ¶42-320 for the cap amount in earlier years).

*Refs: s 291-20(2)), s 960-265*

cap An individual can carry forward unused concessional contributions cap from the previous 5 ﬁnancial years and use it to increase the concessional contributions cap in a ﬁnancial year if the individual's total superannuation balance (¶13-640) is less than $500,000 before the start of a ﬁnancial year (s 291-1: guide to Div 291).

An individual has "unused concessional contributions cap" for a ﬁnancial year if the individual's concessional contributions for the year falls short of the concessional contributions cap (see above) for the year. The amount of the unused concessional contributions cap is the shortfall amount. In such a case, the individual is entitled to contribute concessional contributions that would otherwise exceed the general concessional contributions cap amount for the year by applying any unapplied unused concessional cap for each of the previous 5 years to increase the individual's concessional contributions cap. For the purposes of increasing the concessional contributions cap, the amounts of unused concessional contributions cap for previous ﬁnancial years are applied in order from the earliest year to the most recent year. The ﬁrst year of entitlement to the carry forward unused concessional contributions cap arrangement is the 2019–20 ﬁnancial year (s 291-20(3)–(7)).

The issue of whether an election by a taxpayer is required to apply the unused concessional contributions cap from previous years or whether this applies by force of statute was examined in WTBW 2024 ATC ¶10-733; [2024] AATA 3268. The taxpayer in that case was assessed for Division 293 tax on the basis that he had no excess concessional contributions for the relevant year because his concessional contributions were fully oﬀset by his unused concessional contributions cap carried forward from previous years.

Relevantly, liability to Division 293 tax arises if a person's "taxable contributions" (ie the sum of the person's income for surcharge purposes and "low tax contributions" in a ﬁnancial year) exceed $250,000. The "low tax contributions" amount for a ﬁnancial year is calculated as a person's "low tax contributed amounts" less the person's "excess concessional contributions" for the ﬁnancial year, if any (¶13-620). The AAT held that an ordinary reading of s 291-20(3) (together with subss (4) and (5)) indicated that application of the carried forward unused concessional contributions cap was mandatory (which resulted in the taxpayer having no excess concessional contributions for the relevant year), rather than optional at the taxpayer's election.

The statutory language did not refer to or suggest a discretion or election on the part of the taxpayer or the Commissioner; nor does it provide any mechanism, such as a written election, for the taxpayer to opt to apply only part of the taxpayer's unused concessional contributions cap.
