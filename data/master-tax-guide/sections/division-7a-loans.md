# Division 7A: loans ¶4-210

For the purposes of the Div 7A deemed dividend rules, a "loan" includes: (a) an advance of money; (b) a provision of credit or any other form of financial accommodation; (c) a payment of an amount for another entity if there is an obligation to repay the amount; and (d) a transaction that is in substance a loan (s 109D(3)). A loan is taken to be made at the time the loan is made or anything happens which is within the definition of loan (s 109D(4)). Where a loan made before 4 December 1997 is varied on or after that day by extending the term of the loan or increasing its amount, a new loan on the varied terms is deemed to be made at the time of the variation (s 109D(5)).

The Commissioner's view is that the expression "financial accommodation" (see (b)) extends to cases where an entity with a trust entitlement has knowledge of an amount that it can demand and does not call for payment (TD 2022/11).

A payment that is made by a private company and is converted to a loan before the end of the company's lodgment day for the income year in which the payment is made will be treated as a loan made at the time of the payment (ITAA36 s 109D(4A)). This enables the recipient to repay the loan or to enter into a complying loan agreement before the company's lodgment day to avoid triggering a deemed dividend. Note that s 109D(1) and 109N(1) require the repayment to be made, or the agreement to be entered into, before the lodgment day (not by the end of the lodgment day). This means that the only prudent course would be to convert a payment to a loan at least one day before the lodgment day.

For the operation of the definition of loan where a private company is an unpaid presently entitled beneficiary of a trust, see ¶4-215.

For exclusions from the Div 7A loan rules, see ¶4-225. The exclusion that applies where a complying Div 7A loan agreement is put in place is discussed at ¶4-230.

Loan issues: private company presently entitled to income The way in which the Div 7A loan rules operate where a private company becomes presently entitled to income of a discretionary trust has raised difficulties over the years. The Commissioner's current views on this issue are set out in TD 2022/11. This determination applies in relation to present entitlements of private companies that arise on or after 1 July 2022. The determination is considered below under the heading "Present entitlements: 2022–23 and later income years".

TD 2022/11 replaced a ruling (TR 2010/3) and a practice statement (PS LA 2010/4) both of which applied with effect in relation to present entitlements that arose on or after 15 December 2009 and were withdrawn with effect in relation to present entitlements that arise on or after 1 July 2022. The former ruling and practice statement are considered below under the heading "Present entitlements: 2021–22 and prior income years".

Most recently, in a decision handed down on 28 September 2023, the AAT rejected the Commissioner's views of the Div 7A consequences of a corporate beneficiary's entitlement to income of a discretionary trust not being paid. Details of this decision are given below under the heading "Recent AAT decision".

Present entitlements: 2022–23 and later income years As noted above, the Commissioner's current views on how the Div 7A loan rules operate where a private company becomes presently entitled to income of a related trust are set out in TD 2022/11. The determination applies in relation to trust entitlements that arise on or after 1 July 2022. The determination proceeds on the basis that the phrase "financial accommodation" in the definition of loan that applies for the purposes of Div 7A has a wide meaning and extends to cases where an entity with a trust entitlement has knowledge of an amount that it can demand and does not call for payment. The determination considers 2 situations: (1) where there is an unpaid present entitlement (UPE); and (2) where a present entitlement is satisfied by a sub-trust.

UPE Where a private company beneficiary is made presently entitled to trust income and that entitlement is not satisfied, there is a UPE. A private company beneficiary with a UPE, by arrangement, understanding or acquiescence, consents to the trustee retaining that amount to continue using it for trust purposes if the company:

- has knowledge of an amount that it can demand immediate payment of from the trustee, and

- does not demand payment.

This constitutes the provision of financial accommodation (and, hence, a loan) by the private company beneficiary to the trustee for the purposes of Div 7A. The loan would be made by the private company beneficiary when the financial accommodation is provided which would occur at the point in time when the private company beneficiary has knowledge of an amount that it can demand immediate payment of from the trustee and does not demand payment of the amount.

If the private company beneficiary and the trustee have the same directing mind and will, the private company beneficiary is taken to have knowledge of the amount that it can demand immediate payment of from the trustee when the trustee does. Typically, this would be the case after the end of the income year (ie in the following income year) in which the entitlement arises. This will be the case whether the entitlement is expressed as: a fixed amount from the trust income; a percentage of trust income; or some other part of trust income identified in a calculable manner.

Sub-trust Where a private company beneficiary is made presently entitled to trust income, the trustee may be authorised to set aside an amount from the main trust and hold it on sub-trust for the exclusive benefit of the private company beneficiary. In this case, the amount set aside by the trustee ceases to be an asset of the main trust and forms the corpus of the sub-trust (the sub-trust fund). Where the trustee's obligation to pay the entitlement to trust income is discharged by the setting aside of the amount on sub-trust for the beneficiary, the trustee's obligation in respect of the entitlement to distributed income comes to an end and a new obligation arises for the sub-trustee under a separate trust. The private company beneficiary has a new right to call for payment of the sub-trust fund and can call the sub-trust to an end. A choice by the private company not to exercise that right does not constitute financial accommodation in favour of the trustee in its capacity as trustee of the sub-trust because the sub-trust fund is held for the private company beneficiary's sole benefit.

The situation is different if the private company beneficiary by arrangement, understanding or acquiescence, consents to the sub-trustee allowing the funds to be used by or for the benefit of the private company beneficiary's shareholder or their associate where:

- all or part of the sub-trust fund is used by or for the benefit of that entity, and

- the private company beneficiary has knowledge of this use.

This constitutes the provision by the private company beneficiary of financial accommodation (and thus a loan for the purposes of Div 7A) to the entity using or benefiting from the use of the sub-trust fund (the trustee of the main trust). The loan would be made at the point in time when the private company beneficiary has knowledge of the use of an amount of the sub-trust fund by or for the benefit of its shareholder or their associate and does not call for payment of that part of the sub-trust fund. The amount of the loan in those circumstances is the amount of the sub-trust fund that the private company knows (or is taken to know) is used by its shareholder or their associate.

If the private company beneficiary and the sub-trustee have the same directing mind and will, the private company beneficiary is taken to have knowledge of the use of the sub-trust fund (or part of the sub-trust fund) when the sub-trustee does.

Example On 30 June 2023 Trustee Pty Ltd in its capacity as trustee of the Lomax Family Trust resolved to distribute all of the income of the trust for the 2022–23 income year to Beneficiary Pty Ltd. The quantum of the income of the Lomax Family Trust for the income year is determined on 1 August 2023. Assuming a regular 30 June accounting period, for the purposes of Div 7A loan rules (¶4-230): (i) Beneficiary Pty Ltd will provide financial accommodation (and hence a loan) to Trustee Pty Ltd in its capacity as trustee of the Lomax Family Trust on 1 August 2023; (ii) Beneficiary Pty Ltd and Trustee Pty Ltd will need to enter into a Div 7A compliant loan agreement before the lodgment day of Beneficiary Pty Ltd for the 2023–24 income year; and (iii) Trustee Pty Ltd will need to make the first minimum yearly repayment under the loan agreement by 30 June 2025.

Present entitlements: 2021–22 and prior income years As noted, the Commissioner's views on the way that Div 7A applied where a private company was presently entitled to income of a trust for an income year before the 2022–23 income year were set out in TR 2010/3 (withdrawn) and PS LA 2010/4 (withdrawn). The effect of the ruling and practice statement were as follows.

Where a private company is presently entitled to income of a trust estate there will be cases where there is in fact a loan back by the private company to the trust (so that the present entitlement is extinguished). This includes an express or implied agreement for the loan back and could be the case where the trustee, acting pursuant to a term of the trust deed, applies the trust funds for the benefit of the private company beneficiary by crediting a loan account in the company's name and assuming a corresponding obligation to repay the amount (TR 2010/3).

Where a private company's present entitlement is not extinguished, the Commissioner's view was that where the private company and the trust were in the same family group and there was knowledge that funds representing the unpaid present entitlement were being used for trust purposes, rather than for the private company's sole benefit without any benefit from use accruing to the trust, the non-calling for payment of the unpaid present entitlement amounted to the provision of financial accommodation and, hence, the making of a Div 7A loan. Also, in these kinds of case the overall transaction between the private company beneficiary and the trustee included the beneficiary's authorisation (or acquiescence with knowledge) that funds representing the unpaid present entitlement could be used for the benefit of the trust and effected, in substance, a loan of money to the trust (TR 2010/3).

There would, however, not be a Div 7A provision of financial accommodation (or in-substance) loan if the present entitlement was held on a sub-trust and the use of the funds in the main trust was on terms that entitled the private company to the sole benefit of any income generated by use of the funds.

These views on the operation of the provision of financial accommodation and in-substance loan limbs of the Div 7A definition of loan were a departure from the position that was previously taken by the Commissioner in public documents and only applied to unpaid present entitlements that arose on or after 16 December 2009 (the date of issue of TR 2010/3 in draft form).

PS LA 2010/4 provided guidance on the administration of TR 2010/3 (including where there would be a relevant sub-trust arrangement). Further, in June 2011 the Commissioner issued a fact sheet which addressed certain supplementary issues that arose out of TR 2010/3 and PS LA 2010/4.

For present entitlements that arose in any of the 2010–11 to 2021–22 (inclusive) income years the sub-trust was required to be in place by the lodgment day (¶4-230) of the main trust for the particular income year.

By way of example of the above, if a present entitlement of a private company arose on 30 June 2021 and there was no loan back by the company within the ordinary meaning of loan, a provision of financial accommodation or in-substance loan would have arisen on the lodgment day of the trust for the 2020–21 income year. This would mean that the following courses of action would have been open: (1) putting the unpaid present entitlement on an acceptable subtrust arrangement by that lodgment day. This would mean that the present entitlement of the company would remain unpaid and the Div 7A trust rules (¶4-246) would be potentially applicable to a loan by the trust, or (2) entering into a Div 7A compliant loan agreement before the company's lodgment day for the 2020–21 income year (or paying the present entitlement out before that lodgment day).

The provision of financial accommodation or in-substance loan would effectively extinguish the company's present entitlement so that there should be no scope for the Div 7A trust rules to apply.

For a sub-trust arrangement to be effective, the funds representing the private company's unpaid present entitlement must be held on the sub-trust for the sole benefit of the company. PS LA 2010/4 gave 3 options to satisfy this requirement, 2 of which involved interest-only loan backs to the main trust (for 7 years or 10 years depending on the rate of interest) and the other of which is an investment in a specific income- producing asset. The practice statement explains these options. For the Commissioner's practice where a 7-year or 10-year loan matures, see PCG 2017/13. It should be noted that the withdrawal notice in relation to PS LA 2010/4 states that an entity may continue to rely on the Practice Statement in respect of trust entitlements conferred on or before 30 June 2022. This includes circumstances where the options envisaged by the practice statement (see (1) and (2) above) have been put in place after 30 June 2022 for such entitlements.

Recent AAT decision In a recent decision the AAT rejected the views of the Commissioner as to the operation of Div 7A where a private company is a beneficiary of a discretionary trust, the trustee exercises its discretion to distribute income of the trust to the private company beneficiary and the private company's entitlement is not paid (Bendel & Anor 2023 ATC ¶10-687; [2023] AATA 3074). The trust deed relevant in the case provided that, in those circumstances, any amount set aside for any beneficiary ceased to form part of the Trust Fund and was thenceforth to be held by the trustee on a separate trust for the beneficiary absolutely.

The AAT did not accept the contention that a separate trust in fact arose in any conventional sense that had the effect of discharging or replacing the obligation to pay an entitlement to income. The entitlement to be paid a share of the trust's income continued to exist. After considering various issues, the AAT said that the necessary conclusion was that a loan within the meaning of s 109D(3) does not reach so far as to embrace the rights in equity created when entitlements to trust income (or capital) are created but not satisfied and remain unpaid. The balance of an outstanding or unpaid entitlement of a corporate beneficiary of a trust, whether held on a separate trust or otherwise, is not a loan to the trustee of that trust.

In reaching its conclusion, the AAT had regard to the fact that ITAA36 Subdiv EA (¶4-246) contains specific provisions that deal with the taxation effect of unpaid present entitlements in favour of corporate beneficiaries in prescribed circumstances.

An appeal by the Commissioner from the decision of the AAT in Bendel & Anor has been heard by the Full Federal Court. In an interim decision impact statement the Commissioner stated that, pending the outcome of the appeal process, the ATO will administer the law in accordance with the published views relating to private company entitlements and trust income that are set out in TD 2022/11.
