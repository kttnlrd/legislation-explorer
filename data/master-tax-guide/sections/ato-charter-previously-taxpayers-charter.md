# ATO Charter (previously "Taxpayers' Charter") ¶1-205

The ATO Charter (previously known as the "Taxpayers' Charter") outlines what the ATO must do for taxpayers, what taxpayers are required to do, and what action taxpayers can take if they are not happy with an ATO decision or service. The Charter was refreshed, renamed and relaunched by the ATO in June 2023 following extensive community consultation. The Charter is now presented in an easier-to-understand format, with streamlined content that contains links to more detailed information on speciﬁc topics and commitments.

It is available in 25 languages in addition to English.

Broadly, the Charter states that taxpayers can expect the ATO to: treat them with courtesy, consideration and respect; act with honesty and integrity; be impartial and act in good faith; treat taxpayers as being honest unless given reason to think otherwise; work with people taxpayers have chosen to represent them; be responsive and provide timely, accurate and easy-to-understand information; provide assistance to understand or access ATO services; use multiple layers of cyber security and identity protection to keep taxpayer data and online transactions protected and safe; and explain taxpayer's rights, obligations and review options.

Important obligations on the part of taxpayers are to: treat all ATO representatives with courtesy, consideration and respect; be truthful and act within the law; respond to ATO queries on time and provide all relevant information; meet lodgment and payment obligations (or let the ATO know before the due date if that's not possible); and keep good records and provide them when needed.

Taxpayers can request that ATO decisions be reviewed internally (ie by an independent oﬃcer not involved in the original decision) or, following that, ask for an external review. They may also lodge a formal complaint (¶28-190). Taxpayers that remain dissatisﬁed after making a formal complaint should contact the IGTO and can also apply for compensation if they believe the ATO's actions give rise to a legal liability or they have ﬁnancial losses caused by the ATO's defective administration (¶1-200).
