# Regulation of superannuation entities ¶13-900

The SIS legislation, together with the Financial Sector (Collection of Data) Act 2001, the Financial Accountability Regime Act 2023, the Corporations Act 2001 and TAA, constitute the principal legislation governing the prudential supervision of superannuation funds, ADFs and PSTs. Compliance with the SISA and other regulatory Acts, insofar as is applicable, is mandatory for an entity to qualify as a complying superannuation fund, complying ADF or PST for tax purposes (¶13-100, ¶13-410, ¶13-440), or to be a registrable superannuation entity licensee under the SISA.

The generic term "regulatory provision" is used in SISA to refer to the principal prudential standards and obligations that apply to an entity under the above Acts (SISA s 38A). Where a regulatory provision is contravened, various forms of penalties may be imposed under the SISA and regulatory Acts, in addition to the potential loss of the entity's complying status and entitlement to concessional tax treatment under the tax laws.

Service providers to superannuation entities (such as auditors and actuaries) are also subject to the SIS prudential regime with respect to provision of services and certain duties to the entities. Superannuation fund auditors and actuaries are generally required to report contraventions of a regulatory provision to the ATO or to ASIC under the Corporations Act 2001.

The prudential supervision regime for superannuation entities and their service providers is complex with diverse rules for different types of superannuation entities. Trustees and superannuation professionals responsible for compliance management may refer to other Wolters Kluwer products such as the Australian Master Superannuation Guide 2024/25, Superannuation Law & Practice and Practical Guide to SMSFs for comprehensive coverage.

Particular SIS rules for the preservation and payment of superannuation benefits and prudential requirements for superannuation funds which are related to superannuation benefit payments are briefly outlined below. The acceptance of contributions rules for superannuation funds are summarised at ¶13-925.

Preservation, payment and portability of benefits A member's benefits in a superannuation fund (or an ADF) are categorised as preserved benefits, restricted non-preserved benefits and unrestricted non-preserved benefits for the purposes of the payment standards in Pt 6 of the SISR (commonly called the preservation and payment of benefit rules). Contributions made to or benefits rolled over or transferred to a regulated superannuation fund (or ADF) are taken to be preserved benefits unless the fund trustee is satisfied that they are not preserved benefits (SISR reg 6.15).

The SISR categorisation of benefits above is not the same as the division of superannuation benefits into its tax free and taxable components under ITAA97 for taxation purposes, as discussed in ¶14-000ff. However, superannuation benefits that are paid in breach of the SIS payment standards do not receive concessional tax treatment as superannuation benefits but are taxed as ordinary income at marginal tax rates (¶14-300).

Briefly, the SISR preservation rules set out a person's "preservation age" (¶42-250) for taxation of superannuation benefits' purposes, and other prerequisites which ensure that a member's preserved benefits and restricted non-preserved benefits can only be accessed if the member satisfies a condition of release in SISR sch 1 (subject to a cashing restriction on the form and payment amount in certain cases). The conditions of release cover a range of situations such as retirement on or after preservation age, attaining age 65, death, permanent or temporary incapacity, severe financial hardship or compassionate grounds, and payments under an ATO release authority or to temporary residents departing Australia. Not all conditions of release are applicable to all members. By contrast, a member's unrestricted non-preserved benefits may be accessed at any time. Once a member has met a condition of release with no cashing restriction, any preserved benefits and restricted non-preserved benefits of the member in the fund become unrestricted non-preserved benefits and are accessible at any time.

Under the SISR payment rules, the payment (cashing) of a member's benefits can be voluntary (generally by satisfying a condition of release and cashing restriction (if any), see above), or compulsory (when the member dies). With compulsory cashing, a member's benefits in a regulated superannuation fund must be cashed, or rolled over for immediate cashing as soon as practicable after the member dies (reg 6.21(1), (3)). This rule effectively means that fund members are able to keep their superannuation entitlements in the concessionally taxed superannuation system until their death.

The payment rules also govern the manner, time and extent to which a member's benefits in a regulated superannuation fund must be paid, and the persons to whom the benefits are paid. These rules apply in conjunction with other SISA obligations which require fund trustees to have regard to the SISA covenants, a member's death benefit nomination and any other duty that may arise in a particular case, such as seeking information about beneficiaries and other applicable obligations (SISR regs 6.17A; 6.17AA; 6.17B; 6.17C).

Penalties may be imposed if a person promotes an illegal early release scheme of superannuation benefits which does not comply with the SISR payment rules (SISA s 68B).

Benefit payments in specie and portability of benefits A member's superannuation benefits paid as a lump sum (but not as income streams) may be made in specie, provided the SISR investment provisions and the fund's trust deed are satisfied (reg 6.01(2), definition of "lump sum") (SMSFD 2011/1).

The SISR and RSAR also prescribe other portability of superannuation rules which allow fund and RSA members to move their superannuation interests by way of transfers or roll-overs. Individuals may request the consolidation of their superannuation accounts using the ATO electronic portability scheme or via myTax and myGov (SISA s 34A; SISR regs 6.33; 6.33A; Pt 6A; RSAA s 138A; RSAR regs 4.35C to 4.35P, Pt 4AA).

Special rules govern the transfers and management of unclaimed superannuation to the ATO, which include allowing the ATO to consolidate member accounts proactively in certain circumstances (¶13-950).

TFN provisions in SISA The TFN rules under the tax laws are discussed in ¶33-000 to ¶33-085.

Part 25A of SISA contains specific TFN provisions for trustees of eligible superannuation entities and regulated exempt public sector superannuation schemes (superannuation providers) and their members (SISA ss 299A–299Z).

Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Tax File Number approval No 1 of 2017 (F2017L01262) sets out the "approved manner" in which a TFN can be collected, reported, quoted, dealt with and used. The approval reflects the current Privacy (Tax File Number) Rule 2015 (2015 TFN Rule) issued by the Privacy Commissioner under s 17 of the Privacy Act 1988.

Part 11 of the RSAA contains TFN provisions equivalent to SISA Pt 25A, and a similar TFN approval outlines the approved manner in which a TFN can be collected, reported, quoted, dealt with and used by RSA providers and RSA holders under RSAA ss 134, 135(1), 136(1), 138(2), 139(a) and 142(1) (Retirement Savings Accounts Tax File Number approval No 1 of 2017 (F2017L01270).
