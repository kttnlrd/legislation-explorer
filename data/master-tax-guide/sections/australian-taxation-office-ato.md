# Australian Taxation Oﬃce (ATO) ¶1-200

The income tax system is administered by the ATO, which is headed by the Commissioner of Taxation, several Second Commissioners and a number of First Assistant Commissioners.

Each year the Commissioner presents a report to parliament, containing information on revenue raising, the administration of the tax Acts and the various enforcement strategies adopted by the ATO. The annual report for the 2023–24 year was released on 31 October 2024. Within the Federal Cabinet, the Assistant Treasurer oversees the ATO on administrative matters and the business tax reform process.

ATO advice The ATO provides various forms of advice to the general public and to particular entities in an eﬀort to disseminate the Commissioner's views on, and interpretation of, the tax laws (¶24-500). Depending on the form of ATO advice, various levels of protection against primary liability, penalties and interest are aﬀorded to those relying on the advice.

The ATO oﬀers taxpayers the opportunity to enter into its Early Engagement Process in order to seek preliminary advice in regard to the tax issues of complex transactions under consideration or already implemented. The ATO also provides a New Investment Engagement Service that oﬀers certain investors tailored engagement services with ATO specialists prior to the execution of signiﬁcant commercial transactions, supports business by providing customised guidance, and improves the process for foreign businesses looking to invest in Australia.

Review options Taxpayers who are dissatisﬁed with an ATO decision can seek internal and/or external review of such in accordance with the process outlined in the ATO Charter (¶1-205). The ATO also oﬀers a small business independent review service as a dispute resolution option to eligible businesses undergoing an ATO audit.

The service is available to small businesses with an annual turnover of less than $10 million in relation to disputes about income tax, GST, excise, luxury car tax, wine equalisation tax and fuel tax credits. The service entails an independent technical oﬃcer reviewing the facts and technical merits of the audit before an assessment (or amended assessment) is issued in relation to the audit. The review does not aﬀect the taxpayer's objection rights.

A taxpayer dissatisﬁed with an assessment has the right to object to it (¶28-010). Subsequent review by the Administrative Review Tribunal (which replaced the Administrative Appeals Tribunal on 14 October 2024: ¶28-090) or Federal Court (¶28-110) may be available. ATO decisions that do not relate to the assessment or calculation of tax may be subject to judicial review (¶28-180).

Inspector-General of Taxation and Taxation Ombudsman The Inspector-General of Taxation and Taxation Ombudsman (IGTO) is an independent statutory oﬃce whose function is to review systemic tax administration issues and to report to the government with recommendations for improving tax administration; the IGTO cannot review taxation policy. Thus, for example, the IGTO released a report in December 2023 making 9 recommendations to improve the ATO's administration of the Commissioner's remedial power. The IGTO also releases an annual report highlighting the oﬃce's key achievements and performance indicators for each ﬁnancial year.

The IGTO is also responsible for the tax investigative and complaint handling function. Taxpayers may complain to the IGTO if they are dissatisﬁed with administrative action taken by the ATO or the Tax Practitioners Board. Complaints may be in regard to: debt recovery actions; income tax assessment and instalment activity issues; decisions to bankrupt; conduct of audits; provision of advice; methods of handling inquiries; remission of penalties; handling of correspondence; delays in decision making; handling of private and public rulings; superannuation guarantee audits; operation of the PAYG instalment system; collection of FBT or PAYG withholding; and ATO assessments of compensation claims.

Compensation for loss; breach of privacy Taxpayers have a right to be paid compensation in certain circumstances where the ATO's actions have caused them loss. The 2 broad bases on which the ATO assesses a compensation claim are compensation for legal liability (eg negligence) and compensation for detriment caused by defective administration.

Information about applying for compensation is available on the ATO website. Applicants must complete the approved form "Applying for compensation" (NAT 11669). If compensation is not payable, a taxpayer can apply to the Department of Finance for an act of grace payment.

A taxpayer who thinks that the ATO has breached the Privacy Act 1988 and/or TAA Div 355 (¶1-220) in dealing with the taxpayer's personal information may complain to the Privacy Commissioner.
