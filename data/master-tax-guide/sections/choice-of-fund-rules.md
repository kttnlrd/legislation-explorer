# Choice of fund rules ¶39-260

The choice of fund rules in SGAA Pt 3A (ss 32A to 32ZAB) require employers to provide eligible employees with a choice of superannuation funds to which SG contributions made for them would be paid.

The choice rules apply separately to each employer of an employee. For example, an employee's chosen fund as a result of a standard choice form being given by an employer is only a chosen fund in relation to the operation of the choice rules to that employer (s 32X).

An employer that makes contributions which do not comply with the choice of fund requirements will have an increased SG shortfall for the quarter (see below).

The SG legislation (other than Pt 3A) does not aﬀect the jurisdiction, functions or powers of the Fair Work Commission or the operation of the Fair Work Act 2009. If a conﬂict arises, override rules in Pt 3A prevail over conﬂicting provisions in the Fair Work Act 2009 (SGAA s 5B(1)).

How an employer complies The choice rules apply to employer SG contributions made for employees so that potentially all employees must be oﬀered a choice of funds. However, contributions made for employees to certain funds or in certain circumstances, or under certain superannuation arrangements, comply or are taken to comply with the choice rules so that choice need not be given to the aﬀected employees.

Employers comply or are deemed to comply with the choice of fund requirements in one of the ways speciﬁed in s 32C, by making SG contributions:

- to an employee's "chosen fund", including a former employee's fund (s 32C(1), (10))

- to an employee's stapled fund, where there is no chosen fund for the employee (see "SG

  contributions to an employee's stapled fund" below) (SGAA s 32C(1A))

- to a "default fund" chosen by the employer (ie the employee does not make a choice of fund) and

  the requirements of s 32C(2), (2A), (2AA), (2AB) (about successor funds) and SGAR s 14 (about

  provision of death beneﬁt insurance) are met

- to the Small Business Superannuation Clearing House (ie the SGAA approved clearing house:

  ¶39-240) for the beneﬁt of an employee (s 32C(2B))

- to prescribed Commonwealth or public sector superannuation schemes, except where otherwise

  provided by regulations (s 32C(3) to (5))

- to a fund under or in accordance with certain industrial agreements or awards, or workplace

  determinations or enterprise agreements (s 32C(6) to (8)) (see "Employees covered by industrial

  agreements and workplace determinations" below), or

- to a fund under a Commonwealth, state or territory law prescribed by the SGAR (s 32C(9); SGAR

  s 15).

Industrial agreements, awards, workplace determinations or enterprise agreements may specify a given superannuation fund or funds to which the employer's contributions are to be made for the beneﬁt of the employee (see also "Protecting employers from breaching rules about contributing to a particular fund" below).

An employer's contribution to a fund does not comply with s 32C if the employer imposes a direct cost or charge on the employee for making the contribution to that fund (s 32CA).

SG contributions to an employee's stapled fund The stapled fund rules below apply to limit the creation of multiple superannuation accounts in relation to employees who commence employment on or after 1 November 2021.

For the purposes of the choice of fund rules (see "How an employer complies" above), if an employee has an existing "stapled fund" and has not chosen a fund for SG contributions to be made for them, their employer must make SG contributions to the employee's stapled fund (see "Compliance with choice rules for employees with a stapled fund" below).

Employers (or their agents) may request the Commissioner to identify whether there is a stapled fund for their employees (SGAA s 32R). Such a request can be made only for the purposes of complying with the choice of fund rules relating to stapled funds (SGAR s 17B). For example, a request will not comply with s 17B if the relevant employee already has a chosen fund or the individual is not an employee.

Basic requirements to be a stapled fund A superannuation fund is the "stapled fund" for an employee at a particular time if the SGAR requirements are met in relation to the fund (s 32Q). The basic requirements are:

- the fund must be an RSA or a complying superannuation fund or scheme (fund), and the

  employee must be an RSA holder or a fund member at that time

- insofar as the Commissioner is aware, the fund can accept contributions from the employee's

  employer, and

- the Commissioner can disclose information about the employee or their fund to the employer (and

  the employer's agent if the agent made the request) (SGAR s 17A(2)).

The second dot point ensures that a fund is no longer a stapled fund for an employee if, at that time, the Commissioner becomes aware that the employee's employer cannot make contributions to the fund. In practice, this may occur if the Commissioner notiﬁes an employer that there is a stapled fund (the ﬁrst fund) for an employee and the employer is unable to make contributions to that fund for the employee and notiﬁes the Commissioner of this matter.

In such a case, the ﬁrst fund is no longer the employee's stapled fund and the employer may further request the Commissioner to identify whether another fund could be the employee's stapled fund. If the above situation causes the employer to make contributions for the employee to a fund after the quarterly SG contributions due date (¶39-500), the employer may seek relief from the Commissioner for the individual employee SG shortfall incurred due to the late contributions (see "Consequences of non-compliance with choice rules" below).

The third dot point ensures that if the Commissioner is unable to provide information about an employee or their fund to the employer (and the employer's agent), that fund cannot be a stapled fund. For example, if an employee is classiﬁed as a security assessed client, the Commissioner can comply with obligations under the relevant legislation and policy framework restricting the disclosure of such information.

Tiebreaker rules for multiple eligible stapled fund cases If an employee has 2 or more existing funds that meet the basic requirements to be a stapled fund, the tiebreaker rules below apply to determine the employee's stapled fund: (1) the employee's stapled fund is the most recent fund that has been identiﬁed by the Commissioner to be the employee's stapled fund in the "selection period" (see below) (2) if (1) does not apply to any of the eligible funds, the employee's stapled fund is the fund that received the most recent contribution for the employee during the selection period (3) if (1) and (2) do not apply to any of the eligible funds, the employee's stapled fund is the fund that held the largest account balance at the end of the previous ﬁnancial year (4) if none of the above applies to any of the eligible funds, the employee's stapled fund is the fund selected by the Commissioner having regard to when the employee became a member of each of the eligible funds and any other relevant matters (such as the amount of contributions made on behalf of the employee and the timing and pattern of such contributions) (s 17A(3)(a)–(d)).

The "selection period" is the period starting at the beginning of the previous ﬁnancial year and ending at the time the Commissioner applies the tiebreaker rules (s 17A(4)). For example, if the Commissioner applies the tiebreaker rules to identify the stapled fund for an employee on 16 August 2024, the selection period is the period beginning on 1 July 2024 and ending on 16 August 2024.

Commissioner may change an earlier stapled fund notification The Commissioner may change an earlier stapled fund notiﬁcation given to an employer if there is an error in the earlier notiﬁcation and, if the earlier notiﬁcation stated that there is a stapled fund for the employee, the Commissioner is unaware of any contributions being made by the employer for the employee to the erroneously identiﬁed fund (SGAR s 17C).

Compliance with choice rules for employees with a stapled fund An employer's SG contribution for an employee complies with the choice of fund rules if:

- the employee has not chosen a fund to receive superannuation contributions

- the employer has requested the Commissioner to identify whether the employee has a stapled

  fund and the Commissioner has notiﬁed the employer that the employee has a stapled fund, and

- the employer's contribution is made into the stapled fund (SGAA s 32C(1A)).

The choice of fund rules are also met if the employer has been making contributions to an employee's stapled fund in compliance with s 32C(1A) and:

- the employee's interest in the stapled fund is transferred to a successor fund without the

  employee's consent, and

- the employer makes contributions for the employee to the successor fund (s 32C(2AB)).

In this case, the employer is not required to make a further request to the Commissioner about whether the employee has a stapled fund. This reﬂects that the employee will have an existing superannuation account with the successor fund.

determination/enterprise agreement The employer of an employee starting employment on or after 1 November 2021 cannot satisfy the choice of fund rules for contributions made to the default fund chosen by the employer unless:

- the employer has requested the Commissioner to identify whether the employee has a stapled

  fund, and

- the Commissioner has notiﬁed the employer that there is no stapled fund for the employee (s

  32C(2)(aa)).

The above rule ensures that an employer cannot comply with the choice of fund rules for an employee who has a stapled fund by contributing to the employer's chosen default fund thereby creating a new superannuation account for the employee.

Similarly, an employer cannot comply with the choice of fund rules by making contributions on behalf of an employee in accordance with a workplace determination or enterprise agreement made before 1 January 2021 unless:

- the employer has requested the Commissioner to identify whether the employee has a stapled

  fund, and

- the Commissioner has notiﬁed the employer that there is no stapled fund for the employee (s

  32C(6)(g), (h), (6AAA)).

However, employers can also continue to comply with the choice of fund rules by making contributions to a fund speciﬁed in the workplace determination or enterprise agreement if an employee has selected that fund in exercising choice.

All employers can use the stapled fund rules Employers must comply with the stapled fund rules to satisfy the choice of fund rules for contributions made:

- to the employer's chosen default fund (where an employee has no chosen fund), or

- in accordance with a workplace determination or enterprise agreement made before 1 January

  2021.

Other employers can also use the stapled fund rules in s 32C(1A) to comply with the choice of fund requirements, unless the employee has chosen a fund. This applies even where the employer could have satisﬁed another choice of fund rule (eg by making contributions to an unfunded public sector scheme or to a fund speciﬁed in a preserved or notional state agreement).

Meaning of chosen fund and default fund In SGAA Pt 3A, a fund means a superannuation fund, a superannuation scheme and an RSA (s 32E). The meaning of "chosen fund" is set out in ss 32F to 32J.

A "default fund" must be an "eligible choice fund" (ie a complying superannuation fund or scheme or an RSA: s 32D) which oﬀers MySuper products and complies with prescribed requirements in relation to the provision of death insurance cover or beneﬁts for members (s 32C(2)(c), (d); SGAR s 14).

The rules for the approval of registrable superannuation entity licensees (generally trustees of superannuation funds open to the public) that are authorised to oﬀer MySuper products, and the operation of MySuper products, are set out in SISA Pt 2C. The Fair Work Act 2009 sets out the criteria for the selection and ongoing assessment of superannuation funds that are eligible for nomination as default funds in modern awards.

workplace determinations Employers making SG contributions for employees under or in accordance with an enterprise agreement or workplace determination that was made before 1 January 2021 comply with the choice of fund requirements and their employees do not have the right to choose their own superannuation fund (see above: s 32C(6)(g) and (h)).

The terms "workplace determination" and "enterprise agreement" have the same meaning they have in the Fair Work Act 2009. Whether a determination or agreement is "made" and "applies" is determined in accordance with that Act.

Determinations and agreements made on or after 1 January 2021 For workplace determinations or enterprise agreements made on or after 1 January 2021:

- employers must allow their employees to choose their own superannuation fund, unless

  other circumstances exempt the employer from doing so (s 32C(6)(g) and (h)). In these cases,

  the employees must be given a standard choice form as required under s 32N (see "Choice

  procedures" below).

- existing employees on 1 January 2021 need not be given a choice of fund, but can request a

  choice form from their employer.

If, at the time a contribution is made, an existing employee does not have a chosen fund, the employer can continue to make SG contributions to the same fund to which the employer previously contributed for the employee under the pre-1 January 2021 workplace determination or enterprise agreement (s 32C(6AA)). An employer can specify such a fund as a default fund in the standard choice form given to the employee.

Where a workplace determination or enterprise agreement made on or after 1 January 2021 includes a term that restricts choice of funds, such a term is not enforceable (s 32Z). Terms restricting choice include those which specify the name of the fund to which the employer must contribute, or those which list several funds the employer must choose between to make contributions to.

contributing to a particular fund An employer is not liable to compensate any person for loss or damage arising from anything done by the employer in complying with the choice rules in Pt 3A (s 32ZA).

A Commonwealth or territory industrial award that requires an employer, or a state or territory law that requires an employer that is a corporation, to contribute to a superannuation fund for an employee is not enforceable to the extent that the employer instead makes the contributions to another superannuation fund in compliance with:

- Pt 3A in a case where the other fund is a chosen fund for the employee

- s 32C(1A) (contributions to a stapled fund) or s 32C(2AB)(b)(iii) (contributions to a successor fund

  of a stapled fund) (see above) (ss 32Z, 32ZAA).

Also, SGAA s 32ZAB provides that certain provisions governing certain superannuation schemes that require employers to contribute to a particular superannuation fund (or class of funds) for their employees have no eﬀect if the employer cannot make contributions to the relevant default fund (or class of funds) because a product oﬀered by the fund is subject to the SISA s 60F prohibition on accepting new beneﬁciaries into an underperforming product as a consequence of 2 consecutive fail assessments (¶39-240). Speciﬁcally, s 32ZAB applies to:

- s 16 of the Superannuation Act 2005, and the Public Sector Superannuation Accumulation Plan

  (PSSAP)

- s 15 of the Australian Defence Force Superannuation Act 2015, and ADF Super (within the

  meaning of that Act), and

- a provision of a superannuation fund prescribed by regulations that is analogous to the above

  provisions.

Choice procedures The choice procedures require employers to give eligible employees a standard choice form as set out in s 32N (eg on commencement of employment or if requested by the employee in writing), except in certain circumstances (see below).

The standard choice form must be given by the time speciﬁed in s 32N and must contain the information set out in the form (s 32P; SGAR s 17; Superannuation standard choice form NAT 13080).

An employee is not obliged to choose a superannuation fund or RSA, but if no choice is made, the employer's SG contributions for the employee are paid to the default fund speciﬁed in the standard choice form (s 32P(1); SGAR ss 14; 16).

circumstances

- the employee has chosen a fund (s 32NA(1)), or the employer is making contributions for the

  employee as mentioned in s 32C(3) to (9) (see above) (s 32NA(2))

- the employee is a member of an unfunded public sector scheme (except certain Commonwealth

  employees) or is a deﬁned beneﬁt member of a deﬁned beneﬁt superannuation scheme and certain

  conditions are met (ss 32F(3); 32NA(4), (7) to (9)) (see "Deﬁned beneﬁt schemes" below) (ID

2005/141: deﬁned beneﬁt fund cannot be chosen fund)

- the employee holds a "temporary visa" (as deﬁned in s 30(2) of the Migration Act 1958) (ss

  32C(2AA); 32NA(11)), and

- the employees' superannuation beneﬁts are transferred from a chosen fund or default fund to a

  successor fund as a result of a superannuation fund merger (s 32C(2AB); former s 32NA(1A) note).

Defined benefit schemes Employees who are existing members of certain deﬁned beneﬁt schemes cannot choose another fund (SGAA Pt 3A Div 4). These are schemes where a member's retirement, resignation or retrenchment beneﬁt in the fund would remain unchanged if the employer made contributions to another fund under the choice of fund arrangements (s 32F(3)).

An employer is not required to give an employee who is an existing member of a deﬁned beneﬁt scheme a standard choice form where:

- the scheme is in surplus and s 20(2) is satisﬁed (ie employee has been a member of the deﬁned

  beneﬁt fund before 1 July 2005)

- the employee has accrued their maximum beneﬁt in the scheme and s 20(3) is satisﬁed (ie the

  employee's accrued beneﬁt will not increase other than in prescribed circumstances, such as a

  salary increase or by indexation), or

- the employee is a deﬁned beneﬁt member of a deﬁned beneﬁt scheme and the employee's

  beneﬁts in the scheme will remain the same if the employer contributes to another fund for the

  employee (s 32NA(7) to (9)).

These provisions deny choice of fund to employees who are members of deﬁned beneﬁt schemes because their employers do not incur an increase in their SG shortfall in the circumstances above (see "Consequences of non-compliance with choice rules" below).

Existing employees who are not deﬁned beneﬁt members and who become newly eligible to join a deﬁned beneﬁt scheme do not need to be given a standard choice form unless requested by the employee or another subsection of s 32N applies. New employees who are not existing deﬁned beneﬁt members but are eligible to join a deﬁned beneﬁt scheme must be given a standard choice form within 28 days of commencing employment.

Consequences of non-compliance with choice rules Where an employer's SG contributions for an employee do not comply with the choice rules, the employer's individual SG shortfall for the employee for the quarter is increased by the amount worked out under SGAA s 19(2A) (for contributions to non-deﬁned beneﬁt funds) or s 19(2B) (for contributions to deﬁned beneﬁt funds) (known as the "choice shortfall"). The total choice shortfall amount is limited to a maximum of $500 in a particular quarter or notice period (s 19A). The Commissioner may, after taking account, wherever appropriate, of the operation of s 19A, reduce (including to nil) the choice shortfall amount under s 19(2A) or (2B) in respect of an employee for a quarter (s 19(2E) and note). The Commissioner must have regard to the written guidelines issued under SGAA s 21(1) when deciding whether or not to reduce the choice shortfall (SPR 2021/1: see F2021L01453).

Employer contributions to a deﬁned beneﬁt scheme do not attract an increase in SG shortfall where, for a given quarter, the scheme is in surplus, the employee concerned has accrued their maximum beneﬁt in the scheme, or the employee's beneﬁt from the deﬁned beneﬁt scheme on retirement, resignation or retrenchment is not aﬀected by the employer making contributions for the employee's beneﬁt to another fund (ss 20(1)–(3A), 32F(3), 32NA(7)–(9)). These provisions mirror the circumstances in which employers do not have to provide a choice of fund form to employees who are members of deﬁned beneﬁt schemes (see above).

An employer may also incur individual employee SG shortfalls for making late contributions due to the non- acceptance of earlier contributions by a notiﬁed stapled fund for an employee. The Commissioner may reduce the SG shortfall where the conditions in s 19(2G) apply (see "Reduction of SG shortfall for late contribution due to non-acceptance by stapled fund" in ¶39-300).
