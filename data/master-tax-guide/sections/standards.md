# standards ¶14-125

Superannuation funds, RSA providers and life insurance companies providing superannuation income streams which meet the SISR and RSAR pension and annuity standards (generally referred to as "minimum standards" or "pension standards") are entitled to an earnings tax exemption on the income derived from assets that are held to support these income streams (¶13-140). Income streams complying with the standards are "pensions" and "annuities" for the purposes of SISA and RSAA, and are "superannuation income streams" and "superannuation income stream benefits" under ITAA97 (see below).

A benefit that is not paid as a "superannuation income stream" cannot be a "superannuation income stream benefit" as defined in ITAA97 s 307-70(1). Instead, it is a "superannuation lump sum benefit" for the purposes of s 307-65(1) of ITAA97.

Superannuation income stream benefits A "superannuation income stream benefit" is defined as a superannuation benefit specified in the regulations that is paid from a superannuation income stream (ITAA97 s 307-70(1)). A superannuation income stream has the meaning given by ITR2021 s 307-70.02 as discussed below (s 307-70(2)).

standards A pension or annuity income stream that is paid by a superannuation fund (or RSA provider) from an individual's superannuation interest (¶14-130) in the fund or RSA must comply with the relevant minimum standards prescribed in SISR reg 1.05(1) (for an annuity) and in reg 1.06(1) or RSAR reg 1.07 (for a pension) in order to qualify as a superannuation income stream benefit for taxation purposes (s 307-70.02(1)(a) and (b)). The standards cover a range of matters such as annual payment requirements, commutation restrictions and rules on residual capital value.

The SISR or RSAR standards applicable to a pension or annuity are based on the first commencement date of the income stream, and whether they are account-based (eg allocated pensions, transition to retirement pensions) or non-account-based (eg lifetime pensions, market linked (term allocated) pensions).

An "account-based annuity" means an annuity that is provided under a contract described in reg 1.05(11A)(a) and meets the standards in reg 1.05(11A), and an "account-based pension" means a pension provided under fund rules that are described in reg 1.06(9A)(a) and meets the standards in reg 1.06(9A) (or equivalent rules in the RSAR) (SISR reg 1.03(1)). In this paragraph, the expression "account-based pension" is used to refer to both account-based annuities and pensions.

Where the prescribed standards impose a minimum annual payment requirement, the minimum income stream amount in a year is worked out in accordance with the Schedules in the Regulations applicable to the particular type of pension or annuity, based on its commencement date, the pensioner's age and account balance as at 1 July of that year (SISR sch 1A cl 3A, 3B; sch 1AAB cl 3A, 3B; sch 6 cl 10, 11; sch 7 cl 4A, 4B; RSAR sch 1 cl 3A, 3B; sch 1A, cl 3A, 3B; sch 4 cl 10, 11).

Commutation restrictions on certain pensions and annuities The SISR minimum standards include restrictions on certain pensions and annuities which prevent them from being commuted, except in prescribed circumstances (eg to pay a contributions surcharge liability or to give effect to a superannuation splitting entitlement) (regs 1.05(2)(f), (9)(h) and (10)(d); 1.06(2)(e), (7)(g) and (8)(d); 1.07B(3) and 1.07D(1)). The commutation restrictions have been relaxed in specified circumstances: see "Exception to commutation restrictions for certain income stream products" and "Limited period for full commutation of legacy superannuation products" below.

Standards for innovative income stream products Innovative income stream products which meet the standards in SISR reg 1.06A are also pensions and annuities under SISA and are superannuation income stream benefits for tax purposes (ITR2021 s 307-70.02(1)(b)). The governing rules for these products do not meet the annuity and pension standards in reg 1.05(11A) or 1.06(9A). Instead, the rules must ensure that payment of the benefit is made at least annually or the benefit is a deferred superannuation income stream, and the conditions set out in reg 1.06A(3) are satisfied.

For example, under reg 1.06A(3), no payment of the benefit is made before the primary beneficiary satisfies a specified condition of release in the SISR, the benefit is payable throughout the life of the beneficiary (primary or reversionary) after payments of the benefit start, the amount of benefit payments is determined through returns on a collective pool of assets or the mortality experience of the beneficiaries, and the benefit complies with the commutation rules as prescribed.

Death benefit payments A lump sum death benefit or new income stream benefit is covered by the "superannuation income stream" definition if it is payable from the time that a person who was receiving an income stream dies until as soon as it was practicable to pay the lump sum or new income stream benefit (ITR2021 s 307-70.02(2) to (6)).

This extended definition allows the earnings tax exemption for a complying superannuation fund to continue until the deceased member's benefits have been paid from the fund (¶13-140). standards

The components are worked out under the provisions set out in s 307-120(2)(a)–(e).

The taxable component of the superannuation benefit may consist of an element taxed in the fund or an element untaxed in the fund, or both. The determination of the taxed and untaxed elements is specified by ITAA97 Subdiv 307-E (¶14-150).

The tax free and taxable components of a superannuation fund member's superannuation benefit are taken to be paid in the same proportion as the tax free and taxable components of the member's interest in the superannuation fund.

A superannuation provider is not required to reduce either the tax free component or taxable component of a superannuation interest which is not providing an income stream as these components do not have to be calculated until a superannuation benefit that requires the calculation of each of the components is paid.

For a superannuation lump sum benefit, the tax free and taxable components are determined immediately before each benefit payment is made. Over time, the general proportioning rule for superannuation lump sums will result in the percentage of the tax free component of each benefit progressively decreasing and the percentage of the taxable component progressively increasing.

For superannuation income stream benefits, the proportions of the tax free and taxable components in each benefit payment are determined at the time the income stream first commenced to be paid. Where an income stream first commenced before 1 July 2007, the "deductible amount" of the income stream calculated under ITAA36 s 27H(1) constitutes the tax free component of the benefit, and a recalculation of the components of the benefit will occur when a "trigger event" happens (see "Deductible amount for pre-1 July 2007 superannuation income streams" below).

Proportioning rule The proportioning rule provides for the tax free component and taxable component of a superannuation benefit to be calculated by: Step 1 — determining the proportions of the "value" of the "superannuation interest" (see below) that those components represent, and Step 2 — applying those proportions to the benefit (s 307-125(1)).

The purpose of the proportioning rule is to remove an individual's capacity to reduce a potential tax liability by manipulating the tax components of a superannuation benefit when it is paid from a superannuation plan, including by way of a roll-over of the superannuation benefit. The rule ensures that a superannuation benefit is taken to be paid in a way such that each of the components of the benefit bears the same proportion that they have to the value of the superannuation interest (s 307-125(2)). Each benefit payment will therefore consist of the same proportion of the tax free and taxable component and the individual cannot choose to have the benefit paid from only a particular component.

Example A lump sum benefit of $100 is payable by a superannuation fund to a member. Just before the benefit is paid, the value of the member's superannuation interest in the fund is $1,000, of which $200 is the tax free component and $800 is the taxable component.

For the $100 lump sum benefit that is paid to the member, the tax free component is $20 and the taxable component is $80.

Where a fund member fully commutes a current pension with an "internal roll-over" of the remaining balance of the pension account to the accumulation phase, the fund must recalculate (in accordance with s 307-125) the tax free component and taxable component of any new benefit subsequently paid from the fund.

Example Amy receives an account-based pension from her SMSF with her superannuation interest in the fund which is made up of 50% tax free component (TFC) and 50% taxable component (TC).

On 30 June 2024, Amy commutes the pension and the commutation lump sum was returned to the accumulation phase in the fund on that day.

Assume that Amy's pension account balance was $60,000 on 30 June 2024. The TFC and TC of the commutation lump sum would be $30,000 for each component in accordance with s 307-125(3)(c).

On 1 December 2024, Amy decides to commence a new account-based pension with the full amount of her accumulation interest ($75,000), then comprising the $60,000 commutation amount from the first pension and $15,000 investment returns up to 30 November 2024. rule At the time just before the new pension is commenced, the percentage of the TFC of Amy's accumulation interest is 40% ($30,000 ÷ $75,000) and the percentage of the TC of the interest is 60% (100% − 40%). Amy's new account-based pension will have a TFC percentage of 40% and a TC percentage of 60%.

Superannuation interest A "superannuation interest" is an interest in a superannuation fund, an ADF, an RSA or a superannuation annuity (s 995-1(1)) (see "Step 1. The superannuation interest" below). The "value" of a superannuation interest is the value determined by a method specified in the regulations or, if method is prescribed, the total amount of all superannuation lump sums that could be payable from the interest at that time (s 307-205).

A superannuation benefit that is an unclaimed money payment or a small superannuation account payment is treated as a superannuation benefit paid from a superannuation interest, and the amount of the benefit is the value of that interest just before the benefit is paid (s 307-125(6)).

There are 2 steps when determining the value of a superannuation interest: Step 1 — work out what the superannuation interest is, and Step 2 — work out the value of the superannuation interest.

Step 1. The superannuation interest A superannuation interest may be treated as 2 or more interests, or 2 or more interests may be treated as one interest (s 307-200). Based on the nature of the superannuation interest, the following rules apply:

- Every amount, benefit or entitlement that a member holds in an SMSF comprise one

  superannuation interest unless provided otherwise by ITR2021 Subdiv 307-D (ITR2021 s

307-200.02).

- A superannuation interest within a public sector superannuation scheme is to be treated as

  more than one interest where different amounts are subject to different benefit payment rules and

  administrative rules prevent them being treated as a single interest. This means that every amount,

  benefit or entitlement within a public sector scheme is able to be treated as a separate interest, eg

  an amount sourced from contributions or earnings into the scheme can be treated as a separate

  interest to ensure that funded amounts within a public sector scheme are treated separately from

  unfunded amounts (s 307-200.03).

- If a superannuation income stream is payable (or will be payable and it is a deferred

  superannuation income stream), an amount held in the fund supporting the income stream is

  always treated as a separate superannuation interest (s 307-200.05). This applies for the purposes

  of the proportioning rule to ensure all payments made from the superannuation interest supporting

  the income stream have the same proportions of tax free component and taxable component (TR

2013/5 paras 88–90).

For tax purposes, a "superannuation income stream" means an income stream that is taken to be an annuity or pension under the SISA or RSAA because the income stream is provided under the rules of a superannuation fund or a contract which meet the pension and annuity standards in the SISR or RSAR (ITR2021 s 307-70.02: ¶14-125).

income streams The "deductible amount" of a superannuation income stream which first commenced before 1 July 2007 (as calculated under ITAA36 s 27H(1): ¶14-510) constitutes the tax free component of the benefit until a trigger event (see below) occurs (ITTPA s 307-125).

*Refs: s 307-125)*

rule Assume that Michael, age 57, is receiving a superannuation pension which first commenced before 1 July 2007. The annual deductible amount for the pension is $6,000 (as calculated when the pension first commenced to be paid) and Michael receives monthly pension payments of $1,000 (ie $12,000 pa). The annual deductible amount is apportioned to each pension benefit made to him and this apportioned amount is the tax free component of the pension benefit received by him for an income year.

The tax free component of each $1,000 pension benefit is calculated as follows: Step 1 — Calculate the proportion of the pension benefit to the total pension benefit received in the year, ie $1,000/($12,000) = 8.33% Step 2 — Multiply the annual deductible amount by the percentage calculated in Step 1, ie $6,000 × 8.33% = $500 The tax free component of each pension benefit is $500 and the taxable component is $500 ($1,000 − $500).

Trigger events Four events trigger the recalculation of the tax free and taxable components of a superannuation income stream that first commenced to be paid before 1 July 2007 for continuing payments of the income stream after the events. The trigger events are the holder of the income stream was 60 years of age or over at 1 July 2007, the holder turns 60 years old or has died, or the income stream has been partially or wholly commuted (ITTPA s 307-125(3)).

The recalculation is made at the earliest of the events in accordance with the methods set out in ITTPA s 307-125(4) to (8).

Modification for lump sum disability benefits If an individual receives a superannuation lump sum that is a "disability superannuation benefit" (¶14-220), the tax free component of the benefit is the sum of the tax free component of the benefit and the amount worked out using the formula in s 307-145(3), as below: Days to retirement Amount of benefit × Service days + Days to retirement In the formula, "Days to retirement" is the number of days from the day on which the person stopped being capable of being gainfully employed to his/her last retirement day, and "Service days" is the number of days in the service period for the lump sum.

The tax free component cannot exceed the amount of the benefit. The balance of the superannuation benefit is the taxable component of the benefit.

If a fund member's benefits are transferred from an accumulation sub-fund to a pension sub-fund to facilitate the commencement of an account-based pension, the transfer is not the payment of a superannuation lump sum and s 307-145 cannot be applied to increase the tax free component of that superannuation lump sum (ID 2009/125).

Tax treatment of military superannuation benefits Two requirements must be met in order for a superannuation payment to come within the meaning of "superannuation income stream benefit" in s 307-70(1). It must be a superannuation benefit and is paid from a "superannuation income stream" (¶14-125). A superannuation benefit that does not satisfy these requirements defaults to be a superannuation lump sum under ITAA97 s 307-65(1).

The Full Federal Court considered 3 broad issues, as below, in test cases brought by the Commissioner against AAT decisions on the taxation of invalidity benefits received pursuant to the Military Superannuation and Benefits Act 1991 (MSBA) (Burns 2020 ATC ¶20-773; [2020] FCAFC 220, Walker 2020 ATC ¶20-773; [2020] FCAFC 220) and the Defence Force Retirement and Death Benefits Act 1973 (DFRDBA) (Douglas 2020 ATC ¶20-773; [2020] FCAFC 220): 1. whether the income tax regulations (ITR97 as it then applied) did specify superannuation benefits to be superannuation income stream benefits rule 2. whether the invalidity benefit pension was a superannuation income stream under subpara 995-1.01(1)(a)(ii) of ITR97 because it was a pension under the SIS Act where the rules under which the benefits were paid complied with the SISR pension standards, and 3. in Burns and Douglas, whether the invalidity pension was a superannuation income stream under para 995-1.01(1)(b) of ITR97 because it was an income stream that had commenced before 20 September 2007.

The Full Court determined that invalidity pension payments under the MSBA superannuation scheme and the invalidity pay under the DFRDBA (collectively referred to as invalidity benefits) commencing on or after 20 September 2007 are not superannuation income streams. This meant that the invalidity benefits are taxed as superannuation lump sum payments, rather than superannuation income stream benefits (Commissioner's appeal dismissed in Walker and Douglas and upheld in Burns; ATO Decision Impact Statement).

The Douglas decision identified a discrepancy in the statutory definition of a superannuation income stream which, contrary to the policy intent of the 2007 Simple Super changes, had the effect that the Douglas types of pensions did not meet the superannuation income stream definition and defaulted to being taxed as superannuation lump sums.

Post-Douglas — tax treatment of military invalidity benefits To address the issues raised by the Douglas decision, ATO determinations provide an alternative method to calculate the tax free and taxable components of a superannuation benefit under ITAA97 s 307-125 for recipients of military invalidity pensions affected by the Douglas decision to ensure that no affected pension recipient is disadvantaged by the recalculation (MS 2021/1: F2021L00901 — payments before 1 July 2021; LI 2022/1: F2022L00004 — payments between 1 July 2021 and 30 June 2022; LI 2022/3: F2022L01347 — payments during the 2022–23 financial year) (ATO legal database–Legislation and supporting material/ Legislative instruments).

An ATO remediation program provided for historical income tax assessments and superannuation reporting for individuals impacted by the Douglas decision (Fact Sheet Treatment of military invalidity benefits following Full Federal Court decision).

Section 301-100 is a transitional arrangement that applies for income years up to and including the 2021–22 income year. The section applies to certain recipients of permanent incapacity benefits who had previously had their superannuation benefits assessed on the basis that they were a superannuation lump sum, and that assessment was first made before 4 December 2020 (the date of the Douglas decision). In practice, this covers taxpayers whose superannuation benefits commenced before the 2020–21 income year.

The s 301-100 transitional arrangement has been extended for 2 further years (the 2022–23 and 2023– 24 income years) to provide further time for affected funds and individuals to report incapacity benefits as superannuation income streams in accordance with the Schedule 9 amendments (Income Tax (Transitional Provisions) (Permanent Incapacity Benefits) Rules 2024 (F2024L00129), as amended by F2024L01471).

The Rules mean that if the Commissioner makes an assessment on the basis that a permanent incapacity benefit is a superannuation lump sum for the 2022–23 or 2023–24 income year, the Commissioner is prevented from amending that assessment on the basis of the Schedule 9 amendments. However, the Commissioner may amend the assessment for reasons such as fraud or dishonesty, or if the taxpayer requests a review of the assessment.
