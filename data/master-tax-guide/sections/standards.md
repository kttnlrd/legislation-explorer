# standards ¶14-125

Superannuation funds, RSA providers and life insurance companies providing superannuation income streams which meet the SISR and RSAR pension and annuity standards (generally referred to as "minimum standards" or "pension standards") are entitled to an earnings tax exemption on the income derived from assets that are held to support these income streams (¶13-140). Income streams complying with the standards are "pensions" and "annuities" for the purposes of SISA and RSAA, and are "superannuation income streams" and "superannuation income stream beneﬁts" under ITAA97 (see below).

A beneﬁt that is not paid as a "superannuation income stream" cannot be a "superannuation income stream beneﬁt" as deﬁned in ITAA97 s 307-70(1). Instead, it is a "superannuation lump sum beneﬁt" for the purposes of s 307-65(1) of ITAA97.

A superannuation income stream has the meaning given by ITR2021 s 307-70.02 as discussed below (s 307-70(2)).

standards A pension or annuity income stream that is paid by a superannuation fund (or RSA provider) from an individual's superannuation interest (¶14-130) in the fund or RSA must comply with the relevant minimum standards prescribed in SISR reg 1.05(1) (for an annuity) and in reg 1.06(1) or RSAR reg 1.07 (for a pension) in order to qualify as a superannuation income stream beneﬁt for taxation purposes (s 307-70.02(1)(a) and (b)). The standards cover a range of matters such as annual payment requirements, commutation restrictions and rules on residual capital value.

The SISR or RSAR standards applicable to a pension or annuity are based on the ﬁrst commencement date of the income stream, and whether they are account-based (eg allocated pensions, transition to retirement pensions) or non-account-based (eg lifetime pensions, market linked (term allocated) pensions).

An "account-based annuity" means an annuity that is provided under a contract described in reg 1.05(11A)(a) and meets the standards in reg 1.05(11A), and an "account-based pension" means a pension provided under fund rules that are described in reg 1.06(9A)(a) and meets the standards in reg 1.06(9A) (or equivalent rules in the RSAR) (SISR reg 1.03(1)). In this paragraph, the expression "account-based pension" is used to refer to both account-based annuities and pensions.

Where the prescribed standards impose a minimum annual payment requirement, the minimum income stream amount in a year is worked out in accordance with the Schedules in the Regulations applicable to the particular type of pension or annuity, based on its commencement date, the pensioner's age and account balance as at 1 July of that year (SISR sch 1A cl 3A, 3B; sch 1AAB cl 3A, 3B; sch 6 cl 10, 11; sch 7 cl 4A, 4B; RSAR sch 1 cl 3A, 3B; sch 1A, cl 3A, 3B; sch 4 cl 10, 11).

Commutation restrictions on certain pensions and annuities The SISR minimum standards include restrictions on certain pensions and annuities which prevent them from being commuted, except in prescribed circumstances (eg to pay a contributions surcharge liability or to give eﬀect to a superannuation splitting entitlement) (regs 1.05(2)(f), (9)(h) and (10)(d); 1.06(2)(e), (7)(g) and (8)(d); 1.07B(3) and 1.07D(1)). The commutation restrictions have been relaxed in speciﬁed circumstances: see "Exception to commutation restrictions for certain income stream products" and "Limited period for full commutation of legacy superannuation products" below.

Standards for innovative income stream products Innovative income stream products which meet the standards in SISR reg 1.06A are also pensions and annuities under SISA and are superannuation income stream beneﬁts for tax purposes (ITR2021 s 307-70.02(1)(b)). The governing rules for these products do not meet the annuity and pension standards in reg 1.05(11A) or 1.06(9A). Instead, the rules must ensure that payment of the beneﬁt is made at least annually or the beneﬁt is a deferred superannuation income stream, and the conditions set out in reg 1.06A(3) are satisﬁed.

For example, under reg 1.06A(3), no payment of the beneﬁt is made before the primary beneﬁciary satisﬁes a speciﬁed condition of release in the SISR, the beneﬁt is payable throughout the life of the beneﬁciary (primary or reversionary) after payments of the beneﬁt start, the amount of beneﬁt payments is determined through returns on a collective pool of assets or the mortality experience of the beneﬁciaries, and the beneﬁt complies with the commutation rules as prescribed.

Death benefit payments A lump sum death beneﬁt or new income stream beneﬁt is covered by the "superannuation income stream" deﬁnition if it is payable from the time that a person who was receiving an income stream dies until as soon as it was practicable to pay the lump sum or new income stream beneﬁt (ITR2021 s 307-70.02(2) to (6)).

This extended deﬁnition allows the earnings tax exemption for a complying superannuation fund to continue until the deceased member's beneﬁts have been paid from the fund (¶13-140). standards

For a superannuation lump sum beneﬁt, the tax free and taxable components are determined immediately before each beneﬁt payment is made. Over time, the general proportioning rule for superannuation lump sums will result in the percentage of the tax free component of each beneﬁt progressively decreasing and the percentage of the taxable component progressively increasing.

For superannuation income stream beneﬁts, the proportions of the tax free and taxable components in each beneﬁt payment are determined at the time the income stream ﬁrst commenced to be paid. Proportioning rule The proportioning rule provides for the tax free component and taxable component of a superannuation beneﬁt to be calculated by: Step 1 — determining the proportions of the "value" of the "superannuation interest" (see below) that those components represent, and Step 2 — applying those proportions to the beneﬁt (s 307-125(1)).

The purpose of the proportioning rule is to remove an individual's capacity to reduce a potential tax liability by manipulating the tax components of a superannuation beneﬁt when it is paid from a superannuation plan, including by way of a roll-over of the superannuation beneﬁt. The rule ensures that a superannuation beneﬁt is taken to be paid in a way such that each of the components of the beneﬁt bears the same proportion that they have to the value of the superannuation interest (s 307-125(2)). Each beneﬁt payment will therefore consist of the same proportion of the tax free and taxable component and the individual cannot choose to have the beneﬁt paid from only a particular component.

Example A lump sum beneﬁt of $100 is payable by a superannuation fund to a member. Just before the beneﬁt is paid, the value of the member's superannuation interest in the fund is $1,000, of which $200 is the tax free component and $800 is the taxable component.

For the $100 lump sum beneﬁt that is paid to the member, the tax free component is $20 and the taxable component is $80.

Where a fund member fully commutes a current pension with an "internal roll-over" of the remaining balance of the pension account to the accumulation phase, the fund must recalculate (in accordance with s 307-125) the tax free component and taxable component of any new beneﬁt subsequently paid from the fund.

Example Amy receives an account-based pension from her SMSF with her superannuation interest in the fund which is made up of 50% tax free component (TFC) and 50% taxable component (TC).

On 30 June 2024, Amy commutes the pension and the commutation lump sum was returned to the accumulation phase in the fund on that day.

Assume that Amy's pension account balance was $60,000 on 30 June 2024. The TFC and TC of the commutation lump sum would be $30,000 for each component in accordance with s 307-125(3)(c).

On 1 December 2024, Amy decides to commence a new account-based pension with the full amount of her accumulation interest ($75,000), then comprising the $60,000 commutation amount from the ﬁrst pension and $15,000 investment returns up to 30 November 2024. rule At the time just before the new pension is commenced, the percentage of the TFC of Amy's accumulation interest is 40% ($30,000 ÷ $75,000) and the percentage of the TC of the interest is 60% (100% − 40%). Amy's new account-based pension will have a TFC percentage of 40% and a TC percentage of 60%.

Superannuation interest A "superannuation interest" is an interest in a superannuation fund, an ADF, an RSA or a superannuation annuity (s 995-1(1)) (see "Step 1. The superannuation interest" below). The "value" of a superannuation interest is the value determined by a method speciﬁed in the regulations or, if method is prescribed, the total amount of all superannuation lump sums that could be payable from the interest at that time (s 307-205).

A superannuation beneﬁt that is an unclaimed money payment or a small superannuation account payment is treated as a superannuation beneﬁt paid from a superannuation interest, and the amount of the beneﬁt is the value of that interest just before the beneﬁt is paid (s 307-125(6)).

There are 2 steps when determining the value of a superannuation interest: Step 1 — work out what the superannuation interest is, and Step 2 — work out the value of the superannuation interest.

- Every amount, beneﬁt or entitlement that a member holds in an SMSF comprise one

  superannuation interest unless provided otherwise by ITR2021 Subdiv 307-D (ITR2021 s

307-200.02).

- A superannuation interest within a public sector superannuation scheme is to be treated as

  more than one interest where diﬀerent amounts are subject to diﬀerent beneﬁt payment rules and

  administrative rules prevent them being treated as a single interest. This means that every amount,

  an amount sourced from contributions or earnings into the scheme can be treated as a separate

investment income streams The value of a deferred or pooled investment income stream is speciﬁed by ITR2021 ss 307-205.02C, 307-205.02D and 307-205.02E.

A "deferred superannuation income stream" means a beneﬁt supported by a superannuation interest where the fund rules or contract governing the income stream provide for beneﬁt payments to start more than 12 months after the interest is acquired and, thereafter, to be made at least annually (SISR reg 1.03(1)).

A pooled investment pension (PIP) means an income stream that continues for the remainder of an individual's life where the payments are determined having regard to the age, life expectancy or other factors relevant to the mortality of each individual who has that kind of interest in the fund and the collective asset pool held for the beneﬁt of those individuals (ITR2021 s 307-205.02D(2)). A pooled investment annuity (PIA) is a comparable product to a PIP that is provided by a life insurance company (ITR2021 s 307-205.02E(2)).

To ensure that there is no overlap of the valuation rules, a deferred superannuation income stream must not be a PIP or PIA.

The value of these products is also the value that is credited to the individual's transfer balance account (¶14-340) when the interest holder enters the retirement phase. rule

rule Assume that Michael, age 57, is receiving a superannuation pension which ﬁrst commenced before 1 July 2007. The annual deductible amount for the pension is $6,000 (as calculated when the pension ﬁrst commenced to be paid) and Michael receives monthly pension payments of $1,000 (ie $12,000 pa). The annual deductible amount is apportioned to each pension beneﬁt made to him and this apportioned amount is the tax free component of the pension beneﬁt received by him for an income year.

The tax free component of each $1,000 pension beneﬁt is calculated as follows: Step 1 — Calculate the proportion of the pension beneﬁt to the total pension beneﬁt received in the year, ie $1,000/($12,000) = 8.33% Step 2 — Multiply the annual deductible amount by the percentage calculated in Step 1, ie $6,000 × 8.33% = $500 The tax free component of each pension beneﬁt is $500 and the taxable component is $500 ($1,000 − $500).

Trigger events Four events trigger the recalculation of the tax free and taxable components of a superannuation income stream that ﬁrst commenced to be paid before 1 July 2007 for continuing payments of the income stream after the events. The trigger events are the holder of the income stream was 60 years of age or over at 1 July 2007, the holder turns 60 years old or has died, or the income stream has been partially or wholly commuted (ITTPA s 307-125(3)).

The recalculation is made at the earliest of the events in accordance with the methods set out in ITTPA s 307-125(4) to (8).

Modification for lump sum disability benefits If an individual receives a superannuation lump sum that is a "disability superannuation beneﬁt" (¶14-220), the tax free component of the beneﬁt is the sum of the tax free component of the beneﬁt and the amount worked out using the formula in s 307-145(3), as below: Days to retirement Amount of beneﬁt × Service days + Days to retirement In the formula, "Days to retirement" is the number of days from the day on which the person stopped being capable of being gainfully employed to his/her last retirement day, and "Service days" is the number of days in the service period for the lump sum.

The tax free component cannot exceed the amount of the beneﬁt. The balance of the superannuation beneﬁt is the taxable component of the beneﬁt.

If a fund member's beneﬁts are transferred from an accumulation sub-fund to a pension sub-fund to facilitate the commencement of an account-based pension, the transfer is not the payment of a superannuation lump sum and s 307-145 cannot be applied to increase the tax free component of that superannuation lump sum (ID 2009/125).

The Full Federal Court considered 3 broad issues, as below, in test cases brought by the Commissioner against AAT decisions on the taxation of invalidity beneﬁts received pursuant to the Military Superannuation and Beneﬁts Act 1991 (MSBA) (Burns 2020 ATC ¶20-773; [2020] FCAFC 220, Walker 2020 ATC ¶20-773; [2020] FCAFC 220) and the Defence Force Retirement and Death Beneﬁts Act 1973 (DFRDBA) (Douglas 2020 ATC ¶20-773; [2020] FCAFC 220): 1. whether the income tax regulations (ITR97 as it then applied) did specify superannuation beneﬁts to be superannuation income stream beneﬁts rule 2. whether the invalidity beneﬁt pension was a superannuation income stream under subpara 995-1.01(1)(a)(ii) of ITR97 because it was a pension under the SIS Act where the rules under which the beneﬁts were paid complied with the SISR pension standards, and 3. in Burns and Douglas, whether the invalidity pension was a superannuation income stream under para 995-1.01(1)(b) of ITR97 because it was an income stream that had commenced before 20 September 2007.

The Full Court determined that invalidity pension payments under the MSBA superannuation scheme and the invalidity pay under the DFRDBA (collectively referred to as invalidity beneﬁts) commencing on or after 20 September 2007 are not superannuation income streams. This meant that the invalidity beneﬁts are taxed as superannuation lump sum payments, rather than superannuation income stream beneﬁts (Commissioner's appeal dismissed in Walker and Douglas and upheld in Burns; ATO Decision Impact Statement).

The Douglas decision identiﬁed a discrepancy in the statutory deﬁnition of a superannuation income stream which, contrary to the policy intent of the 2007 Simple Super changes, had the eﬀect that the Douglas types of pensions did not meet the superannuation income stream deﬁnition and defaulted to being taxed as superannuation lump sums.

An ATO remediation program provided for historical income tax assessments and superannuation reporting for individuals impacted by the Douglas decision (Fact Sheet Treatment of military invalidity beneﬁts following Full Federal Court decision).
