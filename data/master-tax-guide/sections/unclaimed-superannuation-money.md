# Unclaimed superannuation money ¶13-950

The Superannuation (Unclaimed Money and Lost Members) Act 1999 (SUMLMA) and Regulations 2019 (SUMLMR) provide the scheme for superannuation providers (regulated superannuation funds, ADFs and RSA providers) to pay unclaimed money and certain superannuation amounts to the Commissioner, and for the Commissioner to match and reunify these money with, or pay the money to, persons entitled to it.

In this paragraph, legislation references are to the SUMLMA and SUMLMR unless stated otherwise. A "member" means a member of a superannuation fund, a depositor with an ADF or an RSA holder; a "fund" refers to a superannuation provider. The term "unclaimed money" has a speciﬁc meaning (see below); the generic term "unclaimed superannuation money" (USM) is used in the commentary to refer to unclaimed money and superannuation amounts covered by the SUMLMA.

The Commissioner is required to keep a register of USM (SUMLMA s 19).

The trustees of certain public sector superannuation schemes may comply with the SUMLMA in the same way as superannuation providers.

If a state or territory law regulates unclaimed money, lost member accounts and former temporary residents' unclaimed superannuation in a way consistent with the SUMLMA, public sector superannuation schemes may pay USM to the relevant state or territory authority instead of the Commissioner (ss 18; 18AA; 20JA; 20QH; 22D; 24HA; SUMLMR Pt 7: prescribed public sector superannuation schemes).

residents SUMLMA Pt 3A deals with unclaimed superannuation of former temporary residents (ss 20A to 20P).

The Commissioner must give a notice under s 20C (a "Section 20C notice") to a fund if the Commissioner is satisﬁed that there are reasonable grounds for believing that a particular person has a superannuation interest in the fund and the person is a "former temporary resident" (as deﬁned in s 20AA).

A fund that receives a Section 20C notice must give the Commissioner a statement in the approved form about the member's superannuation interest and pay to the Commissioner the amount that would have been payable if the member had requested payment in connection with departing Australia, reduced by amounts already paid from the fund to the member (ss 20E; 20F).

Inactive low-balance accounts SUMLMA Pt 3B deals with inactive low-balance accounts (ss 20Q to 20QM).

An "inactive low balance account" (ILBA) is an account that relates to a MySuper product or choice product where the account balance is less than $6,000 and the fund has not received an amount for that account in the last 16 months (s 20QA).

Where an ILBA of a member also meets the deﬁnition of "unclaimed money", "temporary resident" or "lost member", the member's account will be paid to the Commissioner under the appropriate SUMLMA requirements instead (s 20QD(5)).

Some accounts are not ILBAs by deﬁnition, eg accounts in an SMSF or SAF, accounts related to a deﬁned beneﬁt interest, and accounts where the member has elected to have or maintain insurance cover under SISA s 68AAB(2) or 68AAC(2) (s 20QA(1)(a)(i), (vii), (viii), (ix)). An account is also not an ILBA if the member has met one of the conditions of release speciﬁed in SUMLMR ss 10 to 12. This recognises that there are some circumstances where a member may no longer be making contributions but has chosen to leave the balance in the account.

From 22 June 2020, members must make an election directly to their fund for their account to not be treated as an ILBA (previously, members made this election to ATO). An election is valid for 16 months, and fund trustees must maintain appropriate evidence of the election. If a fund receives the election after the ILBA has been reported and paid to the ATO, the fund should advise the member accordingly (ATO webpage Inactive low-balance accounts).

The requirement to report and pay an amount to the Commissioner does not apply to a trustee of a state or territory public sector superannuation scheme which reports and makes payments to a state or territory authority under SUMLMA s 18 (SUMLMA ss 20QG, 20QH).

person's best interest A superannuation provider may pay to the Commissioner an amount that it holds on behalf of a member, former member or non-member spouse if it reasonably believes paying the amount to the Commissioner is in the best interests of the member, former member or non-member spouse (Pt 3D, ss 22–22F).

Such payments are voluntary and the amounts can be paid to the Commissioner at any time provided that, at the time of payment, the amount is not covered by one of the compulsory obligations in the SUMLMA to pay that amount to the Commissioner (s 22(5)).

Superannuation providers will need to consider a range of factors when forming a view about whether transferring an amount to the Commissioner would be in the person's best interests. For considerations of a non-member spouse's best interest in superannuation splitting situations, see SISR regs 7A.03G(3)(b), (c), 7A.03G(3A), 7A.03H, 7A.09(3A), 7A.10(1)(c), (2)(b), 7A.16(6), 7A.17(5), and (6), 7A.18(3)(b) and (4).

Repayments of USM by the ATO On application in the approved form by a person or on the Commissioner's own initiative, the Commissioner must, if satisﬁed that it is possible to do so, pay USM held by the ATO in respect of an individual:

- to a complying superannuation fund identiﬁed by the individual

- to a KiwiSaver scheme provider (¶13-380) identiﬁed by the individual

- directly to an individual who has reached eligibility age, or has a terminal medical condition, or the

  amount is below $200, or

- to the beneﬁciaries or legal personal representative of a deceased member (ss 17(2), 20H(2),

  20QF(2), 24G(2)).

consolidation of USM held by the ATO The Commissioner is empowered to proactively consolidate ATO-held USM into an active superannuation account for a person (Pt 4B ss 24N to 24NB). The Commissioner must pay the ATO-held amounts into a superannuation fund that has received contributions for the person where the consolidated account balance will be equal to or greater than $6,000 (ss 24NA, 24NB).

The Commissioner can also recover amounts overpaid under Pt 4B in certain circumstances (s 24NAA).

Interaction of tax laws with USM payments by the ATO Payments by the Commissioner under ss 17(2), 20H(2), (2AA), (2A) or (3), 20QF(2), 21E(2), 22B(2) or 24G(2) are treated as if they are paid from a superannuation fund, ADF or RSA provider (ITAA97 s 301-125). "Unclaimed money payments" as speciﬁed in ITAA97 s 307-5(1) table item 5 are "superannuation beneﬁts" (ie superannuation member beneﬁts or death beneﬁts) for the purposes of ITAA97 and may be subject to tax as such (¶14-100, ¶14-130), except the following:

- an amount of less than $200 — this amount is non-assessable non-exempt income (¶14-310),

  and

- an amount repaid under s 20H that is a departing Australia superannuation payment (DASP)

— this amount is non-assessable non-exempt income but is subject to DASP withholding tax (¶14-390).

An unclaimed money payment that an individual is taken to have received under the constructive payment rule in ITAA97 s 307-15 because it is paid in accordance with the SUMLMA to the Commissioner or a state or territory authority is non-assessable non-exempt income of the individual in respect of whom the payment was made (ITAA97 s 306-20).

Interest payable on USM paid by the ATO Interest payable on USM is worked out in accordance with SUMLMR s 9 when the Commissioner makes a payment under ss 17(2), 20H(2), 20QF(2), 21E(2), 22B(2), 24G(2) or 24NA(2) or (3) on or after 1 July 2013 (see also PSLA 2011/23).
