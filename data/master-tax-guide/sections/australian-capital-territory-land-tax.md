# Australian Capital Territory land tax ¶38-070

Provisions governing the imposition and calculation of liability to land tax in the Australian Capital Territory are contained in the Land Tax Act 2004. Administrative and procedural matters, including powers of the Commissioner, collection of tax, penalties and interest, returns and assessments, objections and appeals, record keeping and general oﬀences, are covered by the Taxation Administration Act 1999.

Land tax in the ACT is assessed quarterly to the owner of the land as at 1 July, 1 October, 1 January and 1 April in each year. It applies to all residential property that is not an owner's principal place of residence (PPR) whether owned by an individual, company or trust, and whether rented or not. Land tax does not apply to commercial properties in the ACT.

Exemptions

- where the owner is moving from one home to another, the former home is exempt for the ﬁrst full

  quarter after the owner vacates the property and the new home is exempt for the ﬁrst full quarter

  after settlement of the purchase

- where the owner dies the former PPR is exempt from land tax for up to 2 years after the date

  of death, or until the land is transferred to a beneﬁciary or is rented. The 2-year period can be

  extended. Homes occupied by a person with a life or term interest under a will are also exempt

- where the owner can no longer live independently, and resides at a hospital or hospice, nursing

  home, an approved mental health or approved community care facility, or with a carer eligible for a

  carer payment under the Social Security Act 1991 (Cth). The property must remain unoccupied for

  the full period of exemption

- where the home is unﬁt for occupation as a place of residence because it is under construction,

  being signiﬁcantly renovated, or has been damaged

- where the home is occupied rent free or where the rent only covers the cost of rates, repairs,

  maintenance or insurance.

Other exemptions apply to rural land, land owned by the Housing Commissioner, retirement villages, nursing homes, religious accommodation, land owned by a not-for-proﬁt housing corporation, and land used for prescribed purposes. Exemption may also be granted for up to one year on compassionate grounds.

From 28 March 2019, exemption is available for up to 125 properties rented through a registered aﬀordable community housing provider.

Calculation and rates Land tax is assessed quarterly and calculated using a ﬁxed charge (FC) plus progressive marginal rates applied to the average unimproved value of the land.

FC + (AUV × rate) × number of days in the quarter divided by number of days in the year The AUV of land is the average of the unimproved values of the land over 5 years, or less for newly classiﬁed or recently acquired land. See below for a table of current rates and thresholds.

Land tax on residential units is based on that proportion of the AUV of the whole residential complex represented by the owner's residential unit entitlement in the complex, and not on the AUVs of the individual units.

Foreign owners From 1 July 2018 an additional annual land tax surcharge of 0.75% of average unimproved value is payable by foreign owners of land in the ACT.

Objections and appeals A taxpayer may lodge a written objection to an assessment within 60 days from date of service of the notice of assessment or later if allowed by Commissioner. Appeals must be lodged within 28 days of the date of the objection decision.

Rates and thresholds From 1 July 2024 the ﬁxed charge for land tax is $1,612. A new AUV threshold also applies at $1 million.
