# The Code of Professional Conduct ¶32-045

An important feature of the tax agent registration regime is a statutory Code of Professional Conduct which must be adhered to by a registered tax agent or a registered BAS agent.

The TPB has released an explanatory paper that deals with the Code of Professional Conduct generally (TPB(EP) 01/2010) and also information sheets and an explanatory paper which consider speciﬁc principles of the Code.

Code principles The principles of the Code are contained in the TASA and in a legislative instrument. The Code principles in the TASA require that a registered tax entity must: (1) act honestly and with integrity (2) comply with the taxation laws in the conduct of their personal aﬀairs (3) account to a client for any money or other property received from or on behalf of a client and which is held on trust. The TPB has issued an information sheet on this principle (TPB(I) 15/2012) (4) act lawfully in the best interests of a client (5) have in place adequate arrangements for the management of conﬂicts of interest that may arise in relation to the activities that are undertaken in the capacity of a registered tax agent. The TPB has issued an information sheet on this principle (TPB(I) 19/2014) (6) subject to any legal duty, not disclose any information relating to a client's aﬀairs to a third party without the client's permission. The TPB has issued an information sheet on this principle (TPB(I) 21/2014) (7) ensure that a tax agent service that they provide, or that is provided on their behalf, is provided competently (8) maintain knowledge and skills relevant to the tax agent services that are provided (9) take reasonable care in ascertaining a client's state of aﬀairs, to the extent that ascertaining the state of those aﬀairs is relevant to a statement to be made or a thing being done on behalf of the client. The TPB has issued an information sheet on this principle (TPB(I) 17/2013) (10) take reasonable care to ensure that taxation laws are applied correctly to the circumstances in relation to which advice is provided to a client. The TPB has issued an information sheet on this principle (TPB(I) 18/2013) (11) not knowingly obstruct the proper administration of the taxation laws (12) advise a client of the client's rights and obligations under the taxation laws that are materially related to the tax agent services provided (13) maintain professional indemnity insurance that meets the TPB's requirements (¶32-035) (14) respond to requests and directions from the TPB in a timely, responsible and reasonable manner (15) without the approval of the TPB not employ, or use the services of, an entity to provide tax agent services on the tax agent's behalf if the tax agent knows (or ought reasonably to know) that the entity is a disqualiﬁed entity (as deﬁned). The TPB has issued an information sheet on this principle (TPB(I) 41/2024), and (16) not provide tax agent services in connection with an arrangement with an entity that the tax agent knows (or ought reasonably to know) is a disqualiﬁed entity (TASA s 30-10). The TPB has issued an information sheet on this principle (TPB(I) 42/2024).

The Minister may, by legislative instrument, determine obligations that relate to the professional and ethical conduct of registered tax agents and BAS agents (TASA ss 30-10(17), 30-12). The obligations may elaborate or supplement any aspect of the Code of Professional Conduct but must not be inconsistent with the Code.

In exercise of this power, a legislative instrument (the Tax Agent Services (Code of Professional Conduct) Determination 2024 (the 2024 Code Determination) was made. The instrument sets out additional professional and ethical obligations of tax agents. The additional obligations (with references to relevant TPB information sheets) relate to:

- upholding and promoting the ethical standards of the tax profession (TPB(I) 44/2024)

- the making of false or misleading statements (TPB(I) 45/2024)

- managing conﬂicts of interest in activities undertaken for government and maintaining

  conﬁdentiality in dealings with government (TPB(I) 46/2024)

- the keeping of proper client records (TPB(I) 47/2024)

- the establishment and maintenance of a system of quality management (TPB(I) 48/2024)

- ensuring tax agent services provided on the agent's behalf are provided competently (TPB(I)

  48/2024), and

- keeping clients (and prospective clients) informed of certain listed matters (TPB(I) 49/2024).

These additional Code obligations apply to tax practitioners from one or other of 2 dates (1 January 2025 or 1 July 2025). The commencement date that is relevant depends on the size (number of employees) of the tax practitioner and their ﬁrm or practice as at 31 July 2024. A tax practitioner (individual, company or trust) is eligible for the 1 July 2025 timeframe if (broadly) the tax practitioner did not have any or had 100 or fewer employees as at 31 July 2024. For other tax practitioners, the new Code obligations apply from 1 January 2025 (the 2024 Code Determination s 100(1)(b)).

The operation of principles (1), (3) and (7) of the Code was considered by the AAT in SRBP & Tax Practitioners Board 2016 ATC ¶10-428; [2016] AATA 456. The operation of principle (7) was considered in DTMP [2016] AATA 684 in the context of the drafting of a notice of objection.

Principle (1) is not conﬁned in its operation to the provision of tax agent services; in other words, the principle may be infringed by conduct that is not itself the provision of a tax agent service (Kishore v Tax Practitioners Board [2016] AATA 764).

A practice note issued by the TPB provides practical guidance and assistance in relation to the use of letters of engagement (TPB(PN) 3/2019). While letters of engagement are not a speciﬁc requirement of the Code, the TPB considers that the provision of such letters is an important and eﬀective mechanism to assist tax practitioners in ensuring that they comply with the requirements of the Code.

The TPB has released practice notes that consider identity fraud (TPB(PN) 5/2022) and the use and disclosure of a client's TFN and TFN information in email communications (TPB(PN) 4/2021).

Sanctions for failure to comply with Code If the TPB is satisﬁed, after conducting an investigation, that a registered agent has failed to comply with the Code of Professional Conduct, the TPB may decide to take no action or to do one or more of the following:

- give the agent a written caution

- give the agent an order that requires the agent to take one or more actions (for example, to

  complete a speciﬁed course of education or training speciﬁed in the order, to provide tax agent

  services for which they are registered only under the supervision of a registered agent speciﬁed in

  the order or to provide only those tax agent services that are speciﬁed in the order)

- suspend the agent's registration

- terminate the agent's registration (¶32-050) (TASA ss 30-15 to 30-30).

With the exception of a decision to give a written caution, a decision of the TPB to impose a sanction for a failure to comply with the Code is reviewable by the ART.

Reporting of significant Code breaches As a result of amendments made in 2023, a registered tax agent must notify the TPB (within a speciﬁed 30- day period) if he or she has reasonable grounds to believe that either he or she or another registered tax agent has signiﬁcantly breached the Code on or after 1 July 2024 (TASA ss 30-35(1)(ba), (2)(ba), (3)(ba), (4), 30-40).

A signiﬁcant breach of the Code is a breach that: (i) constitutes an indictable oﬀence, or an oﬀence involving dishonesty, under an Australian law; (ii) results (or is likely to result) in material loss or damage to another entity (including the Commonwealth); or (iii) is otherwise signiﬁcant, including taking into account any one or more of several speciﬁed matters (TASA s 90-1).

The TPB has released an information sheet relating to these reporting obligations (TPB(I) 43/2024).

Continuing professional education The TPB has developed a continuing professional education (CPE) policy to assist agents to meet their obligations in this regard under the Code. The TPB has issued an explanatory paper on its CPE policy for tax agents and registered BAS agents (TPB(EP) 07/2021).
