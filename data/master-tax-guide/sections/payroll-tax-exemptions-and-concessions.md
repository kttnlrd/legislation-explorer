# Payroll tax exemptions and concessions ¶36-060

Not all wages are subject to payroll tax. The legislation in each state prescribes specific wages that are not subject to the tax and these generally include wages paid or payable by:

- religious institutions

- public benevolent institutions

- non-profit organisations having charitable, benevolent, philanthropic or patriotic purpose as their

  sole or dominant purpose

- public or non-profit hospitals

- schools or colleges providing education at or below the secondary level of education

- local governments

- charitable organisations.

Clarification should be sought in each state as the exemption provisions are not necessarily the same in each state. The legislation may also provide for certain elements of wages to be exempt from payroll tax. Further, each state may have payroll tax concessions and other relief measures to apply to particular employers. Recently announced measures include the following:

- In NSW, an employer is entitled to a payroll tax rebate for wages paid or payable on or after 4

September 2024 to a general practitioner (GP) at a medical centre if the majority (at least 80% for medical centres in Metropolitan Sydney and at least 70% for medical centres located elsewhere) of GP services provided through the medical centre are bulk billed. Wages paid to certain GPs at medical centres are exempt from payroll tax if the payroll tax is unpaid and the wages are paid or payable before 4 September 2024. Payroll tax audits in relation to GPs and their practices have been paused for 12 months from 4 September 2023. A GP is also not liable to pay interest and penalty for a tax default during the relevant period.

- In NSW, amounts paid or payable by a corporate collective investment vehicle (within the

  meaning of the Corporations Act 2001) to its corporate director have been excluded from wages for

  payroll tax purposes.

- In Victoria, from 1 July 2024 the exemption for wages paid or payable by not-for-profit non-

  government schools will only apply to wages paid or payable by schools declared by the Minister

  of Education. Undeclared schools are also not eligible for exemption as religious institutions, non-

  profit organisations or for educational services, in relation to services provided to undeclared

  schools.

- In Victoria, the state government announced that general practice businesses are exempt from

  any outstanding payroll tax assessments on payments to contractor GPs for the period up to 30

June 2024. A further 12-month payroll tax exemption for payments to contractor GPs through to 30 June 2025 will be available for any general practice business that has not already received advice and began paying payroll tax on payments to their contractor GPs on this basis. From 1 July 2025, a payroll tax exemption will be provided for wages paid or payable to contractor GPs and to employee GPs for providing bulk-billed consultations.

- In Queensland, the state government announced a payroll tax amnesty on payments for

  contracted GPs. Medical practices that successfully apply for the amnesty will not be required to

  pay payroll tax on payments to contracted GPs up to 30 June 2025 and for the previous 5 years

(ie 2018 to 2025). From 1 December 2024, an administrative arrangement allows wages paid or payable by a medical practice to a GP to be exempt from payroll tax and the mental health levy (Public Ruling PTAQ014.1.1). For this purpose, a medical practice means a business, other than a hospital, at which services of the type ordinarily provided by a medical practitioner who is registered in the specialty of general practice are provided. Revenue Legislation Amendment Bill 2024 (Qld) has been introduced to give legislative effect to the administrative arrangement.

- In South Australia, the Treasurer has approved a temporary payroll tax amnesty in relation to

  payments made to contracted GPs between 1 July 2018 and 30 June 2024. The amnesty applies

  to designated medical practices that submit an expression of interest and register with RevenueSA

  by 30 November 2023. From 1 July 2024, wages earned by GPs for bulk-billed services provided

  to patients are exempt from payroll tax, whether the GPs are engaged as employees or deemed as

  contractors (Statutes Amendment (Budget Measures) Bill 2024 (SA)).

- In the ACT, the territory government announced the waiver of payroll tax liabilities until 30 June

2023 for medical practices that have not previously paid payroll tax on GP payments. Further, a 2- year payroll exemption on GP payments until 30 June 2025 is provided for health care businesses making payments to GPs that are bulk billing 65% of all patients, have registered for MyMedicare and register with the ACT Revenue Office by 29 February 2024.

- The Northern Territory government announced that payroll tax will be waived for liabilities incurred

  from 1 January 2025 to 30 June 2025 for employers with taxable Australian wages of $2.5 million

  or less. For group employers with group taxable Australian wages of $2.5 million or less, only the

  designated group employer will be able to claim the waiver in the group annual return. All other

  employers in the group must continue to pay payroll tax.

- In the Northern Territory, wages paid or payable to apprentices and trainees will be exempt from

  payroll tax from 1 July 2025. The exemption will not apply if, immediately before commencing their

  traineeship, the trainee had been employed for a continuous period of 3 months or more for full-

  time employees, or 12 months or more for part-time or casual employees (Payroll Tax Amendment

Bill 2024 (NT)).
