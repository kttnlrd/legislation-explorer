# Review by Tribunal ¶28-090

An application for review by the ART of the Commissioner's decision on an objection is generally heard by the Taxation and Business Division. Proceedings before the ART are governed by the ART Act, subject to various modiﬁcations which apply speciﬁcally to taxation reviews (TAA ss 14ZZA to 14ZZJ). It seems that the ART will not have jurisdiction to review an objection decision if the objection is itself invalid, eg because the original decision against which the objection was lodged was not adverse to the taxpayer (Case 21/94 94 ATC 222; [1994] AATA 133), or if the application for review predates the objection decision (Andres 2002 ATC 2183; [2002] AATA 821; cf Walters [2013] AATA 151).

An application for review of an objection decision must be lodged within 60 days of the applicant being served with notice of the decision, although the ART may grant an extension of time (¶28-100). An application for review must be in writing (a form is available from the ART) and set out a statement of the reasons for the application. Documents may be lodged with the ART electronically.

The ART may convene a preliminary conference of the parties or their representatives to clarify the issues.

The ART may also refer suitable matters to conciliation/mediation with the consent of the parties. If the matter proceeds to a hearing, no evidence is to be given of what occurred at the preliminary conference, conciliation/mediation unless both parties agree. If an agreement is reached at a preliminary conference or conciliation/mediation, the ART may make a decision giving eﬀect to that agreement (if within its powers) without holding a hearing. Once the matter has been referred to the ART, the applicant may, if desired, request an early hearing date.

The Commissioner is obliged to lodge with the ART copies of: (a) a statement of the reasons for his decision on an objection; (b) the notice of the taxation decision concerned (eg the assessment), the objection and the notice of the objection decision; (c) any other document which is in the Commissioner's possession or under his control and which he considers is necessary to the review of the objection decision; and (d) a list of the documents being lodged (in the case of an ART extension application (¶28-100), the Commissioner is required to lodge copies of all relevant documents). The ART may also issue oral orders at a preliminary conference, or serve a notice, requiring the Commissioner or applicant to lodge additional documents in their possession or under their control. If the statement of reasons lodged by the Commissioner is inadequate, the ART may order the Commissioner to lodge an additional statement setting out further and more adequate reasons for the decision, ﬁndings of fact and/or details of evidence. The ART has extensive powers to summon a person to give evidence and/or produce documents at the hearing (eg Case 25/95 95 ATC 263; [1995] AATA 95).

The ART may dismiss an application if the applicant fails to proceed with the application within a reasonable time or if it is satisﬁed that the application is frivolous or vexatious. However, in the absence of bad faith, the ART will be reluctant to dismiss an application merely because the taxpayer's case appears to be weak (eg The Christ Circle Oriona Community 95 ATC 2040). The ART may also dismiss an application if the applicant fails to appear or be represented at a preliminary conference, a conciliation or directions hearing, or the hearing itself. In such a case, the ART can order that the applicant pay the Commissioner's costs (¶28-085).

Taxation review proceedings are generally heard by a single member, although a 3-member ART may sit in certain cases. If a presidential member is presiding, any question of law is decided by that member (eg Case 30/95 95 ATC 286; [1995] AATA 109; ART Act s 39). Unlike most other proceedings before the ART, taxation hearings are held in private if the applicant so requests. Where a matter is heard in private, the ART must ensure, as far as practicable, that the reasons for its decision do not disclose the applicant's identity (this does not apply if a notice of appeal to the Federal Court has been lodged). The ART may allow a person to participate in a hearing by means of electronic communication, eg by telephone or video conference (Grbich & Shen 93 ATC 4564; [1993] FCA 360, Dunn [2012] AATA 486). The ART is not required to apply the laws of evidence, but may take evidence on oath or aﬃrmation.

Powers of the ART The ART may exercise all the powers and discretions of the Commissioner, but only for the purposes of reviewing the Commissioner's objection decision (Liedig 94 ATC 4269; [1994] FCA 1058). There are, however, minor restrictions on the ART's power to review decisions relating to the remission of penalties (¶29-410).

In its proceedings, the ART must comply with the rules of natural justice or procedural fairness. This would normally require that the parties be given the opportunity to make submissions on any issues decided by the ART. For example, the AAT (as the ART then was) was not entitled to decide a case against a taxpayer on the ground that the anti-avoidance provisions applied where the question of the application of those provisions had not been raised at any time during the hearing (Fletcher 88 ATC 4834; [1988] FCA 362; 90 ATC 4559; [1990] FCA 209; 91 ATC 4950; [1991] HCA 42; 92 ATC 2045; 92 ATC 4611; [1992] FCA 454).

Procedural fairness also means that only in exceptional circumstances will the Commissioner be allowed to withhold relevant documents (eg Case 25/95 95 ATC 263; [1995] AATA 95; but see also VAI v Forgie 2003 ATC 4263; [2003] FCA 87 where the AAT (as the ART then was) determined that the particulars supplied by the Commissioner were suﬃcient to give the taxpayer a fair picture of the fraud or evasion case made against him). However, this does not include internal legal advice (ACN 154 520 199 Pty Ltd (in liq) [2018] FCA 1140).

The process contemplated by the ART Act is not to be fragmented by applications seeking to challenge intermediate directions or determinations made along the way to reaching an ultimate determination of the issue before the Tribunal. Hence a direction by the ART requiring a party to proceedings to provide further information (or the dismissal of an application for such a direction) is not a judicially reviewable decision (¶28-180) (Beddoe 96 ATC 4967; [1996] FCA 1661, VAI v Forgie 2003 ATC 4263; [2003] FCA 87).

The ART may conﬁrm, vary or set aside the Commissioner's decision on an objection. The ART does not have the power to actually make, amend or set aside an assessment (Stevenson 91 ATC 4476; [1991] FCA 224). Thus, if the ART concludes that the taxpayer's taxable income, and the tax payable thereon, is less than as assessed, it must remit the matter to the Commissioner with a direction that he amend the assessment accordingly. In Case Z36 92 ATC 331; [1992] AATA 277, where the AAT (as the ART then was) concluded that a taxpayer's assessed income had been understated, it could only aﬃrm the objection decisions under review and leave it to the Commissioner to decide whether he had the power to amend the relevant assessments (¶25-300). Once a decision of the ART becomes ﬁnal, the Commissioner must give eﬀect to that decision within 60 days (¶28-160). The ART must give either oral or written reasons for its decision. If the ART gives oral reasons and it later issues written reasons, it cannot change the oral reasons or add diﬀerent ones (MA 92 ATC 4373; [1992] FCA 359).

The ART is ordinarily bound to follow previous court decisions although, where there are conﬂicting decisions of similar authority, it may have to decide which one to follow (Salenger 88 ATC 4449; [1988] FCA 176).

The ART may refer a question of law arising before it to the Federal Court, but only with the consent of the President (ART Act s 185). Where that happens, neither the applicant nor the Commissioner will be liable for ﬁling, setting down or hearing fees.

An appeal against an ART decision may be made to the Federal Court on a question of law (¶28-120).

Replacement of the AAT The AAT is replaced by a new federal administrative body named the Administrative Review Tribunal (ART) which commenced operations on 14 October 2024.

The ART's role is to conduct merits reviews of administrative decisions of the government, including those of the Commissioner of Taxation.

The ART eﬀectively continues the role of the AAT in reviewing taxation decisions. Previous AAT decisions and court decisions relating to the AAT remain eﬀective and authority for the principles espoused.
