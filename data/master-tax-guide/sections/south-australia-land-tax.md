# South Australia land tax ¶38-040

Provisions governing the imposition and calculation of liability to land tax in South Australia are contained in the Land Tax Act 1936 ("the principal Act") and the Land Tax Regulations 2010. Administrative and procedural matters, including powers of the Chief Commissioner, collection of tax, penalties and interest, returns and assessments, objections and appeals, record keeping and general oﬀences, are covered by the Taxation Administration Act 1996.

Land tax in South Australia for 2024–25 is levied on the unimproved land value of all land owned in South Australia as at midnight on 30 June 2024. Land tax liability falls to the owner of the land. An "owner" includes the registered owner of freehold land, any person entitled to legal or equitable ownership, persons with a right to purchase the land and a shareholder in a home unit company. Lessees of land under a Crown lease, tenants in common who lease another portion of the land, and certain long-term lessees or occupiers of "shack sites" on privately owned land near the Murray River are also owners for land tax purposes.

Assessment The total value of land held by individual taxpayers is aggregated, irrespective of whether the land is held directly, or through a trust or company.

Joint owners of land may be assessed jointly on their interests in that land, and each joint owner then assessed separately on their individual interest in that land along with any other interests in land that they own. Similarly, while trustees may be assessed for land held in a trust, beneﬁciaries may also be assessed on their respective interests in the land held in trust aggregated with any other land they own. In all cases a deduction is allowed to prevent double taxation.

Generally, a surcharge applies for land held in trust where beneﬁciaries are not disclosed or can't be determined. See below for more on trusts and the trust surcharge.

Related companies are grouped together and land tax is assessed on group landholdings as if they were a single company. Whether 2 companies are "related" depends on whether a company has a controlling interest in another or whether a person or persons have a controlling interest in both. The meaning of "controlling interest" is similar to that applying to companies in NSW (¶38-010) and Victoria (¶38-020).

Developers and affordable housing Property developers who subdivide land for aﬀordable housing may seek an ex gratia concession for the diﬀerence between the land tax liability attributable to the land and the amount which would have been payable on the land if each subdivided parcel were assessed separately. This concession is available for one ﬁnancial year for each property.

Exemptions

- land used as a principal place of residence (PPR), including retirement villages. If a home

  business activity is carried on at the property a partial exemption may be available. Exemption

  may be available for up to 3 years after a home is destroyed or rendered uninhabitable if an owner

  intends to rebuild or repair the building within that time

- certain land used for primary production

- land that is owned by prescribed associations and is used for relevant purposes (eg sporting,

  recreational, historical and agricultural shows, literature, science, languages, the arts or the

  preservation of historical, traditional or cultural heritage or for similar purposes)

- land used for religious, hospital or library purposes

- land owned (or in certain situations occupied) by certain charitable, educational, benevolent or

  philanthropic organisations

- land owned by a prescribed body and used for the beneﬁt of indigenous people

- land used for caravan parks, residential parks for retired persons and licensed supported

  residential facilities

- land used for the provision of residential care by an approved provider under the Aged Care Act

  1997 (Cth).

Build-to-rent A land tax reduction of up to 50% is available for land used by eligible build-to-rent projects where construction commences on or after 1 July 2023. The concession is available from 2023–24 to 2039–40 years inclusive. An owner must apply to the SRO for this concession. Eligibility depends on compliance with building and planning requirements, minimum tenancy terms, and other requirements to be set by regulation.

Deceased estates In certain circumstances, land used by a deceased person as a PPR immediately before their death may qualify for ex gratia relief from land tax for the ﬁrst ﬁnancial year following the death of the owner (Revenue Ruling LT 001). Similar relief is provided where an owner moves into residential care (Revenue Ruling LT 003).

Trusts surcharge From 2020–21 land with a taxable value greater than $25,000 which is held in a trust may be charged at trust rates of land tax. Trustees must notify RevenueSA of any change in ownership of land held on trust within one month of the change.

Trusts may be taxed at general rates where appropriate notiﬁcation of beneﬁcial interests in the trust is made to RevenueSA. A trustee of a ﬁxed trust or a unit trust must notify RevenueSA of all the beneﬁcial interests in the trust.

Land acquired by a trustee of a discretionary trust on or after 17 October 2019 is taxed at trust rates. Land subject to a discretionary trust as at 16 October 2019 may be taxed at general rates where the trustee lodged a written notice specifying a single designated beneﬁciary by 31 December 2021.

Charitable trusts, superannuation trusts, estate administration trusts and various public and concessional trusts are excluded from surcharge. Land tax exemptions, including the PPR exemption, can apply to the trust surcharge where the land is used and/or occupied in the requisite manner by a beneﬁciary or beneﬁciaries.

Objections and appeals Taxpayers should lodge written objections to assessments within 60 days from date of service of a notice of assessment. Objections can be lodged up to 12 months later if allowed by the relevant Minister. Appeals can be lodged up to 60 days from the date of the objection decision or anytime after 90 days from lodgment of the objection if no objection decision has been made and the taxpayer has given 14 days notice of the intention to appeal.

Rates and thresholds Land tax thresholds are indexed annually based on the average percentage change in site values.
