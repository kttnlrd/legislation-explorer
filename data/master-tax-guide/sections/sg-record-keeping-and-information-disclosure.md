# SG record-keeping and information disclosure ¶39-510

An employer must keep records that record and explain all transactions and other acts engaged in or required to be engaged in by the employer under the SGAA, regardless of whether the employer is liable to the SG charge. Records must be kept in writing, in English and be retained for 5 years. An employer who, without reasonable excuse, fails to comply commits an oﬀence punishable on conviction by a ﬁne (SGAA s 79). An oﬀence under s 79 is a strict liability oﬀence.

An employer who is liable to pay SG charge for a quarter is further required to keep records of the calculation of amounts speciﬁed in the SG statement that is lodged with the ATO under SGAA s 33 or 34 (SGAA s 59(2)).

An administrative penalty may be imposed for failure to keep or retain records as required (TAA sch 1 s 288-25: ¶39-550).

The Commissioner may also issue an education direction under TAA sch 1 s 384-15 for failure to comply with the record-keeping requirements under the SGAA (¶39-550).

Secrecy and disclosure of information under the TAA Employees or former employees may make a complaint to the ATO where they think that their employer has not complied with their SG obligations.

Speciﬁc rules in TAA Div 355 in sch 1 govern the recording and disclosure of protected information by taxation oﬃcers (ss 355-25; 355-50). "Protected information" includes written documents, any other form in which information can be recorded, information obtained directly from a taxpayer or information generated by the ATO, and information about the ATO's compliance activity against a particular taxpayer (s 355-30).

Except as permitted by Div 355, the Commissioner cannot disclose information or provide details about the progress of any action in relation to or against another person in response to an employee's SG inquiries or complaints. In this regard, where the disclosure is to an employee or former employee, the ATO may record or disclose information relating to its response to a complaint about the employer's failure or suspected failure to comply with the SGAA in relation to the employee, but not information about the employer's general ﬁnancial aﬀairs (TAA sch 1 s 355-65, table 2 item 7 and 7A) (see further ¶39-530).

Reporting contributions and member information to the ATO A superannuation provider (superannuation fund, ADF or RSA provider) is required to give the ATO a statement in the approved form in relation to individuals who hold an interest in a superannuation plan (eg fund members, RSA holders) at any time during the period speciﬁed in a determination by the Commissioner (TAA sch 1 ss 390-5, 390-20: life insurance companies). The statement may contain information about contributions made to the superannuation plan (including if no contributions are made), the value of an individual's superannuation interest or account, information relating to the Superannuation (Unclaimed Money and Lost Members) Act 1999 and the LRBA amount (¶13-640) under ITAA97 s 307-231. For the purposes of s 390-5, certain payments are treated as "contributions" (s 390-5(9A)).

The ATO uses the information received for various purposes, including administration of the SG, co- contribution and contributions cap regimes (¶13-760, ¶13-820), and total superannuation balance and transfer balance account regimes (¶13-640, ¶14-320).

A person who is dissatisﬁed with a statement given to the ATO by a superannuation entity may make a complaint to the Australian Financial Complaints Authority (AFCA) in accordance with Corporations Act 2001 Pt 7.10A.

Penalties are imposed for failure to comply with the TAA information and reporting requirements (eg no or late lodgment, or not using the approved form: see ¶29-000ﬀ). An administrative penalty may also apply for non-compliance or providing information that is false or misleading in a particular matter (TAA sch 1 s 286-75; Subdiv 284-B: ¶39-550).

The Commissioner may give superannuation entities an ongoing grace period for correcting false or misleading statements in relation to member information statements without giving rise to penalties (TAA sch 1 s 390-7).

Event-based reporting using MATS and MAAS Superannuation providers are required to comply with event-based reporting to the ATO using the member account transactions service (MATS) and member account attribute service (MAAS).

The MATS is used to report contributions received and annual member balances (ie reporting account phases at a transactional level: see "MATS reporting and dates" below) and the MAAS is used to report attribute changes to a member's account (ie reporting account phases and changes in real time: see "MAAS reporting and dates" below). Superannuation account phases and attributes include, but are not limited to, opening and closing of accounts, deﬁned beneﬁt interests and acceptance of contributions and roll-overs.

The MATS and MAAS complement other superannuation information and employer reporting to the ATO, such as superannuation funds reporting events relating to their members' transfer balance accounts (see below), and STP reporting as discussed in ¶26-620.

The MAAS includes an optional Provision of Details service to assist in reuniting members who are at risk of becoming lost with their account (ATO protocol document).

MAAS reporting and dates The MAAS form is the approved form for giving a statement to the Commissioner under ss 390-5 and 390-20 in relation to an individual's superannuation account phases and attributes.

Superannuation providers (excluding SMSFs) and life insurance companies must report using the MAAS relating to an individual's superannuation account phases and attributes no later than 5 business days after the day on which: (i) an account is opened or a life insurance policy is ﬁrst held, and (ii) any changes to the account phases and/or attributes relating to the account or policy occur (Taxation Administration Member Account Attribute Service – the Reporting of Information Relating to Superannuation Account Phases and Attributes 2018: F2018L00467).

Transfer balance account event reporting The transfer balance account regime is discussed in ¶14-320 onwards. Retirement phase income stream reporting obligations associated with the transfer balance cap are event-based and superannuation providers are required to report events as they occur that result in a credit or debit in an individual member's transfer balance account (also referred to as retirement phase accounts) (¶14-340).

The above reporting obligations enable the ATO to determine whether the member's transfer balance account has exceeded the transfer balance cap and take appropriate action, and to determine the member's total superannuation balance and uncapped notional taxed contributions (ATO website Transfer balance cap reporting).

Employer reporting of contributions under other laws Employers are required to report (on payslips) to an employee either the person's entitlements to superannuation accrued during the pay-period or the actual contributions made (Fair Work Act 2009, s 536; Fair Work Regulations 2009, reg 3.46).

Employers, when making superannuation contributions for their employees, are required to provide the payments and associated data to superannuation funds in a speciﬁc electronic format, in accordance with prescribed SISA requirements (see "Superannuation data and payment standards — SuperStream" in ¶13-900).

ATO may disclose superannuation information An exception to the TAA secrecy provisions (¶39-510) allows the ATO to disclose information held about a member's superannuation interests to superannuation entities and their administrators for the purpose of assisting members to ﬁnd and manage their superannuation interests (eg consolidate, transfer, cash or deal with their interests in any other way). The disclosure may cover amounts held by the ATO (TAA sch 1 s 355-65(3), item 10). Information can also be disclosed where it relates to an individual who has applied to become a member of a superannuation entity.
