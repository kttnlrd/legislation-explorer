# Payroll tax deduction amounts ¶36-040

Employers are entitled to a deduction, so that where taxable wages exceed a speciﬁed threshold, only the excess is subject to payroll tax. In most states, a ﬂat deduction is allowed for all employers.

In South Australia, the maximum annual deduction entitlement is $600,000 ($50,000 monthly). However, a nil payroll tax rate applies where the annual Australian wages do not exceed $1.5 million.

In Queensland, from 1 January 2023 the deduction is reduced by $1 for every $7 by which the wages exceed the tax-free threshold until the deduction tapers to zero.

In the Northern Territory, the deduction is reduced by $1 for every $4 by which the wages exceed the tax-free threshold until the deduction tapers to zero. From 1 July 2025, the deduction will be reduced by $1 for every $2 by which the wages exceed the tax-free threshold (Payroll Tax Amendment Bill 2024 (NT)).

In Western Australia, the tax-free threshold gradually phases out for employers or group of employers with annual taxable wages in Australia between the annual threshold amount (¶36-030) and the upper threshold amount of $7,500,000. The deduction amount is calculated using the "tapering value" (2/13 in 2024–25) as follows: Annual threshold amount – [(Total annual wages – Annual threshold amount) × 2/13] In Victoria, a phasing out of the amount that can be deducted from taxable wages applies from 1 July 2024. Employers and groups with total annual taxable and interstate wages exceeding $3,000,000 but not exceeding $5,000,000 are eligible for a reduced deduction at a phase out rate of 45% (50% for 2025–26 and subsequent ﬁnancial years). Employers and groups with total annual taxable and interstate wages which exceed $5,000,000 will not be eligible for any deduction.

Where employers are grouped, one member claims the exemption threshold and the remaining members must pay a ﬂat amount of tax. Generally, the deduction available is ﬁrst calculated based on total Australian wages. The result is then reduced on a pro rata basis by comparing the wages paid in a particular state with the total Australian wages.
