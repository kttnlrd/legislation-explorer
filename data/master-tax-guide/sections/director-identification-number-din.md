# Director identiﬁcation number (DIN) ¶33-090

The "director identiﬁcation number" (DIN) legislation is found in Pt 9.1A of the Corporations Act 2001. The DIN legislation commenced on 4 April 2021. For details of transitional arrangements that applied to persons subject to the DIN legislation see the 2023 Australian Master Tax Guide — Tax Year End Edition.

The DIN is a unique permanent identiﬁer for each person who consents to being a director. Except in limited circumstances, eg corruption of records, it is not intended that the same DIN will be issued to more than one person or that one person will be issued with more than one DIN. A DIN will provide traceability of a director's relationships across companies and allow regulators and administrators to investigate a director's involvement in unlawful activity including illegal phoenix activity.

Subject to the transitional arrangements (see below), a director of a registered body corporate must apply for a DIN before becoming a director unless an exemption applies. Initially, the DIN will operate only in relation to appointed directors and acting alternate directors and not to de facto or shadow directors. The requirements apply to both Australian companies and registered foreign companies that are body corporates.

Penalties Civil and criminal penalties apply to directors who fail to apply for a DIN within the relevant timeframe. It is a defence if the director applied to the registrar for a DIN prior to being ﬁrst appointed as a director and the application has not yet been dealt with. It is also a defence if the director was appointed without his/her knowledge.

Penalties also apply to conduct designed to undermine the DIN system, eg deliberately providing false identity information, intentionally providing a false DIN to a government body or body corporate, or intentionally applying for multiple DINs.

Applying for a DIN Individuals requiring a DIN can apply online. An agent, or other third party, cannot apply for a DIN on behalf of a director unless the Registrar is satisﬁed that an exception applies. An applicant for a DIN will be required to complete a declaration conﬁrming their identity and their eligibility to obtain a DIN.

From 5 April 2022, any individual appointed as a director under the Corporations Act 2001 must apply for a DIN prior to their appointment. A similar requirement applies from 1 November 2022 to any individual Numbers > Director identification number (DIN) appointed as a director under the Corporations (Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander) Act 2006. For transitional rules see the 2023 Australian Master Tax Guide.

Verifying identification The Registrar is required to provide an individual with a unique DIN if the Registrar is satisﬁed that the individual's identity has been established. The Registrar may request certain personal information or identity documents in order to establish an individual's identity. Individuals will be required to prove their identity using key Australian identity documents, such as a driver's licence, passport, birth certiﬁcate, Australian visa and Medicare card. Data collected via an accredited digital identity provider will be used to authenticate the details provided by an individual. Where relevant, the Registrar may cross check identity information received from an individual with other agencies, including ASIC and the ATO.

Protecting privacy The information obtained by the Registrar is protected information. The Registrar may disclose this protected information to other government agencies in accordance with the disclosure framework (Legislative Instrument F2022L00361). Under the framework, the Registrar may disclose DIN information to government entities, Public Governance, Performance and Accountability (PGPA) bodies, courts and tribunals, to enable those entities to exercise their functions or powers. PGPA bodies may include corporate Commonwealth entities, non-corporate Commonwealth entities, Commonwealth companies and multi-agency taskforces.

The Registrar will take steps to protect the information held about individuals against loss, misuse or unauthorised access, use, modiﬁcation or disclosure. The Registrar, in collecting personal information, will comply with the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) and the National Privacy Principles (NPP).
