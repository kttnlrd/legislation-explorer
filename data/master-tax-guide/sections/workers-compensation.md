# Workers compensation ¶10-180

Weekly or other periodical workers compensation payments (or periodical payments under other legislation) received as compensation for loss of wages (whole or partial) are fully assessable as ordinary income and not under ITAA97 s 15-2 (¶10-060) (Inkster 89 ATC 5142; [1989] FCA 423, Case Y47 91 ATC 433; [1991] AATA 718, Maher 2005 ATC 2083; [2005] AATA 272). The same generally applies to a lump sum paid in lieu of the right to receive weekly payments of income or for loss of employment (Coward 99 ATC 2166; [1999] AATA 132, Brackenreg 2003 ATC 2196; [2003] AATA 824, Riley 2014 ATC ¶10-377; [2014] AATA 664, Edwards 2016 ATC ¶10-436; [2016] AATA 781, Gupta 2016 ATC ¶10-439; [2016] AATA 914; TD 2016/18). However, if the commutation to a lump sum can only be made under certain circumstances, and its calculation requires consideration of other factors, it may possibly be capital (Barnett 99 ATC 2444; [1999] AATA 950). A lump sum commutation referable to the period after the taxpayer reached the age of 65 years has also been held to be capital (Coward 99 ATC 2166; [1999] AATA 132).

A ﬁxed sum workers compensation award for loss of a limb, eye, ﬁnger, etc, is not assessable, nor is a payment for medical, etc, expenses. Nor is a ﬁxed sum awarded in full satisfaction of an employee's workers compensation claim against an employer, even though it represents in part unpaid periodical payments as well as compensation for loss of a limb, etc, and loss of earning capacity. For guidelines on when the receipt of a lump sum compensation/settlement amount is assessable, see TD 93/58.

In Rayner 98 ATC 2310; [1998] AATA 757 it was held that a taxpayer who had to repay workers compensation payments after receiving a lump sum settlement was not entitled to have a previous assessment amended to exclude the compensation payments. In Reiter 2001 ATC 4502; [2001] FCA 1068 workers compensation payments received by a taxpayer after he had recovered damages were not assessable income as they were required to be repaid under Victorian "double dipping" legislation.

In Cooper 2003 ATC 2123; [2003] AATA 296 a lump sum payment representing arrears of invalidity beneﬁts was assessable in the year of receipt. The same applies to arrears of workers compensation paid as a lump sum (Vargiemezis 2008 ATC ¶10-068; [2008] AATA 1152) and a lump sum compensation payment received by a Reserve Force member in respect of lost civilian income.
