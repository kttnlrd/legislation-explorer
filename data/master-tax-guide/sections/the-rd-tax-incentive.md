# The R&D tax incentive ¶20-160

The R&D tax incentive is a broadly based, activity-driven program that allows businesses to access tax oﬀsets for expenditure on eligible activities. However, these R&D activities must be registered with Industry, Innovation and Science Australia (the Board) under Pt III of the Industry Research and Development Act 1986 (IR&D Act). The Board has delegated its oversight functions and powers to the R&D Incentives Committee (the Committee). The Board and the Committee are assisted by the Department of Industry, Science and Resources (DISR).

ITAA97 Div 355, and s 355-100 in particular, provide for R&D tax oﬀsets depending on the size of the R&D business. For years commencing on or after 1 July 2021, these are:

- the refundable tax oﬀset at the company's corporate tax rate plus 18.5% for the ﬁrst $150 million

  of eligible expenditure for eligible entities with an aggregated group turnover of less than $20 million

(as determined under s 328-115), provided they are not controlled by income tax exempt entities.

This provides a 43.5% refundable amount for R&D entities eligible for the base rate entity tax rate and a net after tax beneﬁt of 18.5%, and

- the non-refundable tax oﬀset at the company's corporate tax rate plus a 2-tiered increase in the

  oﬀset as described in s 355-100(1A) for the ﬁrst $150 million of eligible expenditure for all other

  eligible entities. Unused non-refundable oﬀset amounts may be able to be carried forward to future

  income years under s 63-10(1).

The 2 "tiered oﬀset rates" are 8.5% for R&D expenditure which is up to 2% of the R&D entity's total expenses (as determined under s 355-115) and 16.5% for R&D expenditure over 2% of total expenses.

The R&D expenditure threshold of $150 million applies to the amount of R&D expenditure that a company can claim as an oﬀset at these uplifted rates under the incentive. If a company exceeds this amount, the oﬀset claimable on the excess is reduced to the applicable corporate tax rate: ¶42-025 (¶20-170).

As the tax oﬀset replaces tax deductions that may otherwise be available, the net after tax beneﬁt of the oﬀset is the diﬀerence between the total tax oﬀset rate and the applicable company tax rate. However, there is a timing beneﬁt for companies eligible for the refundable oﬀset if they are in tax loss.

*Refs: s 355-100, s 328-115), s 355-100(1, s 63-10(1), s 355-115)*

(1) Small Group Ltd's (SG) corporate tax rate is 25%. Its tax oﬀset rate will be (25% + 18.5%) or 43.5%. If it incurred R&D expenditure of $500,000 on registered R&D activities, the tax oﬀset will be $217,500.

If SG is in tax loss, this whole amount will be refunded reducing carry forward tax losses at 25%. Where the tax payable before R&D is greater than the oﬀset, this reduces the total tax payable by $92,500 (ie 18.5% × the R&D expenditure). If the oﬀset reduces the tax payable to zero, the balance of the total unused tax oﬀset will be refunded to SG. In either case, SG retains the +18.5% tax beneﬁt. (2) Large Group Ltd incurred R&D expenditure of $160 million and reports expenses of $2.9 billion in Item 6 of the company tax return as amended to include capitalised R&D expenditure that can be notionally deducted immediately and R&D-related tax eﬀect account adjustments. The tax oﬀset of $68,110,000 is calculated as the sum of: (1) $160 million × the company tax rate of 30% = $48 million instead of a ITAA97 s 8-1 deduction, plus (2) the R&D premium component of the tax oﬀset: (a) $2.9 billion × 2% = $58 million × 8.5% = $4,930,000, plus (b) $150 million – $58 million × 16.5% = $15,180,000 = $20,110,000.

Total expenses must be amended to include the notional R&D deduction amount rather than the amount of R&D expenditure in the Income Statement (Proﬁt and Loss Statement).

There is no R&D premium amount for the $10 million over the $150 million cap.

An additional "catch up amount" may be deducted for balancing adjustment losses on Div 40 capital assets as modiﬁed by s 355-310 in addition to the R&D tax oﬀset.

The object of the program is to encourage industry to conduct R&D activities that might otherwise not be conducted because of an uncertain return from the activities, where the knowledge gained is likely to beneﬁt the wider Australian economy. This is achieved by providing a tax incentive for industry to conduct, in a scientiﬁc way, experimental activities for the purpose of generating new knowledge or information in either a general or applied form. As the incentive is meant to encourage development activities as well as research, it includes activities to develop new or improved materials, products, devices, processes or services. This can include production activities either as core experimental R&D activities or as activities that are directly related to these unless these latter supporting activities are done for a dominant purpose other than R&D.

The incentive is jointly administered by the Board (assisted by DISR) and the ATO. The Board is primarily responsible for administering the technical eligibility and registration of R&D activities under IR&D Act.

However, the Commissioner may be able to determine the eligibility of R&D activities if the Board has not made a ﬁnding and it is considered unable to make a ﬁnding. This may occur if it is more than 4 years since the R&D activity was registered (GQHC [2024] AATA 409). Further information on the application process of applying for the incentive can be found at business.gov.au. The ATO works closely with DISR in the guidance and review processes to encourage and ensure compliance in the self-assessment of eligibility for the tax credit and in documentary and administrative processes to support the taxpayer's R&D claim.

The general anti-avoidance rules in ITAA36 Pt IVA now apply directly to the R&D tax oﬀset, eg to schemes to overclaim the oﬀset or claim the refundable oﬀset instead of the non-refundable oﬀset. The program administrators are required to report R&D expenditure (adjusted for feedstock clawbacks) for each company.

The ﬁrst R&D annual transparency report on the income year ended 30 June 2022 was released in October 2024. Promoter penalties under TAA s 290-50 may apply where an advisor engages in conduct which results in them being a promoter of tax exploitation schemes to secure R&D oﬀsets for another taxpayer (Bakarich (No 2) [2024] FCA 1448).

*Refs: ITAA97 s 8-1, s 355-310, s 290-50*
