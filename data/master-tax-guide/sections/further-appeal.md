# Further appeal ¶28-120

Appeal to the Federal Court The Commissioner or the taxpayer may appeal to the Federal Court from a decision of the ART on a question of law (ART Act ss 172 and 174). For example, the ART's ﬁndings on the evidence is a question of fact, not law. The appeal is restricted to a question of law (including a mixed question of fact and law: Great Western Railway Co v Bater (1922) 8 TC 231, Haritos 2015 ATC ¶20-513; [2015] FCAFC 92) and does not amount to a fresh hearing of the matter (Brixius 87 ATC 4963; [1987] FCA 612, Rawson Finances 2013 ATC ¶20-374; [2013] FCAFC 26).

However, the court may, under ART Act s 177, make ﬁndings of fact consistent with the ART's ﬁndings of fact (other than ﬁndings made by the ART as a result of an error of law) (Holzberger v Secretary, Department of Health and Ageing [2007] FCAFC 68); the court may also receive further evidence for the purpose of making ﬁndings of fact (ART Act s 177(3)).

An issue may seemingly be raised for the ﬁrst time on appeal, but only if it is one of construction or of law and it is in the interests of justice for the court to consider it (Water Board v Moustakas (1988) 77 ALR 193). An appeal must be initiated within 28 days of the ART's decision being furnished. If the ART initially gives short reasons for its decision and later gives full reasons, the decision is not given until the later date (Fletcher 92 ATC 4611; [1992] FCA 454).

The court may grant an extension of time for lodging an appeal. In Osborne 93 ATC 4801, an extension was granted where there was only a 6-week delay and the disputed tax had been paid. In Logounov, the taxpayer's mental illness and failure of the AAT (as the ART then was) to inform the taxpayer of its decision to dismiss his application were taken into account in granting the extension after a 2-year delay. Where there is a real issue to be decided on appeal, a court will rarely order an individual taxpayer to provide security for costs (Fletcher 92 ATC 4437; [1992] FCA 388). A single judge may state a case or reserve a question for the consideration of the Full Federal Court.

On appeal from an ART decision, the Federal Court may make such order as it thinks ﬁt including, but not limited to, an order aﬃrming or setting aside the ART decision or an order remitting the case to the ART to be heard and determined again. An ART decision can be set aside where it is so unreasonable that no reasonable decision-maker would have made it (eg McCabe 90 ATC 4968; [1990] FCA 389). An ART decision can also be set aside where its reasons were mostly an unattributed reproduction of the Commissioner's submissions; this meant that the AAT (as the ART then was) failed to exercise its jurisdiction and committed an error of law (Thorpe 2012 ATC ¶20-346; [2012] FCA 997). In limited circumstances, the court may make new ﬁndings of fact in order to completely dispose of the matter rather than remit it to the ART (s 177).

If a matter is remitted to the ART to be heard again, the ART is not bound by the earlier ART's ﬁndings of fact (Blackman 93 ATC 4538; [1993] FCA 345). It may, however, reach a decision on the basis of the transcript of the earlier hearing (Fletcher 92 ATC 4611; [1992] FCA 454) unless the court orders that further evidence be heard. The ART need not be constituted by the same person(s) who made the earlier decision, and a person who was a member of the earlier ART is not automatically disqualiﬁed from being a member of the later ART (Fletcher 92 ATC 4611; [1992] FCA 454). However, the court may order that it be heard by a diﬀerently constituted ART (Jagelman 96 ATC 4055; [1995] FCA 1696).

Appeal to the Full Federal Court An appeal may be made against a decision of a single judge of the Federal Court. An appeal must be lodged within 28 days of the relevant judgment being handed down or within such further time as may be allowed.

An extension of time will not be granted merely because this would be administratively convenient for the applicant (Martin 97 ATC 4342). An extension may also be refused where the taxpayer has no prospects of succeeding in the appeal (Atkinson 2000 ATC 4332; [2000] FCA 552). The Full Court may aﬃrm, reverse or vary the judgment appealed, set aside (in whole or in part) the judgment appealed and remit it to the lower court or ART for further hearing and determination, or make such order as it thinks ﬁt. For fees payable on appeal, see ¶28-085.

If a party wants to argue a new case before the Full Court, it must seek the leave of the court to do so. The Commissioner was refused such leave in Dismin Investments 2001 ATC 4377; [2001] FCA 690. In that case, the Commissioner resiled from the position he had argued at ﬁrst instance and sought to attach the disputed tax liability to another step in the transaction. The taxpayer argued that it would have led other evidence had it known this was the Commissioner's position.

Appeal to the High Court An appeal against a decision of the Full Federal Court lies to the High Court, but only with special leave of the High Court. The application should be lodged within 28 days of judgment or within such further time as may be allowed. Special leave to appeal will generally only be granted where the appeal involves a question of fundamental principle and it has been considered by an intermediate appeal court (Australian National Hotels 89 ATC 4157). The High Court may aﬃrm, reverse or modify the judgment appealed.
