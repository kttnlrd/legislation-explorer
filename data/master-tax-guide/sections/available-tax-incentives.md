The availability of these concessions may be subject to the operation of special provisions dealing with limited recourse ﬁnance (¶23-260), certain leasing arrangements (¶23-210) and commercial debt forgiveness (¶16-910).

Foreign currency denominated amounts may be subject to the foreign currency conversion rules (¶23-070) and the foreign exchange gains and losses rules (¶23-075). development (R&D) tax incentive Outline of research and development (R&D) tax incentive Companies that conduct R&D activities may be able to claim a tax incentive for their business expenditure on R&D. R&D is deﬁned by ITAA97 Div 355 and is broadly in line with deﬁnition in the OECD's Frascati Manual 2015. Business expenditure on R&D can be to create new knowledge in "basic or applied research" or "experimental development". For the majority of companies, eligible R&D under the R&D tax incentive is in the "experimental development" of new or improved materials, products, devices, processes or services.

The incentives apply where the company needs to undertake experimental activities to overcome technical or scientiﬁc uncertainties as part of the research or to develop new or improved outcomes.

Example Crossing Pty Ltd designs and builds bridges. Where a bridge project is a reliably predeterminable adaptation of existing designs using standard construction methods and materials within known parameters, then these new bridges will not require R&D activities even if it is a unique or complex design. However, a bridge may use an innovative design, new materials or improved construction methods or it may push existing understandings or parameters. If so then parts of the design, approval, construction and testing of the bridge will need to include R&D experiments to resolve technical uncertainties in outcomes.

The R&D tax incentive (¶20-160) provides a tax oﬀset for the following expenditure: (1) R&D expenditure (eg labour, contractors, consumables, services, hired equipment, software and feedstock inputs to experimental production trials, etc) under s 355-205 (2) the decline in value of R&D assets (ie tax depreciation on tangible depreciating assets and non-building capital works assets used partly or exclusively to undertake R&D activities) — ss 355-305 and 355-520. Where the asset is used partly for R&D, the capital allowance will need to be apportioned (3) R&D expenditure incurred by an associate in an earlier year — s 355-480, and (4) R&D expenditure with an unconnected Research Service Provider (RSP) or as a contribution to a Cooperative Research Centre (CRC) — s 355-580.

The sum of expenditure on the ﬁrst 3 items must exceed $20,000 pa to be eligible for the tax oﬀset. However, there is no minimum expenditure requirement for RSP expenditure or CRC contributions.

For years commencing on or after 1 July 2021, certain entities with an aggregated turnover of less than $20 million may be eligible for a refundable tax oﬀset at their corporate tax rate plus 18.5% on these expenditures. All other eligible entities may be entitled to a non-refundable carry-forward tax oﬀset at their corporate tax rate plus 8.5% or 16.5% on these expenditures. The 16.5% amount only applies to R&D expenditure over the 2% total expenditure threshold — s 355-100(1A) (¶20-170). A portion of the 8.5%, 16.5% or 18.5% net after tax beneﬁt may be "clawed back" where the R&D entity: (1) is entitled to recoupment of costs under a grant or similar federal, state or territory government funding (2) sells or uses tangible goods made or partly made in R&D activities, or (3) makes a proﬁt on disposal of tangible depreciating assets used or partly used in R&D activities.

Note: if a loss on disposal is made, there may be a "catch up" adjustment for an under-claimed net after tax beneﬁt on tax depreciation.

For details on rates and adjustments for the R&D tax oﬀset for tax years that commenced prior to 1 July 2021 see the 71st and earlier editions of the Australian Master Tax Guide.
