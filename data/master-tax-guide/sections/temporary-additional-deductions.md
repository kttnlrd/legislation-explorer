# Temporary additional deductions ¶7-270

There are temporary additional 20% deductions available for certain expenditure by small and medium businesses. Note that the former Technology Investment Boost only applied to 30 June 2023.

Skills and Training Boost The Skills and Training Boost entitles small and medium businesses to a bonus 20% deduction on eligible external training expenditure for employees (ITTPA s 328-445). The bonus deduction does not apply for the training of non-employee business owners, including sole traders, partners in a partnership and independent contractors.

To be eligible, expenditure must be incurred on external training by a non-associated registered training provider for employees of the business and the entity must otherwise be entitled to a deduction for expenditure (ITTPA s 328-450).

The bonus deduction is only available for expenditure incurred from 7:30 pm (AEDT) on 29 March 2022 until 30 June 2024. The expenditure must relate to enrolments or arrangements for the provision of training made or entered into at or after 7:30 pm (AEDT) on 29 March 2022.

Small business energy incentive The small business energy incentive provides an additional 20% deduction for small and medium business expenditure supporting electrification and energy efficiency (ITTPA s 328-465). This bonus deduction is subject to a cap of $20,000, being on expenditure of up to $100,000 per business. The deduction only applies to the cost of eligible assets or upgrades first used or installed ready for use between 1 July 2023 and 30 June 2024 and is only available where the expenditure for the cost is deductible under another provision of the law, even if not wholly claimable in the year incurred.

Eligible expenditure includes the cost of eligible depreciating assets, as well as upgrades to existing assets, that support electrification and more efficient use of energy. Eligible expenditure excludes electric vehicles, renewable electricity generation assets, capital works, and assets not connected to the electricity grid that use fossil fuels.

*Refs: s 328-445), s 328-450), s 328-465)*
