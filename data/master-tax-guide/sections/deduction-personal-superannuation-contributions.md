# Deduction — personal superannuation contributions ¶13-730

Individuals are entitled to a deduction for making personal superannuation contributions if the relevant conditions in ITAA97 Subdiv 290-C are satisﬁed (see "Eligibility conditions — deductible personal superannuation contributions" below).

Subdivision 290-C does not apply to a contribution that is a roll-over superannuation beneﬁt (¶13-125) or a superannuation lump sum that is paid from a foreign superannuation fund or an amount transferred from a scheme that has never been an Australian superannuation fund or from a foreign superannuation fund (ITAA97 s 290-5).

An individual cannot deduct personal contributions, except as provided by Subdiv 290-C (ITAA97 s 290-10).

superannuation contributions An individual's personal superannuation contributions are deductible if the contributions are made to a complying superannuation fund or RSA for the purpose of providing superannuation beneﬁts for the individual, even if the beneﬁts are payable to the individual's "SIS dependants" (within the meaning of the SISA) on the death of the individual (ITAA97 s 290-150).

A deduction is allowed only for the year in which the contribution is made (ITAA97 s 290-150(3)).

If a contribution is the CGT exempt amount arising from the disposal of active assets of a small business and the contributor is under age 55 (¶7-185), the contribution is not deductible to the extent that it is attributable to the exempt amount (s 290-150(4)).

Where applicable, the following conditions must be met for a personal contribution to a superannuation fund to be deductible:

- the fund must be a "complying superannuation fund" (¶13-100), that is not:

– a Commonwealth public sector superannuation scheme in which the individual has a deﬁned beneﬁt interest (s 290-155(1)(a)(i)) – a constitutionally protected fund (CPF) or other untaxed fund that does not include the personal contributions in its assessable income under s 295-190 (s 290-155(1)(a)(ii)), or – a superannuation fund that has notiﬁed the ATO in the approved form, before the start of the income year, of its election to treat all member contributions to the fund as non-deductible or as deﬁned beneﬁt interests within the fund (s 295-180; ITR2021 ss 290-155.01 and 290-155.05) (ATO factsheet How to elect to be non-deductible (or revoke election)).

- if the individual is under age 18, the individual must have derived income from carrying on a

  business or from employment-related activities

- if the individual is between the age of 67 and 75, the individual must have satisﬁed a "work test" in

  the income year or the one-oﬀ exception to the work test (s 290-165: see "Age-related conditions"

- the contribution is not a downsizer contribution (s 290-167), or a re-contribution under the FHSS

Age-related conditions For an individual's personal superannuation contribution to be deductible under s 290-150, the age-related conditions below must be met.

Individuals under age 18 An individual under 18 years of age at the end of the income year in which the contribution was made must have derived income in the income year:

- from the carrying on of a business, or

- attributable to activities, or circumstances, that result in the individual being treated as an

  employee under the SGAA (ITAA97 s 290-165(1)).

Under the SGAA, an employee is a common law employee, or a person covered by the extended meaning of "employee" in the SGAA (eg a person who works under a contract for services), including a person doing work of a private or domestic nature for not more than 30 hours per week who is otherwise not treated as an employee under the SGAA (¶39-020).

Individuals between age 67 and 75 From 1 July 2022, an individual between 67 and 75 years of age must meet a work test, or a work test exception (a one-oﬀ rule), to claim a deduction for personal superannuation contributions. This applies to contributions made during the period starting on the day the individual turns 67 and ending on the day that is 28 days after the end of the month in which the individual turned 75.

To satisfy the work test, the individual must have been gainfully employed for at least 40 hours in any period of of 30 consecutive days during the income year in which the contribution was made (s 290-165(1A)(a)). "Gainfully employed" means being employed or self-employed for gain or reward in any business, trade, profession, vocation, calling, occupation or employment (s 995-1(1)).

The work test and one-oﬀ exception replicate similar requirements which previously applied under the SISR and RSAR before 1 July 2022 for the acceptance of contributions made to superannuation funds and RSAs by individuals aged 67 to 75. A modiﬁcation has been made to the operation of s 290-165(1A) so that an individual is able to meet the work test in s 290-165(1A) if they are an employee under the expanded meaning in the SISA or RSAA (see below).

If an individual makes a contribution to a fund and is unable to meet the work test or one-oﬀ exception, the contribution will remain a non-concessional contribution on the basis that no deduction can be claimed for it.

To provide clarity, no deduction is allowed for a contribution that is made after the 28th day after the end of the month in which an individual turned 75 (s 290-165(2)).

Modifications of s 290-165(1A) work test until 30 June 2028 As noted above, the SISR and RSAR work test that operated to limit the personal superannuation contributions that a superannuation fund or RSA provider could accept was relocated from 1 July 2022 to ITAA97 as one of the conditions for deductible personal contributions made by individuals aged 67 to 75.

The former SISR and RSAR work test also relied on an identical deﬁnition of "gainfully employed" as in ITAA97 (see above). However, a critical diﬀerence was that both the SISA and RSAA contain an expanded deﬁnition of employee which captures a range of persons as employees who may not otherwise be common law employees, eg company directors, constitutional or statutory oﬃce holders, parliamentarians and members of the Australian Defence Force (SISA s 15A(2) to (10); RSAA s 19(2) to (10)).

By contrast, there is no deﬁnition of "employee" in ITAA97 and the term has its common law meaning, As such, there may be people who were previously able to claim deductions (because they fell within the SISA or RSAA expanded deﬁnition of employee) but are not "gainfully employed" for ITAA97 purposes because they are not common law employees or self-employed.

The Commissioner has made a legislative instrument which provides that s 290-165(1A) is modiﬁed to operate as if an individual also meets the requirement:

- in para 290-165(1A)(a) — if the individual worked for gain or reward as a covered person

  for at least 40 hours in any period of 30 consecutive days during the income year in which the

  contribution was made, and

- in subpara 290-165(1A)(b)(i) — if the individual worked for gain or reward as a covered person

  for at least 40 hours in any period of 30 consecutive days during the income year ending before the

  income year in which the contribution was made.

A "covered person" means a person who is an employee under SISA s 15A(2) to (10) (Taxation Administration (Remedial Power – Work Test for Personal Superannuation Contributions) Determination 2023: F2023L00564).

The Commissioner's determination is made under TAA sch 1 s 370-5. It applies in relation to contributions made on or after 1 July 2022 and will cease on 1 July 2028.

Notice of intent to deduct contributions condition To qualify for a deduction for the whole or a part of personal contributions, an individual must give a notice of intent in the approved form to the superannuation fund trustee or RSA provider to claim a deduction for the whole or a part of the contributions covered by the notice, and the trustee or RSA provider must have given the individual an acknowledgment of the notice (ITAA97 ss 290-170(1), 290-175) (ATO form NAT 71121).

Validity of notice and acknowledgment A notice is not valid in any of the circumstances speciﬁed in s 290-170(2) (eg the contribution is covered by an earlier notice or contributions-splitting application).

A trustee or RSA provider is required to acknowledge receipt of a valid notice without delay (normally within 30 days of receipt or by 30 June of the ﬁnancial year in which the contribution is made) (s 290-170(3)).

The trustee or RSA provider may refuse to acknowledge receipt of a valid notice if the value of the superannuation interest to which the notice relates when the notice is received is less than the tax that would be payable in respect of the contribution if the trustee or provider were to acknowledge receipt of the notice (ITAA97 s 290-170(4)).

Revocation and variation of notice An individual cannot revoke or withdraw a valid notice in relation to the contribution or a part of the contribution (ITAA97 s 290-180).

A valid notice can be varied, but only so as to reduce the amount stated in relation to the contribution (including to nil) (ATO approved form).

An individual cannot vary a valid notice in certain cases, but this restriction does not apply if:

- the individual claimed a deduction for the contribution (or a part of the contribution) and the

  deduction is not allowable (in whole or in part), and

- the variation reduces the amount stated in relation to the contribution by the amount not allowable

  as a deduction (s 290-180(3), (4)).

A variation notice is not eﬀective if, when it is made, the individual has ceased to be a member, the fund no longer holds the contribution or has commenced to pay a pension based in whole or in part on the contribution (s 290-180(3A)).

If a fund no longer holds a contribution, or a part of it, because the member has rolled over a part of the superannuation interest held in the fund, a valid deduction notice will be limited to a proportion of the tax free component of the interest that remains in the fund after the roll-over. That proportion is the value of the relevant contribution divided by the tax free component of the interest immediately before the roll-over.

An income stream is based in whole or part on the contribution if it is commenced from the superannuation interest to which the contribution was made (TR 2010/1, para 71, 72, 94–102).

Example Lisa makes a $50,000 personal contribution increasing her interest in her superannuation fund to $200,000.

If she were to commence a pension from the fund using $180,000 of her $200,000 interest before lodging a s 290-170 deduction notice, her fund would have commenced to pay a superannuation income stream based in whole or in part of the contribution.

Any deduction notice that Lisa later gives to her fund will be invalid. This is so even if, after contributing the $50,000, Lisa were to commence a pension of only $140,000 leaving the value of her interest in excess of the amount she intended to deduct.

Notices to a successor fund An individual may give a deduction or variation notice to a successor fund for contributions made to the original superannuation fund (s 290-170(5)). If an individual's account in a superannuation fund is mandatorily transferred to a MySuper product in another complying fund under the SISA, the notice may be given to the transferee fund for the contribution that was made to original fund (s 290-170(6)).

Related tax matters A deduction for personal superannuation contributions under Subdiv 290-C is limited to an individual's adjusted assessable income for the year (before taking into account the contribution deduction) (ITAA97 s 26-55(2); Sutton 2013 ATC ¶10-336; [2013] AATA 661). The deduction cannot create or increase a loss to be carried forward.

A deduction is personal and is not taken into account in calculating the loss or proﬁt of any partnership of which the individual is a member (ITAA36 s 90).

An individual's deductible superannuation contributions are assessable contributions and are subject to 15% contributions tax in the hands of the recipient superannuation fund (¶13-125) or RSA provider (¶13-480). Deductible contributions are "concessional contributions" under ITAA97 (¶13-820). An individual's concessional contribution that is not allowable as a deduction under Subdiv 290-C is a non-concessional contribution for the year (ITAA97 s 292-90: ¶13-860).

Transfers from a KiwiSaver scheme to a complying superannuation fund are not deductible personal contributions (s 312-10(2): ¶13-380).

No deduction is allowed for ﬁnancing costs (eg interest) incurred on borrowings to ﬁnance personal superannuation contributions (ITAA97 s 26-80: ¶16-740).
