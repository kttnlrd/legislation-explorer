# 108-15  Sets of collectables


<a id="s108-15-1"></a>
**(1)** This section sets out what happens if:

> <a id="s108-15-1-a"></a>
> **(a)** you own *collectables that are a set; and

> <a id="s108-15-1-b"></a>
> **(b)** they would ordinarily be *disposed of as a set; and

> <a id="s108-15-1-c"></a>
> **(c)** you dispose of them in one or more transactions for the purpose of trying to obtain the exemption in section 118-10.

> **Example:** You buy a set of 3 books for $900. You apportion the $900 among each book: see section 112-30. If the books are of equal value, you have acquired each one for $300. If you dispose of each book individually, you would ordinarily obtain the exemption in section 118-10, because you acquired each one for less than $500.

<a id="s108-15-2"></a>
**(2)** The set of *collectables is taken to be a single *collectable and each of your *disposals is a disposal of part of that collectable.

> **Example:** To continue the example, the 3 books are taken to be a single collectable. You will not obtain the exemption in section 118-10, because you acquired the set for more than $500. You work out if you make a capital gain or loss from a disposal of part of an asset by comparing the capital proceeds from it with the cost base or reduced cost base (as appropriate) of the disposed part.

> **Note:** Section 112-30 tells you how to apportion the cost base and reduced cost base of a CGT asset on a disposal of part of an asset.

> **Note:** This section does not apply to a collectable you last acquired before 16 December 1995: see section 108-15 of the Income Tax (Transitional Provisions) Act 1997.
