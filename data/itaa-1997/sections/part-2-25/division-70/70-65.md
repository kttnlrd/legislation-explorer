# 70-65  Working out the horse opening value and the horse reduction amount


<a id="s70-65-1"></a>
**(1)** The horse opening value is:

<a id="s70-65-1-a"></a>
**(a)** if the horse has been your *live stock ever since the start of the income year—its *value as *trading stock at the start of the income year; or

<a id="s70-65-1-b"></a>
**(b)** otherwise—the horse's base amount (see subsection (3)).

<a id="s70-65-2"></a>
**(2)** The horse reduction amount is worked out as follows:

<a id="s70-65-2-a"></a>
**(a)** for female horses under 12 years of age: Base amount         Breeding days ´ Reduction factor   Days in income year

<a id="s70-65-2-b"></a>
**(b)** for any male horse: Breeding days Base amount ´ Nominated ´ percentage  Days in income year

<a id="s70-65-3"></a>
**(3)** In this section: base amount is the lesser of:

<a id="s70-65-3-a"></a>
**(a)** the horse's *cost; and

<a id="s70-65-3-b"></a>
**(b)** the horse's *adjustable value when it most recently became your *live stock. breeding days is the number of whole days in the income year since you most recently began to hold the horse for breeding. nominated percentage is any percentage, up to 25%, you nominate when you make the election in section 70-60. reduction factor is the greater of:

<a id="s70-65-3-a-2"></a>
**(a)** 3; and

<a id="s70-65-3-b-2"></a>
**(b)** the difference between 12 and the horse's age when you most recently began to hold it for breeding.
