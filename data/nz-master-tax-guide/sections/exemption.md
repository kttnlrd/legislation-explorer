# exemption ¶10-818

"New build land" is exempt from the interest limitation rules for 20 years (s DH 4(1)). The exemption attaches to the property, not the landowner, so the exemption transfers to subsequent purchasers within that 20- year period. Generally, the 20-year period starts the day the code of compliance certificate (CCC) is issued.

See Examples in Tax Information Bulletin ¶345-105 Vol 34, No 5, June 2022 pp 73–74 to demonstrate the application of this exemption.

New build land is land:

## to the extent to which it has a place configured as a self-contained residence or abode on it, and

## for which a CCC has been issued on or after 27 March 2020.

"To the extent" means that the definition encompasses not until new residential buildings but also land that might include a new build alongside an existing building (eg adding a new residence or a modular home, or relocating home to the land) and conversions (eg converting an existing residence into 2 or more self- contained residences, and converting a commercial building into self-contained residences). See below for a discussion on apportionment.

Also included in the definition of new build land are:

## areas that are used exclusively by residents of the new build

## areas that are shared by a new build and a non-new build (eg a shared driveway or garden area)

## new builds bought off the plans

## former hotel and motel units that are converted into self-contained units or apartments

## residential properties that were previously on the earthquake-prone buildings register that have been

remediated and removed from the register

## leaky buildings that been remediated.

In every case a CCC must have been issued on or after 27 March and/or noted in the local authority's records or building consent.

Although not a defined term, a "self-contained" residence or unit should include its own essential facilities such as a kitchen and bathroom. A sleepout without a kitchen and bathroom would not qualify as a new build. Hotels and motels that are converted into self-contained residences also qualify, as do modular and relocated homes.

*Refs: s DH 4(1)*

## Exemption start date

The 20-year period runs from the earliest of the following dates:

## the date on which the CCC is issued, or

## the date that, in the local authority or building consent records, the relevant conversion or remediation

is recorded as having been "completed" (where applicable), or

## the date that the relevant building works is entered into the records of a local authority or building

consent authority as "substantially completed", in the case of a CCC being issued subject to a building consent waiver or modification under clause B2.3.1 of the Building Code under the Building Act 2004 (where applicable).

## Example: Exemption applies to all interest for new build land

Iona owns land that qualifies for the new build exemption. The new build on the land is always rented out. During the period the exemption applies, Iona decides to add a new deck to the new build. Iona will be able to deduct the interest she incurs on the loan she takes out to add the deck.

Ten years after the new build receives its CCC, Iona decides to repaint it. She takes out another loan to pay for repainting the new build. The interest she incurs on that loan would also be deductible. If Iona is still incurring interest in relation to the new build land after the exemption expires (20 years after the CCC was issued), she will not be able to deduct that interest because the interest limitation rule will apply.

Example reproduced from the "Special report on the interest limitation and additional bright-line rules" published in March 2022 by Inland Revenue.

Once the 20 years have elapsed, interest incurred will be treated as non-deductible in accordance with the general rule on interest limitation. See ¶10-814.

## New build land: exception for off-the-plans acquisitions

Off-the-plans acquisitions are an important part of a common funding model for property developers.

Deposits for new builds acquired off the plans may be debt-funded by the purchaser, which may result in the purchaser acquiring the new build and incurring interest before a CCC is issued for the new build. A special rule is therefore included for new builds acquired off the plans, so that a taxpayer who acquires such a new build can access the exemption from the date they enter into an agreement to acquire it. If the interest would be deductible under existing tax rules, then interest may be deducted from the date of acquisition for new builds acquired off the plans.

## New build land: exception for hotel and motel conversions

The definition of new build land is sufficiently wide enough to cover situations where hotel or motel units are converted to residential accommodation. As hotel and motel units are used for short-stay accommodation and often already have some cooking facilities and bathrooms, less work may be required to convert them into places suitable for long-term accommodation compared to other conversions (for example, conversions of commercial office blocks into apartments). As a result, a CCC may not be issued in these types of conversions. In such cases, the new build exemption applies from the date that the local authority or building consent authority records indicate the conversion was completed.

## Impact of B2.3.1 modification on exemption period for interest

deductibility Clause B2.3.1 of the Building Code concerns the durability of a building once it is completed. Normally the period for which a building is durable is counted from the date a CCC is issued for building work. However, where a CCC is significantly delayed (usually by more than 5 years), the CCC will often be issued subject to a B2.3.1 modification. The effect of a B2.3.1 modification is the durability period for the building is counted from the date the building work for the building was substantially completed, which could be a number of years before the CCC was issued.

Where a CCC is issued subject to a B2.3.1 modification, the exemption will end earlier because the 20- year period is counted from the date the building work to add the new build to the land was substantially completed.

The purpose of the B2.3.1 modification rule is to ensure there is no tax benefit from significantly delaying the issuance of a new build's CCC.

## Example: B2.3.1 modification rule

Rip owns a large section that has a residential rental property on one half of it. The rental was built on the land in the 1980s, so interest deductions would be progressively denied from 1 October 2021 under the interest limitation rules. However, Rip decides to develop the other half of the section to add a new build to it. He claims interest deductions in respect of the portion of the land he is developing under the development exemption. Although the building work for the new build is substantially completed by 24 October 2021, Rip decides he probably wants to sell the property sometime in the next ten years, so he deliberately holds off getting a CCC for the new build to maximise its potential value to a subsequent debt-financed purchaser.

Eight years later, Rip decides he wants to sell the land. Rip gets in touch with his local council to request a CCC for the new build.

The council issues a CCC for the new build on 24 October 2029 but issues it subject to a B2.3.1 modification. On the CCC, the substantial completion date for the new build is recorded as being 24 October 2021, which is the date the durability of the building is counted from (instead of the date the CCC was issued). Since Rip's new build's CCC was issued subject to a B2.3.1 modification, the 20-year period for the new build exemption would be counted from the date of substantial completion (24 October 2021), rather than the date the CCC for the new build was issued (24 October 2029), under proposed new section DH 4(1)(b)(iii). This means that Rip would gain no tax advantage by delaying the CCC for his new build. ## Exemption for development and division of new build land

Related to the "new build" exemption is the exemption for an undertaking or scheme involving development, division or building for the purpose of creating new build land. The existing meaning of "undertaking and scheme" used in ss CB 12 and CB 13 applies. See ¶7-116. This exemption from the interest limitation rules applies from the date an undertaking or scheme begins and ends once the land becomes "new build land" (which is usually when the CCC has been issued).

Exemptions may apply concurrently. The new build exemption can apply for the purchaser of a new build at the same time the land business or development exemption applies for the developer that is developing the land. Usually this would occur where a new build is purchased off the plans (that is, before it is constructed) and the purchaser has borrowed in order to finance the purchase of the new build land.

## Apportionment for new builds

The exceptions and exemptions to the interest limitation rules expressly contemplate apportionment by use of the words "to the extent that" in s DH 5(7). For example, the new build exemption applies to the extent that the property qualifies as new build land. If the interest on borrowings is incurred in relation to both new build land and other land that does not qualify as new build land, an apportionment of interest will be required.

Apportionment should be carried out using a reasonable method, which can include a valuation-based apportionment or a land area apportionment method.
