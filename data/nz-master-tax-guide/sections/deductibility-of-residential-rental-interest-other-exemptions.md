# Deductibility of residential rental interest — other exemptions ¶10-817

Generally, interest expense incurred in connection to residential rental property is denied (¶10-814). The legislation makes exceptions to this general rule for a number of taxpayers and types of land. The complete list is found at ¶10-815. Below is a discussion on the other exemptions.

## Exemption for dealers, builders and developers

Interest deductions continue to be available for those taxpayers who are carrying on business and the land in question is subject to tax under s CB 7 (s DH 4(2)). This provision taxes land dealers, builders and developers. See ¶7-055 for a discussion on s CB 7. These taxpayers are specifically exempt from the interest limitation rules to the extent that any interest paid is in relation to their business. This exemption is intended to apply to any activity that results in a dwelling that qualifies as a "new build".

The exemption is permitted for business-related land even if no work is being actively carried on at the relevant time. The exemption also applies to land dealers who may not necessarily increase housing stock (ie those who purchase land and renovate or remediate the building and then on-sell as part of their business).

If a property development business acquires residential property, however, for a purpose other than property development, then the interest limitation rules will apply. If the use of this land later changes and the land is developed, then interest related to the land will become deductible under the development exemption.

A person undertaking development activity will switch from the development exemption to the new build exemption once code compliance certificates are issued for the new dwellings. See Example in Tax Information Bulletin, ¶345-105, Vol 34, No 5, June 2022 at 69 to illustrate the application of the exemption.

## Land development

There is also an exemption from the rules limiting deductibility if a taxpayer carries out a scheme or undertaking involving development, subdivision or building for the purpose of creating a new build land (s DH 4(3)). This exemption is designed for those taxpayers who are not in business as contemplated by s CB 7 but who carry out development work that results in a new build.

This exemption does not apply to a taxpayer merely purchasing a residential property for renovation and on- selling with the purpose of making a profit (as opposed to carrying on a business). Such a taxpayer may, however, qualify for an interest deduction when the property is disposed of if the disposal is taxable.

The development exemption applies to interest incurred in relation to land that is, or was, subject to a person's undertaking or scheme involving development, division, or building for the purpose of creating new build land. Once the land becomes new build land, if the person retains the land, the development exemption would no longer apply and the new build exemption would instead apply to the land.

Initially when the legislation was drafted the development exemption would not apply once the new build land was disposed of. The final version of the s DH 4(3) allows the development exemption to continue to apply after the land has been disposed of. The development exemption only applies to the developer and not to the new owner of new build land.

## Example: Land transitions from development exemption to new build exemption

Amber and Sandy buy a large section in Orewa. They purchase the land with the intention of adding new builds to the land, renting these out for a short period, and then selling them off for a profit. Once they start an undertaking or scheme to develop and build on the land to add new builds, they qualify for the development exemption. After construction is complete, the new builds receive their CCCs. At this point, the development exemption ceases to apply to the land, and instead, the new build exemption starts to apply.

The new build exemption will continue to apply to the land until 20 years have passed from the date the new builds received their CCCs. Three years after the residences receive their CCCs, Amber and Sandy sell the land to James. The new build exemption continues to apply to the land, but it will only apply for another 17 years – the 20-year exemption period does not reset when the land is sold.

Example reproduced from the "Special report on the interest limitation and additional bright-line rules" published in March 2022 by Inland Revenue.

The new build exemption can apply for the purchaser of a new build at the same time the land business or development exemption applies for another taxpayer that is developing the land. This will usually occur where a new build is purchased off the plans. This is because the developer (or builder) may qualify for the development exemption while they erect the new build, and the purchaser may also qualify for the new build exemption as they have acquired an interest in land and incurred mortgage interest when they acquired the land off plan.

Flowchart 5 from the "Special report on the interest limitation and additional bright-line rules" published in March 2022 by IR demonstrates how to apply the development exemption.

The exemption applies from the date an undertaking or scheme to develop, subdivide or build on the land for the purpose of creating new build land begins. It will end for the person undertaking the development once the land becomes new build land if the person retains the land. Land would generally become "new build land" when a CCC is issued for a self-contained residence or abode that has been added to the land, provided the CCC is issued on or after 27 March 2020.

## Business premises not subject to interest limitation rules

"Business premises" is defined as a person's normal business premises or a temporary workplace, but excluding premises established mainly for the purpose of enjoying entertainment. Therefore, interest can be claimed as an expense on all property used for commercial purposes (such as shops, offices, entertainment venues, warehouses and factories).

## Farmland and farmhouses not subject to interest limitation

rules Farmland is not subject to the interest limitation rules. Farmland is defined as land that is being worked in the landowner's farming or agricultural business (including forestry, horticultural and pastoral businesses) or land that, because of its area and nature, is capable of being worked as an economic unit as a farming or agricultural business. Inland Revenue notes that farmhouse and workers quarters located on the farmland are also excluded from the rules (Tax Information Bulletin ¶345-105 Vol 34, No 5, June 2022).

## Boarding establishments

Boarding establishments are not subject to the interest limitation rules. A "boarding establishment" is defined, for the purposes of the interest limitation rules, as premises used in a business of supplying accommodation if the premises are managed by the business and has:

## at least 10 boarding rooms that are not self-contained, and

## shared living facilities available as appropriate to all residents.

## Student accommodation

"Student accommodation" is defined as commercial boarding premises used for the accommodation of students enrolled at a registered school or premises described in s 5B of the Residential Tenancies Act 1986 (including premises used mainly, but not exclusively, for the accommodation of students). This definition

encompasses halls of residence and hostels, either owned by tertiary education providers or by entities that have a specific arrangement with a tertiary education provider.

## Employee accommodation

"Employee accommodation" falls outside the interest limitation rules as it falls within the definition of "excepted residential land" (see s DH 5(3) read with Sch 15).

It is defined as property that an employer provides to their employees or other workers for accommodation in connection with their employment or service. The property may be owned by the employer, or if the employer is a company, it may be owned by another company in the same wholly-owned group of companies as the employer. The inclusion of property provided to a person's employees takes into account corporate group scenarios where the company employing the workers may not be the same company that holds the residential property.

However, it does not include accommodation provided to employees (or other workers) that are associated to the employer, unless it is necessary to provide accommodation because of the nature or remoteness of a business carried on.

## M#ori excepted land

"M#ori excepted land", which falls outside the interest limitation rules, means:

## M#ori customary land, M#ori freehold land, Crown land reserved for M#ori, or land set aside as a

M#ori reservation

## land provided as a residence to a M#ori shareholder or beneficiary of a M#ori authority (or an entity

eligible to be one) to the extent the land is partly or wholly owned, directly or indirectly, by that M#ori authority, or

## land, owned directly or indirectly by a M#ori authority (or an entity eligible to be one) to the extent to

which the land is from a Treaty of Waitangi settlement, including by exercising a right of first refusal under such a settlement.

## Social, emergency, transitional and council housing

Land used to provide social, emergency, transitional and council housing is exempt from the interest limitation rules. The owner of the property does not need to be the same entity that provides the housing and has the relationship with the tenant. See Example in Tax Information Bulletin ¶345-105 Vol 34, No 5, June 2022 at 42 to demonstrate the application of this rule.
