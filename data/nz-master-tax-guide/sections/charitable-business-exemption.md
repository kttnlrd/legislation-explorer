# Charitable business exemption ¶24-200

Income derived by a business carried on for charitable purposes, or for business income derived by or on behalf of or for the benefit of any trust, society or institution, is exempt if:

## the income is derived by a trustee in trust for charitable purposes, or derived by a society or institution

established and maintained exclusively for charitable purposes and not carried on for the private pecuniary profit of any individual

## the trust, society or institution carries out its charitable purposes in New Zealand

## the trustee, society or institution is a tax charity, and

## no person with some control over the business is able to direct or divert income to the benefit or

advantage of:

## if the charity is carrying on the business for another entity (the controlling entity), a person other than

the charity or the controlling entity except for a purpose of the charity or controlling entity, or

## in any other case, a person other than the charity except for a purpose of the charity.

With effect from 1 April 2020 for the 2020–21 and later income years, the entity carrying on the business must also be registered as a charitable entity under the Charities Act 2005 at the time the income is derived.

To attract the exemption, the business must be carried on for charitable purposes. "Charitable purpose" is defined to include "the relief of poverty, the advancement of education or religion, or any other matter beneficial to the community". The exemption is not available if a person associated with the charity is able to receive some financial benefit from it. This exception precludes application of the exemption in situations in which a taxpayer carries on a business through the medium of a charitable trust or company in circumstances in which it is likely that they would have carried on that business in their own personal capacity (¶24-210).

Example: The business must be carried on for charitable purposes, rather than just the derivation of the income, which must be held for those purposes. For the exemption to apply, the income earned need not be applied immediately for the charitable purposes; it may be held in reserve in the course of carrying on the business. See Calder Construction Co Ltd v C of IR [1963] NZLR 921.

Example: When a business is not carried on in trust for charitable purposes, a subsequent disposition of income to a charity does not make it "charitable". See MK Hunt Foundation Ltd v C of IR [1961] NZLR 405.

Also see Trustees of the Auckland Medical Aid Trust v C of IR (1979) 4 NZTC 61,404, Cowey Mills & Co Ltd v C of IR (1982) 5 NZTC 61,172 and C of IR v NTN Bearing-Saeco (New Zealand) Ltd (1986) 8 NZTC 5,039 regarding charitable companies.

Charitable trusts are attractive to businesses, the main benefit being the exemption from income tax. Any organisation intending to set up a non-profit body would most probably seek to establish a charity as its first option. For example, a large company seeking brand image may choose a charity in which the charity, through the use of the company logo or name, indirectly promotes the institution setting it up. A charity can be set up by a trust deed, an incorporated society or a charitable company that would be limited by shares or by guarantee. For tax purposes, any institution or society may qualify (¶24-195).

IS 24/08 FS 1, "Income tax – Charities – Business income exemption", at [3] and [5] provides illustrations of determining whether a registered charity's business income is exempt, and types of income.

Charity overseas

Business income, in terms of the exemption, is exempt only to the extent that it is derived for charitable purposes within New Zealand. The income of a business having charitable purposes both here and overseas is, accordingly, apportioned by the Commissioner. The exemption is granted only in respect of that part applicable to charitable purposes within New Zealand. See C of IR v Dick (2001) 20 NZTC 17,396.

Under the Charities Act 2005 an entity is not prevented from being registered as a charitable entity just because it has charitable purposes outside New Zealand. Registration requirements under the Charities Act are discussed at ¶24-195.

In cases where registration under the Charities Act is not available, a trustee of a trust, society or an organisation that is non-resident and carrying out its charitable purpose outside New Zealand, may still be capable of approval as a "tax charity" by the Commissioner (s CW 41(5)(c)).

*Refs: s CW 41(5)(c)*

## Questions we've been asked and interpretation statement

In December 2020, the Commissioner released 2 draft questions we've been asked concerning the charities business exemptions.

The first, PUB00359a, "Charities business exemption – when it must be used", considers the situations in which a charitable entity needs to use the business income exemption test in s CW 42 (which contains additional territorial and control restrictions) rather than the exemption test in s CW 41. The draft item concludes that a charitable entity must use the test in s CW 42 to determine if income derived from a business is exempt if:

## the charitable entity is carrying on the business, or

## another charitable entity (the operating entity) is carrying on the business for, or for the benefit of, the

charitable entity (the controlling entity).

A business will be carried on by an operating entity for, or for the benefit of, a controlling entity if all the rights to the income and capital of the business held by the operating entity are held for the benefit of the controlling entity.

The second draft item was finalised in May 2021. QB 21/03, "Charities business exemption – business carried on in partnership", considers whether income derived by a charitable entity from a business will be exempt under s CW 42 if the business is carried on by a charitable entity in partnership with a non-charitable entity. The item concludes that income can be exempt in these circumstances.

Interpretation statement, IS 24/08, "Charities – Business income exemption", discusses the Commissioner's interpretation of s CW 42 when applying the provision to business income derived where the business is carried out exclusively for charity. This interpretation statement covers charities with charitable purposes both in and outside of New Zealand.

There is uncertainty around the application of the provision due to differing interpretations of s CW 42.

This interpretation statement covers the interpretative and practical issues which a charity may face when applying the exemption. For a tax charity's business income to be treated as exempt from tax, all the requirements and restrictions must apply. The restrictions are:

## If income is derived domestically and from overseas, the amounts must be apportioned and only to

the extent that the income is domestically derived, will the exemption apply. This test must be applied each year and the percentage of income from different sources may change.

## If a person has control and is able to divert income from the business, and that control restriction is

beached, then all the business income the charity derives will be taxable income. The control restriction is discussed at ¶24-210.
