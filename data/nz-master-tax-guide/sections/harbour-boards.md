# Harbour boards ¶24-040

As part of the government's policy to privatise certain State enterprises, port companies were established in 1988 to purchase the property and assets of harbour boards and to carry out port-related commercial activities. Most port companies are now owned by local authorities, regional councils or other shareholder entities. Therefore, amounts derived from a port-related commercial undertaking are treated as income.

Example: Some of the tax issues faced by the Auckland Harbour Board upon restructuring under the Port Companies Act 1988 were considered by the High Court. The case considered deductions that the taxpayer had claimed for depreciation on the disposal of assets, ordinary depreciation, legal fees, establishment costs and the expenditure arising from the base price adjustment under the financial arrangements rules on the transfer of a financial arrangement. See Auckland Harbour Board v C of IR (1998) 18 NZTC 13,826 and (1999) 19 NZTC 15,433 (CA). The financial arrangements issue was appealed to the Privy Council. See C of IR v Auckland Harbour Board (2001) 20 NZTC 17,008 (PC).
