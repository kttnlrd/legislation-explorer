# Deceased estate — tax returns ¶25-650

## Estates are not trusts but some trust rules apply

The mere fact a person has died and an executor or administrator has been appointed to manage their estate does not automatically give rise to a trust relationship. However, a testamentary trust is created by way of a statement in a will that specific property of the deceased will be held on trust for a named beneficiary. The testamentary trust will be subject to the trust tax rules.

For tax purposes, regardless of whether a testamentary trust arises following a person's death, the definition of "trustee" under s YA 1 includes an executor and administrator. While executors and administrators can be professionals who are paid for undertaking the role, many are not. Where the terms of a will provide for a gratuitous payment to an executor or trustee such a payment can meet the definition of a "pension" for the purposes of s CF 1.

## Note: The Taxation (Annual Rates for 2025–26, Compliance Simplification, and Remedial Measures) Bill

proposes an amendment to s CF 1, which would specify that a gratuitous payment made to the trustee of the estate of an individual who has died is excluded from the definition of "pension" under s CF 1.

## Tax return to the date of death

The executor or administrator of a deceased person is required to furnish any returns which the deceased would have been liable to furnish had they remained alive (s 43).

This means that the executor or administrator must furnish any necessary returns of income for the year ended on the previous 31 March (or other balance date) if not already filed by the deceased, and for the period 1 April (or other balance date) to the date of death. The return of income up to the date of death must include all assessable income of the deceased that would be assessable income derived as at that date.

The Commissioner may require the furnishing of such further returns relating to the income of the deceased as the Commissioner thinks necessary. Income tax assessed in respect of a deceased person is deemed to be a liability incurred by the deceased during their lifetime and the executor or administrator is liable for its payment. However, liability for income tax in respect of income derived by the deceased before death is limited to the net assets of the estate. This includes tax that may be assessed as the consequence of the Commissioner reopening prior year assessments. Note that the Commissioner can write off outstanding tax if a deceased person's estate has been distributed.

Under s HC 8(1B), reportable income received up to 28 days after a person's date of death can be included in the final return for the deceased person and is not treated as income for the estate.

## Registration of the deceased estate

A deceased estate is treated as a separate entity for tax purposes. The executor or administrator must register the estate as a new tax payer and apply for a new IRD number in the name of the estate using form IR596.

## Trustee return for income derived after death

Apart from any reportable income subject to s HC 8(1B), income derived by the deceased estate after the date of death is treated as trustee income (ss CV 12 and HC 8(2)). Note that the terms of the deceased person's will can be relevant in determining whether income is income of the deceased or income that must

be accounted for as trustee or beneficiary income (¶25-660 and ¶25-680). The executor or administrator must furnish trustee tax returns for the estate using form IR6 until the estate is wound up.

From 1 April 2024, the trustee income of the deceased estate is subject to a 33% tax rate for the income year of death and the following 3 income years (s HC 8B and ¶25-080). After this time period, the trustee income is taxed at 39% unless the estate qualifies as a de minimis trust.

## Final trustee return on wound up

If the final distribution of estate assets or trust income is to be made, the trustee should ensure that sufficient funds have been reserved to meet all tax obligations which will or may prove to be payable either in the trustee's own right or as agent of the beneficiaries. This is because the trustee may be liable for any shortfall.

See The Public Trustee v Flower (1991) 13 NZTC 8,042. This is not to say, however, that the trustee is absolutely liable for the trust tax. An estate could be insolvent and, in such a case, the trustee is not personally liable. The trustee should furnish a final tax return for the trust covering the period from the preceding 1 April to the date of final distribution.

## Non-active deceased estates

From 1 April 2022, executors of estates that do not derive income during a tax year do not have to file a return (s 43B). Application for the exemption is made to the Commissioner.

Notes to the Non-active trust declaration (form IR633 (April 2023)) list a number of factors that should not be taken into consideration when establishing whether a trust or estate will be non-active. They are:

## bank and other administrative charges of less than $1,500

## reasonable fees paid to professional trustees

## reportable income of less than $1,000

## insurance, rates and other incidental expenditure incidental to the occupation of a dwelling incurred

by beneficiaries who are living in a dwelling owned by a trust.
