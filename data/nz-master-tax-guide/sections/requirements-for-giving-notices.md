# Requirements for giving notices ¶1-645

#Note: The Taxation (Annual Rates for 2025–26, Compliance Simplification, and Remedial Measures) Bill is proposing an amendment to clarify that when the Commissioner is required to publish information under the TAA, the IT07, or the GST Act, he can do so when the information is made accessible and available on an internet site maintained by or on behalf of IR.

The TAA, IT07, GST Act and KiwiSaver Act 2006 often require parties to give notice. The rules for giving notices were changed with effect from 2 June 2016. These rules are discussed below. (Note that the Student Loan Scheme Act 2011 (SLSA) contains its own rules for giving notice under that Act (ss 210–214 of the SLSA).) The rules clarify the communication options available and also provide general rules governing methods of communication delivery. In addition, where legislation requires the Commissioner, a taxpayer or a third party to ask, request, inform, apply or notify, these verbs have been defined.

*Refs: ss 210, KiwiSaver Act 2006*

## Communications framework

A communications framework, contained in ss 14–14G of the TAA, facilitates the information flows between the Commissioner and a person, and between 2 persons where the tax legislation governs that interaction.

The rules preserve the precedence of prescribed forms and formats and any specific requirements covered within particular sections. Practically, this means that where paper forms have been prescribed, but no electronic equivalents have been made available, taxpayers will be required to continue to file paper returns on prescribed forms until electronic equivalents are made available. Section 14E also preserves the overriding effect of double tax agreements and other inter-governmental treaties, by applying the new communication rules only to the extent to which they are not inconsistent with the application of the particular agreement.

## Three tiers of communication

Sections 14B–14D create 3 distinct tiers to accommodate various modes of communication ranging from the informal (including oral communication) to electronic (whether by electronic filing through IR's website, email or other electronic means) to more formal methods requiring paper or original documents. The tiers are signalled by the use of the following verbs — ask, request, or inform; apply or notify; and formally notify.

Ask, request or inform The first tier is found in s 14B and deals with person A:

## asking person B for something

## requesting something from person B, or

## informing person B about something.

In each case, person A may communicate:

## by telephone

## orally in person in a manner acceptable to the Commissioner

## by electronic means for an item of information delivered in an appropriate way (see below)

## in print and delivered in an appropriate way (see below), or

## in another manner permitted by the Commissioner.

Communication under this tier does not include communication on the internet or by other electronic means if person B is not directly alerted to the communication in some manner.

Apply or notify

The second tier is found in s 14C and deals with person A:

## applying to person B for something, or

## notifying person B about something.

In each case, person A may communicate:

## by electronic means for an item of information delivered in an appropriate way (see below)

## in print and delivered in an appropriate way (see below), or

## in another manner permitted by the Commissioner.

Communication under this tier also does not include communication on the internet or by other electronic means if person B is not directly alerted to the communication in some manner.

Formally notify The third tier is in s 14D and deals with provisions that refer to or describe person A formally notifying person B. In this case, person A may communicate only in print, delivered personally or by registered post.

Communication under this tier does not include communication by email, the internet or other electronic means.

General rules Communication by electronic means must comply with Pt 4 of the CCLA. If communication is in print, it does not matter whether the document is handwritten, typewritten or otherwise visibly represented or whether it is copied or reproduced on paper.

The first and second tiers allow for the Commissioner to permit new modes of communication, thereby allowing the framework to be expanded and adapted as systems are upgraded or new technologies emerge, without the need to extensively amend the legislation each time.

## Delivery methods

Sections 14F and 14G establish delivery rules that ensure that communications, in particular electronic communications, are delivered only to appropriate contact addresses. This is important to protect both IR and taxpayers from the risk of misdirected communications and to guard against an inadvertent breach of the tax secrecy provisions.

Section 14F sets out the rules that apply when person A is required to deliver information to person B. The applicable rules depend upon whether the Commissioner is person A, person B or neither.

Communications by the Commissioner When the Commissioner is person A, the Commissioner may communicate with person B (or a representative authorised to act on behalf of person B):

## if person B is not a corporate body, either by personal delivery to person B or by personal delivery to

person B's usual or last known place of residence

## if person B is a corporate body, by personal delivery to person B's office during working hours, or

## by delivery to person B's contact address as described in s 14G (see below).

Communications to the Commissioner When the Commissioner is person B, person A may communicate with the Commissioner:

## by electronic means

## by personal delivery during working hours to an office of the department that is available to accept

communication of the information, or

## by post to the street address of an office of the department or to the post office box number of the

department.

Communications between others

Where the Commissioner is neither person A nor person B, then person A may communicate with person B:

## if person B is not a corporate body, by personal delivery to person B

## if person B is a corporate body, by personal delivery to person B's office during working hours, or

## by delivery to person B's contact address as described in s 14G (see below).

Contact addresses For these purposes, a person's contact address may be one of the following:

## for delivery by electronic means to a person who is not a corporate body:

## an email or other electronic address they have provided

## their last known email or other electronic address

## an email or other electronic address of the person that is otherwise available, if there are reasonable

grounds to suppose that the person will receive the communication

## for delivery by electronic means to a person who is a corporate body:

## an email or other electronic address of the corporate body provided by a person acting for the

corporate body

## the last known email or other electronic address of a person acting for the corporate body

## an email or other electronic address of the corporate body that is otherwise available, if there

are reasonable grounds to suppose that a person acting for the corporate body will receive the communication

## for delivery by post:

## the street address of their usual or last known place of residence

## the street address of any of their usual or last known places of business, or

## any other address of the person, if they have notified the Commissioner that they accept delivery at

the address General rules A communication by post is treated as having been given at the time the communication would have been delivered in the ordinary course of the post.

An electronic communication is taken to be received:

## in the case of an addressee who has designated an information system for the purpose of receiving

electronic communications, at the time the electronic communication enters that information system, or

## in any other case, at the time the electronic communication comes to the attention of the addressee.

For the purposes of ss 14F and 14G, delivery by electronic means does not include a communication to a fax number.

## Accepting electronic signatures

Section 13B allows for documents to be "signed" with a digital or electronic signature. It applies to all information provided to the Commissioner including, for example, electronically submitted tax returns or application forms.

When a person provides an electronically signed document, unless there are reasonable grounds to suppose otherwise, the document will be treated as signed by that person and the person will be treated as complying with the Commissioner's guidelines and Pt 4 of the CCLA.

## Standard for use of electronic signature

Operational guideline OG 17/02, "Standard for the use of a valid electronic signature on documents provided to the Commissioner" (updated in August 2017 to take account of the enactment of the CCLA) confirms IR

will accept an electronic signature on all documents and information that currently require a conventional signature, where this option is specified in the relevant document or associated guidelines.

Customers may only use an electronic signature when submitting documents and information to IR where they are using IR's online services, or they provide an electronic signature (whether directly or via a tax agent or other intermediary) that complies with the requirements set out in the standard. The conditions for presumption of reliability in s 228 of the CCLA must be met.

The item states that third party software providers must implement administrative, physical and technical safeguards that are no less rigorous than accepted industry practices, including certain specified ISO standards, to protect confidential information. If the integrity of a transaction is questioned, the third party provider must be able to demonstrate the validity of the transaction on their system or intermediary systems.

The software must maintain log files and the log files must be considered and treated as "business records" for the purposes of the TAA and the Evidence Act 2006.
