# Notice of proposed adjustment (NOPA) issued by Commissioner ¶4-130

Tax disputes are formally initiated by a NOPA. Such notice requires the relevant party to inform the respondent of any proposed adjustments to a person's tax position and to state the reasons for those adjustments.

Usually a NOPA is issued by the Commissioner after completing the audit of a taxpayer's affairs. Generally, the Commissioner will not issue an assessment until after the disputes procedure has been concluded.

However, in a limited number of situations, the Commissioner may issue an assessment without first issuing a NOPA. Those situations are outlined at ¶4-145.

Sometimes IR may discover an item that needs to be adjusted but is unable to issue a NOPA. This would occur when the proposed adjustment is already subject to a challenge or when the statutory time bar has expired (¶4-165).

The Commissioner may issue one or more NOPAs in respect of a tax return or an assessment. If an audit spans several years and the time bar is pending, it is possible for the Commissioner to issue one notice that covers more than one return period, issue and tax type to ensure that the assessment can still be issued within the time bar period.

Notice of proposed adjustment (NOPA) issued by taxpayer

The taxpayer is able to initiate the dispute resolution procedure by issuing a NOPA to the Commissioner in certain circumstances. The taxpayer (or their agent) is able to issue a NOPA to the Commissioner:

## within 4 months of the date of an assessment made by the Commissioner

## within 4 months of the date on which the taxpayer's notice of assessment is received at an IR office

for an assessment made by the taxpayer (and provided that the Commissioner has not already issued a NOPA to the taxpayer in respect of that assessment)

## within 4 months of the date of a disputable decision if the Commissioner issues a notice of disputable

decision (not being a notice of assessment) that affects the taxpayer. #Note: There was arguably a loophole in the disputes procedures that allowed a taxpayer to circumvent the time limit for challenging an assessment. This involved the taxpayer making a voluntary disclosure for a particular item, which arguably allowed them to reopen the entire original assessment, circumventing the time limit. However, from 30 March 2021, an amendment closed this potential loophole by providing that if the assessment by the Commissioner is an amended assessment, the taxpayer's entitlement to dispute the assessment is limited to disputing a new or increased liability imposed.

## Returns to be furnished

If a taxpayer has not filed an income tax or GST return for an assessment period, but wishes to dispute an assessment made by the Commissioner for that period, a return must first be filed for that assessment period: C of IR v Taxation Review Authority (2004) 21 NZTC 18,707. The High Court held that s 89D(2) of the TAA makes a taxpayer's right to issue a NOPA dependent on the taxpayer first satisfying the taxpayer's statutory obligation to file a tax return. That requirement is an additional requirement of the disputes procedures in which a default assessment is involved. To be valid, a NOPA must be issued within 2 months of the date of the assessment. This time limit is not affected by the requirement for the taxpayer to file a tax return.

Also see Case W16 (2003) 21 NZTC 11,156 and C of IR v Taxation Review Authority (2004) 21 NZTC 18,634 at ¶4-225.

From 1 April 2019, when the Commissioner makes a default assessment for an individual because they are not satisfied the income information in their pre-populated account is correct, the individual may dispute the assessment only by making an adjustment to their final account. However, if the Commissioner makes a default assessment under s 106(1A) of the TAA because they consider the information provided in an individual's final account is not likely to be correct, the individual may only dispute the assessment under the disputes procedure.

Despite its transparency and the fact that there is no assessment of the company, a look-through company (LTC) may issue a NOPA in respect of a return the company has made if the Commissioner has not previously issued a NOPA in respect of the same return.

Form and content of notice of proposed adjustment (NOPA)

The NOPA must be in the prescribed form (form IR770) and must also contain sufficient detail of certain matters to identify the issues arising between the Commissioner and the disputant.

For a NOPA issued by the Commissioner, there must be sufficient detail to:

## identify the adjustment or adjustments proposed to be made to the assessment

## provide a concise statement of the key facts and the law to advise the disputant of the grounds for the

proposed adjustment or adjustments, and

## state how the law applies to the facts.

For a NOPA issued by a disputant, there must be sufficient detail to:

## identify the adjustment or adjustments proposed to be made to the assessment

## provide a statement of the facts and the law in sufficient detail to advise the Commissioner of the

grounds for the proposed adjustment or adjustments, and

## state how the law applies to the facts.

Copies of the documents that are significantly relevant to the issues arising in the dispute of which the disputant is aware at the time that the NOPA is issued are also required.
