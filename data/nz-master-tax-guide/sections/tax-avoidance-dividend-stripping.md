# Tax avoidance — Dividend stripping ¶33-175

Section GB 1 makes provision for a specific form of tax avoidance arrangement. Essentially, the subsection applies where a taxpayer disposes of shares in return for money or other consideration and that consideration would have been dividend income had the taxpayer's avoidance arrangement not been entered into. The subsection does not, of itself, void any arrangement.

The subsection does not apply to a transaction in which no consideration passes for the transfer of shares, even where the purpose is to avoid tax on dividends. However, a dividend transaction of that description might fall within the general anti-avoidance provisions where it forms part of a larger tax avoidance arrangement.

The elements which must be present before s GB 1 applies are:

## a sale or disposal of company shares for consideration under a tax avoidance arrangement, and

## some or all of the consideration derived from the transaction is in substitution for a dividend in an

income year.

The existence of a purpose of avoiding tax on dividends is not required before the section can apply.

If the purchasers of the shares subsequently declare a dividend from the same account in respect of which the Commissioner has deemed dividends relating to all or part of the consideration to have been derived (eg the profit and loss appropriation account), those dividends may nonetheless be income derived by the purchasers. Although the deemed dividends and the actual dividends are declared from the same source, the latter are still dividends within ss CD 4 and CD 5. Even if this were not so, s GB 1 has no counterpart to s GA 1(6), which provides that amounts deemed by the Commissioner to be income under an adjustment under s GA 1(2) are deemed not to be income of any other person. See Case P34 (1992) 14 NZTC 4,247.

Note that the decision in Case P34 discusses the concepts of "tax avoidance" and "tax mitigation". The Privy Council has since declared the distinction between these 2 concepts to be "unhelpful". See O'Neil v C of IR (2001) 20 NZTC 17,051 at 17,057.

Sections BG 1 and GA 1 have no application to the purchaser of shares under a dividend-stripping arrangement, always assuming the purchaser obtains no tax advantage from the arrangement. The specific anti-avoidance provisions in ss GB 35–GB 37 relating to the dividend imputation regime should also be borne in mind (¶17-120 and ¶17-115).

*Refs: Section GB 1, s GB 1, ss CD 4, s GA 1(6), s GA 1(2), ss GB 35*

## Revenue Alert

In Revenue Alert, RA 18/01, "Dividend stripping — some share sales where proceeds are at a high risk of being treated as a dividend for income tax purposes" the Commissioner's view is that where a person or persons sell shares in a company (the target) to another company (the acquirer) in which they have a significant ownership interest, s BG 1 or GB 1 can apply in a range of circumstances. For example, a sale can be subject to s BG 1 where the target has no retained earnings at the time of sale and where the purchase price is simply left owing to the vendors. A tax avoidance arrangement may also arise where a holding company structure is used to facilitate the exit of a shareholder or the merger of 2 companies. The Revenue Alert also states the Commissioner would have tax avoidance concerns where an arrangement inappropriately creates available subscribed capital for a company in situations where a shareholder in reality has not provided anything for the issue of shares by the company.

The Revenue Alert contains a number of examples that highlight the Commissioner's concerns and a series of questions and answers were also released with the Revenue Alert.

In February 2020, the Commissioner released a questions and answers sheet to accompany the Revenue Alert that provides answers to some questions frequently asked by taxpayers in this area, such as whether

the Commissioner will consider transactions are part of a tax avoidance arrangement if there are commercial reasons for the restructure, whether the Commissioner will be concerned with a restructure done more than 5 years ago, and whether a dividend can be spread (RA 18/01a).
