# Permitted disclosure for international purposes ¶1-636

Section 18I provides that the confidentiality rule for revenue officers (¶1-604) does not apply to sensitive revenue information disclosed for the purposes of an international obligation of the Commissioner prescribed by Sch 7 pt D.

*Refs: Section 18I*

## Reciprocal laws or arrangements — Sch 7 cl 48

The Commissioner may communicate information to any authorised officer of an overseas Government where a revenue law affecting the incidence of tax or duty is expressed to be conditional on the existence of a reciprocal law or concession in that country or where a reciprocal arrangement has been made with that Government affecting the incidence of tax or duty. Any such communication is limited to such information as is necessary to enable the overseas Government to give effect to the reciprocal law or concession or to the reciprocal arrangement.

## Arrangements for relief of double taxation — Sch 7 cl 49

The Commissioner is not prevented from disclosing required information under a double tax agreement or tax recovery agreement to a person authorised to receive such information under the law of the territory in relation to which the double tax agreement or tax recovery agreement has been made.

The Court of Appeal considered the disclosure of information by the Commissioner to the Australian Tax Office (ATO) in Avowal Administrative Attorneys Ltd v District Court at North Shore (2010) 24 NZTC 24,252.

The High Court had found ((2009) 24 NZTC 23,252) that it was permissible to use the Commissioner's powers under the former s 16 (now s 17) of the TAA to obtain information for the purpose of supplying that information to the ATO under art 26 of the Australia–New Zealand DTA. On appeal it was argued that the power to pass that information on was limited by former ss 81 and 88 (now Sch 7 of the TAA). The Court of Appeal held that despite the duty to maintain secrecy in relation to tax information, the exception under former s 88 applied in this case because an exchange of information process between the 2 countries had taken place based on the fact that both countries were investigating the same large-scale tax mitigation scheme which straddled the 2 jurisdictions.

The Court of Appeal briefly touched on whether former s 88 applies only where the Commissioner is required to provide the information under the DTA and not to information that is voluntarily provided, but found it unnecessary to deal with that argument in detail. See [60] of the judgment.
