# Salary of partner ¶23-132

Under general law, a partner who works for the partnership does so in the capacity of partner and not as an employee. Any remuneration paid by the partnership for that work is by way of drawings and not as salary or wages. A partnership, therefore, may not claim a deduction for tax purposes for "wages" paid to a working partner. See Case F123 (1984) 6 NZTC 60,157 and Case L28 (1989) 11 NZTC 1,172. However, a deduction, subject to the exclusion, is allowed for payments (treated as salary or wages) made by a partnership to any working partner for services performed under a written contract of service, including any bonus. A "working partner" is a partner who personally and actively performs any of the duties to be performed in carrying on the partnership business (s DC 4(1)). Note, where this section applies the partnership is treated as opaque — not transparent (s DB 4(4B)).

A "contract of service" is an agreement that satisfies all of the following criteria:

## the terms and conditions of the services to be performed by the partner are specified in the

agreement

## the amount payable to the partner for the performance of the services is specified in the agreement,

and

## the agreement is in writing and is entered into by all the partners in the partnership.

With respect to the deduction for any bonus paid, a deduction will still be allowed to the partnership for this payment whether or not it is authorised by the contract.

Note no deduction is available for a partnership that is engaged wholly or mainly in investing money or in holding, or dealing in, shares, securities, investments, or estates or interests in land (s DC 4(2)).

Since the partnership is paying salary, the payments are subject to the PAYE rules, ie the payments are included in the definition of "salary or wages". Similarly, the payments are "earnings as an employee" for accident compensation levy purposes. No FBT liability arises where fringe benefits are provided to salaried partners who are treated as employees for tax deduction purposes; rather, the cost of providing the benefit is non-deductible to the extent of the private use element.

*Refs: s DC 4(1), s DB 4(4B), s DC 4(2)*

## Accident compensation levies

Active partners are liable to pay Accident Compensation Corporation (ACC) levies on their total share of partnership income. Salaries paid to working partners are included in the definition of "salary or wages" and as such are PAYE income payments. PAYE income payments are included in the definition of "earnings as an employee", which means that the partnership must deduct the ACC levies with the PAYE on the payment of the salary or wages to the partner and account for the work account levy on the salary or wages using the classification that reflects the business activity of the partnership.

Sleeping partners (ie partners whose involvement is limited to the investment of capital, without taking any part in the management or day-to-day running of the business) are not liable for ACC levies on their share of the partnership income. This is because such income is not dependent on the person's personal exertions and so does not amount to "earnings as a self-employed person". See Tax Information Bulletin ¶133-106 Vol 13, No 3, March 2001 at 17. However, they remain liable to account for ACC levies on wages paid to partnership staff.

See Chapter 31 for a more comprehensive discussion of ACC levies and the Accident Compensation Act.

## Pensions paid to former partners

A deduction, subject to the exclusion below, is allowed for a reasonable amount of pension paid to a former partner or the surviving spouse, civil union or de facto partner of a former partner in a partnership, under a

deed providing for a pension for a fixed period, life or remarriage of the surviving spouse or partner (s DC 3(3)). Where this section applies the partnership is to be treated as opaque — not transparent (s DC 3(3B)).

Note no deduction is available for a partnership that is engaged wholly or mainly in investing money or in property, equities or other securities (s DC 3(2)).
