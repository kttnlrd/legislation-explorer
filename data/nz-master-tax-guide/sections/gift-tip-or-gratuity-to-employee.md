# Gift, tip or gratuity to employee ¶3-450

A gift or tip made to an employee that relates to the employment or service of the person rather than to any personal relationship between the employee and the giver is normally treated as assessable income.

Whether a payment is a gift is essentially a question of fact. While the payment is usually of a voluntary nature, it arises because services have been performed.

To be classified as income, a tip or gratuity must be received by the taxpayer in recognition of employment or service provided. The sum paid need not come directly from an employer.

If a gratuity is paid to an employee directly by a customer, or placed in a common pool for a group of employees, the amount received should be returned in the employee's annual tax return.

Where a gratuity is paid via a service charge added to the customer's bill and later paid by the employer to the employee, the employer should treat this amount as wages and deduct PAYE at the appropriate rate.

Payments or gifts made by way of personal esteem, even though of a recurring nature, are not income.

Expenditure on gifts made to employees can be deductible to the donor. See Case U10 (1999) 19 NZTC 9,090 at 9,099. Fringe benefit tax may apply to gifts made to employees (¶21-425).

## Interpretation statement on assessable gifts

Interpretation statement, IS 23/11, "Income tax: Income – when gifts are assessable income" and "Fact Sheet Income tax: Income – when gifts are assessable income" covers circumstances when the general rule that a gift is not subject to income tax does not apply, and when a gift is taxable in the recipient's hands. In respect of payments made by an employer to an employee, the courts consider a number of factors when deciding whether a voluntary payment such as a gift is actually employment income of the person receiving the gift. Some factors that support a conclusion that a gift is employment income are:

## the amount of the payment reflects the extent of any services provided by the employee

## the payment is made in the hope of future services or to encourage further efforts by employees

## the recipient's employment agreement contemplates the payment (although payment of employment

income terms may be under a separate agreement)

## the payment is recurring, or it is foreseeable that it will recur, rather than being a one-off payment

## the payment is related to duties that are expected of the employee (although if an employee willingly

does something beyond their usual duties and is paid for that, the range of duties is enlarged to encompass it)

## the payments are commonly paid as a matter of practice in the occupation, profession or industry

## the gift is one commonly provided to the holder of a position or office that has a degree of

continuance and independence from the current incumbent.

The following factors support a conclusion that the gift is not employment income:

## the payer expresses the payment in terms of a mere personal gift. However, the payer's motive is

usually not determinative. Simply stating a payment is a personal gift does not rule out the possibility that it is employment income.

## the recipient has already been fully remunerated for any services to which the payment might be

related

## the employment relationship has ended at the time of the payment and is unlikely to resume

(although a payment may be employment income where it is connected with a past, present or future employment relationship. See commentary above).

## the payer is not a person with whom the taxpayer has an employment relationship. However, a

payment may be employment income even where the payer is a person with whom the recipient has no employment relationship.

The following are some factors that are not necessarily relevant to the issue:

## The employment is unpaid.

## The recipient of the payment had to be an employee to be eligible for the payment.

## Voluntary activities

A gift is treated as income under s CO 1 when the gift and the volunteer's activity have a sufficient connection. That is when the gift is "a product of" or "in connection with" the person's voluntary activity.

Similar considerations as for "employment income" above apply to voluntary activities.
