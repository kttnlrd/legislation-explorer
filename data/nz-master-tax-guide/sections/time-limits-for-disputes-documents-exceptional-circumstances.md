# Time limits for disputes documents — exceptional circumstances ¶4-165

## Taxpayer outside time limit for NOPA

If an exceptional circumstance has prevented a taxpayer from responding to a NOPA within the 2-month time period, the Commissioner may accept a late NOR. The effect is that the taxpayer's rejection of the Commissioner's NOPA is treated for all purposes under Pt 4A of the TAA as if it had been made within the statutory response period. Exceptional circumstances may also be considered in relation to the late filing of a SOP or a NOPA in respect of a self-assessment.

For these purposes an exceptional circumstance is defined as an event or circumstance outside the taxpayer's control that provides reasonable justification for not responding within the response period. An act or omission of an agent is not an exceptional circumstance unless the act or omission was caused by an event or circumstance beyond the agent's control that could not have been anticipated and could not have been avoided if accepted standards of business organisation and professional conduct had been maintained.

An exceptional circumstance includes the situation in which a taxpayer or agent is late in issuing a NOPA, NOR or SOP, but the Commissioner considers that the lateness is minimal or results from one or more statutory holidays falling in the response period.

The Commissioner may also accept a late notice or SOP if they consider the taxpayer has a demonstrable intention to enter into or continue the disputes resolution process at the relevant time.

Any request for consideration of a late NOR, NOPA or SOP must be in writing and must include:

## a full explanation as to why the NOR, NOPA or SOP is late, together with any supporting evidence,

and

## the NOR, NOPA or SOP that the Commissioner would have received had it been issued on time.

Example: The taxpayers had a long-standing dispute with the Commissioner concerning the correct assessment of income tax to be deducted from superannuitants who derived pensions from the United Kingdom. The Commissioner disallowed the taxpayers' self- assessments and the taxpayers issued NOPAs. The Commissioner rejected the proposed adjustments, and the taxpayers gave notice that they did not accept the Commissioner's rejection. The taxpayers exercised their right to have the matter decided by the TRA by sending the Commissioner a notice of claim. The taxpayers had, however, neglected to file the notice of claim with the Registrar of the TRA before the time limit expired. The taxpayers applied for leave from the Authority to commence a challenge out of time. The Authority refused, as the taxpayers were unable to establish that their failure to file a notice of claim in time was an "exceptional circumstance". See Case V1 (2001) 20 NZTC 10,001.

Standard practice statement SPS 23/01, "Disputes process", sets out IR's interpretation of "exceptional circumstance" at [301]–[318] of the statement.

If the Commissioner does not exercise the discretion to accept a late document, the Commissioner must issue a refusal notice within one month of the taxpayer's notice being sent. If the Commissioner fails to issue a notice, then such a notice is deemed to have been sent at the end of one month, if it has not actually been sent. Receipt of this notice, or a deemed notice, allows the taxpayer the right to challenge the notice in the TRA within 2 months of the notice's issue. The statutory time bars are extended to include this period between the date of the refusal notice and the date the subsequent challenge is judged successful by a court, or when the Commissioner concedes.

When a taxpayer is late in issuing a NOPA or a SOP, the Commissioner's response period starts from the time when it is decided "exceptional circumstances" exist and the taxpayer's late document is to be allowed.

This ensures the Commissioner is not required to issue a substantive response to a dispute that may not have a procedural basis while a decision is still being reached.

## Commissioner outside time limit for NOPA

If an exceptional circumstance has prevented the Commissioner from rejecting a taxpayer's NOPA within the 2-month response period, the Commissioner can apply to the High Court for an order allowing the Commissioner to respond outside the time limit. An exceptional circumstance is defined for these purposes as an event or circumstance beyond the Commissioner's or other IR officer's control that is justification for not rejecting a NOPA within the response period.

## Preserving fair trial rights under New Zealand Bill of Rights is

exceptional circumstance Commissioner's statement, CS 20/04, "The disputes resolution process and fair trial rights", sets out the broad approach the Commissioner is taking to preserve a taxpayer's fair trial rights in criminal proceedings when there is a contemporaneous civil dispute.

A taxpayer will be advised when criminal proceedings have commenced, or are contemplated, before the taxpayer is required to commence or continue the disputes process. The Commissioner considers that preserving a taxpayer's rights in current or potential criminal proceedings is an "exceptional circumstance" under s 89K. A taxpayer, therefore, may elect not to file an outstanding disputes document until the question of prosecution has been determined. This may prevent the commencement of, or pause, the disputes process. The Commissioner will notify the taxpayer once the issue of prosecution has been determined, at which time the dispute can commence or resume. The taxpayer will then have to issue their outstanding dispute document by the later of 2 months from this notification or the original due date for the document.

An alternative is for the parties to agree to pause the dispute at the conference stage until the matter of prosecution is resolved. The taxpayer may also choose to voluntarily take part in the disputes resolution process, despite the prosecution or contemplated prosecution.

A recent case that analyses a taxpayer's right to a fair trial and the effect on those rights when the Commissioner decides not to allow s 89K to be invoked is Commissioner of Inland Revenue v Parore (2021) 30 NZTC ¶25-013. In this case it was found that the Commissioner was contemplating a criminal prosecution from the outset. Nevertheless, the Commissioner went ahead and issued default assessments. Mr Parore was not advised that he could postpone any response under the exceptional circumstances provision contained in s 89K of the TAA. Rather, the Commissioner informed Mr Parore that if he wished to dispute the assessments he had to file a NOPA along with his GST returns within 4 months of the issue date of the default assessments. This was held to breach his fair trial rights and the court stayed proceedings as a result. See also ¶4-128.

Debt recovery steps will not be taken by IR if the disputes process has been halted, unless the Commissioner considers there is a significant risk the debt will not be paid. This will only be relevant if an assessment has been issued by the Commissioner.

Halting the disputes process may impact on the time bar. In that case, options are available to ensure that the appropriate steps are taken before the time bar expires, eg the taxpayer can agree to waive the time bar, or the Commissioner can apply to the High Court for an extension.

## Commissioner outside time limit for challenge notice

The Commissioner may apply to the High Court for an order allowing the Commissioner to issue a challenge notice after the statutory 4-year period (¶4-192) if:

## the Commissioner considers that an exceptional circumstance applies or has prevented the

Commissioner from issuing the challenge notice within the 4 years, and

## the Commissioner applies within 4 years.

An "exceptional circumstance" for the purposes of the application is an event or circumstance beyond the control of the Commissioner or an IR officer that provides the Commissioner with a reasonable justification for not rejecting the adjustment within the response period.

The preservation of a taxpayer's right to a fair trial was accepted as an exceptional circumstance under s 89L permitting the Commissioner to issue a challenge notice outside the 4-year time period in Commissioner of Inland Revenue v Faghriyar (2020) 29 NZTC 24-067.

The High Court may either make an order as it sees fit or decline to make an order.

## Taxpayer outside time limit for filing challenge proceedings

If an exceptional circumstance has prevented a taxpayer from commencing challenge proceedings within 2 months of an assessment being issued, the High Court or TRA may allow a late challenge (¶4-225).
