# Early-payment discount of income tax ¶2-123

To reduce small business tax compliance costs, "small-business persons" (see below) are entitled to an early-payment discount of income tax. The purpose of the discount is to encourage the payment of income tax in the income year before the income year in which the small-business person is required to pay provisional tax. This avoids the requirement to pay both terminal tax and provisional tax in the second year of business, which, in some cases, may lead to serious cash flow problems and financial difficulties.

## Small-business person

A "small-business person" is defined as a taxpayer who satisfies all of the following criteria:

## the business is conducted on the person's own account — the taxpayer is acting alone or as a

partner in a partnership, or as the owner of a look-through company

## a company or a trust is not used to conduct the business, and

## the income derived is predominantly from the business and not from interest, dividends, royalties,

rents or beneficiary income.

Broadly, the early-payment discount will apply only to self-employed people and individuals who receive income from partnerships or look-through companies.

## When the early-payment discount applies

A small-business person is entitled to the early-payment discount if all of the following conditions are met:

## the small-business person has no obligations to pay provisional tax in the income year under s RC

3(3) because their residual income tax for the preceding year is $5,000 or less

## income tax payments are made for the income year on or before the small-business person's balance

date for the income year

## for the period from the small-business person's balance date for the income year to the taxpayer's

terminal tax date for the income year, a credit of tax is held in an account with the Commissioner of at least:

## the amount of tax paid for the income year before the person's balance date, or

## the amount of terminal tax payable for the income year before the application of the early-payment

discount

whichever is the lesser, and

## for earlier income years, the small-business person has never been required to pay provisional tax

and:

## has never received an early-payment discount, or

## has not derived assessable income from a business in the four income years beginning after the

latest income year for which the early-payment discount has been received, or

## has not derived assessable income from a business in the four income years beginning after the

latest income year for which the taxpayer was required to pay provisional tax.

In effect, the early-payment discount must be claimed before the small-business person is required to pay provisional tax. A small-business person who is required to pay provisional tax before receiving self- employed or partnership income is not entitled to the early-payment discount.

Application for discount

To claim the early-payment discount, a small-business person must file a tax return for the income year that the discount is claimed and also apply for the early-payment discount on or before the due date for filing the tax return for the income year that the discount is claimed.

## Amount of discount

The early-payment discount is calculated on:

## the amount of income tax paid for the income year on or before balance date, or

## 105% of the small-business taxpayer's residual income tax for the income year

whichever is the lesser.

The discount rate may be prescribed by Order in Council or, in the absence of such an Order, the rate is 2% greater than the Commissioner's paying rate for the use-of-money interest at the end of the preceding tax year. The rate has been set at 6.67% for the 2024–25 income year, and 6.30% for the 2025–26 income year.

The discount is given by way of credit to the small- business person's income tax account.

## Credit for discount treated as payment of income tax

A credit for an early-payment discount is treated as a payment of income tax. The payment is treated as being made on the day after the last day of the income year for which the discount is claimed.

Example: Maddie has been a full-time mother for the last five years. During that time, she has neither derived any income from a business nor has she been required to pay provisional tax. Maddie started up a new business on 1 July 2024. On the advice of a friend who is a chartered accountant, Maddie makes voluntary income tax payments for the 2024–25 income year amounting to $9,000. The payments are all made before 31 March 2025 (Maddie's balance date). Maddie's first year of business is better than she expected.

Her residual income tax for the 2024–25 income year is calculated to be $15,000. Maddie files her 2024–25 tax return on 30 June 2025 and also applies for the early-payment discount. Assuming no discount rate has been prescribed by Order in Council, the amount of Maddie's early-payment discount is $600 (ie 6.67% of $9,000). This amount is credited to Maddie's 2024–25 income tax account and is treated as an income tax payment made by Maddie on 1 April 2025.
