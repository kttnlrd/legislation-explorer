# Exclusion from FBT — employees of non-resident employers ¶21-594

Excluded from the definition of fringe benefit are benefits received by an employee who receives PAYE income payments that are not assessable in New Zealand in that FBT period. When a non-resident employee works in New Zealand for a non-resident employer, the employee's PAYE income payments may be exempt in New Zealand under the Act or under provisions of a double tax agreement. The exclusion from FBT is intended to address this situation so that any non-monetary benefits provided to such employees are not subject to FBT (Tax Information Bulletin ¶76-106 Vol 7, No 6, December 1995 at 8).

IR has set out its views on the application of the PAYE and FBT rules to non-resident employers in an operational statement, OS 21/04: "Non-resident employers' obligations to deduct PAYE, FBT and ESCT in cross-border employment situations". The operational statement applies from 1 December 2021.

An employer who is not New Zealand tax-resident will have an obligation to account for New Zealand PAYE on amounts paid to an employee if:

## the employer has a sufficient presence in New Zealand, and

## the services performed by the employee are properly attributable to the New Zealand presence.

The requirement to deduct PAYE will not apply (even if the above 2 conditions are met) if the employee is exempt from tax under s CW 19 (¶26-420) or if a relevant double tax agreement (DTA) provides the employee relief from taxation.

Relief from PAYE obligations will also apply if the amount is non-resident's foreign-sourced income of the employee. This is a payment that is not sourced in New Zealand and derived while the employee is non- resident for New Zealand tax purposes.

Similarly, a non-resident employer may also face FBT and ESCT obligations if the employer has a sufficient presence in New Zealand and the services performed by the employee are properly attributable to the employer's New Zealand presence.

## Note: A proposed amendment contained in the Taxation (Annual Rates for 2025–26, Compliance

Simplification, and Remedial Measures) Bill will exempt certain fringe benefits received by non-resident visitors who meet certain conditions from FBT. Broadly, a natural person will qualify as a "non-resident visitor" if they are in New Zealand for 275 days or less in an 18-month period and who is not performing work for a New Zealand-resident employer (or a New Zealand branch of an overseas employer). Certain other requirements must be met. The proposed amendment is intended to apply from 1 April 2026.

## Developments for cross-border employment situations

Certain fringe benefits received by a non-resident employee are not subject to FBT if the benefit does not relate to a period of work in New Zealand (s CX 26(2)). The rule rectifies the situation that can arise where an employee who has lost their New Zealand tax residency receives a trailing payment from their employer.

If that payment is subject to New Zealand tax, any fringe benefits provided are subject to FBT if provided in the same period as the trailing payment. However, a legislative amendment provides that with effect from 1 April 2024, FBT is payable only to the extent that the benefit relates to time spent in New Zealand.

## Safe-harbour for non-resident employers

From 1 April 2023, a safe-harbour provision applies if a non-resident employer has incorrectly determined that they do not have a sufficient presence in New Zealand (¶26-430). If the requirements of the safe- harbour are met, the employer will not face penalties and interest on any unpaid employment taxes. The safe-harbour requirements are that the non-resident employer has:

## 2 or fewer employees in New Zealand at any point in the relevant income year, or $500,000 or less in

gross New Zealand employment-related taxes for the income year, and

## the employer takes reasonable measures to manage their employment-related tax obligations within

60 days of a relevant breach.

In addition, from 1 April 2023, an employer has a 60-day grace period to meet or correct the PAYE, FBT or ESCT obligations for an employee where the conditions of a tax exemption (like those set out in s CW 19) have been breached (¶3-367).

## Flexibility for cross-border employees

If a cross-border employee receives a fringe benefit in relation to a period when they are providing employment services in New Zealand, the employer and employee may agree that the employee is liable for the employment-related taxes and document this in an agreement. If this is the case, the value of the fringe benefit is treated as a PAYE income payment and PAYE applies to the value of the benefit (s RD 62B). This concession is available from 1 April 2023. A "cross-border employee" is defined in s YA 1.

Example: Wotan Industries is an overseas incorporated company which provides software services to clients around the world. Freyja, an employee of Wotan, decides for personal reasons to relocate to New Zealand and agrees with her managers that she will work for Wotan from New Zealand.

At the time of the relocation, Freyja is the sole employee working in New Zealand. Her work has no necessary connection to New Zealand (that is, it does not need to be done from New Zealand, nor is it for New Zealand clients). Her work will not result in a permanent establishment for Wotan Industries in New Zealand.

Wotan determines that it does not have a sufficient presence in New Zealand. As such, Wotan decides it will not register as an employer in New Zealand for tax purposes. Instead, her manager agrees that Freyja will be responsible for the payment of taxes on her employment income and record this agreement as a condition of the transfer.

Freyja is paid monthly. In addition to a salary, Wotan pays a monthly employer superannuation contribution to its pension scheme (which is a foreign superannuation scheme for New Zealand tax purposes). Wotan also pays an annual medical insurance premium to the provider of Freyja's choice. Freyja chooses a New Zealand medical insurer.

Wotan Industries' responsibility is to provide the amounts paid to the foreign superannuation scheme and medical insurer to Freyja.

Freyja's responsibility is to include the amounts paid in her IR 56 returns and pay the taxes due. The annual payment of medical insurance will be included as PAYE income, as Freyja is not required to file and pay FBT. The monthly superannuation contributions will be captured as subject to ESCT in her IR 56 return. (Example reproduced from Tax Information Bulletin ¶356-102 Vol 35 No 6, July 2023, p 45).
