# Grouping for GST ¶32-086

The GST group registration rules allow a group of related entities to be treated as a single entity for GST purposes. Once the entities are grouped, a representative member is responsible for most compliance matters and is deemed to carry on all the taxable activities of the group members. Supplies made or received by a group member are treated as made or received by the representative member. Taxable supplies between group members may be ignored. In this way, the GST creates a statutory fiction — something is treated as having been done by one person (the representative member), when in fact it has been done by another (the group member).

## Who can group for GST?

Interpretation statement IS 24/03, "GST – Who can group register?" covers companies with common ownership, grouping where one taxpayer is not a company and grouping in relation to non-residents. The statement is detailed and contains varied examples. Below is a summary of the key points:

## Groups of companies

Two or more companies can group register if they are a "group of companies" or part of a "group of companies" under s IC 3 of the IT07. This requires that a "group of persons" holds 66% common ownership interests in the companies. Points to note:

## For GST purposes, look-through companies, multi-rate portfolio investment entities (PIEs) and listed

PIEs can be members of a group of companies under s 55(1) if they meet the 66% common ownership interests test.

## Companies that are not GST-registered can be members of GST groups of companies provided the

group meets a "75% taxable supplies" test.

## Non-resident companies can be members of GST groups of companies (subject to the restrictions for

non-residents registered under s 54B).

Note that if company shares are held on care trust, the shares are treated as if they are held by the beneficiary of the bare trust for the purposes of s IC 3.

## Mixed groups

The requirements for grouping under s 55(8) are as follows:

## there is a group of 2 or more "registered persons"

## at least one of the persons is not a company (or is a company but is a limited partnership), and

## the Commissioner is satisfied that one person in the GST group controls each of the other members

of the group, or one person outside the GST group controls all of members of the group, or a partnership controls all the members of the group. "Control" means legal control and will differ depending on the entity involved. For example, in a company, legal control will be the shareholder holding a majority of shares; for a trust, this may be the trustees or the person having the power of appointment. See below for further discussion on trusts and grouping for GST.

## Intra-group supplies

Section 55(1AC) to (1AI) provides taxable supplies made in New Zealand between members of the GST group are disregarded in the preparation of the group GST return for each taxable period. An exception to this rule is for a supply of services that is treated by s 8(4B) as being made in New Zealand. Section 55(1AF) has been amended and effective from 30 March 2025, a non-taxable supply by a non-registered GST group

member is treated as a supply by the representative member as a registered person and is therefore a taxable supply.

## Grouping non-residents registered under s 54B

Non-residents may be required to register for GST under the general registration provision (s 51), or can register voluntarily. Interpretation statement 24/03, " GST — Who can group register?" states the following:

## non-residents must be registered under s 54B

## non-residents registered under s 54B cannot group register with New Zealand residents

## non-residents registered under s 54B can only group register with other non-residents.

In practice, the Commissioner will only allow a s 54B registered person to be the representative member of a GST group if all members of the group are s 54B registered persons.

## The "75% taxable supplies test"

This test requires the companies in the group, over a 12-month period (including the current time when determining if the test is met) make at least 75% taxable supplies (as a percentage by value of their taxable and other supplies) to persons outside the eligibility group (see Example 1 in IS 24/03). "Taxable supplies" includes supplies that would be treated as "taxable supplies" if a registered person had made them.

## Trusts and grouping for GST

IS 24/03 replaces QB: GST Group Registration of Trusts. Legal control of a trust lies with the trustee(s) in their capacity as trustee of that specific trust. Due to the different capacity in which trusteeship is held, the same trustee(s) cannot usually group with each other. Put another way, the trustee of one trust is treated as a different "person" from the same trustee acting in their capacity as trustee of another trust. The general rule about who controls the trust may differ is there is another person who has the power to appoint and remove trustees (an Appointer). The Commissioner considers that the person holding the power to and remove trustees controls the trust.

Further detailed discussion can be found at [70] of IS 24/03 onwards. The statement covers the standard position in relation to trusts, the exception to the standard position and the test applied to special purpose corporate trustees of post-settlement governance entities.

## Control test for mixed groups

As GST grouping hinges on the control test, determination of who controls a mixed group is imperative.

A partnership Under s 51 of the Partnership Law Act 2019, a majority of the partners generally exercise legal control of a partnership.

Where a majority of the partners exercise legal control, there will be no "one person" that controls the partnership.

However, where all partners have consented to one partner making decisions about ordinary matters connected with the partnership business, that partner will have legal control of the partnership.

A limited partnership The general rule is that a partner "controls" the limited partnership if they can make resolutions of the limited partnership without the support of the other partners. Under a standard limited partnership agreement, a partner will be able to do so if they have contributed at least 75% of the partnership capital.

If there are multiple limited partners (or two 50% limited partners), there may be no "one person" that controls the limited partnership.

In a limited number of cases, legal control may be determined by referring to the partners' respective partnership interests.

A joint venture The general approach to determining legal control of a joint venture is by referring to the respective interests of the joint venture partners in the joint venture.

In most cases it will be sufficient to refer to the voting powers of the joint venturers contained in the joint venture agreement.

If the joint venture agreement requires decisions to be made unanimously, this approach will establish that no "one person" controls the joint venture.

In some cases, where voting powers do not accurately reflect legal control of the joint venture, it may be necessary to adopt an alternative approach of referring to the profit-sharing arrangements and the joint venturers' respective capital contributions.

Consequences of grouping

Interpretation statement IS 24/02, "GST Grouping for Companies" explains the consequences of GST grouping. The statement has examples of how the GST grouping rules apply to a range of situations. The consequences of GST grouping for companies are summarised as follows:

## the representative member is treated as carrying on all group members' activities, as a registered

person

## taxable supplies made by group members to persons outside the GST group are treated as taxable

supplies made by the representative member, as a registered person

## taxable supplies made to group members from persons outside the GST group are treated as taxable

supplies made to the representative member

## taxable supplies between group members are mainly disregarded

## non-taxable supplies made by or to group members are treated as made by or to the representative

member

## intra-group non-taxable supplies are not disregarded

## the representative member claims all input tax deductions

## the representative member makes all input tax adjustments where there is a change in the use of

goods or services that were acquired by group members.

Under the GST grouping rules, most supplies made by group members to persons outside the GST group are treated as made by the representative member, as a taxable person. The statement notes this can have significant implications for the treatment of a supply. For example:

## if a non-registered company is part of a GST group and makes supplies to persons outside the GST

group, those supplies will be treated as being made by a registered person and therefore GST is charged, and

## if a non-resident company is part of a GST group and the representative member is a New Zealand

resident, supplies made by that non-resident company are treated as being made from New Zealand.
