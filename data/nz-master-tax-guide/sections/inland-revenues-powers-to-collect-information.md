# Inland Revenue's powers to collect information ¶1-590

#Note: The Taxation (Annual Rates for 2025–26, Compliance Simplification, and Remedial Measures) Bill is proposing an amendment that would repeal section 17GB and the aim of the proposed amendment is to increase taxpayer privacy.

The powers of the Commissioner and, by delegation, of other officers of IR are quite extensive and, in some respects, are more comprehensive than police powers. Most of those powers relate to the collection of information to enable the making of assessments of income tax and the other taxes and duties which IR is responsible for collecting. Many of them are implicit in provisions of the IT07. However, the TAA gives specific additional powers. The obligation to provide information to the Commissioner is imposed on all persons, not just a taxpayer under investigation. For example, in Next Generation Investments Ltd (in liq) v C of IR (2006) 22 NZTC 19,775, the High Court held that liquidators, when faced with a request for information under s 17 of the TAA, are obliged to permit the Commissioner to inspect the books and accounts of a company without a court order (as required under s 256 of the CA93) even where the Commissioner is a creditor of the company.

The Commissioner's powers concerning the production of taxpayer-related information are contained in Pt 3 subpart 3A of the TAA. Broadly, the statutory framework comprises:

## purposes and principles of the subpart: ss 16–16C

## collection and use of information by Commissioner: ss 17–17M

## confidentiality of sensitive revenue information: ss 18 and 18B (¶1-604)

## permitted disclosures of sensitive revenue information: ss 18C–18J (¶1-606–¶1-640).

*Refs: section 17G, s 17, s 256, ss 16, ss 17, ss 18, ss 18C*

## Purposes and principles of subpart 3A

The stated purposes of the provisions in subpart 3A are:

## to provide the Commissioner with the necessary powers to enable—

## the collection of all taxes or duties imposed by the IR Acts

## the carrying into effect of any of the IR Acts

## the carrying out of functions lawfully conferred on the Commissioner

## to enable the collection by the Commissioner of revenue information, including the power to—

## gain access to property or documents

## remove documents to make copies

## remove and retain documents for review

## to require a person or entity to produce documents or to provide or allow access to information to the

Commissioner

## to set out the Commissioner's powers to copy, remove or retain documents

## to provide a regulation-making power for the regular collection of bulk data

## to describe how revenue information may be used

## to protect the confidentiality of sensitive revenue information

## to facilitate efficient and effective government administration and law enforcement by allowing

permitted disclosures of sensitive revenue information for certain specific or appropriate purposes: s 16. "Revenue information" is defined as information that is acquired, obtained, accessed, received by, disclosed to, or held by the Commissioner under or for the purposes of a revenue law and the collection supporting the purposes of the subpart (see above), or under an information-sharing agreement: s 16C(2).

"Revenue law" means the IR Acts (listed in Sch 1 of the TAA), the Accident Compensation Act 2001 and earlier such Acts, the New Zealand Superannuation Act 1974 and any Act imposing taxes or duties payable to the Crown: s 16C(1).

A document is widely defined as meaning:

## a thing that is used to hold, in or on the thing and in any form, items of information or

## an item of information held in or on a thing, or

## a device associated with a thing referred to in the first bullet point.

The collection of revenue information may be made for one or more of the following purposes:

## to protect the integrity of the tax system

## to carry into effect the revenue laws

## to carry out or support a function lawfully conferred on the Commissioner

## to encourage compliance with the revenue laws

## for any other function lawfully conferred on the Commissioner (s 16B).

## Commissioner's access to property and documents

When collecting information, the Commissioner (and any authorised officer of IR) may access property or documents for the purpose of inspecting a document, property, process, or matter the Commissioner considers:

## is necessary or relevant for one of the purposes listed above, or

## is likely to provide information that would otherwise be required for the purposes of the IR Acts and

any function lawfully conferred on the Commissioner (s 17).

The power of access is extremely wide. It applies despite anything in any other Act, and is subject only to restrictions in the case of a private dwelling (see below).

For these purposes "property or documents" includes documents, whether in the custody or under the control of a public officer, a body corporate or any other person, and all lands, buildings, places or other premises.

The occupier must provide all reasonable facilities and assistance for the effective exercise of the powers which the Commissioner may use under s 17(1) (access to property and documents) and s 17C (inspecting, copying and removing documents).

The occupier must also answer all proper questions relating to the effective exercise of the powers under s 17, as required by the Commissioner.

The Commissioner may be accompanied by another person if it is considered that the presence of the other person is necessary for the effective exercise of the powers under s 17.

## Private dwelling

The Commissioner must not enter a private dwelling to access property or documents except with the occupier's consent or under a warrant issued under s 17D. A private dwelling is a building or part of a building occupied as residential accommodation and includes:

## a garage, shed and other building used in connection with the private dwelling, and

## any business premises that are a private dwelling or are within a private dwelling.

If an occupier has not given consent, a warrant to search the premises must be obtained from an issuing officer, who must be satisfied that physical access to the premises is required for the exercise of a function lawfully conferred on the Commissioner. An issuing officer is defined as a judge or a person, such as a Justice of the Peace, Community Magistrate, Registrar, or Deputy Registrar, who is for the time being authorised to act as an issuing officer under s 108 of the Search and Surveillance Act 2012 (SSA).

The criteria to be considered before the issuing of a search warrant were discussed in Tauber v C of IR (2011) 25 NZTC ¶20-071 (HC), (2012) 25 NZTC ¶20-143 (CA).

The Court of Appeal also held, in Avowal Administrative Attorneys Ltd v District Court at North Shore (2010) 24 NZTC 24,252, that in exercising the access power there is an obligation on the Commissioner to act reasonably. The reasonableness depends on the circumstances of the case and is to be assessed having regard to the time, place and circumstances in which the search occurs. The reasonableness obligation is founded on s 21 of the New Zealand Bill of Rights Act 1990.

The form of warrant is prescribed by subpart 3 of Pt 4 of the SSA, and s 103 of the SSA sets out what particulars the warrant must contain.

Every person exercising the power of entry conferred by the warrant is required to produce the warrant and evidence of identity on first entering the private dwelling, and after that whenever reasonably required to do so: s 17D(4) of the TAA.

R v Hewitt (2005) 22 NZTC 19,309 (DC) confirmed that IR officers could copy documents obtained at a private dwelling.

A prison cell can be the living premises and the usual abode of a prison inmate and, thus, come within the definition of "private dwelling". See Re C of IR (application for a search warrant) (2005) 22 NZTC 19,123.

## Information demands for production of documents

A person who receives an information demand from the Commissioner under s 17B must provide all information the Commissioner considers is necessary or relevant for any purpose relating to:

## the administration or enforcement of an IR Act

## the administration or enforcement of any matter arising from or connected with a function lawfully

conferred on the Commissioner.

A requirement to provide information includes a requirement to produce a document.

A person includes an officer employed in, or in connection with, a government department or public authority, and any other public officer.

The notice from the Commissioner may require information to be verified by a statutory declaration, provided to a particular IR office or provided in a manner acceptable to the Commissioner.

There are some exceptions to the requirement to comply with one of the Commissioner's information demands. These encompass documents covered by legal professional privilege (¶1-595) and tax advice documents (¶1-600).

## Information treated as being in a person's knowledge, possession or control

Information or a document in the knowledge, possession or control of a non-resident is treated as being in the knowledge, possession or control of a New Zealand resident if the New Zealand resident controls the non-resident directly or indirectly (s 17E(1)). For this purpose, the following rules apply:

## a New Zealand resident is treated as holding anything held by an associated person resident in New

Zealand or an associated controlled foreign company, and

## a law of a foreign country relating to the secrecy of information is ignored.

## Information from large multinational groups

Similar provisions apply to a member of a large multinational group of companies. Information or a document relevant to the taxation of the group is treated as being in the knowledge, possession or control of a member of the group if that information or document is in the knowledge, possession or control of the member or any other group member (s 17E(2)). Again, the secrecy laws of foreign countries are to be disregarded.

In practice, it is anticipated that the Commissioner will request the information from the group member who is resident or potentially subject to tax in New Zealand. This group member will then source the required

information from non-resident members of their group. The information will be passed on to the relevant New Zealand taxpayer who will then supply this information to the Commissioner.

In addition, s 17G is designed to incentivise large multinational groups to comply with a request for information under s 17B by setting out some specific consequences that can be applied if the multinational fails to provide an adequate response within 3 months. These consequences enable the Commissioner to make a tax assessment based on the (limited) information it has available and to prevent the requested information from being admitted as evidence in a dispute or court proceeding.

A civil penalty also applies to members of a large multinational group if they fail to comply with a request for information (¶14-055).

These rules only apply to members of large multinational groups. A large multinational group is defined in s 3 to include multinational groups that exceed the revenue threshold of €750 million of consolidated global turnover (based on the threshold in para 5.53 of the OECD transfer pricing guidelines).

## Information relating to offshore payments

The Commissioner also has the power to issue an information demand requiring a person to produce information concerning offshore payments for which a deduction may be allowed (s 17F and ¶10-730).

## Information for tax policy development

From 7 December 2020, s 17GB clarifies that the Commissioner's information-gathering powers extend to requiring information solely for the purpose of tax policy development. The section provides that the Commissioner can require a person to provide any information the Commissioner considers relevant for a purpose relating to the development of policy for the improvement or reform of the tax system, which includes the social policy products administered. The Commissioner is not permitted to use any information obtained under this section as evidence in proceedings against the person providing the information.

Draft operational statement, ED0237 "Section 17GB notices – the Commissioner's power to obtain information for tax policy purposes", outlines the proposed procedures IR will follow when issuing notices under s 17GB to obtain information to support the development of tax policy. Requests for information under s 17GB includes an obligation for the taxpayer to provide documents in any form of storage, including paper documents and electronic documents. A New Zealand resident will be required to provide information of a non-resident that they directly or indirectly control under these powers, and information of a large multinational group can be required from a member of that group. Due to the wide ambit of this power, only senior officials within IR will have authority to issue these information requests.

## Limits on the use of information collected under s 17GB

The draft operational statement, ED0237 states that the Commissioner cannot use information provided by a person in response to a notice issued under s 17GB as evidence in proceedings against them. This includes criminal prosecutions and proceedings commenced under Part 8A (eg civil tax challenges). Information obtained under s 17GB will be held in a manner so that it cannot be used as evidence in proceedings. This limitation does not extend to any information subsequently obtained by the Commissioner under another section of the TAA.

## Copying and removal of documents

Once a revenue officer has obtained access to a document, they may take an extract from it, copy it or remove it for copying, all without paying any charge. The officer may also remove and retain a document for a full and complete inspection (s 17C). These powers apply to documents accessed under s 17, or produced under one or more of ss 17B,17GB, 17H(6) and 17I.

Documents removed for copying must be copied and returned as soon as practicable. Documents removed and retained for full inspection can be retained for as long as is necessary to undertake the inspection if the occupier consents or the documents have been obtained by warrant under s 17D.

The owner of a document removed by the Commissioner has the right to inspect it and copy it at the premises to which it has been removed. There is an on-going right to copy and inspect at all reasonable times thereafter. However, it appears that copying of a document by its owner before a revenue officer removes it from the person's premises is not specifically excluded under s 17C(5).

A copy of a document certified by (or on behalf of) the Commissioner is admissible as evidence in court as if it were the original.

## Court order for production of information or return

The Commissioner may ascertain that a person required to file a tax return has not done so or has not fully complied with a notice under s 17B or s 17GB. In any of these circumstances, the Commissioner may apply to the District Court for an order that the information or return is to be provided. Notice of the application must be given to the person against whom the order is sought and also to any other person as the court directs (s 17H).

A prosecution arising from the person's non-compliance may also be brought.

The Commissioner and all parties notified of the application have a right of audience before the court.

The court may order the information be provided to the court and review the information to determine whether:

## it should be provided to the Commissioner

## it is subject to legal professional privilege

## it is contained in a tax advice document and, if so, whether it must be disclosed under s 20E, 20F

or 20G (which deal largely with disclosure of attachments to tax advice documents and tax contextual information).

The District Court may order a person to produce all or a part of the information at issue to the Commissioner, if and to the extent the court is satisfied:

## no legal professional privilege applies, and

## the information is likely to be relevant for a purpose relating to the administration or enforcement

of a revenue law, or a matter arising from (or connected with) a function lawfully conferred on the Commissioner.

A person who is ordered to produce information to the Commissioner that is not kept in a document must make necessary arrangements to ensure the information can be viewed and copied.

Section 17H is paramount and applies despite any enactment or rule of law that may oblige a person to keep information secret, or not to disclose information, or not to perform an obligation. Compliance with a court order under s 17H is not a breach of the enactment, rule or obligation.

A person is not excused from having to comply with s 17H on the ground that:

## providing the information could or might prove a breach of a tax obligation or subject the person to a

fine, penalty, or conviction, or

## the person could claim another privilege in relation to the provision of the information in proceedings

in a court.

A person who fails to comply with the terms of a court order under s 17H commits an offence (s 143G and ¶14-270).

Commissioner's inquiry

Under s 17I, the Commissioner is authorised to notify a person that they must:

## attend and provide information, or

## produce documents in their possession or control which either contain the information or are likely to

contain it.

These powers are exercisable for the purpose of obtaining information:

## in relation to a person's tax liability, or

## that is required for the purposes of the administration or enforcement of an IR Act, or in carrying out

any other lawful function of the Commissioner.

The Commissioner may require any person to attend and give evidence on oath, orally or otherwise. An authorised IR officer may administer an oath.

It is a criminal offence to refuse or wilfully neglect to appear, to take an oath as a witness, to answer any question touching upon the subject matter of the inquiry or to produce documents (s 143F and ¶14-275).

The person summoned or examined by the Commissioner cannot be excused from answering questions on the ground that the answers may be incriminating. However, any answer given is inadmissible in criminal proceedings except when those proceedings are for perjury. A person who is summoned is entitled to receive a reasonable amount on account of travelling expenses and loss of time (s 17K).

## District Court inquiry

The Commissioner may apply to a District Court judge requesting that an inquiry be held for the purpose of obtaining information relevant to a person's tax liabilities or for the purposes of the administration or enforcement of the IR Acts or for carrying out any other lawful function of the Commissioner (s 17J).

At the inquiry the judge has the same jurisdiction and authority in relation to the summoning and examination of a person that the judge would have in respect of a witness in a civil action in the judge's ordinary jurisdiction. The person who is summoned and examined has, subject to the IR Acts, all such rights and liabilities as they would have if they were a witness in a civil action.

Sections 17K and 143F, discussed above in relation to the Commissioner's inquiry, also apply to a District Court inquiry.

## Regulations providing for regular collection of bulk data

Section 17L provides explicitly for regulations to be made allowing the Commissioner to collect, on a continuing and regular basis, bulk information in the form of datasets if it is considered necessary or relevant for a purpose relating to the administration or enforcement of a matter arising from or connected with a function lawfully conferred on the Commissioner.

Certain safeguards are included to protect the privacy of taxpayers, such as requiring the Minister of Revenue be satisfied that the regulations are made for relevant purposes, the information collected is necessary for revenue purposes and that the regulations will not unreasonably impinge on the privacy of individuals.

On 14 November 2022, an Order in Council enacted the Tax Administration (Regular Collection of Bulk Data) Regulations 2022 authorising the collection of merchant sales information from payment service providers under s 17L. The purpose of collecting the bulk data is it to enable IR to ensure that business taxpayers comply with their tax obligations, by identifying those who may not be paying the correct amount of tax (that is to strengthen the integrity of the tax system), enable IR to offer improved services and support through more focused education and marketing initiatives, and focus the compliance resources.

Commissioner's use of information

Section 17M sets out how IR can use information collected from or in relation to taxpayers. Notwithstanding any other Act, nothing prevents the Commissioner from:

## using or re-using information obtained under the TAA for the purposes of carrying into effect any IR

Act, or carrying out or supporting a function lawfully conferred on the Commissioner, or

## using or re-using information obtained under an IR Act for the purpose of carrying into effect the

provisions of the TAA or carrying out or supporting a function lawfully conferred on the Commissioner.

Use of information by the Commissioner includes use in a permitted disclosure (¶1-604).

## Commissioner's practice

Standard practice statement SPS 12/01, "Recording Inland Revenue interviews" outlines IR practice when electronically recording an interview with a taxpayer and applies from 19 April 2012. Operational statement OS 25/05 "Section 17B notices" sets out the procedures the Commissioner follows when issuing notices under s 17B and applies from 26 June 2025. OS 25/05 replaces OS 13/02.

## SPS 12/01

SPS 12/01 sets out the Commissioner's practice when electronically recording an interview with a taxpayer.

The statement notes that when an interviewee is attending an interview voluntarily, the interview will be recorded only with the consent and co-operation of the interviewee. However, all interviews carried out under (now) s 17I of the TAA must be recorded. The interviewee's consent is not required for these interviews.

Attendance at an inquiry by the Commissioner under s 17I is compulsory.

## OS 25/05

In addition to other things, OS 25/05 also comments on:

## requests for significant amounts of documentation

## the effect of legal professional privilege

## the right to claim non-disclosure for tax advice documents (¶1-600)

## requests relating to advice and other work papers prepared by accountants

## requests to persons other than the taxpayer

## requests for certain information from tax agents, and

## non-compliance with a s 17B notice.

Non-compliance with the information request notice will result in IR invoking the statutory remedies to compel taxpayer cooperation. For example, the Commissioner may seek a court order under s 17H(1) that the information be provided.

Generally, an offence would be committed should there be any failure to comply with the request. A defence is recognised by s 143A(2) when the recipient of the notice is able to prove that the information is not within the knowledge, possession or control of the person. Another exception is legal professional privilege (¶1-595).

Note that the Court of Appeal has held that the terms of an operational statement issued by the Commissioner for the guidance of taxpayers does not amount to a limitation on the scope of the Commissioner's particularly broad statutory power to issue (now) s 17B notices. In Chatfield & Co Ltd v C of IR (2017) 28 NZTC ¶23-015, the Court of Appeal confirmed that OS 13/02 is no more than a comprehensive and correct explanation of the nature and extent of the Commissioner's statutory power to demand information and the circumstances in which the Commissioner proposes to exercise that power. The only limitation on the Commissioner's power is found in s 17B itself.

The information-gathering power is very wide, but it does not mean that the Commissioner's powers cannot be impugned. Any notice given in exercise of the power must in its terms conform to the statutory limitations if it is to be valid. See FC of T v The Australia and New Zealand Banking Group Ltd 79 ATC 4039. Section 17B must be utilised in conformity with the purposes set out; it cannot be used for an improper purpose.

See Schwass and Robertson v Mackay (1983) 6 NZTC 61,641. In exercising the power under s 17B, it is likely that the courts will say that the Commissioner or the Commissioner's officers must act fairly (the courts have said this at least in relation to the pre-assessment phase). However, the Commissioner is not required to identify particular taxpayers when requesting information under s 17B. The Commissioner is entitled to request information concerning a class of unidentified potential taxpayers.

## Abuse of powers

The Commissioner has a statutory responsibility to identify persons who are not returning taxable income and paying tax. The legislation does not require that the information obtained must be relevant to the tax liability of some specific person or persons whose tax liability is under investigation. In New Zealand Stock Exchange v C of IR; The National Bank of New Zealand Ltd v C of IR (1991) 13 NZTC 8,147, the Privy Council commented that if the particulars sought went beyond what was required for the purposes of enabling the Commissioner to carry out the Commissioner's statutory functions, to the extent that they were unduly oppressive or burdensome, the court would be entitled to intervene. But a court would intervene only if it was satisfied the Commissioner had exceeded or abused their powers. In Green v Housden (1993) 15 NZTC 10,053, the Court of Appeal held that, in the circumstances, a request by IR under s 17 of the Inland Revenue Department Act 1974 (now s 17B of the TAA) to the taxpayer's professional advisers for the production of books and documents was unreasonable and an abuse of the Commissioner's powers.

However, in Singh v C of IR (1996) 17 NZTC 12,471, the High Court noted that s 17 of the Inland Revenue Department Act implicitly abrogates the right to refuse to answer questions tending to incriminate. The taxpayer's failure to provide information, books and documents as requested was a breach of that section.

## Limitation of powers under ss 16 and 17

Although advance notice of an inspection is not required, there are some limitations on the rights of access and inspection. An officer must be authorised by the Commissioner. If the officer does not carry a warrant giving them the required authority to inspect, it appears that access can validly be refused. Access to private dwellings is expressly limited to where the officer has the consent of the occupier or a warrant has been issued under s 17D of the TAA. The provisions do not provide for entry by force. A reasonable request that legal advice be obtained before complying with the Commissioner's request must be allowed. The taxpayer has only to produce those documents which the Commissioner, acting reasonably and having properly considered the question, deems necessary or relevant to the inquiry. Also, the subject of a search must be allowed a reasonable and legitimate opportunity to approach the court for a relief from compliance for the request of access. There is no general power for the Commissioner to raid a taxpayer's premises and remove anything the Commissioner chooses. Although the Commissioner has a right to remove documents, this right extends only to documents that have been produced by the taxpayer. The Commissioner's more likely practice is to use the courts to impose sanctions (fines) rather than the seizure of material by force.

## Additional provisions under the TAA

In addition to the powers discussed above, there are further sections in the TAA that have the effect of enabling the Commissioner to obtain information. For example, under s 44 the Commissioner may require a special return of income from such persons as non-resident traders or persons the Commissioner believes are about to depart from New Zealand or have ceased to carry on a business. Under s 58, the Commissioner may require bodies such as charitable organisations to produce records showing the source and application of their funds.
