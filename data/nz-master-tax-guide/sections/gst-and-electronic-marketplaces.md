# GST and electronic marketplaces ¶32-640

The GST electronic marketplace rules came into effect on 1 April 2024 and apply to "listed services". Listed services include taxable supplies of accommodation (eg Air BnB), ride-sharing (eg Uber), beverage and food delivery services (eg Uber Eats), and other services that are closely connected with these services. The rules require electronic marketplace operators to collect and return GST on these services provided through electronic marketplaces. These activities are also referred to as the "platform economy".

From 1 April 2024, GST-registered online marketplaces providing listed services must account for GST on their sales. The registered marketplace must collect and return 15% GST on the services. GST also applies to closely connected services, like holiday rental cleaning fees, if they are facilitated through the online marketplace and charged for over and above the rental fee.

If the supplier of the service is also GST-registered, the transaction between the supplier and the electronic marketplace will be zero-rated (s 11A(1)(jc)).

*Refs: s 11A(1)(jc)*

## Example: GST-registered seller/service provider

Annemieke is GST-registered. She rents out a property on AirBnB for $100 per night. The property is located in Whangarei and is available for short-term holiday accommodation. AirBnB adds 15% GST to the nightly rate, charging guests $115. AirBnB includes $115 as sales and income in their GST return. In Annemieke's GST return, she includes $100 as sales and income (Box 5) and $100 as a zero-rated supply (Box 6). The zero-rated supply is subtracted from sales and income resulting in $0 total GST collected (Box 7).

Annemieke claims back GST on the purchases and expenses incurred to rent out her property as short-term accommodation.

## Listed services connected to New Zealand

Listed services are deemed to be supplied in New Zealand if they are performed, provided, or received in New Zealand. The following factors are not relevant to the determination:

## whether the electronic marketplace is a New Zealand resident or not

## whether the underlying supplier is New Zealand resident or not

## whether the recipient of the services is New Zealand resident or not.

QWBA, QB 25/19, "GST listed services rules: When is a supply of listed services made through an electronic marketplace?" considers one of the key requirements for when the GST listed services rules apply and concludes that: "An underlying supplier supplies listed services to a recipient 'through' an electronic marketplace operator when the marketplace is involved in, and facilitates, supplies between underlying suppliers and recipients. Supplies of listed services are not made through an electronic marketplace when a platform advertises on behalf of, redirects customers to the underlying suppliers' own websites or makes its own supplies to customers." QWBA, QB 25/20, "GST listed services rules: How do the rules apply when there is a supply of listed services and other goods or services?" explains what listed services are. The item further clarifies that when a supply includes listed services and other goods or services, standard GST principles apply to determine whether there is a single supply or multiple supplies. The listed services rules apply only to any supplies of listed services that are separate from other supplies.

## Flat-rate credit scheme

The flat-rate credit scheme applies when the underlying service provider is not GST registered. This scheme requires marketplace operators to pass on a proportion of the GST collected on supplies of listed services to

underlying unregistered suppliers. The flat-rate credit scheme provides a "credit" to the underlying suppliers of the service. Most of these suppliers are expected to be unregistered because their total supplies are below the registration threshold of $60,000. The credit is intended to recognise that these unregistered suppliers incur the final GST liability on goods and services used to make the supply of listed services.

The flat-rate credit is determined with reference to the average amount of input tax that GST-registered suppliers of listed services recovers. The scheme does not recognise GST on capital assets such as land or vehicles used to make supplies of listed services. This is because an unregistered person, that supplies listed services, would not account for output tax on the disposal of such asset.

The taxpayers who provide the underlying listed services may still voluntarily apply for GST registration and apply the standard rate of GST, if they do not want to apply the flat-rate credit scheme.

## Example: GST non-registered seller/service provider

Mick is not GST registered and he provides driving services in Auckland on Uber. Uber charges a customer a fare of $30 for Mick's services. The platform adds 15% GST to this fare, charging the customer a total of $34.50. Because Mick is not GST registered, the GST amount of $4.50 is accounted for in the following way:

## $1.95 (6.5% GST) is returned to IR, and

## $2.55 (8.5% GST) is passed on to Mick.

Mick will receive a monthly statement from Uber confirming the flat-rate credits they have passed on to him.

Flat-rate credit receipts are non-taxable receipts. In turn, expenses incurred in deriving listed services income through an electronic marketplace are tax deductible on a GST exclusive basis (s DB 2(2B)). From 1 April 2024, the taxpayer may either treat the flat-rate credit as excluded income (s CX 1B) or treat it as assessable income (s CH 5B), see ¶10-113.

## Listing intermediaries and the flat-rate credit scheme

A common arrangement is where an intermediary, like a property manager, manages a rental property for the owner, and the intermediary engages the electronic marketplace to advertise the property on the platform. The contract is entered into in the name of the intermediary. The electronic marketplace will advertise the GST-inclusive price of the service and pass the entire amount to the intermediary. The transaction between the electronic marketplace and the intermediary is a zero-rated supply. If the underlying owner is GST registered, the supply between the owner and the intermediary is also zero-rated. The flat-rate credit scheme is operated by the intermediary if the owner of the property is not GST registered. The 8.5% tax credit is passed from the property manager to the owner of the property in this case.

## Optional accounting rule for taxable accommodation

From 1 April 2024, s 19DB provides a registered person, who may be an operator of an electronic marketplace, a listing intermediary, or the underlying supplier (depending on the circumstances) may choose to account for output tax (and/or input tax for the flat-rate credit) in or before the taxable period that includes the date that is 7 days after the performance of the services is completed. In practice, the "date that is 7 days after the performance of the services is completed" would be the date that is 7 days after guest check-out occurred. The accounting rule in s 19DB is optional to apply for any given supply covered by the section, meaning the registered person could choose to apply the rule to some supplies of taxable accommodation made through an electronic marketplace but would not have to apply it to all such supplies. The person does not need to notify IR of their choice to apply the rule, but they must keep evidence of the choice for a minimum of 7 years in accordance with the record-keeping rules in the GST Act.

If a person chooses to apply the optional rule, output tax on the supply is attributed to the taxable period in which the person chooses to account for it (s 20(4)(e)). This taxable period must not be later than the taxable period that includes the date that is 7 days after the performance of the services is completed. If the person is liable for both output tax on a supply and to meet requirements relating to the flat-rate credit for the supply,

they must account for output tax and take an input tax deduction for the flat-rate credit on the supply in the same taxable period.

See Tax Information Bulletin ¶375-102 Vol 37, No 5, June 2025 at 84 for detailed commentary and Examples 37-41.

## Opting out

Some underlying suppliers may opt out of the electronic marketplace rules. The result is that these underlying suppliers continue to be responsible for accounting for GST on supplies of listed services.

Those underlying suppliers who contract with a large-scale marketplace, like Air BnB, can opt out provide they meet the following criteria:

## it is agreed between marketplace operator and underlying suppliers who choose to opt-out of the

rules, or

## underlying suppliers with more significant operations can unilaterally opt-out of the marketplace

rules by notifying the marketplace operator. By opting out, they remain responsible for their own GST obligations in respect of supplies they make through electronic marketplaces.

To qualify the underlying provider of services must:

## make more than $500,000 of supplies in a 12-month period (eg significant operations like hotel

chains), or

## provide accommodation and meet a 2,000-night threshold, based on the number of nights of

accommodation listed through a single online marketplace.

Accommodation providers must enter into an agreement with their marketplace operator to opt out of the rules if they meet the criteria above. Note that the 2,000-night threshold can be applied on a group basis.

However, it is not permitted to aggregate accommodation nights across multiple online marketplaces.

## Opting out option for hostels and motels – DET 25/01

Determination DET ¶25/01, "GST on supplies through electronic marketplaces - hostel and motel opt-out agreement criteria" applies to hostels and motels that:

## were registered for GST on or before 1 April 2024 and exceed the GST registration threshold (making

supplies of more than $60,000 for the last 12 months or expecting to make supplies of more than $60,000 for the next 12 months), and

## make supplies both through an electronic marketplace and directly to recipients of accommodation

services.

These hostels and motels would otherwise be unable to opt-out of the electronic marketplace rules as they do not satisfy the statutory opt-out thresholds (ie they make taxable supplies of $500,000 or less in a 12-month period and do not have 2,000 nights of accommodation listed as available on one electronic marketplace for a 12-month period).

This determination is made under s 60C(2BC) of the GST Act and applies for the taxable periods starting on or after 1 April 2025 and ending on or before 31 March 2028.

This determination replaced DET 24/02, "GST on accommodation supplied through electronic marketplaces – opt-out agreement criteria".

## Commissions

An electronic marketplace may charge a fee to the underlying supplier for listing the services on their platform. These may be described as a service fee, commission or facilitation fee. The standard GST rules apply to the electronic marketplace's supply of facilitation services irrespective of whether the supplier is GST registered or not. A GST registered supplier- can claim an input tax deduction on the fee.
