# Agents and returns ¶2-036

## Filing requirements

An agent may be required to furnish a return, make an assessment and satisfy all tax liabilities on behalf of a principal. The Commissioner may treat a relationship between two persons engaged in business as a principal and agency situation. This might arise through either overseas or local control of a New Zealand business actually conducted by another. Agency status for tax purposes is attributed in the following circumstances:

## a guardian or a manager of a person under disability (s HD 9)

## a mortgagee in possession of income derived on behalf of a mortgagor (s HD 10)

## a nominated company in respect of a consolidated group or an imputation group (s HD 11)

## a trustee for income of beneficiaries (excluding a beneficiary of a community trust) (ss HC 32 and HD

12(1))

## a settlor of income tax payable by the trustee (s HC 29)

## a trustee of a unit trust in relation to income of the unit trust (s HD 13)

## a company using the AIM provisional tax method making tax payments for a shareholder–employee

for the purposes of the residual income tax definition (s HD 13B)

## a debenture company in respect of income derived by debenture holders (s HD 14(1)) — in practice,

however, a debenture company is able to avoid agency tax responsibilities for resident debenture holders by supplying the Commissioner with the certified list provided for by s HD 14(2)–(6)

## a reconstituted company for outstanding tax of the original company (s HD 15).

With the Commissioner's agreement, a principal and an agent may agree that the principal will furnish a return that the agent would otherwise be required to furnish (and make an assessment and satisfy any income tax liability the agent would otherwise be required to meet) (s HD 4).

Sections HD 18–HD 30 deal with agents for absentees and non-residents.

Law: IT07 ss HC 29, HC 32, HD 3, HD 4, HD 6, HD 9–HD 15
