# Disputes resolution procedure — background ¶4-120

Part 4A of the TAA relating to disputes resolution procedures and Pt 8A relating to challenges came into effect on 1 October 1996.

A subsequent review in 2003, its purpose to ensure that the procedure operated fairly and efficiently, gave rise to legislative amendments, enacted in 2004 and effective 1 April 2005. For a brief summary of the changes, see CCH, 2005 New Zealand Master Tax Guide, at ¶4-120.

The disputes resolution and challenge provisions of the TAA apply to all issues concerning taxes and duties administered by the Commissioner, other than assessments issued to liable parents pursuant to the Child Support Act 1991.

The procedures anticipate considerable dialogue and disclosure by both the taxpayer and the Commissioner, including the issue of a NOPA, a response notice, a disclosure notice from the Commissioner and a taxpayer's SOP. Once the exchange of views and information seems to have crystallised the parties' respective positions, the Commissioner will issue the appropriate assessment. The taxpayer then has 2 months to commence proceedings under the challenge provisions of Pt 8A.

The disputes resolution process is broken down into 6 identifiable phases:

## issue of a NOPA

## conference (an administrative phase)

## disclosure

## adjudication and review (an administrative requirement only — this is not required by law)

## assessment or amended assessment, and

## litigation.

The disputes procedures envisage that the final step in the process is the issue of an amended assessment.

Previously, under the objection and case stated procedures, further dialogue and consideration normally followed the issue of an amended assessment.

The disputes resolution procedures are designed to settle arguments over the correctness of an assessment or amended assessment before it is issued. This is intended to reduce potential litigation, remove unnecessary compliance costs and secure prompt and equitable resolution of disputes.

The dispute resolution process is not changed by taxpayer self-assessment.
