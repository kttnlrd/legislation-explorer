# Request for non-standard balance date ¶2-055

Persons can apply to file tax returns on the basis of a corresponding income year that ends with their year- end. Generally, only a person carrying on a business will be given approval for a non-standard balance date.

Any approved non-standard balance date continues to apply until the person requests that it be altered. The corresponding income year for a tax year is an income year that ends in the period starting on 1 October in the tax year and ending on 30 September immediately after the tax year. A person with an early balance date files a return for a tax year on the basis of a corresponding income year that ends on a date between 1 October and the following 30 March (both days inclusive). For a person with a late balance date, the corresponding income year ends on a date between 1 April and the following 30 September (both days inclusive).

Example:

Accounting year/Corresponding income year Tax year 29 February 2025 31 March 2025 31 October 2025 31 March 2025 31 July 2025 31 March 2025

Example 2: TP Ltd has an approved non-standard balance date of 31 August. Income derived during the year ended 31 August 2025 will constitute the company's income for the corresponding income year to the tax year ended 31 March 2025.

The Commissioner has the power to make any assessment considered to be necessary to give effect to the change in balance date.

The taxpayers listed below are excluded from adopting a non-standard balance date:

- a qualifying individual (broadly, those who only derive reportable income) whose final account for the

  tax year is treated under s 22I(1)(b) as an assessment (¶2-016), and

- a multi-rate PIE that does not calculate and pay tax using the provisional tax calculation option under

  s HM 44.

See also ¶2-065 and ¶2-070.

The Commissioner has set out their practice for considering applications for consent to a change in balance date for income tax purposes in Standard Practice Statement, SPS 24/01, "Requests to change a balance date"..

*Refs: s 22I(1)(b), s HM 44*

Retrospective elections

Ideally, elections to change a balance date will be made, and the Commissioner's consent received, before the commencement of a new income year, so taxpayers can avoid additional compliance costs should consent be withheld.

The Commissioner's practice is that late applications for a change of balance date will be accepted if made before the earlier of the return filing date under s 37(1) of the TAA for the current balance date and that for the proposed balance date. Consent will be provided where taxpayers can show that:

## it is possible to file returns for all the income years

## the late election was not made for reasons of tax deferral or tax avoidance, or to take undue

advantage of any tax incentive or concession, and

## any l tax deferral as a consequence of the proposed balance date is only insignificant when

compared with their total tax liability.

## Circumstances in which non-standard balance date approved

The Commissioner normally allows a non-standard balance date, ie other than 31 March, in the following circumstances:

## The nature of the business makes a 31 March balance date impractical.

## The business wishes to align with an agreed industry balance date. For example, the following

balance dates are commonly recognised:

## apiarists 30 November or 31 December

## education/child care related

services 31 December

## farmers: cattle 31 May

— dairy 31 May, or 30 June, or 31 July — sheep 30 June

## fishing industries 30 September

## horse breeders 31 July

## kiwifruit orchardists 31 January, 28 February, or 31 March

## meat processing and export 31 August, or 30 September

## orchardists, pipfruit 31 March, or 30 June, or 31 December

## seed dressers 30 November

## tobacco growers 31 July

## For shareholder-employees, when the date coincides with the balance date of the company in

which they are shareholders and from which they derive their primary source of income. If they are shareholders in more than one company, their balance date should be aligned to the balance date of the company that provides their primary source of income.

## For subsidiary companies, when the date coincides with the balance date of a parent company,

except when the change would result in a significant deferral of income.

## For business entities with a close working relationship, aligning balance dates where they share a

common business/management accounting system or central administration structure and already have an approved non-standard balance date (including a joint venture that has elected to be treated as a partnership for income tax purposes wanting to adopt a balance date in common with a joint venturer).

## For managed funds, allowing them to adopt a balance date in common with a fund manager or

trustee when it can be demonstrated that a parent-subsidiary like relationship exists between parties (excludes a multi-rate PIE that does not make payments of tax under s HM 44 of the IT07).

## For entities deemed to be agents of non-resident insurers, allowing them to file "as agent" returns in

terms of s HD 16 (excludes a multi-rate PIE that does not make payments of tax under s HM 44).

## For taxpayers that perform business activities in New Zealand that have a centre of management

outside New Zealand (does not apply to passive investment income), allowing them to adopt a balance date applicable to a non-resident taxpayer's tax jurisdiction.

## For continuing estates, when the date coincides with the date of death of the deceased taxpayer.

## For a previously tax-exempt entity, allowing it to continue to use a balance date consistent with an

existing date for financial reporting purposes. (For example, a charity that had only exempt income so was not previously required to file tax returns and is now required to file returns, may continue to use the non-standard balance date they had used before entering the tax base.)

## Where a taxpayer can demonstrate that unreasonable or excessive compliance costs will be incurred

if income has to be returned to 31 March.

## Where a franchisee is required as a condition of a franchise agreement to use a non-standard

balance date for financial reporting purposes (where the applicable balance date has been recognised via an agreement between the Commissioner and the master franchisor).

## Where a taxpayer receives passive income as well as business income and wishes to adopt a non-

standard balance date for returning their business income. If the business income has a source in a related business entity, a customer may elect to return income to the balance date of that related entity.

When providing consent to a new balance date, the Commissioner will only agree to a balance date that is the last calendar day of a month, eg 30 November rather than 15 November — except for a continuing estate that elects to adopt a balance date that coincides with a deceased taxpayer's date of death.

The Commissioner will not agree to a change in balance date when:

## the non-standard balance date is the anniversary date of the commencement of a business, unless it

coincides with an agree industry balance date

## the reasons for the change is tax deferral, tax avoidance or to take advantage of any tax incentive or

concession

## the request has been made to smooth out administrative workloads within the business or to smooth

the workflow of a manager, trustee or tax agent

## the taxpayer has investment income and no direct involvement in a business activity

## functions have been contracted out to a third party (eg a specialist administration manager) and the

taxpayer wishes to adopt the manager's balance date The SPS also sets out how to make a request and the information that must accompany a request.
