# Dividends — overdrawn shareholder current account ¶16-582

Shareholder current (or loan) accounts are often informal arrangements between shareholders and close companies that record advances made between them. An overdrawn shareholder current account at year end means a shareholder withdrew more money from the company than they have advanced to the company, and this can give rise to a number of tax issues.

Interpretation statement IS 24/09, "Income tax — overdrawn shareholder loan account balances", considers the common tax implications of an overdrawn shareholder current account for a close company along with practical examples. These key tax issues are summarised below.

## Deemed dividends

Deemed dividends arise if the shareholder pays no or low interest on their overdrawn shareholder current account. Note however that if the shareholder is also an employee of the company, the FBT rules will apply (¶21-410).

## Reducing the deemed dividend amount

The current account may be treated as having been repaid to the later of the beginning of the company's income year or the day the loan was made where (s CD 39(9)):

## the shareholder to whom the loan was made is paid an amount of salary, wages, an extra pay,

dividends or interest

## the amount so paid constitutes income of the shareholder, and

## the amount so paid is not subject to RWT, NRWT or PAYE tax deduction or is a fully-imputed

dividend.

The intention is to allow for a fully-imputed dividend to be backdated to expunge, or at least reduce, an overdrawn current account balance. It has therefore been clarified that the rule in relation to a fully-imputed dividend applies irrespective of any RWT obligation that may arise because of the relevant shareholder's RWT rate. This avoids or limits the deemed dividend arising and reduces compliance costs.

## Charging interest on an overdrawn shareholder current

account The amount of deemed dividend arising from an overdrawn shareholder current account is calculated for each quarter during which the account is overdrawn. The deemed dividend is treated as being paid 6 months after the end of the company's income year, unless the company gives the shareholder earlier notice of the amount of the dividend (s CD 39(2) and (3)). A company has 2 options to calculate the amount of the dividend (s CD 39(5)):

## the benchmark interest and the actual amount of interest accruing on the loan, or

## the benchmark interest and the amount of income accruing to the company in the quarter calculated

under the yield to maturity method.

For detailed discussion on the above calculation methods, see ¶16-620. Also see IS 24/09 [39] for a calculation example.

For detailed discussion on the RWT obligations for shareholders, see ¶15-070 and ¶15-220. Also the flowchart in IS 24/09 at [76] summarises the circumstances for when a shareholder is required to withhold RWT.

## Deductibility of interest expense for shareholder

Interest paid by a shareholder on their overdrawn shareholder current account is generally not deductible, unless the shareholder draws money for use in an income-earning activity or a business and sufficient documentation is required to support the interest deduction (¶10-650).

## Interest income for the company

Interest charged on an overdrawn shareholder current account is taxable income for the company. The FA rules should be applied to determine the timing of derivation of the interest income. The most common method for smaller companies is the straight-line method which requires very simple calculation (see IS 24/09 [85] to [90]).

## Application of the FA rules and BPA calculation for the company

A company must apply the FA rules to an overdrawn shareholder loan account unless the loan is interest- free, repayable on demand and in New Zealand dollars. Under the FA rules, a BPA must be calculated by both parties (in addition to the dividend rules) when one of the following applies:

## The shareholder is discharged from making all remaining payments owed on their overdrawn

shareholder loan account without fully adequate consideration.

## The shareholder is released from making all remaining payments owed on their overdrawn

shareholder loan account under the Insolvency Act 2006 or the C93.

## All remaining payments owed on the shareholder's overdrawn shareholder loan account become

irrecoverable or unenforceable through the lapse of time.

See IS 24/09 [95] to [123] for detailed discussion of the application of the FA rules and calculation examples.
