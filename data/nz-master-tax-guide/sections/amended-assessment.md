# Amended assessment ¶4-090

The Commissioner is authorised under s 113 of the TAA to make any alterations to an assessment in order to ensure the correctness of that assessment, notwithstanding that the tax already assessed may have been paid. If any such alteration or addition has the effect of imposing any fresh liability or increasing any existing liability, the Commissioner must issue a notice of proposed adjustment (NOPA) to the taxpayer. A NOPA is most commonly issued after an audit by the Commissioner. If issues are identified during the course of the audit and agreement on them cannot be reached, the Commissioner issues the NOPA. This will lead to full disclosure and statement of position (SOP) by each party (¶4-185).

*Refs: s 113*

## Amending an assessment during the disputes process

The Commissioner's power to amend an assessment under s 113 of the TAA is restricted if all of the following criteria are satisfied:

## a NOPA has been issued

## the dispute has not been resolved by agreement between the Commissioner and the disputant, and

## none of the following situations (set out in s 89N(1)(c)) apply:

## the Commissioner notifies the disputant that, in the Commissioner's opinion, the disputant has

committed an offence under an Inland Revenue Act in the course of the dispute and this has delayed the completion of the disputes process

## the Commissioner has reasonable grounds to believe that the disputant, or an associated person of

the disputant, might take steps in relation to the disputant's assets to avoid or delay the collection of tax from the disputant

## the disputant has begun judicial review proceedings in relation to the dispute

## an associated person of the disputant involved in another dispute with the Commissioner on similar

issues has begun judicial review proceedings in relation to that other dispute

## the disputant has failed to comply, within the specified period, with a requirement under a statute to

produce information received from the Commissioner relating to the dispute

## the disputant and the Commissioner agree, recording their agreement in a document, that they have

reached a position in which the dispute would be resolved more efficiently by being submitted to the court or Taxation Review Authority (TRA) without completion of the disputes process — also known as truncation of the disputes resolution process (¶4-179), or

## the disputant and the Commissioner agree, recording their agreement in a document, to suspend

proceedings in the disputes process pending a decision in a test case (¶4-250) or

## the NOPA is accepted.

If the above criteria are satisfied, the Commissioner will not be able to amend an assessment under s 113 until one of the following events has occurred:

## the Commissioner or the disputant accepts a NOPA, notice of response (NOR) or SOP issued by the

other party, or

## the Commissioner considers a SOP issued by the disputant.

This restriction will not prevent the Commissioner from making an application to the High Court for an order that allows more time for the completion of the disputes process or for an order that completion of the disputes process is not required (s 89N(2) of the TAA and C of IR v Rabobank New Zealand Ltd (2009) 24 NZTC 23,170). Such an application must be made within the period of time during which the Commissioner would otherwise be required to make an amended assessment.

If an application is made to the High Court for an extension of time to complete the disputes process or for an order that the disputes process is not required, the Commissioner will be required to make an amended assessment by the last day of the period that:

## begins on the day following the day by which the Commissioner, in the absence of the application,

would be required to make the amended assessment, and

## contains the total of:

## the number of days between the date the application is filed in the High Court by the Commissioner

and the earlier of the date of the High Court's decision or the date the dispute is resolved, and

## the number of days allowed by an order of the court as a result of the application.

See standard practice statement, SPS 23/01, "Disputes process".

## IR10 and amended assessments

Taxpayers may file an IR10 form with their income tax returns instead of attaching a full set of financial statements. IR's policy on reopening assessments where an IR10 is filed in substitution for financial statements is set out in operational statement OS 16/01, "Filing an IR 10 and section 108 of the Tax Administration Act 1994". The statement sets out the Commissioner's preference for the majority of taxpayers to file an IR10 rather than financial statements to bring income to the attention of the Commissioner when filing their annual return. The statement amalgamates and replaces all previous items published by the Commissioner about income disclosure using an IR10 and the impact of this on the time bar under s 108 of the TAA, and applies to all decisions that may require the re-opening of a return made on or after 26 May 2016.

The statement makes clear the Commissioner can reopen a statute-barred assessment if the return was fraudulent or wilfully misleading, regardless of which method was used to disclose income.

In the case of omission of income:

## If the IR10 discloses the income, gain or receipt, the time bar will apply.

## Where the IR10, although fully completed and consistent with the financial statements, does not

disclose the income, gain or receipt due to limitations in the IR10 form, the following approach will apply:

## If the unfiled financial statements disclose the income, gain or receipt, the return will not be amended

even though the financial statements were not sent to IR at the time the income tax return was filed.

## If neither the IR10 nor the unfiled financial statements disclose the income, gain, or receipt, the

return will be subject to Critical Task Assurance and review by senior Service Delivery management to determine whether the time bar applies.

The statement notes that this approach will not apply if an IR10 is filed, instead of a financial statement, in 2 circumstances — reconstructed income and aggressive tax schemes or tax avoidance.

## Self-assessment of tax liability

Under the disputes resolution process, a taxpayer may dispute an assessment made by the Commissioner.

However, self-assessment means that the Commissioner no longer makes the initial assessment. Therefore, taxpayers disputing an initial assessment are, in effect, "disputing" their own assessment. Although the taxpayers' ability to amend their own assessments might be seen as inconsistent with the necessary element of finality that characterises an assessment, an advantage to taxpayers in being able to dispute an assessment is to eliminate their exposure to shortfall penalties.

The taxpayers' right to amend their own assessments is retained under self-assessment in accordance with s 89DA of the TAA. The amendments are initiated by a NOPA.

A taxpayer has 4 months to amend the taxpayer's own assessment. The 4-month period begins on the date on which the taxpayer's notice of assessment is received at an IR office. #Note: There was arguably a loophole in the disputes procedures that allowed a taxpayer to circumvent the time limit for challenging an assessment. This involved the taxpayer making a voluntary disclosure for a particular item, which arguably allowed them to reopen the entire original assessment, circumventing the time limit. However, from 30 March 2021, an amendment closed this potential loophole by providing that if the assessment by the taxpayer is an amended assessment, the taxpayer's entitlement to dispute the assessment is limited to disputing a new or increased liability imposed.

Taxpayers must also self-assess their GST liability and have the right to amend their GST assessments within 4 months of the date on which the self-assessment is received at an IR office.

See also SPS 23/01, "Disputes process".

## Commissioner's practice

Standard practice statement SPS 20/03, "Requests to amend assessments", outlines the exercise of the Commissioner's discretion to amend assessments to ensure that they are correct. The statement applies in respect of the exercise of the Commissioner's discretion to amend assessments under s 113 of the TAA. The statement applies from 2 June 2020 and replaces all previous statements on the exercise of the Commissioner's discretion under s 113.

Assessments corrected without entering disputes process

Certain qualifying taxpayers can have their income tax assessments corrected without issuing a NOPA.

A "qualifying individual" is a person whose income in a tax year is constituted only of "reportable income".

Reportable income is the types of income IR regularly receives information on during the course of a tax year up to 31 May following the end of the tax year. It is typically income like employment income with PAYE withheld by employers and interest income from banks which withhold RWT and NRWT at source which is reported against the individual's IRD number (¶2-112 and SPS 20/03, "Requests to amend assessments").
