# Deductibility of residential rental interest ¶10-814

## Interest deductibility from 1 April 2024

From 1 April 2024 interest deductibility is being restored in a phased manner. Reintroduction of deductibility of interest applies to all taxpayers, irrespective of when the residential rental property was acquired. The date of acquisition of the previously "disallowed residential property" does not apply.

Interest deductibility is phased back in as follows:

Date interest incurred % of interest that can be deducted 1 April 2024 to 31 March 2025 80% 1 April 2025 onwards 100%

From 1 April 2025 subpt DH is substantially repealed. The rules relating to the ability to claim a deduction for interest denied under subpt DH on disposal are retained so that taxpayers who dispose of property that was subject to the rules can claim a full deduction of the denied interest on disposal of that property.

The current rules (below) which determine the types of land and taxpayers subject to the interest limitation rules, including the anti-avoidance rules, will continue to apply while deductibility is being reintroduced.

Interest deductibility from 1 October 2021 to 31 March 2024 Most residential property owners are affected by the interest limitation rules introduced in 2021 in subpt DH.

The government's stated purpose in introducing the rules was to slow down investor interest in the property market and make houses more affordable for first-time home buyers. Keeping good financial records of interest payments made (and retaining these for longer than the statutory requirement of 7 years) is prudent, given that interest that is limited or denied a deduction may later be deductible upon disposal.

Residential rental property purchased on or after 27 March

Broadly, taxpayers who purchased residential rental property ("disallowed residential property" or "DPR") on or after 27 March 2021 are denied interest deductions on any interest incurred from 1 October 2021 until 1 April 2024. See above for limited deductibility of interest for the 2025 income year.

Residential rental property purchased before 27 March 2021 Interest deductions on loans drawn down before 27 March 2021, for residential rental property acquired before that date, are progressively denied for the following income years. Interest deductibility is denied as follows (s DH 8):

Date interest incurred % of interest that can be deducted 1 April 2021 to 31 March 2022 1 April 2021 to 30 September 2021 – 100% (transitional year) 1 October 2021 to 31 March 2022 – 75% 1 April 2022 to 31 March 2023 75% 1 April 2023 to 31 March 2024 50%

1 April 2024 to 31 March 2025* 80% (originally limited to 25%) 1 April 2025 onwards* 100% (originally 0% and fully denied)

The phasing out of interest, along with apportionment, financing and disposal issues, are discussed in more detail below. Special rules apply to companies owning residential rental properties are also discussed below. * Note that the phasing out of interest was reversed from 1 April 2024. Originally interest deductibility would have been denied completely on or after 1 April 2025.

## Disallowed residential property – which properties are caught?

The interest limitation rules deny (or limit) interest deductions incurred for "disallowed residential property" (DRP). This is defined as land in New Zealand to the extent to which it has as a residence or abode (whether it is used as such), including any appurtenances belonging to or enjoyed with the place.

Also captured is (i) land where the owner has an arrangement to erect a place configured as a residence or abode on the land, and (ii) bare land that may be used to erect a place configured as a residence or abode under the rules of a relevant operative district plan. This is regardless of whether the bare land will in fact be used as a residence or abode. The definition is broad and encompasses residential homes used for short- stay accommodation such as Bookabach or Airbnb.

## Exemption for new builds

Interest incurred in relation to "new build land" is not subject to the interest limitation rules and continue to be fully deductible (s DH 4(1)), subject to a 20-year time limit (see below). In addition, the 10-year bright-line test for residential property dispositions does not apply to new builds. Instead, new builds are only subject to the 5-year bright-line test (¶7-053). See ¶10-818 for detailed commentary on the exemption for new builds.

## Exemption for dealers, builders and developers

Interest deductions will continue to be available for those taxpayers who are carrying on business and the land in question is subject to taxation under s CB 7 (s DH 4(2)). This provision taxes land dealers, builders and developers. This exemption is intended to apply to any activity that results in a dwelling that qualifies as a "new build". See ¶10-817 for detailed commentary on the exemption for property development.

## Other exemptions

Apart from those mentioned above, there are a number of types of property are exempt from these rules.

See ¶10-815 for a list of types of land exempt from the interest limitation rule.

## Companies

The automatic deduction of interest for companies in s DB 7 is expressly overridden: s DB 7(6C).

Close companies (companies with 5 or fewer natural person or trustee shareholders who hold more than 50% of the shares) will be subject to the new limitation rules, regardless of the proportion of assets that are residential rental properties: s DH 3(a).

Unless one of the exceptions or exemptions outlined at ¶10-815 applies, a company is subject to the interest limitation rules if it is a close company (¶16-010), apart from exempt M#ori companies. For companies larger than a close company, the interest limitation rules apply if the company is:

## a residential land company, or

## a member of a residential land wholly-owned group.

An exception, as noted above, is an "exempt M#ori company". These are defined as a company that is not a residential land company or a residential land wholly-owned group member and is a M#ori authority or eligible to be a M#ori authority, or wholly-owned by a M#ori authority or a company or trust that is eligible to be a M#ori authority.

An "exempt M#ori company" is a company that is a M#ori authority (¶24-288) or wholly owned by a M#ori authority and is not a residential land company or a member of a residential land wholly-owned group.

A "residential land company" is essentially a company where the combined value of its "disallowed residential property" (DRP) (excluding DRP subject to the land business or development exemptions) and the shares it holds in other residential land companies makes up more than 50% of the value of its total assets. Similarly, a "residential land wholly-owned group member" is a member of a wholly-owned group where the combined value of its DRP (excluding DRP subject to the land business or development exemptions) and the shares it holds in other residential land companies makes up more than 50% of the value of its total assets (s DH 5(8)(11)).

In both cases, the 50% threshold is measured on a daily basis, so that a company may become a residential land company if it meets or exceeds the 50% threshold on any given day during the income year. See Example from Tax Information Bulletin ¶345-105 Vol 34, No 5, June 2022 at 46 to illustrate the application of the rules.

Companies that are subject to the interest limitation rules are required to trace their interest expenditure. See further below under the heading Phasing out of interest – "grandparented residential interest".

Phasing out of interest – "grandparented residential interest" Interest on loans first drawn down before 27 March 2021 — for residential rental property (DRP) acquired before that date — are referred to as "grandparented residential interest". Deductions are limited and apply on these dates regardless of a taxpayer's balance date:

## 1 April 2021 to 30 September 2021 — 100% deductible

## 1 October 2021 to 31 March 2022 — 75% deductible

## 1 April 2022 to 31 March 2023 — 75% deductible

## 1 April 2023 to 31 March 2024 — 50% deductible

## 1 April 2024 to 31 March 2025 — 80% deductible.

Also captured in this phased approach are:

## Loans first drawn down on or after 27 March 2021 if the person acquired an estate or interest in the

DRP before that date (which in most cases means the purchaser had a binding sale and purchase agreement pre-27 March but settlement did not occur until after that date).

## Loans drawn down on or after 27 March 2021 if the acquisition of the DRP resulted from an offer

made on or before 23 March 2021 that became irrevocable before 27 March 2021 (which is a common scenario when property is purchased by way of a tender).

Any loans denominated in a foreign currency do not qualify for the phased approach and are therefore fully non-deductible from 1 October 2021. (Refinancing in New Zealand dollars is an option for such taxpayers – see below.)

## Refinancing

Refinancing on loans on or after 27 March 2021 is subject to the phased approach, provided the original loan was drawn down pre-27 March 2021. A foreign denominated loan will also qualify for phased deductions if the loan is refinanced in New Zealand dollars (effective from the time that the New Zealand dollar loan is drawn down).

## Calculating interest on variable balance or revolving loans

A special rule exists for taxpayers with "variable balance" loans (also variously described as "revolving credit facilities", "overdraft facilities" or "revolving loans", among other things). To simplify the calculations required

to work out the amount of interest to be phased out, a "high water mark" rule is available for taxpayers if they choose to use it.

Broadly, the balance of the loan on 26 March 2021 in relation to disallowed residential property is the benchmark against which a subsequent loan balance is measured. All interest can be treated as "grandparented" and the phased approach (outlined above) used if:

## the loan has been used solely for DRP, and

## the loan balance remains at or below the loan balance at on 26 March 2021, all interest can be

treated as "grandparented" and the phased approach (outlined above) used.

If the loan has been applied to mixed purposes such as DRP and private (not deductible), or DRP and commercial (fully deductible) the calculation is more complex. Refer to Tax Information Bulletin ¶345-105 Vol 34, No 5, June 2022 for a detailed explanation.

## Tracing loans – difficulty in identifying relevant interest

The interest on pre-27 March 2021 loans that are used for both residential rental property (DRP) and non- residential property needs to be traced in order to separate out and quantify the DRP portion (as interest deductibility is phased out between 1 October 2021 and 31 March 2025). See Tax Information Bulletin ¶345-105 Vol 34, No 5, June 2022 at 90 for a number of examples demonstrating the application of the rules.

## Interposed entities: funds to acquire interest in a company or

trust Special rules concerning "interposed entities" exist to prevent a person from claiming an interest deduction when borrowing funds to acquire shares in a company (or to become a beneficiary of a trust) that holds disallowed residential property. Without the interposed entity rules, it would be a simple matter to circumvent the interest limitation rules via a company or trust.

The rules deny interest deductions for taxpayers who indirectly hold DRP through an interposed residential property holder (IRPH) and borrow money to acquire ownership interests in, or become a beneficiary of, the IRPH. An IRPH is a company or trust that has DRP as a percentage of its total assets that exceeds the percentage of disqualifies assets over total assets. If the only asset held is a disallowed residential property, then 100% of the interest incurred by the IRPH will be disallowed.

Refer to Tax Information Bulletin ¶345-105 Vol 34, No 5, June 2022 at 113 for more detail. Further reading around the amendments made to s DH 6 can be found in Tax Information Bulletin ¶356-102 Vol 35, No 6, July 2023 at 121, and includes discussion around how s DH 6 affects mixed-use assets.

## Rollover relief for certain types of land transfers

If ownership of a residential rental property changes during the period that interest deductions are being phased out (ie 1 October 2021 to 31 March 2025), rollover relief exists in cases where the economic ownership essentially remains unchanged. Rollover relief, in this instance, means that the transferee can continue to deduct interest during the phasing out period as if they were in the shoes of the transferor. In all cases, the loan amount transferred must be equal to or less than the loan balance of the transferor at the time of the transfer.

The following transfers of DRP are eligible for rollover relief:

## a settlement of relationship property

## on death, a transfer to an executor/administrator or to a beneficiary of a deceased estate

## to and from a look-through company or a partnership (ie to and from people within that entity, in a

different capacity)

## from a person to a M#ori trustee, or from a M#ori trustee back to the original settlor

## from an amalgamating company to an amalgamated company under a resident's restricted

amalgamation.

Also eligible for rollover relief are transfers of DRP to or from trustees in circumstances where a family trust meets the definition of a "rollover trust", that is:

## all relevant transfers to the trustees of the trust must be by beneficiaries, and at least one of those

beneficiaries is a principal settlor of the trust

## all principal settlors must be beneficiaries of the trust

## all principal settlors must be close family associates, and

## all beneficiaries are either "close family beneficiaries" or trustees of another trust and at least one

beneficiary of the other trust is a close family associate of a beneficiary of the trust in question.

For a detailed analysis of the operation of the rollover relief for interest limitation see Tax Information Bulletin ¶356-102 Vol 35 No 6, July 2023.

## Disposal of property affected by the interest limitation rules

If a residential property is subject to tax on disposal, interest deductions denied under the interest limitation rules may be claimable under a wash-up calculation when the disallowed residential property is disposed of:

## If the disposal is taxable under the bright-line test (s CB 6A or CZ 39), interest deductions are treated

as part of the cost of the property under s DB 23 and be deducted in the year of disposal. (If a net loss arises, this is ring-fenced but may be carried forward.) See s DH 11(1).

## If the disposal is taxable under one of the other land disposal provisions in ss CB 6–CB 14, interest

deductions are permitted in the year of disposal under s DH 11(2). The residential ring-fencing rules apply (¶7-365).

## Loans denominated in foreign currency

Originally interest incurred on loans denominated in foreign currency was non-deductible: s DH 9. There was no phasing out of interest deductions for these types of loans, the government noting that the rules around such loans would be overly complex. However, s DH 9 was repealed with retrospective effect because foreign currency loans were already excluded from the application of s DH 8(2) because they cannot generate grandparented residential interest.
