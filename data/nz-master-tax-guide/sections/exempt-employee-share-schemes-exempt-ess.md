# Exempt employee share schemes (exempt ESS) ¶3-471

The IT07 previously provided a concessionary regime to encourage employers to offer shares to employees under certain widely-offered "share purchase schemes" (commonly known as "exempt schemes").

Those rules were out of date, complex and no longer fit for purpose.

The new rules simplify and clarify the legislation relating to exempt employee share schemes ("exempt ESS"), while retaining many of the key features of the original schemes and their tax treatment.

Shares provided to employees under schemes that meet certain criteria are exempt income to the employees. Benefits provided under schemes that qualified for the previous tax-exempt treatment simply continue to qualify under the new legislation, but such schemes are entitled to provide the same level of exempt benefit as new schemes.

For all exempt ESS, employers are explicitly denied a deduction for the cost of providing the shares (other than costs relating to establishing and managing the scheme). The previous 10% notional interest deduction is repealed. The changes apply from 29 March 2018, and from 6 April 2017 (the date of introduction of the relevant Bill) if shares under the exempt ESS were acquired on or after that date in other than the ordinary course of the exempt ESS.

The automatic exemption from FBT for loans provided under ESS in s CX 10(2) continues.

The new rules have been designed to ensure existing schemes that meet the criteria described below can continue to operate without unnecessary disruption. Therefore, to the extent possible, the existing rules have been retained and simply clarified. For a scheme to be an exempt ESS, all the following criteria must be met (s CW 26C):

## The cost to employees of shares made available for purchase must not exceed their market value at

the date of purchase but may be less.

## The maximum value of shares provided under an exempt ESS is $7,500 pa (before 1 April 2025, the

threshold was $5,000 pa).

## The maximum discount an employer can provide to an employee is $3,000pa (before 1 April 2025,

the threshold was $2,000 pa).

## 90% or more of full-time permanent employees who are not subject to securities law of other

jurisdictions must be eligible to participate in the scheme. If the scheme applies to part-time employees or to seasonal employees, the same threshold applies. Previously, all full-time employees had to be eligible to participate.

## If the scheme has a minimum spend requirement, the amount can be no more than $1,000 pa.

## Any minimum period of service that may be required before an employee becomes eligible to

participate must not exceed 3 years (or the equivalent of 3 years full-time service).

## If a trust holds shares in trust for an employee, the arrangement must provide that the trustee pays

dividends directly to the employee and the dividends are treated as derived by the employee.

## If the employee is required to pay any amount for the shares, then the employer must provide a loan

for that amount or allow the employee to pay for or buy the shares in regular instalments.

## Loans to employees for the purchase of shares must be free of all interest and other charges.

## Employees will repay loans by regular equal instalments at intervals of not more than one month over

a period between 3 and 5 years from the date of the loan.

## Generally speaking, the shares have to be held (either by the employee or by a trustee of a trust

on behalf of the employee) for the longer of 3 years and when the loan is repaid — this is to ensure that the scheme is really an exempt ESS, and not a mechanism for providing cash remuneration.

However, the employee is not required to hold the shares beyond the date their employment ends.

If the employee has paid full market value for the shares, then they only have to hold them until they have fully repaid the loan — at this point they are like any other shareholder and should not have extra restrictions placed on them.

*Refs: s CX 10(2), s CW 26C*

## The employee can choose to withdraw from the scheme by giving the employer one month's notice

and have their shares purchased back for the lesser of market value and cost.

## If participation in the scheme is causing serious hardship for the employee, the terms of payment can

be varied or employees can be allowed to withdraw from the scheme and receive the market value of their shares.

## If the employee leaves employment before the 3 years is up, then:

## if they leave because of death, accident, sickness, redundancy or retirement at normal retiring age

they can keep the shares (subject to repayment of the loan) or have the shares bought back for the lesser of market value and cost, and

## if they leave for any other reason (so-called "bad leavers"), the company may choose whether they

are permitted to keep their shares, or whether the shares are bought back at the lesser of cost and market value.

## The exempt ESS is not required to be approved by the Commissioner. However, the Commissioner

must be notified of the scheme's existence and the employer must advise the Commissioner annually of the grants made under the scheme (s 63B of the TAA).

## There is no requirement for a scheme to have a trust — many schemes have trusts as a matter of

convenience. Removing this requirement provides greater flexibility (especially for small employers who may not want the administrative expense of operating a trust for a fairly small scheme).

With effect from 30 March 2021, it has been clarified that the trustee of an exempt ESS can be treated as a nominee of the company providing a scheme (as is already the case for an ESS other than an exempt ESS).

*Refs: s 63B*
