# Exempt income limitation for deductions ¶10-080

The notion of exempt income refers to income that would otherwise be assessable but for the exemption. It does not encompass distributions of a capital nature: C of IR v Brierley (1990) 12 NZTC 7,184 (CA). On that basis, an individual investor was allowed a deduction for the interest paid on borrowings raised to purchase shares that generated very little assessable income.

One area in which the exempt income limitation can be expected to have application is in relation to a holding company that derives only exempt dividends from its subsidiaries (¶16-800). A common practice is for the holding company to be paid management fees to cover the costs of managing the affairs of the subsidiaries. The subsidiaries can be expected to be allowed a deduction for the management fees as a recurrent cost of their trading operations: Lockwood Buildings Ltd v C of IR (1996) 17 NZTC 12,483.

The exempt income limitation may apply to a premium paid under a sick, accident or disablement policy that will produce benefits that are exempt income (¶10-540).
