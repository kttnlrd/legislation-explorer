# Determination of income tax liability ¶2-110

The determination of a person's income tax liability follows the steps set out in s BC 6. The following diagram illustrates the process used by persons (except non-filing taxpayers) to calculate income tax liability.

Section BC 6: income tax liability of filing taxpayer

*Refs: s BC 6, Section BC 6*

Section BC 6(1) states that the income tax liability for a tax year of a filing taxpayer is the amount calculated by multiplying taxable income for the tax year by the basic tax rate.

The "basic tax rate" is defined in s YA 1 to mean:

## the basic rate of income tax for the person under Sch 1 (basic tax rates: income tax, employer's

superannuation contribution tax (ESCT), retirement scheme contribution tax (RSCT), resident withholding tax (RWT) and attributed fringe benefits) and Sch 2 (basic tax rates for pay as you earn (PAYE) income payments), and

## the basic rate of income tax under Sch 1 apportioned on a time basis in the case of a person filing

a return in accordance with s 39 of the TAA ie in a transitional year on a change of balance date (see example in ¶2-075).

*Refs: Section BC 6(1), s YA 1, s 39*

## Satisfaction of income tax liability

Once a person has determined their tax liability in accordance with s BC 6, the liability is first satisfied by total tax credits available and then through the payment of terminal tax.

Generally, tax credits are an allowance for tax paid or withheld at some other time during the relevant tax year.

A person has terminal tax for a tax year in which their tax liability exceeds their total tax credits as determined in accordance with Pt L (credits). The amount of the terminal tax is the difference between the two. To satisfy the person's income tax liability, the terminal tax must be paid.

The following example illustrates the situation where a person's income tax liability exceeds total tax credits.

## Treatment of excess credits

In some instances, credits may exceed the income tax liability for the tax year. In that event, specific rules apply to determine how the credits are utilised:

## the excess credits are first set off in accordance with s LA 4, and

## any remaining tax credit is dealt with in accordance with s LA 5.

Section LA 4 provides that the person's credits are to be treated as if they were set off against the person's income tax liability in the following order:

## non-refundable tax credits

## tax credits for supplementary dividends

## tax credits for imputation credits

## carried forward R&D tax credits

## R&D tax credits, and

## refundable credits.

Non-refundable tax credits include credits such as the independent earner tax credit, tax credits for payroll donations, and tax credits for foreign income tax paid.

The policy behind the ordering rules above is to allow maximum credit for foreign taxes and other amounts that are capped at the level of domestic income tax liability.

Any remaining tax credits after applying the above ordering rules (other than refundable tax credits) are dealt with as follows:

## non-refundable tax credits are extinguished (with a few exceptions)

## tax credits for supplementary dividends may be made available to another company in the same

wholly-owned group

## imputation credits are either carried forward or converted to losses

## R&D tax credits are refunded to a specified maximum or carried forward.

## Excess imputation credits received

Excess imputation credits received by the following taxpayers are converted to a loss and carried forward:

## a company (that is not a life insurer)

## a trustee (other than a Maori trustee)

## a Maori authority

## a life insurer (but only for imputation credits to the extent to which the amount is for their shareholder

base).

The conversion rates are set out below (s LE 2(3) and Sch 1, pt A):

Taxpayer Conversion rate Company 28 Trustee of a disabled beneficiary trust, an estate referred to in s HC 8B, a 33 lines trust or a de minimis trust (from 1 April 2024) Trustee other than a trustee referred to above or a Maori trustee (from 1 39 April 2024) Trustee other than a Maori trustee (prior to 1 April 2024) 33 Maori authority 17.5 Life insurer (for shareholder base) 28

For taxpayers who do not fall under any of these groups (eg individuals), the excess imputation credits received for an income year are carried forward as a credit of tax to the next income year and so on, until it is

extinguished by the Commissioner when outstanding tax is written off (¶2-119) or it is used up. See ¶17-010 for a worked example.

A group company with excess imputation credits may offset the loss arising on conversion wholly or partly against the net income of another group company in the income year that the excess imputation credits arise.

To claim a credit, the person is required to furnish the shareholder dividend statement (or other sufficient evidence in writing) indicating the imputation credits claimed.

The Commissioner has the power to disallow a credit of tax (in whole or in part) for imputation credits received if:

## the Commissioner is satisfied that the imputation credit claimed is in respect of insufficient income tax

or further income tax having been paid by the company that issued the credit, and

## it would be inappropriate for the person to be allowed the credit.

The disallowance is made in a manner that the Commissioner considers fair and equitable. However, if sufficient income tax or further income tax is subsequently paid by or on behalf of the company, the credit will be allowed to the extent of that payment.

Claims for credits of tax for imputation credits received that are in excess of the proper amount are not allowed to the extent of the excess.

## Refundable tax credits

Refundable tax credits include credits for PAYE, provisional tax paid, RWT, Working for Families tax credits, non-resident withholding tax (NRWT), RSCT, RLWT, Maori Authority tax credits, and certain tax credits in relation to multi-rate PIEs.

The Commissioner is required to apply the amount of any remaining refundable tax credits in accordance with specific ordering rules outlined in s LA 6(2).

## Ordering rules

With the exception of NRWT credits, tax credits under the Working for Families scheme, R&D loss tax credits under subpart MX, FamilyBoost tax credits, and tax credits for charitable donations, refundable tax credits must be applied in the following order:

## towards previous tax years' unpaid tax liability (if any) and, if this amount exceeds the tax credit

available, to the extent of the available credit

## towards unpaid tax liability for the following tax year or years (if any)

## towards unpaid provisional tax for the following tax year or years (if any)

## as tax paid in excess that can be transferred to other tax types of other taxpayers

## for a company using the accounting income method (AIM) of paying provisional tax, towards the

provisional tax liabilities of the AIM company's shareholders, and

## as a refund of tax, subject to ss RB 4, RM 2–RM 8, and RM 10.

Tax credits under the Working for Families scheme, R&D loss tax credits under subpart MX, FamilyBoost tax credits, and tax credits for charitable donations are either treated as refundable or as tax paid in excess which can be transferred.

NRWT credits must be treated, firstly, as tax paid in excess that can be transferred, and secondly, as refundable to the taxpayer.
