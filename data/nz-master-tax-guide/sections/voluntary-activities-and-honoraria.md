# Voluntary activities and honoraria ¶3-455

## General tax treatment

Generally amounts derived by a person from voluntary activities are taxable income to that person, unless the amounts received are reimbursement payments for expenditure actually incurred by the volunteer.

## Volunteers defined

Volunteers are defined as "people who freely undertake activity in New Zealand that has been chosen either by them or a group of which they are a member". The activity must provide a benefit to a community or to another person. The voluntary activity must be done with no intention of private pecuniary gain on the part of the volunteer. However, the voluntary activity can be done for a business that has profit as an objective.

## Reimbursement payments

Reimbursement payments to volunteers may be exempt income. For a reimbursement payment to be treated as tax-exempt the following provisions must be met:

## The payment must be based on actual expenditure incurred by the volunteer in undertaking the

voluntary activity.

## Payments based on an estimate of the expenditure incurred will also be treated as exempt income,

if the paying organisation has a process in place for making a reasonable estimate of the amount of expenditure likely to be incurred by volunteers for whom reimbursement is payable.

Non-cash reimbursements such as petrol vouchers or meal vouchers still qualify for the exemption.

Where a payment to a volunteer exceeds the actual cost incurred, the payer must treat the excess as an honorarium. An honorarium is a schedular payment from which tax must be deducted at the rate of 33% (Sch 4 pt B cl 1) (if the payee has not elected another rate and is not subject to a special or prescribed rate (¶3-044)).

Paying organisations may make combined payments to volunteers on condition that they:

## clearly identify which portion of the payment made to a volunteer is reimbursement

## correctly withhold tax from the honorarium portion, and

## return the honorarium as a schedular payment in their employment income information.

Example: Miriama is a voluntary regional coordinator for a national sports organisation, SportCo. Some travel to local centres, as well as attendance at tournaments is involved. Miriama uses her own car. SportCo pays Miriama an honorarium of $50 per month for six months of the year. SportCo estimates that Miriama travels an average of 250 kilometres per month in the course of her voluntary activities, including her travel to and from her home. SportCo decides to reimburse Miriama's travel expenses by paying her a travel allowance of 70 cents per kilometre. The honorarium and travel allowance are paid together. SportCo also gives Miriama meal vouchers to buy meals on the days that she has to attend tournaments. Miriama receives a total payment of $225 in each of the six months of the year. SportCo will deduct tax of $16.50 from the monthly payment of $225. The $50 honorarium is subject to withholding tax at the 0.33 rate, and the $50 is included as a schedular payment in the employer monthly schedule. SportCo will pay the $16.50 tax deduction to Inland Revenue. The $175 travel allowance is treated as exempt income. The meal vouchers fall within the scope of the exempt income treatment.

Example from Tax Information Bulletin ¶218-139 Vol 21, No 8, October/November 2009, Pt II at 119.

Local authorities

The Commissioner treats elected members as self-employed. Their remuneration is in the form of honoraria subject to a 33% withholding tax under Sch 4 pt B (if the payee has not elected another rate and is not subject to a special or prescribed rate (¶3-044)).

## Others

When honoraria are paid to a person who is a true employee of the payer, they will be regarded as employment income under s CE 1 and will be subject to PAYE and the ACC earners' levy. For honoraria paid to school trustees and Plunket members, see ¶3-457.

Expenditure incurred in the derivation of a schedular payment

The Commissioner may determine what amount or proportion of any particular schedular payment, or schedular payments of any specified class, shall be regarded as expenditure incurred in the derivation of the payment. A determination may be varied or revoked by the Commissioner at any time. The practical effect of the determination is an exemption from withholding tax for the amount determined (s RD 8(3)).

## Sporting event or competition prize money

Prize money paid to a resident or non-resident entertainer (collectively referred to as an "entertainer") at sporting events or competitions are generally subject to withholding tax at the rate of 20%.

A determination in relation to these payments provides that when an entertainer receives prize money in relation to a sporting event or competition of any nature (specified activity), the sum of $500 shall be regarded as expenditure incurred in the production of the payment (Tax Information Bulletin ¶95-104 Vol 9, No 5, May 1997 at 3). However, if the total amount of the prize money is less than $500, the total amount of the prize money is regarded as expenditure incurred in the production of the payment. The determination applies to each separate prize paid to each separate entertainer for each separate event that the person has entered at the sporting event or competition.

Example: Deborah and Jean compete as a pair in an aerobics competition. Deborah also competes in the women's singles category. Deborah and Jean win first prize of $900 in the women's pairs category. Deborah also wins first prize of $600 in the women's singles category. The total prize amount for the women's pairs category exceeds $500; however, the amount per person is under $500.

Therefore, no withholding tax is required to be deducted from the $900 payment and Deborah and Jean both receive $450 each.

With respect to the women's singles prize of $600, withholding tax must be deducted from $100 (being the difference between the amount of the prize of $600 and the exemption amount of $500). This amounts to withholding tax of $20. Deborah receives the net amount of $580 ($600 # $20).

Deborah has won total prize money of $1,050, but only $100 is subject to withholding tax. This is because the determination applies to each separate prize paid to each separate participant for each separate event.

## School trustees

Honoraria paid to school trustees are generally subject to tax withheld at the rate of 33%.

A related determination provides that when a trustee receives honoraria for attendance at a board meeting, the sum of $55 per member, or $75 for the chair, shall be regarded as expenditure incurred in the production of the payment (Tax Information Bulletin ¶137-114 Vol 13, No 7, July 2001 at 51–52). The maximum annual amount of the specified withholding payment must not exceed $605 per member, or $825 for the chair.

However, if a trustee receives any reimbursement (in addition to honoraria) for expenditure incurred in attending that meeting, the exempted amount ($55, or $75 for the chair) shall be reduced by the amount of that additional reimbursement.

Example: A member receives a payment of $90, made up of an honorarium of $70 and reimbursement for travel expenses of $20. The payer must deduct withholding tax from $35 (being honorarium of $70, reduced by the difference between the determined amount of $55 and the reimbursement payment of $20, ie $70 # ($55 # $20)).

Royal New Zealand Plunket Trust

Honoraria paid to members of the Royal New Zealand Plunket Trust (Plunket) are generally subject to withholding tax at the rate of 33%. Determination DET 18/02 provides that when a Plunket member receives honoraria as reimbursement of expenditure that member incurred in carrying out Plunket-related activities, that payment, up to a maximum of $800 pa, is regarded as expenditure incurred in the production of that payment (Tax Information Bulletin ¶311-108 Vol 31, No 1, February 2019 at 55). However, if the member receives any reimbursement (in addition to honoraria) for expenditure incurred, the exempted amount ($800) is reduced by the additional reimbursement.

## Daily allowances paid in the screen production industry

Determination DET 25/03 provides that up to $100 per day of a daily (or per diem) allowance ($80 before 1 July 2025) paid to contractors and entertainers working away from their town of normal residence in the screen production industry will be treated as expenditure incurred in the production of the schedular payment. Accordingly, no withholding tax is required to be deducted from this amount of the payment.

The exemption applies to resident and non-resident contractors and resident and non-resident entertainers.

The exemption will continue until the Commissioner varies or revokes the determination.

However, if the contractor/entertainer is also provided with goods and services for which the allowance is paid, either by the payer or another party acting on the payer's behalf, then no amount will be treated as expenditure incurred in deriving the schedular payment.

The amount of daily allowance paid must be included in the employment income information as part of gross earnings and/or schedular payments, even if no withholding tax has been deducted. See IR newsletter, Payroll News, Issue 56, September 2003.

Example: A contractor has been paid $1,550 for one day's work, and a daily allowance of $55. Withholding tax (at 20%) of $310 has been deducted from the $1,550. Because the allowance paid is less than $100, no withholding tax has been deducted from this payment.

On the employment income information, the gross amount of schedular payment is shown as $1,605 and the withholding tax is $310. When the contractor files her tax return at the end of the year, the $55 allowance can be claimed as an expense.
