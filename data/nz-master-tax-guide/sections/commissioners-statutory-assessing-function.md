# Commissioner's statutory assessing function ¶4-020

Two points should be noted in relation to the Commissioner's statutory assessing function (ie generally, to make default or amended assessments). First, when the Commissioner is discharging this statutory assessing function, the Commissioner is performing a duty imposed on them in imperative and unconditional terms. See C of IR v Lemmington Holdings Ltd (1982) 5 NZTC 61,268. Secondly, the Commissioner's statutory functions are directed to the quantification of the liability for tax which is imposed by the statute itself. The Commissioner may not be estopped by their past conduct from performing their statutory obligations in making assessments reflecting their current judgment as to that statutorily imposed liability.

See North Island Wholesale Groceries Ltd v Hewin (1982) 5 NZTC 61,289, Case S74 (1996) 17 NZTC 7,459, Golden Bay Cement Co Ltd v C of IR (1996) 17 NZTC 12,580.

Example: The making of an assessment, including an amended assessment, requires the exercise of judgment on the part of the Commissioner in quantifying that liability on the information then in the Commissioner's possession. It involves the ascertainment of the taxable income and of the resulting tax liability. A decision that is tentative, provisional, conditional or subject to adjustment is not an assessment within the meaning of the tax legislation. To constitute an assessment for income tax purposes, the Commissioner's decision must be definitive as to the liability of the taxpayer at the time it was made and final subject only to challenge through the objection process. See C of IR v Canterbury Frozen Meat Co Ltd (1994) 16 NZTC 11,150.

An assessment by the Commissioner is made as part of the care and management function imposed by ss 6 and 6A of the TAA (¶1-512).
