# Sustenance allowances ¶3-449

Sustenance allowances are tax free for the employee, when the employer has an established policy or practice of paying sustenance allowances and:

## the employee works a minimum of 7 hours on the day, and

## their employment requires them to:

## work outdoors and away from their employment base for most of the day, and

## undertake a long period of physical activity in travelling on foot or by bicycle, and

## it is not practicable for the employer to provide sufficient sustenance when the employee is working

outdoors, and

## the allowance recognises:

## the demanding physical nature of the employee's work, and

## that the employer would normally provide tea, coffee, water, or similar refreshments at the

employment base in the course of their business.

The exemption above was introduced primarily for the benefit of postal delivery workers who do not have the capacity to carry food and drinks with them due to carrying mail bags. These workers need to purchase items along their mail route to ensure they maintain their energy levels while delivering mail. It was recognised that these employees do not have access to employer-provided drink facilities that are normally available to employee at little or no cost.

Law: IT07 ss CW 17C(2), CX 19(1)(c)

## Health or safety payments [¶3-449a]

[¶3-449A]

From 1 April 2025, an amount that an employer pays to or on behalf of an employee in relation to the employee's health or safety is exempt income of the employee to the extent that the payment is for a health or safety benefit that would have been exempt from FBT under s CX 24 if the benefit had been a non-cash benefit provided to the employee. See ¶21-628 for commentary on the health or safety FBT exemptions.

This provision ensures that employees are not worse off if their employers take the reimbursement route for health or safety related costs.

Law: IT07, ss CW 17D, CX 24
