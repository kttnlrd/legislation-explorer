# Rent paid to partnership ¶23-134

An anti-avoidance provision applies to ensure that the amount of rent paid by a relative of a partner or a related company to a partnership is "adequate rent", and for such a transaction the partnership treated as opaque (s GC 5(2)(d)). An adequate rent is an amount that the Commissioner determines to be adequate for the relevant property (generally, a market rent). That amount is deemed to have been payable for the period specified in any relevant lease agreement or, if no rent is payable under the lease, for each day of the lease term on a pro rata basis.

The amount of deemed rental is income derived by the partnership. The Commissioner has issued a public binding ruling, BR Pub 06/02, "Section GD 10 [of the Income Tax Act 2004] — rent deemed to be payable by the lessee", which concludes that a corresponding deduction will be allowed to the lessee in terms of the general permission. The ruling applies for an indefinite period to leases entered into on or following 1 February 2006.

*Refs: s GC 5(2)(d), Section GD 10, Income Tax Act 2004*

Deductibility for partnership of interest paid to partner

The proper tax treatment of interest paid on capital contributed by a partner depends upon the sort of capital involved. This is because, under partnership law, only certain sorts of capital contributions are entitled to payment of interest. Section 47 of the Partnership Law Act 2019 provides that a partner is not entitled to interest on capital subscribed by them apart from interest at 5% pa (subject to any agreement between the partners) on any actual payment or advance beyond the capital that they have agreed to subscribe.

The Commissioner does not allow the partnership any deduction for interest paid on the partner's initial capital contribution. The Commissioner treats any such payment of "interest" as a distribution of profits and as not having the character of interest in the hands of the partner.

The Commissioner does allow the partnership a deduction for any interest paid on any loan or advance made to the partnership over and above the partner's initial capital contribution. For this purpose, a loan or advance also extends to any partnership profits that the partner has not drawn out but has left in with the partnership. The rate of interest that the partnership might pay can exceed the statutory 5% (s 47 of the Partnership Law Act 2019), if the parties agree otherwise. Moreover, since any such payment is recognised as truly a payment of interest, it retains that character in the hands of the partner.

*Refs: Section 47, s 47*

## Public rulings on interest deductibility for partnerships

The Commissioner has issued a series of public rulings applying the principles set down in the Australian decision FC of T v JD Roberts; FC of T v Smith 92 ATC 4380 (known as Roberts and Smith) to the deductibility of interest under s DB 6. In Roberts and Smith, interest on borrowings raised by a partnership to repay partnership working capital was allowed as a deduction, even though the repayment was able to be used by a partner to fund private expenditure. The character of the refinancing was the same as the original borrowing, which was that of the provision of working capital. It was held that the interest was deductible even though the borrowed funds were not used directly in deriving income. Capital contributions, past years' profits and debt were all considered capable of being replaced and repaid in this way. The case only applies where the amount replaced and repaid is owed to a person separate to the business, like a partner in a partnership or a shareholder of a company.

In BR Pub 15/04, "Interest deductibility — funds borrowed by a partnership to return capital contributions to a partner", the Commissioner allows a deduction for interest in respect of funds used by a partnership to return capital to partners who previously invested that capital. Any partner's share of the interest is deductible by that partner to the extent that the partner's capital contribution was used directly in the partnership's business or used to repay borrowed funds on which interest was deductible. That interest is not deductible to the extent that the borrowed funds are used by the partnership to pay current year income to the partner or are purported to be used to make a payment out of unrealised asset revaluations or internally generated goodwill.

In BR Pub 15/05, "Interest deductibility — funds borrowed by a partnership to return past years' profits to a partner", the Commissioner allows a deduction for interest in respect of funds used by a partnership to pay profits to partners, but only to the extent that the profits are past years' profits that were used directly in the partnership's business, or used to repay borrowed funds on which interest was deductible. That interest is not deductible to the extent that the borrowed funds are used by the partnership to pay current year income to the partner or are purported to be used to make a payment out of unrealised asset revaluations or internally generated goodwill.

In BR Pub 15/08, "Interest deductibility — funds borrowed to repay debt", the Commissioner allows a deduction for interest in respect of funds used by a partnership to repay borrowed funds to a person who invested those funds in the partnership in certain specified circumstances.

The rulings apply for an indefinite period from 24 May 2015.
