# Appeal to Court of Appeal ¶4-265

Either party may appeal to the Court of Appeal from a determination of the High Court when that court is the court of first instance, hearing a case under ss 24, 26 and 26A of the Taxation Review Authorities Act 1994.

The appeal may be on a question of fact, or law, or both. Either party may appeal to the Court of Appeal from a determination of the High Court on a case stated on a question of law by the TRA, whether the case was stated before the Authority's determination or afterwards on an appeal. An appeal lies to the Court of Appeal on questions of law, or fact, or both, from a determination of the High Court on an appeal from the Authority on a question of fact or fact and law. However, the Court of Appeal will be reluctant to consider fresh points.

*Refs: ss 24*

Example: In a case concerning a partner's entitlement to an interest deduction, the Commissioner was prevented by the Court of Appeal from raising an argument regarding the nexus between the interest expense and the production of assessable income not raised previously in the lower courts. See Finnigan v C of IR (1995) 17 NZTC 12,170 (CA).

## Costs

In any proceeding on objection before the courts, the courts may award costs against either party. This includes proceedings on a reference by the Authority on a point of law.

## Time limits

Either party wishing to appeal a High Court decision to the Court of Appeal must notify the High Court in writing within 20 working days of the date the High Court delivers its decision.
