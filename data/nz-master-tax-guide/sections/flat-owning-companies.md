# Flat-owning companies ¶24-060

A flat-owning company normally derives income in the form of subscriptions or levies imposed upon shareholders. This income is applied to meet administration and management costs, as well as depreciation, repairs and maintenance and other outgoings on the property. As a matter of practice, the Commissioner of Inland Revenue (Commissioner) does not assess a flat-owning company on income of this nature. The same practice applies to surplus contributions held in suspense to meet the outgoings to be satisfied in future years. A flat-owning company remains assessable on income not raised by way of subscription to meet outgoings on the property. This means that the company would be assessable on any rents and investment income.

## Dividend exemption

Making company property available for the use of any shareholder for inadequate consideration is generally a dividend for tax purposes. However, a dividend does not arise when a flat-owning company makes residential property available to its shareholders. To be eligible for the exemption, the company's constitution must provide for the entitlement of each shareholder to the use of residential property, and the company's assets must be restricted to residential property and funds reserved for maintenance and other outgoings on the property. Natural persons, trustee and company shareholders are all entitled to the exemption (Tax Information Bulletin ¶79-101 Vol 7, No 9, February 1996 at 1).

## Qualifying company regime

A flat-owning company could be a qualifying company (for companies electing before 1 April 2011).

However, a flat-owning company is not eligible to be a look-through company (¶19-165).
