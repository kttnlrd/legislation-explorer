# Cross-border employees ¶3-367

## Who is a cross-border employee?

From 1 April 2023, s CE 1F provides specific treatment for cross-border employees receiving PAYE income payments. A "cross-border employee" is defined as:

## an employee of a non-resident employer (if the employee provides services in New Zealand)

## a resident employee (if the person provides services outside New Zealand).

The position is not affected if the employee has left New Zealand when a payment is made, provided the services were provided in New Zealand. The definition includes a secondee or a person who provides a service for or on behalf of a person who is not resident in New Zealand.

The tax residence rules for natural persons are important when determining the application of these rules (¶1-120).

For a cross-border employee who remains on the employer's payroll in a country outside New Zealand, the PAYE income payment is derived by the person on the 20th day after payment, if the employer has chosen to report the payment as employment income for a shadow payroll employee (¶3-023).

If tax is not paid or is underpaid, the employee must perform the relevant tax obligations as if they were an employer and may pay the initial amount of tax as a lump sum. This applies to PAYE income payments, FBT (¶21-010) and employer superannuation contributions (¶3-101).

60-day grace period From 1 April 2024, a 60-day grace period is allowed for the performance of employment-related duties that have been omitted in respect of a cross-border employee. However, regular anticipated salary and wage expenses generally do not qualify for this concession.

Section CE 1F(3C) allows for a 60-day grace period during which the employer must make reasonable efforts to meet or correct their tax obligations for PAYE income payments, employer superannuation cash contributions or fringe benefits provided to the employee for the time the employee was in New Zealand. The grace period is also available to other persons subject to these tax obligations.

The grace period applies when:

## the employer (or other person) must have taken reasonable measures to manage their employment-

related tax obligations, and

## an unexpected PAYE income payment is made to a New Zealand resident person while that person

is working outside New Zealand, or

## the employee, while present in New Zealand, receives an unexpected PAYE income payment,

breaches a threshold under a double tax agreement or breaches a threshold under s CW 19 (¶3-291).

The 60-day grace period begins on the earlier of:

## the date of the breach or payment, as appropriate

## the date on which the employer could reasonably foresee that a breach or a payment, will occur.

The grace period should apply in the following situations:

## New Zealand tax resident employees working overseas but it is not possible to meet the strict timing

requirement of New Zealand's employment-related tax rules

## The intention is that the employee's New Zealand tax residency will cease but, due to subsequent

events, this does not occur

## There is employee share scheme income, the employer has elected to withhold and pay tax, however

the ordinary timeframe for compliance is unable to be met

## There is a trailing bonus and the ordinary timeframe for tax compliance is unable to be met

## an employee manages their own tax affairs under IR56 arrangements (¶3-303), or

## another person undertakes employment-related tax obligations on the employer's behalf (eg where

employees' taxes are withheld, paid or reported through the New Zealand payroll of an associated entity.)

## Bespoke PAYE arrangement

From 1 April 2024 an employer may apply to the Commissioner to enter into a bespoke annual arrangement in respect of a class of cross-border employees under s RA 15(4B). If special circumstances exist, the Commissioner may agree that the payment of tax for PAYE income payments made to the class of cross- border employees in a tax year be deferred to the 31 May following the end of the relevant tax year.

The employer must establish that special circumstances exist before such an arrangement will be granted.

IR will provide guidance on what constitutes "special circumstances" in due course.

Where a bespoke arrangement has been entered into, employers must report the associated employment income information on an annual basis, that is, by 31 May following the end of the relevant tax year. The usual deadlines for reporting the information under payday filing (¶3-020) will not apply.
