# Permitted disclosure to reduce or remove harm ¶1-638

A permitted disclosure of sensitive revenue information may be made if disclosure is necessary to prevent or lessen a serious threat to public health or public safety, or to the life or health of a person: s 18J of the TAA.

A serious threat is defined as a threat that the Commissioner reasonably believes to be serious having regard to—

## the likelihood of the threat being realised

## the severity of the consequences if the threat is realised, and

## the time at which the threat may be realised.

The Commissioner has confirmed that a disclosure of this type must only be made to the person, body or agency that is able to do something to prevent or lessen the threat.

*Refs: s 18J*

Example: An IR staff member is speaking with a customer on the phone. During the call the customer makes comments and threats leading the staff member to think that the customer may harm themselves or others. In this situation information could be disclosed to the Police so they can take appropriate steps to prevent the harm.

Example taken from Tax Information Bulletin ¶314-102 Vol 31 No 4, May 2019 at 51.

Law: TAA ss 16C(3), 18J

*Refs: ss 16C(3)*

Permitted disclosure: misapplication of charitable funds

The Commissioner may disclose to the Minister of Revenue instances of the application of funds by a gift- exempt body for a purpose that is not of a charitable, benevolent, philanthropic or cultural nature.

Grounds for such a disclosure exist when the Commissioner has reason to believe funds might be or are being applied for a purpose other than one referred to above. The name of any person who has made a donation to the gift-exempt body must not be revealed to the Minister.

The nature of a charitable purpose is discussed at ¶24-195.

Law: TAA s 18K
