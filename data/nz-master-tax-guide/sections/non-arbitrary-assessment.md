# Non-arbitrary assessment ¶4-030

When making an assessment, the Commissioner is not entitled to act arbitrarily in disregard of the law or the facts as known to them. If their assessment is not made on an intelligible basis, it may not stand.

Example: In formulating an assessment, the Commissioner might be required to exercise their judgment in determining the assessable income of the taxpayer. The taxpayer would be able to discharge the onus of proof if they could show that the Commissioner's assessment was no more than arbitrary conjecture or was demonstrably unfair, or was unsupportable for lack of any sensible basis. See Lowe v C of IR (1981) 5 NZTC 61,006.

Example: Three alternative notices of amended assessment issued simultaneously to a taxpayer were void. The Commissioner had issued the assessments because they considered that there were at least 3 ways of calculating the tax benefit received by the taxpayer. In judicial review proceedings, the Federal Court held that the assessments were invalid because the taxpayer's liability was not fixed.

See Stokes v FC of T 96 ATC 4393.

Example: A rigid following of IR policy, and a failure by the Commissioner to properly consider and weigh the merits of a taxpayer's position, led to the Court of Appeal finding that the Commissioner's decision not to exercise their discretion and make certain adjustments and issue reassessments was not a valid exercise of that discretion. See Lawton v C of IR (2003) 21 NZTC 18,042.
