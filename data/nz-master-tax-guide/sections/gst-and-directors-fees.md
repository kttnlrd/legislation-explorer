# GST and directors' fees ¶32-635

A "taxable activity" is defined in s 6 of the GST Act (¶32-024). Excluded from the definition are the activities of a person in relation to any engagement, occupation or employment under any contract of service or as a director of a company (s 6(3)(b)). However, the exclusion does not apply to a person who accepts any office in carrying on any taxable activity. In this case, the services supplied as the holder of that office are deemed to be supplied in the course or furtherance of that taxable activity. Therefore, if a GST-registered sole trader, in carrying on their taxable activity, accepts a company directorship, s 6(5) applies and GST is chargeable on the director's fees paid.

In addition, s 6(4) provides that if an employee is engaged by a third party to be a director or other person referred to in s 6(3)(c)(iii) (eg chairperson or member of any local authority, statutory board, council, committee or other body) and the employee is required to account to their employer for any payment received, the payment will be treated as consideration for a supply of services by the employer to the third party. The employer will therefore account for GST and the third party will be able to claim an input tax deduction. From 30 March 2025, amendments have been made to allow GST-registered organisations to deduct input tax on fees paid to a board member who was appointed by the Governor-General or the Governor-General in Council and accounts to their employer for those fees.

Operational statement, OP 23/02, "Commissioner's operational position on professional directors and board members incorrectly registered for GST" which gives guidance on how the 3 public binding ruling (see below) which were issued in relation to these taxpayers will be applied. The Commissioner is of the view that some directors may be incorrectly registered for GST and has advised that these directors must deregister with effect from 30 June 2023, or such other date as determined by the Commissioner. Deregistration will have an effect for those directors that have acquired assets as part of their taxable activity (¶32-149). The statement notes that IR has concluded that a person who provides only directorship services is not eligible to be registered for GST. A similar view is stated for board members.

OP 23/02 states that directors and board members operating through personal services companies (ie not in their personal capacity) are able to register the personal service company as long as the personal service company's level of activity is sufficient to be a taxable activity as defined in s 6(1) of the GST Act. This is because the Commissioner views the supply of directorship services as distinct from the supply of personally being a director. See commentary on QB 23/07 below.

If a professional director determines that they are incorrectly registered for GST, deregistration can take place by utilising the "cancel your GST registration" process in myIR, or by completion of an IR315 (business cessation form).

*Refs: s 6, s 6(3)(b), s 6(5), s 6(4), s 6(3)(c)(iii), s 6(1)*

## Public binding rulings issued

## BR Pub 23/01 - Directors' fees

On 22 February 2023, IR released 3 public binding rulings relating to directors' fees. The rulings apply from 1 April 2023 and give directors a brief period to rectify their GST position, if they fall within any of the ruling scenarios. The binding rulings are stated to be a reissue of BR Pub 15/10.

Public binding ruling, BR Pub 23/01, "Goods and Services Tax – Directors' fees", considers the application of GST in relation to the payment of fees to a director of a company. The ruling applies to arrangements where the engagement, occupation or employment of a person is as director of a company. A number of contractual arrangements are covered by the ruling. They are:

## direct contract between the company and the director

## indirect contract where the company contracts with:

## a third party

## the director's employer, or

## a partnership of which the director is a partner.

Directors may have a taxable activity in another capacity, like where they are an accountant or lawyer and run an accounting or legal business. If a director has not accepted the office of director in the course of the taxable activity, the appointment as director is excluded from the term "taxable activity" under s 6(3)(b), and s 6(5) does not apply. In this case, the company is not able to claim an input tax credit for any directors' fee paid.

Where the director has accepted the office in carrying on the director's taxable activity, then:

## the activity is specifically excluded under s 6(3)(b). However, s 6(5) will apply and the services will be

deemed to be supplied in the course or furtherance of the director's taxable activity. As a result:

## if the director is registered (or liable to be registered) for GST, the director is required to account for

GST on fees received for the supply of directorship services, and

## if the company the director is contracted to is registered for GST, the company can claim a deduction

for input tax under s 20(3) for any GST charged on the directorship services.

## Third party contracts with company to provide director services

Binding ruling BR Pub 23/01 also covers arrangements where a third-party contracts with a company to provide director's services and the person accepts the position, but not as part of carrying on a taxable activity (eg an accountant accepts the role of director, but unrelated to their accounting practice). In this case:

## The director's engagement falls within the exclusion in s 6(3)(b) and is therefore not a taxable activity.

Section 6(5) does not apply because the director did not accept the position within a taxable activity.

## Section 6(3)(b) does not apply to the third party's provision of director services because that third

party is not engaged as the director.

## If the third party is registered for GST (or liable to be registered for GST), the third party must account

for GST charged on the supply of services provided by the director.

## The company can claim a GST input tax credit.

If the third-party contracts with the company to provide director services, and the director accepts the office while carrying on the director's taxable activity, then:

## the director's engagement falls within the exclusion under s 6(3)(b), but because the director is

executing the office in the course of their taxable activity s 6(5) applies.

In this case there are 2 supplies for GST purposes. They are:

## the director providing their services to the third party (Supply 1)

## the third party providing the director's services to the company (Supply 2).

For Supply 1: the director must account for GST charged on the supply of their services and if the third party is carrying on a taxable activity, it can claim a GST input tax credit.

For Supply 2: If the third party is registered for GST, the third party is required to account for GST. Section 6(3)(b) does not apply. If the company is carrying on a taxable activity and is GST registered, the company may claim a GST input tax credit for GST charged on the director's services.

## Director's services, as employee, are contracted by employer to the company

Where a director is engaged, as part of their employment (under a contract of services), the director's engagement is specifically excluded by s 6(3)(b) the office is accepted as part of their employment contract.

The employer, if registered for GST, is required to charge GST on the supply of those services under s 8.

The company receiving the director's services, can claim a GST input deduction if it carries on a taxable activity and is registered for GST.

## Director engages with company directly and is employee obliged to account to

employer Where there is no contract between a company and an employer, and the employee contracts directly with a company to provide director services, the employment agreement needs to be checked. If there is an agreement that the employee is obliged to account to their employer for the director's fees received, s 6(4) will apply. This section states that despite the exclusion under s 6(3)(b) any fees paid by the company to the director are treated as consideration for the supply of services by the employer. If the employer is GST registered, the employer must account for GST on that supply. The company can claim a deduction for input tax on the deemed supply if it is registered for GST.

Director services provided by partner to company in a partnership where there is an obligation to account If a director is a partner in a partnership and accepts the office as part of the partnership business, the director is obliged to account to the partnership for the director's fees received. In this case, s 57(2)(b) applies and the partnership is deemed to supply the services. If the partnership is GST registered, it must charge GST on the supply and the company can claim a GST input tax credit if it is GST registered.

In summary, BR Pub 23/01 provides that where a director accepts the position as an employee or a partner, the director does not return GST on their director's fee. The employer or partnership are to return GST.

## BR Pub 23/02 – Board members' fees (not appointed by the Governor-General)

Public Binding Ruling BR Pub 23/02, "Goods and Services Tax – Fees of Board Members not appointed by the Governor-General or Governor-General in Council" applies to arrangements that are the engagement, occupation or employment of a person as chairperson or member of a local authority, board, council, committee, or other body (collectively referred to in the ruling as the organisation). It covers engagement where the board member contracts directly with the organisation, and where there is an agreement between the organisation and:

## a third party

## the board member's employer, or

## a partnership of which the board member is a partner.

If the board member has not accepted the office in the course of carrying on a taxable activity, then the engagement is excluded from the term "taxable activity" by operation of s 6(3)(c)(ii). In turn, the organisation cannot claim an input tax credit for any fees paid to the board member.

However, if the board member accepted the office in the course of carrying on their taxable activity, s 6(5) will deem the services to be supplied in the course or furtherance of their taxable activity. If the board member is registered for GST, or liable to be registered, the board member is required to account for GST on the supply of those services. The organisation can then claim the GST input tax under s 20(3) for any GST charged on those services.

## BR Pub 23/03 – Board members' fees (persons appointed by the Governor-General)

Public Binding Ruling BR Pub 23/03, "Goods and Services Tax – Fees of Board Members appointed by the Governor-General or Governor-General in Council" was issued by IR and states that if a person is appointed

as a Chairperson, or board member of a local authority, board, council, committee or other body (referred to as the Organisation in the ruling) by the Governor-General, the board member's engagement is excluded from the term taxable activity under s 6(3)(c)(iia). The result is that even if the Organisation is registered for GST, an input tax credit cannot be claimed.

If the board member is appointed by the Governor-General, or Governor-General in Council, where:

## the board member is an employee who is required to account to the employer for the board member

fees, and

## there is no contract between the employer and the Organisation

then:

## the board member's engagement is excluded under s 6(3)(c)(iia) and 6(5) does not apply to these

services.

## s 6(4) does not apply to s 6(3)(c)(iia) either

## the organisation is unable to claim a GST input tax credit.

In contrast, where the board member is a partner in a partnership and is obliged to account to the partnership for board member fees received, the board member's services are deemed to be supplied by the partnership under s 57(2)(b). In this case, if the partnership is registered for GST, or liable to register for GST, the partnership must account for GST output tax on the supply of the board member's services.

The organisation can claim a GST input deduction for the GST on the board member services as s 57(2)(b) operates to similar effect to s 6(4). According to the ruling, s 6(3)(c)(iia) does not apply to the partnership's provision of board member services because the partnership is not engaged to provide the services.

## Provision of directors' services through a personal services

company QWBA QB 23/07, "GST – Directors and board members providing their services through a personal services company" considers the GST treatment of services rendered by a director or board member who provides their services through a personal services company. The question posed was if a director provides their services through a personal services company will the company be able to register for GST when the director would not be able to register if they were providing their services in their capacity as a natural person? The item concludes that, yes, a personal services company contracted to supply such services can be registered for GST, despite a person being disallowed to where such a natural person rendered the same services without the envelope of the corporate structure. The answer is based on the fact that those supplies of directorship services are not excluded from the definition of "taxable activity" by s 6(3)(b) (or s 6(3)(c)(iii) for board members). IR views the provision of director services by a personal services company to differ from the provision of director services by a natural person. This is because the personal services company is supplying the services of a person to the company to fulfil the director role, and this type of supply is not excluded by the sections above.

The situation is less certain when the director contracts directly with the company to be a director but where the director is obliged to account for the directorship fees to the personal services company because the personal services company is the employer of the director. In cases like this, where the director personally enters the contracts directly with companies, s 6(3)(b) will exclude each office held from the definition of "taxable activity" which will result in the director not having a taxable activity relating to their services as a director. Where the director is an employee of their personal services company and is obliged to account to for fees received from the directorships under the employment contract, s 6(4) applies. This means the accounting of fees to the employer is treated as consideration for a supply of services by the employer to the company that made the payment to the director. Section 6(4) deems the employee's payment to the personal services company as consideration for the supply of services to the company. The uncertainty lies in how a court would apply the law and whether application of s 6(4) would result in an interpretation that the personal services company is supplying the services of the director but without the formal contractual relationship between the 2 companies. The alternative proposition would be that the personal services company would not charge GST and the company would not take an input tax deduction. The Commissioner

states in the item that they will be satisfied, on balance of probabilities, that the personal services company can registered for GST as long as the personal services company can prove that the supply of director services is carried on continuously and regularly as required by s 6(1). See QB 23/07 for more detail.
