# Adjustments required when balance date changed ¶2-075

## Average rate of tax

Upon the adoption of a new balance date, a return will be prepared for a period of more or less than 12 months. The taxpayer must calculate an average tax rate, and this is used as the basic tax rate for calculating the person's income tax liability for the transitional year. The calculation of this average tax rate is based on an annualised amount of business taxable income plus other income, such as wages. This annualisation is necessary because a transition year will either be shorter or longer than a 12-month period.

A person must determine:

## the amount of income from activities (net of allowable deductions, if any) that are unrelated to the

changed balance date

## the annualised amount of income (net of allowable deductions) from those business activities for

which the Commissioner has agreed the new balance date, and

## the average tax rate on the aggregate of those 2 amounts.

## Example: Transition year greater than 12 months

Bob, a partner in Bobbin & Co, sought a change in the balance date for the partnership from 31 March to 31 May. Approval for the change was given on 14 Dec 2019 for the 2020 tax year. A return of income is filed for the period 1 April 2019 to 31 May 2020 (427 days, as 2020 is a leap year).

Bob's share of the partnership income for the 14 months to 31 May 2020 (2020 tax year) is $100,000. Unrelated to the change in balance date, he also derives interest and dividends for the 12 months to 31 March 2020 (2020 tax year) of $200,000.

Bob must calculate the average tax rate on the following amount: $200,000 + ($100,000 × 366 ÷ 427) = $285,714 Using the marginal rates of tax from Sch 1 of the IT07 (based on the rates for the 2017–18 tax year and assuming no change in rates), the income tax payable on this annualised amount of taxable income for the 2020 tax year is $85,205.62.

The basic tax rate is calculated as equal to the average rate of tax on that annualised income, as follows: $85,205.62 ÷ $285,714 = 29.82% The income tax liability for the 2020 tax year is therefore calculated using this basic tax rate: ($200,000 + $100,000) × 29.82% = $89,460 In the absence of s 39, the income tax liability on $300,000 would be $89,920 (ie overtaxed).

Example adapted from Tax Information Bulletin ¶295-106 Vol 29, No 5, June 2017 at 157.

## Overseas income

New Zealand resident persons may elect to return certain overseas income in the New Zealand tax year in which their balance date in the overseas jurisdiction falls. See ¶26-032.

## Adjustments where no change of balance date

The provisions of s 39 of the TAA do not apply if there has not been a change of balance date in terms of s 38 of the Act. For example, the first return of income of a person who has not previously been required to file a tax return and carries on business is made from the commencement of that business to the following 31 March or other approved balance date. Although such returns are for a period of less than 12 months, the Commissioner cannot apply the provisions of s 39 to reduce the annual tax rate for income.

Also, if a balance date other than 31 March has been approved for a person who has never been liable to furnish returns, s 39 cannot be applied to adjust the tax rate. That section only applies to changes of existing balance dates. A person who has not previously been required to file a tax return does not have an existing balance date to change.
