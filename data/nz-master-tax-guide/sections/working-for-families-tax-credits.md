# Working for families tax credits ¶50-041

Family scheme is available to low- to middle-income earning families with dependent children under the "Working for Families Tax Credits" package. It comprises the following tax credits, collectively known as WFF tax credits:

## family tax credit — this is a credit of tax paid for each dependent child

## in-work tax credit — this credit is available to sole parents who work (before 1 July 2020, at least 20

hours a week) and couples that work (before 1 July 2020, at least 30 hours a week between them) and who do not receive a main benefit

## minimum family tax credit — this credit ensures a minimum gross family income for those families

falling below its threshold, and

## Best Start tax credit — this credit provides a weekly amount per child to help with the costs in a

child's early years.

The family tax credit and the in-work tax credit are grouped together and abated as a family's net income (family scheme income) increases, giving what the legislation refers to as an "abating WFF tax credit". Note that the Best Start tax credit also abates, but only in the second and third years, and it is not included within an "abating WFF tax credit".

## Abating WFF tax credit

The available abating WFF tax credit for an entitlement period is calculated under the following formula: family tax credit + in-work tax credit # credit abatement These credits may be paid either fortnightly into the principal caregivers' bank account or applied as a tax credit at year-end.

## Family tax credit

The family tax credit is calculated as follows: prescribed amount × days ÷ 365 The prescribed amounts for the 2024–25 and later tax years are:

## for the eldest dependent child, $7,524 per year, and

## for additional dependent children, $6,103 per year.

## In-work tax credit

Note: The in-work tax credit is impacted by the mid-year legislative increases to thresholds.

The in-work tax credit is calculated, for 1 April 2024 to 30 July 2024, as follows: ($3,770 + $780 × (children # 3)) × weekly periods ÷ 52 From 31 July 2024 to 31 March 2025, the in-work tax credit is calculated as follows: ($5,070 + $780 × (children # 3)) × weekly periods ÷ 52 "Children" is the greater of 3 or the number of children in relation to whom the principal caregiver is entitled to the in-work tax credit.

Credit abatement

The credits referred to above abate when family scheme income reaches a certain level.

Family credit abatement The formula for the family credit abatement is as follows: full-year abatement × days in entitlement period excluding months when protected family support recipient ÷ 365 where "full-year abatement" is:

## if the person has no spouse, civil union partner, or de facto partner during the entitlement period and

the person's family scheme income for a specified period containing the entitlement period is more than $42,700, 27 cents for each complete dollar of the excess, or

## if the person has a spouse, civil union partner, or de facto partner during the entitlement period and

the person's family scheme income, the family scheme income of the person's spouse, civil union partner, or de facto partner, or the sum of those incomes, as the case may be, for a specified period containing the entitlement period is more than $42,700, 27 cents for each complete dollar of the excess.

The family credit abatement is applied:

## first, to reduce the amount of family tax credit, and

## second, to reduce the in-work tax credit.

Note: Effective from 1 April 2026, the abatement threshold has been increased from $42,700 to $44,900 and the abatement rate will increase from 27% to 27.5%.

## Minimum family tax credit

Note: The threshold increased from $35,204 to $35,316 from 1 July 2024. This means that there will be an effective composite rate if a person receives a family tax credit throughout the 2024–2025 income year.

The minimum family tax credit applies to ensure a minimum after-tax family income that then abates once prescribed levels of income are reached. The formula for calculating the minimum family tax credit for working families in period from 1 April 2024 to 30 June 2024 is as follows— ($35,204 # net family scheme income) × number of one-week periods as a full-time earner ÷ 52 and from 1 July 2024 to 31 March 2025, it is as follows: ($35,316 # net family scheme income) × number of one-week periods as a full-time earner ÷ 52 For the 2023–24 tax year, the minimum family tax credit threshold amount was $34,216.

## Best Start tax credit

The Best Start tax credit applies from 1 July 2018 and replaced the parental tax credit. It is a payment of up to $3,838 a year (or $73.81 per week) per child to help families with the costs in a child's early years, until the child turns 3. It is available to all families in the first year of a child's life. For the second and third years, support continues for low- and middle-income families, with the credit abating at 21 cents for every dollar that exceeds $79,000.

From 1 April 2024, the Best Start tax credit increased from $3,632 a year to $3,838 a year.
