# GST adjustments for mixed-use assets under the Income Tax Act 2007 ¶32-089

## Note: The mixed-use asset rules are repealed from 1 April 2024. The repeal of ss 20(3JB) and 20G has

effect from the registered person's first adjustment period beginning on or after 1 April 2024. If, on 31 March 2024, a registered person has begun an adjustment period for a mixed-use asset to which s 20G applies, they will continue to use s 20G until the end of that adjustment period. They will begin to use the ordinary adjustment rules under ss 21 and 21A at their first adjustment period beginning on or after 1 April 2024.

Subpart DG of the IT07 deals with certain mixed-use assets (¶10-035). These are assets that are partly used for business purposes, partly used for private purposes and partly unused during an income year. The rules apply to land, including buildings, boats and aircraft, although only boats and aircraft with a cost of $50,000 or more are included. The rules deal with the apportionment of deductions for expenditure incurred on these mixed-use assets. Where an asset is unused for part of a period, the difficulty is whether expenditure incurred during that period relates to the income-earning or private use of the asset. For example, for a holiday home that is rented out part of the time, used by the owners for part of the time and left vacant part of the time, how should expenditure incurred during the time the home is vacant and general repairs and maintenance expenditure be treated. The same issue arose for GST for apportioning between taxable and non-taxable use in these circumstances. Changes were therefore made to the GST Act consistent with those made in the Income Tax Act for mixed-use assets. These changes ensured that asset owners that were registered for GST were able to claim input tax deductions in a similar way as they would be able to claim income tax deductions for the same item.

Sections 20(3JB)(b) and 20G required adjustments to be made in later periods for any subsequent change of use.

Some GST-specific rules catered for the fact that:

## GST has always recognised that supplies can be made for below-market value to non-associates

## an asset will have a GST component that will need to be apportioned over the ownership period

(whereas for income tax purposes this would be capital expenditure)

## some items of expenditure relevant for the income tax calculation are not relevant for GST (such as

interest), and

## GST is not calculated on an annual basis.

Owners of mixed-use assets are required to apportion their input deductions in a way that reflects their relative taxable and non-taxable use of the asset. The formula used for calculating GST deductions in s 20G incorporated the income tax definitions as far as possible (in an attempt to keep the compliance costs down).

The main differences between the income tax and GST definitions related to:

## the treatment of supplies for less than market value, and

## what constituted "expenditure".

These differences reflected the different nature of the 2 taxes. The GST formula replaced "expenditure" with "input tax".

Note that for the purposes of s 20G, a registered person did not include a widely-held company — s 20G(8).

A "widely-held company" is defined in s YA 1 of the IT07 as a company that, at the time, has no less than 25 shareholders (treating all associated shareholders as one person), and is not a closely-held company.

*Refs: ss 20(3JB), s 20G, ss 21, s 20G(8), s YA 1*

## Link with apportionment rules

The apportionment rules (¶32-088) require registered persons to apportion input tax in accordance with the taxable and non-taxable use of the supply. As mixed-use assets are also used partly for private and

partly for business purposes, the apportionment rules also apply to expenditure on these assets. To this end, the definitions used in the apportionment rules — "percentage intended use", "percentage actual use", "percentage difference" and "previous actual use" — were extended to apply to the mixed-use asset formula in s 20G.

Section 20G apportioned input tax on expenditure that a person incurred in the use of an asset to which s DG 3 of the IT07 applies. The formula apportioned input tax deductions based on the proportion of income- earning days in the GST period relative to the total number of days in the period that the asset was in active use.

As with the general apportionment rules and the formula used for income tax, s 20G required a registered person to perform annual calculations to determine the level to which they could claim input tax deductions.

Section 20G then required the registered person to pay any output tax or claim input tax on any positive or negative adjustment produced by the formula.

As with the income tax rules, no apportionment was required for input tax that related solely to the taxable use of an asset and no deduction was available for amounts that related solely to the non-taxable use of the asset.

## Applying the formula

One issue specific to GST is that GST is not generally calculated on an annual basis. GST-registered owners of mixed-use assets were therefore required to file returns on a monthly, 2-monthly or 6-monthly basis.

The total income-earning days referred to in the formula in s 20G was the total number of days in the period on which the person supplied the asset for use and derived consideration for the supply, whether at, above or below market value, including days on which:

## the asset was used to derive income for a particular period and the asset was used during the period

solely in the ordinary course of business

## the asset was being repaired for damage incurred while used to earn income

## the asset was relocated to earn income and the amount derived from the use of the asset included an

amount payable for the cost of relocation

## the asset had become unavailable for use because another person who had earlier reserved the

asset for their own use subsequently did not take advantage of that reservation, or

## a fringe benefit tax liability arose.

Sections 20(3JB)(b) and 20G required a person to perform the calculation at the end of an adjustment period, as defined. This was usually an annual period. However, the rules required the registered person to estimate their taxable use of a supply in the intervening periods and calculate their actual taxable use at the end of each adjustment period. This required a comparison between the level of anticipated private use of an asset and the actual private use over an adjustment period (s 20G(5)). This wash-up calculation determined the person's true tax position for each of the taxable periods within the adjustment period.

To ease the compliance burden on registered persons, the rules required input tax in the adjustment to be aggregated. Only if the estimate deductions were 10 percentage points or greater than the actual taxable use (or less than 10 percentage points but more than $1,000) was a wash-up necessary (s 20G(6)).

An alternative approach, which ensured greater accuracy but might reduce cash flow, would be for the registered person to delay claiming input deductions in the intervening periods and instead claim their annual entitlement at the end of each adjustment period when the calculation was performed. (Section 20(3) allows deductions from output tax to be claimed any time up to the second anniversary of the relevant supply.)

## Disposal

Section 20G(7) provided that ss 8 and 21F applied and the disposal of the relevant asset by a registered person was a taxable supply. This meant that output tax was payable on the disposal and a registered person was able to apply the s 21F formula to claim additional input tax.
