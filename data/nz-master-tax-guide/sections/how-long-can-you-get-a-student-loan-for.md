# How long can you get a student loan for? ¶8-022

Each new course requires a new loan and loan accounts run for 52 weeks.

There is a lifetime limit of 7 EFTS for student loans.

Full-time students usually have study loads of between 0.8 EFTS and 1.2 EFTS per a year. For a full-time student, 7 EFTS is equal to about 7 or 8 years of study. Part-time students use less EFTS each year.

Once any part of the student loan is used for living costs, fees or course-related costs, the EFTS for that loan will count towards the 7-EFTS limit.

A student can use more than 7 EFTS (eg post-graduate study); however, most students will not be able to receive more than 10 EFTS of student loan entitlements.

New Zealand-based borrowers under student loan scheme

Different repayment obligations apply to borrowers depending on whether they are based in New Zealand or overseas (¶8-040 and ¶8-045).

A borrower will be treated as New Zealand-based where they are physically in New Zealand (or treated as being physically in New Zealand) for a period of 183 consecutive days. If a borrower leaves the country during the 183-day period they will remain New Zealand-based provided they are not absent for more than a total of 31 days and they are physically present on the first day of that 183-day period.

Under s 22(2) a period of 183 consecutive days may include any days before the day on which a person becomes a borrower.

A borrower is treated as being New Zealand-based from the later of the day they became a borrower and the first day of the 183-day period.

*Refs: s 22(2)*

## Borrowers treated as being physically in New Zealand

A borrower may be treated as being physically present in New Zealand if the Commissioner considers it is fair and reasonable to do so because the borrower: (a) is in the service of the New Zealand Government (b) is working as a volunteer or for token payment for a charity (c) has an unexpected delay (d) has an unplanned personal absence (e) is required to be overseas because of the borrower's employment or occupation (f) is accompanying their spouse, civil union or de facto partner overseas (g) is undertaking overseas study or an internship that meets the criteria of cl 7, 8, 9, 9A or 9B of Sch 1 of the SLSA 2011 (h) is in Niue, the Cook Islands, Tokelau or the Ross Dependency, or (i) has a serious illness as defined in s 25(4) and is unable to meet their repayment obligations.

Other than for the serious illness exception, the specific criteria that must be satisfied for the above exceptions are detailed in Sch 1 of the SLSA 2011 (¶8-130–¶8-165).

Interpretation statement, IS 25/20, "Student Loans – Overseas borrowers and their obligations" sets out the rules for determining when a borrower will be considered as a New Zealand-based or an overseas-based borrower and includes illustrative examples. The statement also discusses the significance of tax residency when determining the status of the borrower.

An overseas-based borrower who is physically in New Zealand must, as soon as practicable after becoming New Zealand-based or aware they will be New Zealand-based, notify the Commissioner of the date they returned to New Zealand and provide supporting documentation to the Commissioner to determine whether the borrower should now be treated as New Zealand-based (s 29).

Overseas-based borrowers under student loan scheme

A person will be overseas-based if they are not treated as being New Zealand-based. A New Zealand-based borrower who is physically absent, or treated as physically absent, from New Zealand for 184 days or more consecutively will be treated as an overseas borrower. Where a borrower returns to New Zealand within the 184-day period they will remain overseas-based providing they are not physically present in New Zealand for a period exceeding 31 days in total. See Examples 7 and 8 in IS 25/20.

There is a tie-breaker provision that ensures a borrower cannot be treated as absent from New Zealand for the purposes of the 184-day test for any day on which they are treated as being physically present in New Zealand under s 22 (¶8-025). Thus, borrowers who return to New Zealand after being overseas for less than 6 months will not become overseas-based borrowers.

It is important to note that under s 23(2) the 184-day period can begin before the person becomes a borrower. A borrower will be treated as being overseas-based from the later of the day they became a borrower and the first day of the 184-day period.

Borrowers can use the myIR tool to calculate if their travel dates will result in them becoming overseas- based.
