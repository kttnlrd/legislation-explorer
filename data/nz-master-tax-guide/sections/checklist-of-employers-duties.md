# Checklist of employer's duties ¶3-010

The routine duties of an employer in relation to the PAYE system and employee remuneration (including schedular payments) are summarised below:

## new employee:

## obtain a completed tax code declaration form (IR330) showing the tax code appropriate to the new

employee's circumstances. This form is to be retained by the employer and kept with the employer's business records for a minimum of 7 years after the last salary/wages payment made to the employee.

The non-notified tax code rate of 45¢ in the dollar, plus earners' levy, should be used if the employee has not completed form IR330 (¶3-030)

## give the new employee (excluding employees not automatically enrolled in KiwiSaver) a Your

introduction to KiwiSaver — employee information (KS3) fact sheet, a KiwiSaver deduction form (KS2) and a KiwiSaver opt-out request form (KS10) within 7 days of their start date. If the employer has a chosen KiwiSaver scheme, the employer must provide the scheme's investment statement and advise new employees in writing that they will be allocated to that scheme unless they choose their own

## obtain from the new employee a completed KS2 or KS10. If the employee provides nothing,

KiwiSaver contributions should be deducted at the default contribution rate of 3%

## complete a New employee and KiwiSaver details form (IR346K) for the new employee if the

employee is not already a KiwiSaver member and submit to IR with the next employment information.

For more information on KiwiSaver, see ¶29-200.

## each pay day: deduct the appropriate amount of tax each pay day for each employee and make a

record of overtime and shift work hours

## PAYE: pay PAYE deductions to IR once a month by the 20th (or, in the case of large employers,

twice-monthly by the 5th and the 20th) (¶3-020)

## employment information: file an employment information form (IR348) on a payday or twice-monthly

basis (¶3-023)

## schedular payments: tax must be withheld from payments made to certain contract workers (¶3-044),

and such workers should either fill in an IR330C (tax rate notification for contractors form) or have tax deducted at the no-notification rate

## termination of employment: when an employer stops employing employees permanently, the

employer has 30 working days after the date they stop employing to notify the Commissioner (¶3-110)

## accident insurance: pay ACC work account levies to fund the cost of work place accidents, including

any residual claims levy (¶31-010 onwards)

## ACC earners' levy: deduct earners' levy from salary and wages paid to employees to cover non-work-

related injuries (the earners' levy is built into the PAYE tables) (¶31-048)

## employee and employer KiwiSaver contributions: if payable, these must be paid along with PAYE

deductions by the due date for those deductions (¶29-230)

## employer's superannuation contribution tax: if payable, this must be paid along with PAYE deductions

by the due date for those PAYE deductions (¶3-101)

## child support deductions: if any, these are due to IR by the 20th of the month following the month of

deduction or, in the case of large employers, they may choose to pay these on the 5th and 20th of each month (¶3-025)

## student loan deductions: if any, these must be paid along with PAYE deductions by the due date for

those deductions (¶8-055)

## fringe benefits: an employer must account for any fringe benefits provided to employees either on a

quarterly, income year or annual basis (¶21-010 onwards), and

## payroll donations: an employer must, if they have elected to participate in the payroll giving scheme,

transfer donations to the relevant donee organisation within the specified time period and keep a record of the amounts transferred and the tax credits attached for each pay period (¶11-063).
