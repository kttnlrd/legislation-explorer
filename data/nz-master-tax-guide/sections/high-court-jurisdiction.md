# High Court jurisdiction ¶4-245

For taxpayers filing a challenge in the High Court, the procedure is as follows:

## the taxpayer files statement of claim (completed in accordance with the High Court Rules) and notice

of proceeding, and pays the filing fee, all within the 2-month response period

## the taxpayer serves copies of the statement of claim and notice of proceeding on IR's Director of

Litigation Management as soon as practicable after they are filed (either by post or in-person) at the addresses shown below, and

## the Commissioner files a statement of defence within 25 days after the date of service of the

statement of claim and notice of proceeding.

Section 17 of the Taxation Review Authorities Act 1994 allows the TRA to receive evidence that would not ordinarily be admissible in a court of law. This relaxation of the rules of evidence previously extended to cases stated under Pt 8 of the TAA, which were heard in the High Court (s 136(16)). However, there is no comparable provision dealing with challenges heard in the High Court under Pt 8A. It appears that evidence in High Court challenges is subject to the rules of admissibility governing ordinary civil proceedings unless the Commissioner and the taxpayer agree that these are to be waived.

Note that the evidence exclusion rule applies in respect of High Court proceedings (¶4-190). Also, security for costs may be awarded by the High Court in tax cases just as in any civil case: see Reefdale Investments Ltd v C of IR (2004) 21 NZTC 18,683.

The Commissioner's address for service is: Delivery Legal Services Inland Revenue Level 5 Asteron House 55 Featherston Street Wellington 6011.

*Refs: Section 17, s 136(16)*
