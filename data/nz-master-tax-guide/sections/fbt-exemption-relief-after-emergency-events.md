# FBT exemption — relief after emergency events ¶21-665

From 1 April 2025, an exemption from fringe benefit tax is available in respect of fringe benefits provided to employees for the purpose of alleviating the effects of an emergency event in the 8-week period following the relevant event.

To qualify for the exemption, the benefit (s FP 21):

## must otherwise be a fringe benefit and must be provided for the purposes of relieving the employee

from the adverse effects of an emergency event

## must be received by the employee in the 8-week period beginning on the first day of the relevant

emergency event, and

## must not replace normal salary/wages or other PAYE income payments, and must not depend on the

seniority of the employee.

If the benefit is provided to an employee who is associated to the employer, other non-associated full-time employees must be eligible to receive the benefit. Further, the benefit must be treated as not being a fringe benefit by the employer.

A $5,000 limit has been placed on the value of cash payments and fringe benefits which can be valued by the employer (eg provision of vehicle for private use). The FBT exemption will apply to these fringe benefits to the extent that the exemption for cash payments (¶5-715) has not been utilised.

For "sundry" fringe benefits (eg benefits provided at a drop-in centre) where the employer cannot estimate the value, there is no monetary limit.

Note an "emergency event" is defined under s 3(1) of the TAA as an emergency as defined in s 4 of the Civil Defence Emergency Management Act 2002 that is declared an emergency under that Act, or subject to a power exercised under s 121 or s 122 of the Biosecurity Act 1993.

Prior to 1 April 2025, a similar exemption applied to fringe benefits provided to employees for the purposes of relief from the aftermath of the North Island flooding events (s CZ 24B).
