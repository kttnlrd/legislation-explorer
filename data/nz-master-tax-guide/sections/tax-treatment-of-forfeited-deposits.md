# Tax treatment of forfeited deposits ¶7-510

It is common for a deposit to be payable by the purchaser, on signature or within a short period of time thereafter, of a sale and purchase agreement for the purchase of land. The purpose of a deposit is to guarantee performance by the purchaser. On settlement the deposit is credited as part of the purchase price.

If the purchaser defaults, the deposit may be forfeited. Such forfeited deposits are not income under the land taxing provisions as there is no disposal of land under the failed agreement. However, the amount of the deposit may be income to the seller under other provisions.

QWBA QB 23/09 "Income tax – forfeited deposits on cancelation of land sale agreements" clarifies the Commissioner's view of the circumstances when a forfeited deposit from a cancelled land sale agreement is deemed to be income to the seller of the land.

The seller needs to consider whether the forfeited deposit may be taxable income in their hands. This will be the case when the deposit is:

## business income to the seller (see s CB 1 and ¶5-172)

## income derived from a profit-making scheme (see s CB 3 and ¶5-192), or

## income under the ordinary concepts (see s CA 1(2) and ¶5-167).

If the land subject to the sale and purchase agreement was part of the operational business, like for persons dealing in land, or incidental to that business, then the forfeiter deposit will be business income. See [7]–[22] of QB 23/09.

*Refs: s CB 1, s CB 3, s CA 1(2)*

## Example: Forfeited deposit as part of ordinary business

Instant Homes Ltd is a property developer. It regularly purchases land, builds houses or renovates existing properties for sale with the intention of making a profit. Recently the company purchased a property, built a home on it, and sold it to Mr. Brown. Mr. Brown paid a 10% deposit but was unable to pay the balance of the purchase price on settlement date. Instant Homes Ltd cancelled the agreement and retained the deposit. The forfeited deposit is business income to Instant Homes Ltd under s CB 1. Instant Homes Ltd is in business and it derives the forfeited deposit from the business. The sale of the land is part of the ordinary business of Instant Homes Ltd and the receipt of the forfeited deposit is an amount which arises from that transaction. The possibility that the sale may fall through, and the deposit may be forfeited if Mr. Brown defaults is part of doing business as a property developer and a contemplated outcome of the transaction.

Example reproduced from QB 23/09.

If a person is carrying out a profit-making undertaking or scheme, the amount of the forfeited deposit may be taxable income. There must be sufficient connection between the scheme and the deposit for this to be the case. See [23]–[29] of QB 23/09.

## Example: Forfeited deposit is income under a profit-making scheme

Conor is an accountant who lives in Auckland. In 2017 he purchased some bare land near Wanaka with the intention of building a home on it and moving there permanently. However, circumstances changed, and Conor decided to stay in Auckland. He decided to subdivide the Wanaka land into four lots, provide services to each property and do some work to make the sections attractive to potential purchasers who might like to build a home there. This included clearing the land, significant earthworks to construct building platforms, putting in driveways, fencing and letterboxes and grassing the land. Connor hired a project manager to arrange for the work to be done. The development was finished in early 2023 and Connor put the sections on the market. He received an unconditional offer from Ambrosia for one section and she paid a 10% deposit. However, Ambrosia changed her mind about building a home and did not pay the balance of the purchase price on settlement date. Conor cancelled the agreement and retained the deposit. The forfeited deposit is income to Connor from a profit-making scheme he is carrying on. He has a coherent plan to develop the land and sell it for a profit. The forfeited deposit is connected to the profit-making scheme – the fact that a sale might not be completed is a contemplated outcome of the scheme and the contract of sale provides for it. As income under s CB 3 the forfeited deposit will contribute to any taxable profit arising from that scheme.

Example reproduced from QB 23/09.

Alternatively, the amount may be income under the ordinary concepts which is a very broad general provision that considers all the facts and circumstances around the forfeiture of the deposit. See [30]–[47] of QB 23/09. The item also contains a number of examples illustrating the Commissioner's view of when a forfeited deposit will be taxable income under the ordinary concepts of income. One of the examples relates to a forfeited deposit on a sale subject to the bright-line test. The Commissioner views the deposit as taxable, despite the sale not proceeding.
