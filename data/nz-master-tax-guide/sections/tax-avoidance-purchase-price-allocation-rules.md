# Tax avoidance — Purchase price allocation rules ¶33-200

The purchase price allocation rules have been introduced to apply for agreements for the disposal and acquisition of property entered into on or after 1 July 2021. The purchase price allocation rules tighten the rules that govern how parties to a sale of two or more assets with different tax treatments (a mixed supply) allocate the total price between the assets for income tax purposes. They do not affect existing law relating to the determination of the price when the purchaser assumes any vendor liabilities. The rules are designed to improve the integrity of the income tax rules by requiring consistency between the parties' allocations. The objective of the provisions is to prevent an overall revenue loss when vendors and purchasers adopt different price allocations that minimise their own tax liabilities.

The rules are contained in ss GC 20 and GC 21 and are summarised below:

## Purchase price allocations are to be made when a person disposes of items of property ("purchased

property") to another person that fall into 2 or more of the following classes:

## trading stock, other than timber or a right to take timber

## timber or a right to take timber

## depreciable property, other than buildings

## buildings that are depreciable property

## financial arrangements

## purchased property for which the disposal does not give rise to assessable income for the vendor or

deductions for the purchaser.

## If the parties agree an allocation for the classes of purchased property and record it in a document

before the first of their tax returns including the transaction is filed, they must both follow that allocation in their tax returns.

## If the parties do not agree an allocation and record it in a document before the first of their tax returns

is filed, the vendor may determine the allocation if they use different income tax treatments for two or more of the classes of purchased property. The vendor must notify both the purchaser and the Commissioner of the allocation within 3 months of the change in ownership of the purchased property.

The amount allocated to each class of property must be the greater of the relative market value and the tax book value of the class. The tax book value minimum protects the purchaser from an unreasonably low allocation to taxable/deductible property by the vendor. However, the vendor will not be able to comply with the tax book floor where the aggregate tax book value of all the classes of taxable property plus the relative market value of the class of non-taxable property exceeds the total purchase price.

This could be the case in a distressed sale where the vendor is making a genuine loss. In this case, the excess will first reduce any amount allocated to non-taxable property (eg goodwill or non-taxable land).

If there is still excess, the tax book values of the taxable property must be reduced proportionately until they collectively equal the purchase price.

## If the vendor does not make an allocation within the 3-month timeframe, the purchaser is entitled to

determine the allocation and notify both the vendor and the Commissioner of it within 6 months of the change of ownership of the property (ie within a further 3 months). The allocated amounts must reflect the relative market value of the relevant class of property proportional to the other classes of property.

There are no other constraints on the purchaser's allocation.

## The purchaser is not entitled to any deductions in relation to the purchased property until the first

income year for which they file a return of income on a timely basis after the earlier of the purchaser's notification of their allocation to the Commissioner and the Commissioner's notification of his allocation to the purchaser. This rule is designed to incentivise the purchaser to notify the vendor and the Commissioner of an allocation when they have the right to allocate. Any deductions that are deferred will be able to be claimed in the return that is filed on a timely basis after an allocation is notified to or by the Commissioner.

## The Commissioner may impose his own allocation if no allocation is made on a timely basis or if he

considers the parties' allocation is not based on relative market values. However, the Commissioner

*Refs: ss GC 20*

cannot impose his own allocation on an item of depreciable property if its original cost for the vendor was less than $10,000, the total amount allocated for the item and any identical property is less than $1 million, and the amount allocated for the item is no less than its tax book value and no greater than its original cost.

## The unilateral allocation rules (ie allocation by the vendor, the purchaser or the Commissioner)

will not apply if the total consideration for the purchased property is less than $1 million, or the only purchased property is residential land (together with its chattels) and the total consideration is less than $7.5 million.

## A unilateral vendor, purchaser or Commissioner allocation is binding on the parties, with no right

for a party to contest the allocation by filing a notice of proposed adjustment or to take any other proceedings to challenge the allocation.

## Any existing provision of the Act that expressly requires the use of market value for purchased

property is overridden by the purchase price allocation rules to the extent to which the amount allocated to that property is determined under the purchase price allocation rules. This prevents parties from attempting to use 2 different market values for an item or class of property and also allows the tax book value floor on a unilateral vendor allocation to operate correctly.

## From 1 April 2024, where the parties to a disposal are associated at the time of disposal, any trading

stock, timber or a right to take timber is deemed to be disposed of for market value.

## Standard sales and purchase agreement - Purchase price

allocation The purchase price allocation rules grounded in s GC 20 and s GC 21 have given rise to changes to the standard sale and purchase agreement form. The 11th edition of the REINZ Sale and Purchase Agreement contains an addendum specifically for the purchase price allocation. The front page of the agreement signals that the purchase price allocation rules may apply to a transaction, and the updated form also contains a warranty. Best practice is for parties to agree on the allocation prior to signing the agreement. However, this may not be possible.

The purchase price allocation rules are mostly triggered on residential sales over $7.5 million and non- residential sales over $1 million.

The addendum provides various definitions, including "agreed allocation" and "tax asset classes". The addendum provides for the parties to agree and record an allocation of the purchase price by the settlement date, or at least within 60 working days after the settlement date. Until this is done, under the standard addendum wording, no party may file a tax return relating to the transaction. The vendor is to propose an allocation within 5 working days after the date of the agreement, with a "reasonable endeavours" obligation on the parties to try to agree. If the parties cannot agree, then there is provision for an expert to determine the allocation is contained in the addendum.

## Question We've Been Asked QB 25/22

QWBA QB 25/22, "Do the purchase price allocation rules alter the tax book values of Farmland Improvements and listed horticultural plants under subpart DO?" confirms that for the sale of farmland including farmland improvements and listed horticultural plants, the purchase price allocation rules under ss GC 20 and GC 21 do not alter the purchaser's opening tax book values for the improvements and plants as specified under subpart DO.
