# Acquiring or possessing ESS tools ¶14-125

IR have introduced a new civil offence relating to acquisition or possession of ESS tools.

An "electronic sales suppression tool" is a software program, device, or other thing that can modify, falsify, destroy or prevent the creation of a record that a person is required to keep under tax law and that records a sale, and the use of which would lead to a reasonable conclusion that one of its principal functions is to facilitate the modification, falsification, destruction or prevention of the creation of a record.

Under s 141EE an electronic sales suppression penalty applies to taxpayers who knowingly acquire, possess, or control an ESS tool. It also applies to those persons who have the right to use the ESS tool.

In both cases, the acquisition, possession, control or right to use must be accompanied by the purpose of evading the assessment or payment of tax. It need not be their own tax but can also relate to another person's tax assessment or payment.

The only exception is for taxpayers who acquire a business whose operations include the use of a suppression tool if the taxpayer could not reasonably have known of the tool's existence, and they have not used the tool. This measure is aimed at ensuring taxpayers who accidentally acquire a tool in this way are not penalised for this unintentional acquisition.

The ESS penalty amount is $5,000 and the taxpayer is only liable for a single penalty for all related tax types.

Note that under s 141FB(6), there is no reduction for "good behaviour" when a person has an ESS penalty imposed.

Revenue alert, RA 22/01 "Consequences of acquiring, possessing or using electronic sales suppression tools" highlights the possible consequences a taxpayer will face if they are found to have acquired, possessed or used ESS tools.
