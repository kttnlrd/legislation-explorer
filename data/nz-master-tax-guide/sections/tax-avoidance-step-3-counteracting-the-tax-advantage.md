# Tax avoidance — Step 3: Counteracting the tax advantage ¶33-125

The application of s GA 1 of the IT07 is the third step of the 3-step analysis referred to in ¶33-085. The conceptual justification for the existence of the Commissioner's power to made adjustments is that the arrangement in question is void as against the Commissioner. This links back to s BG 1(2) (¶33-010).

*Refs: s GA 1, s BG 1(2)*

## Tax advantage

Section GA 1 provides the Commissioner with power to adjust the taxable income of a person affected by the arrangement in a way the Commissioner thinks is appropriate in order to counteract a tax advantage obtained from or under the arrangement. The phrase "tax advantage" is not meaningfully defined in the Act for the majority of situations to which s GA 1 will be relevant. Hence the case law is strongly influential as to what the phrase means. Following Ben Nevis Forestry Ventures Ltd v C of IR; Accent Management Ltd v C of IR (2009) 24 NZTC 23,188 (SC) there appears to be a basis for concluding that there is a conceptual link between the concept of a tax advantage and the manner in which the tax benefit is obtained outside of the contemplation of Parliament. In Ben Nevis the licence premium and the insurance premium were found to have produced tax benefits outside of the contemplation of Parliament. It was those items of deduction that were disallowed in their entirety: see ¶33-105 and Ben Nevis at [169]–[171].

It has been suggested that, had the Commissioner sought to, say, tax the exempt income otherwise receivable by the charity used in the Trinity scheme, that could have been successfully resisted on the grounds that it was not a tax advantage. It would not have been a tax advantage because the charity was not used in the Trinity scheme in a manner inconsistent with the contemplation of Parliament.

## Power of adjustment exercised against anyone benefiting

The Commissioner's power of adjustment can be exercised against anyone benefiting from the tax avoidance arrangement. There is no question of mutuality being needed nor does the taxpayer need to be aware that they are benefiting from or under a tax avoidance arrangement: see Ben Nevis at [164].

## No requirement for counterfactuals

The Commissioner may consider counterfactuals, however Commissioner is not obligated to conjure up counterfactuals as the putative alternative arrangement that the taxpayer may have entered into: see Accent Management Ltd v C of IR (2007) 23 NZTC 21,323 (CA) at [155] and Ben Nevis at [170].

It is permissible for a taxpayer to advance evidence and argument to prove what would have happened, or would in all likelihood have happened or might have been expected to have happened. That evidence may enable a submission that the adjustment under s GA 1 is wrong by being too excessive: see Ben Nevis at [171]. The taxpayer has the onus of proving that the adjustment is wrong and by how much.

Tax advantages may occur at multiple points in an arrangement. The Commissioner can select which ones they will counteract. The Commissioner's choice in this matter cannot be set aside as long as they are using a consistent hypothesis: see O'Neil v C of IR (2001) 20 NZTC 17,051 (PC) at [31].

## Not always necessary to reconstruct

Where the avoidance focuses on an impermissible deduction then it is possible for the Commissioner to simply invoke s BG 1, which will void the arrangement and hence the basis for the deduction. There is no need for the Commissioner to go on and reconstruct under s GA 1. Where the Commissioner

does reconstruct under s GA 1 they do not have to substitute the next most favourable tax option as the counterfactual. See Alesco New Zealand Ltd v C of IR (2011) 25 NZTC ¶20-099 (HC) at [154]–[160]; (2013) 26 NZTC ¶21-003 (CA) at [119]–[126]. This is particularly so when the avoidance is in claiming deductions in a situation where there is no underlying economic costs. In those circumstances it is not open to argue that as an alternative the taxpayer would have incurred an actual interest cost which would have produced greater deductions than the artificial "interest" costs claimed but disallowed under s BG 1. That is, a taxpayer cannot say that in the alternative they would have actually paid interest and hence no reconstruction would take place.

## Power to adjust lost once challenge filed

Once a challenge has been filed, the Commissioner loses their power to reconstruct under s GA 1. Under s 138P of the TAA the hearing authority may, on hearing a challenge, make an assessment which the Commissioner was able to make at the time the Commissioner made the assessment to which the challenge relates. It would be clearly inconsistent with the parliamentary intention implicit in s 138G of the TAA, which contains the exclusion rule as a consequence of exchanging statements of position, for the court to make an alternative reconstruction when statements of position have been exchanged. This is because the point at which the Commissioner makes the assessment in the context of exchanges of statements of position is after the adjudication unit has looked at the matter. At that point the statements of position are in place and the Commissioner is not in a position to assess contrary to their terms without re-engaging in the disputes process.

It has been held that, when considering the Commissioner's adjustment, the evidence available to them when they made the assessment is relevant: Westpac Banking Corp v C of IR (2009) 24 NZTC 23,834 at [626]–[629]. It is doubted that that statement is correct because it clashes with the principle that, absent the effect of the evidence exclusion rule, a taxpayer can prove based on admissible evidence that an assessment of the Commissioner is wrong or excessive. The challenge hearing is not a review of the Commissioner's decision-making.

The Commissioner also has a power of adjustment (under s GA 2) specific to arrangements altering the incidence of FBT (¶21-130).

The Commissioner's view of the adjustments that may be made under s GA 1 is discussed in Part 9 of interpretation statement IS 23/01: (¶33-137).
