# Evidence exclusion rule ¶4-190

The evidence exclusion rule has been relaxed so that parties are now only limited to the issues and propositions of law. Previously, they were limited in any challenge to the facts, evidence, issues and propositions of law disclosed in their SOPs.

Once a disclosure notice has been given and the parties have completed their SOPs, the evidence exclusion rule applies. Under this rule, both the taxpayer and the Commissioner are limited in any challenge to the issues and propositions of law disclosed in their SOPs. See Case W31 (2004) 21 NZTC 11,311 for illustration of the rule. The Court of Appeal suggested that there is no comparable rule applying to parties earlier in the dispute resolution process. See C of IR v Zentrum Holdings Ltd (2006) 22 NZTC 19,912 (CA).

However, the evidence exclusion rule does not restrict the parties to matters raised in their own SOPs. If they were so restricted, neither party would be able to respond to the other party's SOP because neither could rely on legal propositions raised by the other party. See C of IR v Delphi Fishing Co Ltd (2004) 21 NZTC 18,525 at 18,533.

The evidence exclusion rule is subject to judicial discretion to admit previously undisclosed matters. The applicant must show that they could not, with due diligence, have found or discovered this material at the time of discovery of their SOP and that admission of those items is necessary to avoid manifest injustice.

See Case X22 (2006) 22 NZTC 12,278.

Where, in a taxpayer-initiated dispute, the parties have agreed to submit the dispute to a court or TRA without requiring the Commissioner to submit a SOP (under s 89N(1)(c)(viii)), the taxpayer is bound by the evidence exclusion rule but the Commissioner is not. This ensures the Commissioner can rely on information that would otherwise have been included in their SOP. However, where the Commissioner has not been required to submit a SOP under any of the other sub-paragraphs of s 89N(1)(c), neither the Commissioner nor the taxpayer are bound by the evidence exclusion rule (¶4-185).

*Refs: s 89N(1)(c)(viii), s 89N(1)(c)*
