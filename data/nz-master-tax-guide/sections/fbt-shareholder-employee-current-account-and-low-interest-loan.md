# FBT — shareholder-employee — current account and low- interest loan ¶21-410

Fringe benefit tax is payable when there is a debit balance in a shareholder current account and the interest charged, if any, to the shareholder-employee is less than the interest that would be charged at the prescribed rate for the quarter or tax year in which there is a debit balance. To eliminate the FBT liability, interest at the prescribed rates may be charged on the overdrawn current account; however, this may give rise to other tax consequences for both the company and the shareholder-employee (see IS 24/09: "Income tax — overdrawn shareholder loan account balances", [44] to [61]).

A special rule permits the retrospective crediting of salary or wages for the purposes of calculating the value of the loan benefit. Where the shareholder-employee is allocated further salary or wages (not being subject to PAYE) after the end of the tax year, that income is treated as having been credited to the current account on the later of either the first day of that income year or the day on which the balance of the current account first became overdrawn during that income year.
