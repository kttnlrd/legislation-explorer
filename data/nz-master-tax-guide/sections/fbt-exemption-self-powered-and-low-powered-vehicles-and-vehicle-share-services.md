# FBT exemption — self-powered and low-powered vehicles, and vehicle-share services ¶21-660

Generally, public transport fares that are subsidised by an employer, mainly for the purpose of their employees travelling between their home and place of work, are subject to FBT (¶21-440).

However, in order to align the FBT outcomes of travelling to work via car and public transport, and in order to encourage a more environmentally friendly choice of transport for employees, an exclusion from FBT has been introduced.

From 1 April 2023, an exemption applies for self-powered and electric vehicles. An exclusion for vehicle- share services was also introduced.

## Electronic and self-powered vehicles

If an employer provides an employee with any of the following forms of transport (in order to travel between work and home), the cost of the transport is excluded from being a fringe benefit and therefore does not attract fringe benefit tax. This exclusion applies when the vehicle is one of the following:

## a bicycle

## an electric bicycle (e-bike)

## a scooter

## an electric scooter

## any other vehicle declared under s 168A of the Land Transport Act 1998 to be:

## a mobility device, or

## not a motor vehicle.

## Vehicle-share services

When an employer provides an employee with financial assistance for the employee's costs related to using a vehicle-share service, for the main purpose of travelling between work and home, the payment is not a fringe benefit (s CX 19D(2)). Section CX 19D(4) defines "vehicle-share services" to mean a transport service that allows users to hire a vehicle for a point-to-point trip through a mobile communication device. This exemption is limited to the same vehicles listed above, being self-powered or low-powered vehicles.

This exemption is not applicable for ride-share motor vehicles, like Uber. Uber cars generally fall within the definition of "motor vehicles" and therefore are excluded from this exemption (s CX 19D(2)(e)(ii)).
