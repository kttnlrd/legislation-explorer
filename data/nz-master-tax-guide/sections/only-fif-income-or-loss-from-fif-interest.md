# Only FIF income or loss from FIF interest ¶26-131

A person is generally treated as having no income or deductions other than FIF income or loss from a FIF interest when one of the cost-based methods is used. Any other amount derived is treated as excluded income, although a rebate of fees will be income if the person was allowed a deduction for payment of the fees. For other income and deductions to be disregarded, the calculation method for FIF income or loss must be:

## the FDR method, unless the FIF is:

## a company resident and taxable in Australia (not subject to exemptions for offshore business

activities or offshore banking units), and

## (for income years starting after 30 June 2014) not a unit trust, or a unit trust subject to income tax

under Australian law as a company, and

## the shareholding is a direct income interest of 10% or more (although that exclusion will not apply if

the FIF is a foreign PIE equivalent and the taxpayer is a PIE, eligible to be a PIE or a life insurer)

## the CV method

## the cost method, or

## the deemed rate of return method (although there is additional FIF income to the extent that a gain

upon disposal of the FIF exceeds total deemed rate of return FIF income).
