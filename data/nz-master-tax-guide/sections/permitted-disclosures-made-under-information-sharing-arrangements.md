# Permitted disclosures made under information-sharing arrangements ¶1-609

The confidentiality rule in s 18 does not apply to the disclosure of "sensitive revenue information" (¶1-604), and other information, if the disclosure is made under:

## an approved information-sharing agreement under s 145 of the Privacy Act 2020 (before 1 December

2020, s 96J of the Privacy Act 1993): s 18E(2)

## an agreement to share information for public service purposes when disclosure occurs with the

consent of the person to whom the information relates: s 18E(3), or

## regulations authorised by s 18F (¶1-615).

*Refs: s 18, s 145, s 96J, s 18E(2), s 18E(3), s 18F*

## Approved information-sharing agreements

Section 145 of the Privacy Act 2020 (before 1 December 2020, s 96J of the Privacy Act 1993) allows the Governor-General to approve an information-sharing agreement made by Order in Council on the recommendation of a government Minister.

The information-sharing agreement may relate to information that is sensitive revenue information, whether personal or non-personal information. For this purpose, an approved information-sharing agreement may extend a restricted information-sharing provision in an IR Act.

IR has concluded several information-sharing agreements that have been approved by the following Orders in Council:

## the Privacy (Information Sharing Agreement Between Inland Revenue and Internal Affairs) Order

2018 — which allows Internal Affairs to share information it gathers as part of the passport application process with IR for the purposes of locating overseas-based student loan borrowers and child support liable parents living overseas who are in default of their repayment and contact obligations.

## the Tax Administration (Information Sharing with Accident Compensation Corporation) Order 2015 —

which allows IR to communicate information relating to assessing levies and to assessing eligibility for, and calculating of, earnings-related compensation.

## the Privacy (Information Sharing Agreement between Inland Revenue and Ministry of Social

Development) Order 2017 — which enables the Ministry of Social Development (MSD) and IR to share information for the purposes of assessing eligibility for benefits, enforcing obligations related to benefits, assessing tax obligations and registering new customers or updating customer contact information.

## the Privacy (Information Sharing Agreement between Inland Revenue and Ministry of Social

Development) Amendment Order 2021, which came into force on 8 October 2021, approves amendments to the information sharing agreement between IR and MSD entered into in 2017 (above).

The 2021 Order allows the parties to share personal information for use in policy development.

Personal information exchanged may not be used for enforcement or any other purpose for which information may be shared under the 2017 order but allows information that is capable of being used for enforcement or other purposes to be used for policy development purposes.

## from 1 October 2020, the Privacy (Information Sharing Agreement Between Inland Revenue, New

Zealand Police, New Zealand Customs Service, and Serious Fraud Office) Order 2020 — which allows IR to share personal information with the participating agencies for the purposes of the prevention, detection, or investigation of, or use as evidence in the prosecution of, a serious crime. This Order revoked the 2014 agreement with the New Zealand Police.

Schedule 7 cl 45 also provides that the Commissioner may exchange information with the MSD for the purpose of giving assistance to the government of a country with which New Zealand has a social security agreement (¶1-633).

## Disclosures made with taxpayer's consent

The Commissioner may enter an agreement to share certain information if the agreement:

## is made for public services purposes

## relates to the disclosure of sensitive revenue information held about a person or entity

## specifies appropriate conditions for the security and use of the information, and

## stipulates a process to ensure the consent is properly obtained and recorded.

In addition, the Privacy Commissioner, after consultation, must agree the disclosure is appropriate, and the person or entity must consent to the disclosure. "Public service" is defined as a public function or duty imposed on an agency by law or government policy.

An "agency" (for the purposes of ss 18E and 18F) is defined in s 138 of the Privacy Act 2020 and means a New Zealand private sector agency (non-government agency) or a public sector agency (a department, organisation or local authority).

## Provenance inquiry

The Commissioner must undertake a provenance inquiry before proposing a new information-sharing agreement, a new consent agreement or new regulations for public service purposes. The inquiry must assess the likely provenance (origin) of the data it is proposed will be shared. Consideration must also be given to whether conditions are required for security and use of the information.

Note that s 95(1) of the Privacy Act requires any person bound by a secrecy obligation imposed under any enactment to supply information or documents sought, or answer questions posed, by the Privacy Commissioner. Compliance with such a request by the Privacy Commissioner is not a breach of the secrecy obligation to which the person is subject.
