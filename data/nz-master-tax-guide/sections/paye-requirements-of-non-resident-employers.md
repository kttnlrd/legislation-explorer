# PAYE requirements of non-resident employers ¶26-430

The Commissioner requires non-resident companies to deduct PAYE from the salary or wages of a non- resident employee, irrespective of whether the employee spends less than 92 days working in New Zealand, until the employee's right to relief from New Zealand tax is established to the Commissioner's satisfaction.

PAYE must be accounted for once or twice per month, depending on whether the employer's gross annual PAYE is $500,000 or more (¶3-020).

Operational statement OS- 21/04, "Non-resident employers' obligations to deduct PAYE, FBT and ESCT in cross-border employment situations" confirms that that a non-resident employer has an obligation to withhold PAYE from a PAYE income payment made to an employee only if:

## the employer is subject to New Zealand tax because it has a "sufficient presence" in New Zealand,

and

## the services performed by the employee are properly attributable to the employer's presence in New

Zealand.

A non-resident employer may also have an FBT or ESCT liability for a benefit provided to, or a contribution made for, an employee in the same circumstances. #Note: subsequent amendments have been made to the legislation that affect this statement.

An employee's non-resident foreign sourced income will not be subject to PAYE as such income is not taxable in New Zealand. Similarly, income of a non-resident contractor that is subject to relief under s CW 19 or a DTA does not attract PAYE deductions.

From 1 April 2024, the PAYE, FBT, ESCT and non-resident contractors' tax (NRCT) rules have been changed to provide certainty and flexibility for cross-border workers and their employers. "Cross-border employees" includes employees of non-resident employers who perform services in New Zealand, as well as New Zealand resident employees who perform services outside of New Zealand. An employer has a 60-day grace period to meet or correct the PAYE, FBT or ESCT obligations for an employee where the conditions of a tax exemption (eg those set out in s CW 19) have been breached. Section RD 23 (bonds given by employers for non-resident employees) has been repealed. From 1 April 2023, there is relief for a non-resident employer who has incorrectly determined that they do not have a sufficient presence in New Zealand. If the requirements of the safe-harbour are met, the employer will not face penalties and interest on any unpaid employment taxes.

An employer may also seek a bespoke arrangement with IR and the ability to transfer PAYE obligations to an employee when the non-resident employer does not have an obligation to account for PAYE (¶3-367).
