# Records of charity ¶24-270

A charitable organisation that is fully exempt from income tax is not required to file an income tax return under s 33(1) of the TAA but may still choose to file one. This position made clear through an amendment to s 33(1), effective 30 March 2025, before which interpretation of s 33(1) read as requiring charitable organisations to file a return of income.

However, sufficient accurate records must be kept in New Zealand as enable the Commissioner to ascertain the source of donations made to the organisation and the application of its funds, whether in New Zealand or overseas. From 30 March 2025, records must be kept in English or te reo M#ori, or any other language with the approval of the Commissioner, for at least 7 years after the income year to which they relate.

In addition, the Commissioner may request the organisation provide a return of funds derived and/ or received during the tax year, the return showing the source and application of those funds. The Commissioner may advise the Minister of Finance when they believe that funds are not being applied in accordance with the charitable objects of the particular organisation.

Gift-exempt bodies are also bound by the above.

A charitable organisation that is a member of a M#ori authority may file a tax return to claim a refund of M#ori authority credits attached to a distribution received from the M#ori authority (s 33(1B) of the TAA and ¶24-317).

The registration, reporting and monitoring of charities contained in the Charities Act 2005 is summarised at ¶24-195.

*Refs: s 33(1), s 33(1B)*
