# Manufacturing, supplying, acquiring or possessing ESS tools ¶14-265

IR have introduced 2 new criminal offences related to ESS tools as the spread of ESS tools threatens the integrity of the tax base in more recent years.

An "electronic sales suppression tool" is a software program, device, or other thing that can modify, falsify, destroy or prevent the creation of a record that a person is required to keep under tax law and that records a sale, and the use of which would lead to a reasonable conclusion that one of its principal functions is to facilitate the modification, falsification, destruction or prevention of the creation of a record.

While using a suppression tool constitutes tax evasion and could be penalised under existing anti-evasion provisions, it was not previously illegal to manufacture, sell, buy, or possess such tools. Therefore, the provisions have been inserted into the TAA with application from 30 March 2022.

## Offence of manufacturing or providing suppression tools — s

## 143BB

Section 143BB of the TAA establishes that it is an offence to:

## manufacture, develop, or publish an ESS tool that is provided to a person who is liable for an ESS

penalty, who commits an offence under s 143BC, or who commits an offence under s 143BC (see below), or

## knowingly supply, make available for use, or otherwise provide a suppression tool, or the right to use

a suppression tool, to a person resident in New Zealand, or knowingly provide a service to a person resident in New Zealand that includes the use of a suppression tool.

The penalty for committing either offence is a fine up to a maximum of $250,000.

## Offence of acquiring or possessing suppression tools — s

## 143BC

Section 143BC of the TAA establishes the criminal equivalent of the civil ESS penalty in s 141EE (¶14-129).

Section 143BC makes it an offence for a taxpayer required to keep records under a tax law to knowingly acquire, possess or control a suppression tool, or have a right to use the suppression tool, with the purpose of evading their own or some other person's assessment or payment of tax.

The penalty for committing this offence is a fine of up to a maximum of $50,000.

Similar to the ESS penalty, a taxpayer who is convicted of an offence under this section is treated as committing a single offence in relation to all tax types and periods, so only one fine is imposed. However, a further penalty may be imposed for a later period of time if the taxpayer continues to possess, control or have the right to use the ESS tool.
