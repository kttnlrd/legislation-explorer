# Residual income tax ¶22-060

Residual income tax (RIT) is used to calculate the amount of provisional tax a taxpayer is required to pay.

RIT, for a person and a tax year, is defined as the positive amount (if any) that remains after subtracting from the person's income tax liability for the tax year, the following amounts:

## credits allowed against the income tax liability under:

## s LB 1 — for PAYE income payments

## s LB 3 — for resident withholding tax

## s LB 5 — for non-resident withholding tax

## s LB 6 — for retirement scheme contribution tax

## s LB 6B — for residential land withholding tax

## s LC 13 — for independent earners

## s LE 1 — for imputation credits

## s LJ 2 — for foreign income tax

## s LK 1 — relating to attributed CFC income

## s LK 6 — relating to use of tax credits by group companies

## s LO 1 — for Maori authority credits

## s LY 1 — for R&D tax credits

## tax credits for a supplementary dividend paid under subpart LP (excluding excess credits carried

back or set off between wholly-owned group companies under s LP 3(3))

## tax paid by a trustee for a beneficiary or by an agent for a principal.

When calculating residual income tax for an income year no deduction is made (at this point) for any provisional tax payments made during that year.
