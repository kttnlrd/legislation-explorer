# Prize for sales competition ¶3-310

When a competition is run by an employer among their sales personnel with a cash prize for the best sales results, the cash prize is assessable income of the recipient. Tax deductions should be made according to the salesperson's tax status and applicable tax rate.

Non-cash prizes are not assessable income in the hands of the recipient, although they may be subject to FBT. The status of non-cash prizes as non-assessable income was confirmed in the case Sixton v C of IR (1982) 5 NZTC 61,285. The Court found that the benefit received by the taxpayer was not an allowance.

It was a non-monetary perquisite or emolument and was not included within the meaning of the term "allowance". The fact that the benefit may have been convertible into cash is irrelevant once it has been decided that the benefit is not an allowance.
