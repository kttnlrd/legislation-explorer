# Excluded income — grants and subsidies ¶5-760

Certain government grants or subsidies paid to a person for a business that the person carries on are excluded income. To be excluded income, the payment must:

## be made by a local authority (¶5-610), or a public authority (¶5-605), or a public purpose Crown-

controlled companies (PPCCC)

## be in the nature of a grant or subsidy, or be a grant-related suspensory loan, and

## not be in the nature of an advance or loan (other than a grant-related suspensory loan), and

## correspond to:

## expenditure the person incurs for which they would be allowed a deduction but for s DF 1

(Government grants to businesses), or

## expenditure the person incurs in acquiring, constructing, installing or extending an asset for which the

person would have an amount of depreciation loss but for s DF 1.

A grant-related suspensory loan is a loan made by:

## a public authority (excluding a specified suspensory loan that would, if remitted, be income of the

person — ¶5-320) and includes the term that the liability of the borrower may be wholly or partly remitted, or

## the Rural Banking and Finance Corporation of New Zealand as an irrigation suspensory loan and

designated as such, or

## the Rural Banking and Finance Corporation of New Zealand as a West Coast drainage suspensory

loan and designated as such.

The exclusion from income in s CX 47 does not apply to:

## grants made under the Agriculture Recovery Programme for the Lower North Island and Eastern Bay

of Plenty, to the extent that the grant relates to expenditure incurred before the grant was received and for which a deduction would be allowed but for s DF 1 (s CX 47(3)), or

## a person who has chosen to exclude a grant payment to the extent to which it is made to the person

for the person's business as a research and development growth grant, the payment is withheld until the conditions of the grant are satisfied, and in the absence of s DF 1 the person would have been allowed a deduction for expenditure or depreciation loss to which the payment corresponds (s CX 47(4)).

The amount of a person's deduction for expenditure incurred that has been financed out of a grant or loan meeting the above criteria is restricted (¶10-513). This restriction and the exclusion from income of the amount of the loan places the person in a tax-neutral position.

Interpretation statement IS 23/06, "Income tax – Government payments to businesses (grants and subsidies)", provides guidance on how the legislation applies to payments of grants and subsidies made to businesses. In summary, where ss CX 47 and DF 1 apply, the amount is not taxable and the expense which was funded is not deductible. The statement includes PPCCCs in the payers of grants and subsidies.

A PPCCC is a company listed in Sch 35, or a company that is wholly owned by a company that is listed in Sch 35 and had a primary purpose of carrying out a public policy objective of the government.

The intent of the legislation, as mentioned above, is to make grants tax neutral. If the amount of the expense is greater than the amount received, then a deduction is allowed for any additional amount of expenditure.

For depreciable property, the cost base of the asset acquired with the funds is reduced by the value of the amount of the payment and the depreciation loss is calculated on the reduced cost of the asset.

The interpretation statement sets out cases when the grant provisions apply, when they do not and the Commissioner's considerations when the situation may be unclear.
