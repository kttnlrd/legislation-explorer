# Deductibility of residential rental interest — main home exemption ¶10-816

The rule that disallows interest deductions for residential properties (¶10-814) does not apply to a person's main home. A deduction is not allowed for a person's mortgage interest under the private limitation under s DA 2(2) where the sole purpose of the property is the primary residence. However, where a portion of the home is rented out, a deduction may be permitted.

*Refs: s DA 2(2)*

## Main home not subject to interest limitation rules

A person's main home means, for a person, the one dwelling:

## that is used as a residence by the person (a home), and

## with which the person has the greatest connection, if they have more than one home.

The effect of this is that if a person lives in the main home and rents out part of it to flatmates, boarders, or for short-term stays, the person can claim an interest deduction for that portion of the home. The same applies to a person using part of the house for business use.

The Commissioner has confirmed in QB 23/08 "Income tax – deductibility of expenditure – renting to flatmates" that a dwelling that a taxpayer uses as their residence and rents out to a flatmate may still fall within the definition of a "main home". The item acknowledges that there may be some doubt as to whether a dwelling can be excluded from the interest limitation rules where considerable areas of the dwelling are for the exclusive use of a flatmate. However, the Commissioner's view is that the definition for the purpose of the interest limitation rules does not require that the owner has full use and enjoyment of the dwelling, nor does it use any language of apportionment. Provided that the dwelling is used as the owner's residence, it may qualify for the main home exclusion and therefore excluded from the interest limitation rules. The Commissioner's view is based on the following assumptions:

## the owner of the property is the person renting the dwelling to a flatmate, and the person is not

carrying on a rental business

## the property concerned has a single dwelling on it, and the land is not new build land as set out in s

DH 4

## the property in question is the person's only home, and

## the flatmate pays a market value rent.

While the main home exception is aimed at natural persons, shareholders of look-through companies (LTCs) who live in a residential property owned by the LTC can also utilise the exemption. The exception can also apply to trusts if a beneficiary of the trust uses the land as their main home and either:

## a principal settlor of the trust does not have a main home, or

## the place is the principal settlor's main home.

In other words, a property falls within the main home exception if the property is the main home of one of the trust's beneficiaries and a principal settlor does not have a different main home of their own.

For properties with more than one dwelling on the title, the main home exception would apply only to the dwelling that is used as the owner's main home. Examples of such properties would be a house and a "granny flat" that is let out, or a 2-storey house that is in 2 separate units.
