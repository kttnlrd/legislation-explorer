# Deductibility of goodwill ¶10-502

Generally, goodwill is a capital asset and consequently a payment for goodwill is non-deductible. See C of IR v LD Nathan & Co Ltd [1972] NZLR 209. However, a payment made to preserve goodwill may be deductible.

See Cooke (I of T) v Quick Shoe Repair Service (1949) 30 TC 460.

Example: A payment made by a taxpayer on an agreement to lease and option to purchase 3 properties was misdescribed as a "goodwill payment". The payment was part of the purchase price for the properties and was non-deductible. See Case P6 (1992) 14 NZTC 4,038.

Although not goodwill, a premium paid upon the grant of the right to use land is depreciable intangible property that may be eligible for a deduction by way of depreciation (¶13-034).
