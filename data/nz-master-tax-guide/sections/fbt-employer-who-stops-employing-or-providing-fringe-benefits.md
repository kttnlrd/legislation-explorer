# FBT — employer who stops employing or providing fringe benefits ¶21-125

Employers who stop employing staff and do not intend to replace them during the tax year must calculate FBT for the quarter in which they stop employing. Employers in this situation may apply one of the alternate rate calculations to the fringe benefits provided or pay FBT at 63.93%. If the single rate option is chosen, employers must calculate the FBT payable for the period from the start of the tax year to the date on which the employer stops employing staff. The final amount of FBT payable is determined by deducting any earlier payments made. If the alternate rate calculation is chosen, the quarter in which the employer stops employing is treated as a final quarter. The employer may choose between the full alternate rate, short form or pooled alternate rate options.

This does not apply if benefits continue to be provided to former employees.

The due date for filing of the final return and payment of FBT is 2 months after the end of the quarter in which the employer stops employing staff. For example, if an employer ceases to employ staff on 25 July 2026 (September 2026 quarter), the final FBT return and FBT payable are due on or before 30 November 2026.

A return is required even if an employer has not provided or granted any fringe benefits during the relevant quarter. A "nil return" is filed for these quarters.

IR's "Fringe benefit tax guide", IR 409, June 2025 provides guidance for employers who do not provide or do not intend to provide fringe benefits but still employ staff. Employers who have provided fringe benefits in any of the first 3 quarters but have since ceased to provide fringe benefits permanently must file quarterly returns up to and including the fourth quarter. They must notify IR that the fourth quarter return is the final return either by writing "final return" on the IR420, or via myIR, or by telephone (pp 7 and 30 of the IR guide).
