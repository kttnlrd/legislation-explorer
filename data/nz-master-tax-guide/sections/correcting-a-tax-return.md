# Correcting a tax return ¶2-112

Generally, once it is filed, a tax return cannot be amended or corrected by filing an amended return.

However, under s 113 of the TAA the Commissioner is granted a broad discretion to amend assessments to ensure their correctness, or following the application of the dispute resolution process.

In Westpac Securities NZ Ltd v C of IR (2014) 26 NZTC ¶21-118, the High Court held that where a taxpayer in error adopts a correct but disadvantageous tax position, the Commissioner has the power under s 113 to correct the taxpayer's assessment so as to negate that disadvantage by adopting another correct tax position. However, the Court said that whether the Commissioner considers her discretionary power should be exercised in that way was a matter outside the scope of the judgment and was therefore a matter for the Commissioner.

*Refs: s 113*

## Standard practice statement on amending assessments

Standard practice statement SPS 20/03, "Requests to amend assessments" sets out how the Commissioner will exercise the Commissioner's discretion to amend an assessment of income tax or goods and services tax (GST), without having to go through the disputes resolution process (¶4-130–¶4-232). The statement provides both direction to those IR staff delegated to use the discretion in s 113, and guidance to taxpayers and their advisors in formulating requests for amendments.

In considering s 113 requests, the Commissioner must be assured that the amendment the taxpayer seeks will ensure the assessment is correct when amended, even if it was also correct beforehand. If a request is made to correct an arithmetic, transposition or keying error made by either the taxpayer or Commissioner, the correction will be made without further consideration.

Where the Commissioner is not initially convinced that the amendment requested will result in a correct assessment, a decision must be made to commit IR's limited resources to considering the request further. In that case, the Commissioner will consider factors such as:

## whether the taxpayer is attempting to circumvent the statutory disputes procedure

## whether the subject-matter of the request could apply to other taxpayers

## how similar requests have been treated

## whether the request is a voluntary disclosure

## whether the taxpayer took their original position relying on advice from the Commissioner

## whether there has been a delay in making the request

## the size of the proposed amendment

## the taxpayer's compliance history, and

## any other considerations relevant to a particular case.

Where it is decided to apply additional resources (which the statement states will most often be the case), the merits of the request are then considered.

When the Commissioner is satisfied, the amendment requested will lead to the making of a correct assessment, that assessment will be made unless a relatively rare circumstance exists that suggests that, on balance, the integrity of the tax system will be undermined. These circumstances can include, for example:

## where the request is, or is part of, a tax avoidance arrangement

## where a taxpayer requests the Commissioner to amend an assessment from one correct tax position

to another position that is also correct.

Requests to correct obvious errors, such as arithmetic, transposition and keying errors, may be made to IR by telephone or in writing.

Requests to amend returns where the tax effect of the amendment requested is $10,000 or less may generally be made by telephone or in writing. Where the tax effect is greater than $10,000, the request must be made in writing.

Where a taxpayer makes an amendment request, all relevant information to support the request must be provided, including:

## the tax types and periods containing the tax position the taxpayer wishes to amend

## the decrease in tax liability that will result from any amendment (a request that results in an increase

in tax liability is a voluntary disclosure and is dealt with under SPS 19/02, see ¶14-130)

## a description of the original tax position, including the background circumstances and reasons the

original tax position was taken

## the nature of the amendment, including any relevant tax laws

## how and why the need for the amendment was identified

## where relevant, details of any incorrect advice given directly to the taxpayer by IR and how the

taxpayers relied on that advice

## the action required to ensure correctness

## all other relevant documents and records or other information supporting the amendment request,

and

## whether the taxpayer is aware of any relevant view published by the Commissioner and the extent to

which the taxpayer's amended tax position is consistent with that published view.

## Interest and penalties

Notice is provided to the taxpayer where a request for amendment leads to a fresh tax liability or increases an existing tax liability. Interest applies to any tax that has been underpaid or overpaid as a result of a genuine error. A shortfall penalty may be applied if the taxpayer has not met the requisite standards of care (¶14-100).

## Correcting minor errors in later returns

If a person's income tax, fringe benefit tax or GST assessment or return contains one or more errors and the total tax discrepancy caused by the error or errors is $1,000 or less, then the person is allowed to rectify the error or errors in the next return due after discovering them under s 113A.

In addition, a person may also rectify an error or errors in a subsequent return if, for that person, the error or errors are not material errors. For these purposes, the error or errors are not material if the total tax discrepancy caused by the error or errors in the return is equal to or less than the lower of $10,000 and 2% of the person's annual gross income or GST output tax, as applicable. However, note that a person will not be able to correct an error if they have applied the materiality threshold for the main purpose of delaying the payment of tax.

SPS 20/03 discusses the relationship between ss 113 and 113A, and states that while the Commissioner is not prevented from exercising the discretion under s 113 where the taxpayer is able to make the required correction themselves in a later period, the Commissioner's practice is generally not to expend resources in these circumstances. The taxpayer does not need to request that the Commissioner amend an assessment under s 113 to make the correction. However, in certain circumstances, the Commissioner will exercise the discretion under s 113, notwithstanding that the taxpayer is able to make the required correction using s 113A. For example, this may occur where a taxpayer has made a final return and there is no future return in which to make an adjustment, or where not correcting the error in the earlier period using s 113 will negatively impact an entitlement of the taxpayer, such as a Working for Families tax credit entitlement.
