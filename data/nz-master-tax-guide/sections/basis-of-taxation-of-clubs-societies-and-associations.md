# Basis of taxation of clubs, societies and associations ¶24-080

The definition of a company is very wide and applies to companies incorporated under the Companies Act 1993, unit trusts, incorporated societies and clubs and credit unions registered under the Friendly Societies and Credit Unions Act 1982. The effect of this definition is that any club, society, association or trade union is liable to be treated for tax purposes as if it were an ordinary company. If the association is not incorporated, it would be taxable as if it were an individual person. There are many special rules that create significant exceptions to this general position.

On 5 April 2022, the Incorporated Societies Act 2022 received Royal assent. The purpose of the Act is to put in place a modern framework of basic legal, governance, and accountability obligations for incorporated societies and the individuals who run them.

## Summary

A mutual association is taxed on certain types of transactions under the special basis of taxation provided for mainly in subpart HE (¶24-090).

There is a limited concession available to non-profit bodies in the form of a special deduction. Non-profit bodies may claim a deduction for the lesser of $1,000 or the amount that would be the net income of the body but for that section (¶24-120).

Amounts derived by a friendly society are generally treated as exempt income so long as those amounts are derived within the circle of membership (¶24-131).

Subpart CW contains a number of other exemptions for various bodies including sporting bodies (¶24-130), racing clubs and certain other racing organisations (¶24-136), scientific or industrial research promoters (¶24-135), district improvement bodies (¶24-137), veterinary services and herd improvement bodies (¶24-139).

It may be a requirement of these exemptions that an organisation's funds must not be used or be available for use by any member, proprietor, shareholder, or any of their associates, as applicable.

## Qualifying for an exemption

Apart from friendly societies (which are registered under a specified Act), there are 3 main conditions that an organisation has to meet if it is to qualify for any of the exemptions. First, the Commissioner must consider and approve any application (ie see the rules, certificate of incorporation, etc). Secondly, the main aims and activities of the organisation should meet the specific requirements of the section for which the exemption is requested. Thirdly, no part of the organisation's income or other funds is to be used or be available for the private use of any member or an associated person of any member.

When the club, society or association is unable to attract any of these exemptions, it becomes taxable on the same basis as any other taxpayer.

## IR's policy on taxation of clubs and societies

IR has consistently adopted the policy that clubs, societies and other similar non-profit organisations are not required to return as income amounts derived from certain member transactions. These are also known as trading "within the circle of membership" (¶24-090). Examples include membership subscriptions or levies. However, income arising from certain transactions with members (such as the sale of trading stock to members or the supply of services) appears to be subject to income tax in the normal way (IR9G, "Clubs or societies return guide 2025", March 2025 at 17).
