# Control over charity's business ¶24-210

There is a considerable practical limitation on the exemption for business charities. The exemption is not available if a person with some control over the charity's business is able to direct or divert an amount to the benefit or advantage of:

## if the charity is carrying on the business for another entity (the controlling entity), a person other than

the charity or the controlling entity except for a purpose of the charity or controlling entity, or

## in any other case, a person other than the charity except for a purpose of the charity.

If the charity fails to satisfy the limitation, the whole of the charity's business income is returned as assessable income. No regard is had to the quantum of the benefit, so that the exemption is lost no matter how large or small the benefit.

## Person treated as having some control

A person is treated as having some control over the business if:

## they can determine or materially influence the determination of the nature or the amount of the benefit

or advantage or the circumstances in which it is received, gained, achieved or derived, and

## their ability to determine or influence the benefit arises because they are:

## the settlor or trustee of the trust that carries on the business

## a shareholder or director of the company carrying on the business

## a settlor or trustee of a trust that is a shareholder of the company carrying on the business

## a person associated with any settlor, trustee, shareholder or director as mentioned above.

Note that, previously, s CW 42 excluded businesses carried on by a company for charitable purposes and owned by a charitable trust from the income tax exemption. This was because the former test referred to a person with some control over the charity's business being able to direct or divert an amount to their own benefit or advantage, and the charitable trust was deemed to have control over the business and to be able to direct or divert amounts from the business for its own benefit or advantage. Practically, this meant that a charity was not able to derive tax-exempt charitable business income in a subsidiary company, but that same income would be tax-exempt if derived directly by the shareholder charitable trust. This was an unintended consequence and the section was amended retrospectively from 1 July 2008. A similar amendment was made for wholly-owned subsidiaries of tertiary education institutions (¶24-275) and community housing entities (¶24-132).

## Definition of "settlor" and "business" for these purposes

For the purposes of this test, the settlor of a trust is not confined to the person who simply settles property on a trust. A settlor is deemed to include a person who disposes of an asset to the trust in circumstances in which there is a reservation of an interest in the asset or the asset will revert back (s CW 42(6)). The concept of a business is also given an extended meaning that applies if the trustee derives rental income from an asset that will revert back, or if there is a reservation of an interest in the asset to the person who disposed of it to the trust. In such a situation, the holding of the asset for the derivation of rental income is deemed to constitute the carrying on of a business by the trustee. In the absence of any such specific provision, it would have been arguable that the mere holding of rental property does not amount to the carrying on of a business. When there is a person whose association comes within any of the above categories, the business will fail to attract the exemption for business income of a charity. There must, however, be a nexus between the person concerned and the benefit received. This nexus is to the effect that by reason of the relationship to the trust or business, the person is able to materially influence the nature or amount of the benefit or income or the circumstances in which it is to be received.

Example: If the settlor or a trustee is able to receive a benefit from the trust or can materially influence the nature or amount of any such benefits, there will be no exemption under s 61(27) of the Income Tax Act 1976 [now s CW 42]. Whether or not these parties actually benefit is not relevant. See C of IR v Dick (2001) 20 NZTC 17,396.

## Persons treated as having no control

A person who provides professional services to a charity is not treated as having some control over the charity's business if the ability to determine or influence the benefit arises only because they provide professional services as part of carrying on a professional public practice. This also applies to a statutory trustee company, the Public Trust and the M#ori Trustee providing professional services.

## Benefit or advantage

A benefit or advantage may include an amount regardless of whether it is convertible into money. It also includes retaining or reserving an interest in an asset that has been disposed of to a charitable trust.

No benefit or advantage arises when the benefit is in the form of interest on a loan made at current commercial rates, bearing in mind the nature and term of the loan. For this purpose, the Commissioner uses the current commercial rates published by the Reserve Bank as a yardstick.
