# Calculation and satisfaction of tax liability ¶2-105

New Zealand income tax is imposed on the worldwide income (subject to some exceptions) of all New Zealand residents whether they are companies or individuals. Income tax is also imposed on New Zealand- sourced income of non-residents, although the liability may be reduced by the provision of a double tax agreement.

The method for determining a person's income tax liability is dealt with by s BC 1. This section makes a distinction between:

## a non-filing taxpayer (income tax liability is the total tax withheld from annual gross income)

## a filing taxpayer (income tax liability calculated under ss BC 2–BC 6), and

## a filing taxpayer with schedular income (income tax liability calculation is modified by s BC 7).

The tax liability for each class of person is appropriately defined. The person's income tax liability will be satisfied by crediting any tax paid or withheld, or by the payment of terminal tax.

Satisfaction of the income tax liability of the person for the income year is addressed by s BC 8. That provision states that the liability may be satisfied by crediting any tax paid or withheld for a tax year. If the credits are insufficient to satisfy the total amount of the income tax liability, the person must pay terminal tax.

*Refs: s BC 1, ss BC 2, s BC 7, s BC 8*

## Annual gross income

The annual gross income of a person for a tax year is the total of the amounts of assessable income under s BD 1 that are allocated to the corresponding income year. This proposition is stated by s BC 2.

Determination of annual gross income is a step in ascertaining the person's income tax liability for the income year.

## Assessable income

Assessable income is income that is not exempt income, excluded income or non-residents' foreign-sourced income; see further at ¶1-055. Assessable income is described in s BD 1, and allocation to income years is dealt with by s BD 3.

## Annual total deduction

Section BC 3 provides that a person's annual total deduction for a tax year is the total of the deductions that are allocated to the corresponding income year.

Deductions are described in s BD 2 and allocation to income years is dealt with by s BD 4.

## Net income and tax loss

A person's net income or tax loss for a tax year is defined by s BC 4.

Where a person's annual gross income is more than their annual total deduction, the difference is the person's net income. It is also recognised that net income for the income year can be zero. This is where the person's annual gross income equals annual total deduction. An example of where this can occur is where profits and losses are offset within a group of companies.

A person's tax loss for the income year arises when their annual total deduction is more than their annual gross income. The difference is the person's tax loss for the tax year with the person having a net income of zero. The treatment of a tax loss is that the person includes it under Pt I in their tax loss for the tax year that may be:

## subtracted from net income in a future tax year

## made available to another person to subtract from that person's present or future net income, or

## used in other ways.

## Taxable income

The taxable income of a person for a tax year is determined by subtracting any available tax losses of the person in accordance with Pt I from the person's net income. See s BC 5.

An available tax loss will be a loss balance carried forward from an earlier tax year or the tax loss of another company allowed to be subtracted from net income. Deduction of the loss is the final step in determining taxable income.

## Non-filing taxpayer

The definition of the income tax liability for a tax year of a "non-filing taxpayer" is contained within s BC 1(1).

The liability is the total of the tax withheld from income included in the taxpayer's annual gross income for the year. In other words, tax deducted at source becomes the year's tax liability.

The concept of a "non-filing taxpayer" is defined in s YA 1. It refers to:

## a non-resident entertainer whose only New Zealand-sourced income is from schedular payments and

who chooses not to file a return

## a non-resident who pays non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) as a final tax, or

## a person whose only New Zealand-sourced income is derived as a non-resident seasonal worker.

## Filing taxpayer with schedular income

The income tax liability for an income year of a filing taxpayer with schedular income is determined in accordance with ss BC 1(3) and BC 7. A distinction is drawn between a person with one type of schedular income and multiple types of schedular income. The person must calculate an income tax liability for each class of schedular income as if each category was the person's only income. The income tax so calculated is added to the tax on income that is not schedular income (as discussed above) to give the income tax liability for the tax year.

The concept of schedular income is defined (in s YA 1) to mean income of the following types:

## schedular policyholder base income of a life insurer

## category A income of a group investment fund

## income of a multi-rate PIE or a portfolio tax rate entity

## attributed PIE income derived by a natural person investor in a multi-rate PIE

## non-resident passive income for which NRWT is a final tax

## income of a non-resident general insurer that is not a company

## income of a non-resident shipper

## income of Lloyd's of London from life insurance premiums

## income to which s GB 3BAB (Arrangements to inject income into companies carrying forward loss

balances) applies

## lump sum accident compensation earnings-related payments and personal services rehabilitation

payments

## lump sum payments from the Ministry of Social Development

## income subject to the minor beneficiary rule in s HC 35

## income subject to the corporate beneficiary rule in s HC 38, and

## an assessable withdrawal amount subject to transfer scheme withholding tax (from 1 April 2026).

The feature common to these classes of income is that they are dealt with in a specific manner such as being subject to separate return requirements or to different tax rates. This makes it necessary to preserve the separate identity of these classes of income. The income tax liability for a tax year of a person who has any schedular income is stated by s BC 7 to be the total of:

## the person's schedular income tax liability for the year, and

## the amount that would be their income tax liability for the year if the person had no schedular income.

If the person has one type of schedular income, the person's schedular income tax liability is the tax liability on that schedular income. If the person has multiple types of schedular income, the schedular income tax liability is the total income tax liabilities calculated on each class of schedular income. For example, s RB 3 states that tax for each class of non-resident passive income that is schedular income, the schedular income tax liability must be calculated in accordance with the formula set out in that section.
