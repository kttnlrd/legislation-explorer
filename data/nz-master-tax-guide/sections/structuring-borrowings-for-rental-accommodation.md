# Structuring borrowings for rental accommodation ¶7-521

## Note: Interest incurred on loans for residential rental properties have limited deductible from 1 October

2021 to 31 March 2025 under the interest limitation rules. The ability to deduct interest expenses on residential rental properties was disallowed unless it applies to "grandparented" loans. Deductibility for interest on these loans was being reduced on a sliding scale depending on the tax year in which the interest was incurred (¶10-814). From 1 April 2025 interest will again be deductible in relation to residential rental properties.

It is important for persons seeking to deduct interest costs on rental property to structure their borrowings carefully. Interest may be deductible providing the property is used for deriving rental income, is not a disallowed residential property, and the objective of achieving a capital gain on sale does not prevent that result (¶10-650). There have been a number of historical tax cases on this point. See Case R8 (1994) 16 NZTC 6,049, Case S17 (1995) 17 NZTC 7,127, Case S87 (1996) 17 NZTC 7,545 and Case T16 (1997) 18 NZTC 8,095. All these cases were decided when the tax laws around borrowing for rental properties were quite different. At all times, when structuring borrowings, the general anti-avoidance provisions should be considered to ensure that the proposed arrangement does not fall foul of the rules.

In public binding ruling BR Pub 12/03 "Deductibility of break fee paid by a landlord to exit early from a fixed interest rate loan on sale of rental property", the Commissioner states the repayment of the loan will trigger a base price adjustment under the financial arrangements rules. The amount of the break fee is included as part of the "consideration" in the base price adjustment formula. A negative base price adjustment is deemed to be interest expenditure. Companies may be entitled to an automatic deduction under s DB 7 of the IT07.

For those taxpayers who do not qualify for an automatic deduction, a deduction will be permitted under ss DB 6 and DA 1 (the general permission) (¶10-190).
