# Introduction to assessments ¶4-010

For income tax purposes, an assessment is defined as an assessment of tax by either a taxpayer or the Commissioner, any amendment made by the Commissioner to an assessment and an assessment of net loss, terminal tax or a refund. Under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, an assessment means an assessment of a GST refund.

Taxpayers who are required to file income tax returns (¶2-010) must assess their own tax liabilities. A notice of self-assessment is required with the taxpayer's return of income. These taxpayers are responsible for:

## considering the facts relating to their own financial affairs

## interpreting and applying the law to those facts

## determining the amount of tax owing, and

## making that determination with an appropriate degree of finality.

The assessment is made on the date that Inland Revenue (IR) receives the return.

For individuals to whom Pt 3, subpart 3B of the Tax Administration Act 1994 (TAA) applies (¶2-016), they are treated as having made an assessment on the date their pre-populated account is finalised under s 22H.

Self-assessment does not apply to a taxpayer if the taxpayer has been assessed by the Commissioner.

The liability for tax exists whether or not a taxpayer assesses their own liability. Consequently, the late filing penalty continues to apply.

The Commissioner has the power to issue default assessments or assessments following an investigation.

*Refs: s 22H, Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, Tax Administration Act 1994*
