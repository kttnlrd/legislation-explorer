# Income under share-lending arrangements ¶5-288

Any replacement payments derived under a share-lending arrangement are income under s CD 54. Any share-lending collateral derived under a share-lending arrangement is excluded income under s CX 54.

For share sales under a share-lending arrangement entered into on or after 30 March 2025, s EA 5 allocates income from disposal of an original share under a share-lending arrangement to the following income year if the identical share is acquired during that later income year.

Specific share-lending rules were introduced to allow "qualifying" share-lending transactions to be taxed on the basis of economic substance (a loan) rather than legal form (a sale of shares). Returning share transfers that meet certain criteria (being share-lending arrangements) are taxed as if they were loans and not disposals of shares. Returning share transfers that do not qualify as share-lending arrangements are taxed as disposals of shares. For a discussion of what constitutes a qualifying share-lending transaction and a returning share transfer, see ¶17-116. For deductions, see ¶10-660.

Although share-lending arrangements are treated as if they were loans for tax purposes, they are excepted financial arrangements (¶6-065). Share-lending arrangements also fall outside the rules contained in s GC 1 (Sale or other disposition of trading stock for inadequate consideration) and subpart ED (Valuation of excepted financial arrangements) (¶33-146, ¶9-060 and ¶9-065).

When a replacement payment is made under a share-lending arrangement, the share user must provide the share supplier with a replacement payment statement in accordance with s 30B of the Tax Administration Act 1994 (TAA).

An anti-avoidance provision ensures that taxpayers do not attempt to structure transactions that fall outside the returning share transfer definition (s GB 49).

For further discussion and examples of the operation of the share-lending rules, see ¶185-120 at 86.
