# Quarterly filing for FBT ¶21-140

Employers are usually required to file FBT returns on a quarterly basis unless they qualify for and elect to use the small business option (¶21-145) or the close company option (¶21-150). Quarterly filers have 2 options to calculate their FBT liability in quarters 1 to 3:

## 63.93% single rate, or

## 49.25% alternate rate.

## Single rate option

Under the single rate option, employers pay FBT at a rate of 63.93% of the taxable value of fringe benefits provided to employees in all 4 quarters. This is the most administratively easy option, but it can result in overpayment if employees earn less than $180,000 a year. The rate of 63.93% is a "gross-up" of the top personal marginal tax rate of 39%. Employers using the single rate option can still choose to do the alternate rate calculation for the final quarter.

## Alternate rate option

Under the alternate rate option, employers can choose to pay FBT at the rate of 63.93% or 49.25% of the taxable value of fringe benefits provided to employees in the first 3 quarters. If the entity has elected and paid FBT using the alternate rate option in any of the first 3 quarters, it must complete the alternate rate calculation option in the fourth (and final) quarter. FBT paid in the first 3 quarters is then subtracted from the fourth quarter calculation to arrive at the amount payable.

## Full alternate rate calculation

For the fourth (and final) quarter, a detailed calculation is required to determine the amount of FBT payable.

This is like a square-up calculation and requires fringe benefits to be attributed to particular employees (¶21-155 and ¶21-165).

## Short form alternate rate calculation

As an alternative to performing a full alternative rate calculation, the highest rate of 63.93% can be applied to all attributed fringe benefits (¶21-155).

FBT paid in the first 3 quarters is then subtracted from the fourth quarter calculation to arrive at the amount payable.

## Pooled alternate rate option

A further option for the fourth quarter, as an alternative to the full alternate rate calculation, is to use the pooled alternate rate option (from the 2021–22 income year). The pooled alternate rate option is available to quarterly filers, as well as those employers using the small business and close company options.

Under this option, employers can apply a rate of 49.25% on attributed benefits provided to an employee if all of the following applies:

## the taxable value of all fringe benefits provided to the employee is $13,400 or less, and

## the cash pay of the employee is $160,000 or less.

For each employee that does not meet the monetary thresholds outlined above, the rate of 63.93% must generally be used for attributed benefits. However, the employer may elect to use the 49.25% rate on

attributed benefits if the employee's all-inclusive pay (¶21-165) does not exceed $129,681. To this end, 2 pools may need to be created for attributed benefits, one for the 63.93% rate and another for the 49.25% rate.

FBT paid in the first 3 quarters is then subtracted from the fourth quarter calculation to arrive at the amount payable.

## Note: A retrospective amendment contained in the Taxation (Annual Rates for 2025–26, Compliance

Simplification, and Remedial Measures) Bill will change the threshold for all-inclusive pay ($129,681) discussed above to $130,724. The change is expected to apply from the 2025–26 and later income years.
