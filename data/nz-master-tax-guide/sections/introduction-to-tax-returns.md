# Introduction to tax returns ¶2-010

The provisions governing the liability to furnish returns of income are contained in Pt 3 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 (TAA).

Every taxpayer must furnish a return of income for a tax year. An exception applies from 1 April 2025 — entities that derive only exempt income are not required to file income tax returns.

For qualifying individuals, a pre-populated account is prepared by Inland Revenue (IR), containing the income information IR holds for the taxpayer. This pre-populated account is used to create an assessment and the taxpayer's tax position is effectively automatically calculated. Refunds are paid out without taxpayers having to request them, and certain amounts of tax to pay are written off.

The rules are discussed in detail in ¶2-016 below.

Companies and persons carrying on business for any part of an income year must furnish returns, regardless of whether they have incurred a profit or a loss. Equally important is the capacity in which an individual has received income. For example, an individual might derive income personally, as the agent for a non-resident, in the capacity of trustee or as a member of a partnership. Different tax returns are required in each case.

See ¶2-015.

A return must be signed, contain all necessary information, and be accompanied by all necessary documents. The obligation is discharged only when the return is received by an IR office.
