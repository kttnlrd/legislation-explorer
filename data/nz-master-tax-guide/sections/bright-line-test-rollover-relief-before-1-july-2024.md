# Bright-line test — rollover relief before 1 July 2024 ¶7-540

## Note: This commentary covers the position relating to the rollover relief under the bright-line test applicable

to residential land transfers from 1 April 2022 and before 1 July 2024.

Certain land transactions are subject to rollover relief under the bright-line test. Under the bright-line test (¶7-049 and ¶7-050) when land is gifted or disposed of for below market value, the law deems the gross income from the disposal to be the market value of the land. However, it was recognised that in some circumstances the disposal transaction may not be the appropriate taxing point and that ignoring the disposal transaction is justified for the purposes of the bright-line test. Alternatively, there may be justification for partial relief by limiting the value of the deemed disposal so that the transaction is taxed based on the actual amount of consideration rather than market value. In both situations the bright-line date is not reset for the parties to the transaction. Put another way, the bright-line period does not start again.

Rollover relief is not an exemption from income tax. It defers the taxing point to a later disposal of the land. The recipient of the land is deemed to step into the shoes of the transferor and the original date of acquisition applies to the recipient, along with the cost base of the transferor.

Where residential land is transferred in a number of contexts, the transfer is treated as being at cost to the transferor (or at the amount paid by the transferee, if that is greater). The transferor is treated as having disposed of the residential land for that consideration and the transferee is treated as having acquired the residential land for the same amount.

If a rollover relief provision applies to a transfer of residential land, the recipient of the land is subject to the same bright-line test as the transferor was (that is, either the 2-year, 5-year or 10-year bright-line test).

Generally, rollover relief applies to transfers of residential land on or after 1 April 2022 and this commentary covers the rules before 1 July 2024. For detailed commentary on the rollover relief applicable after 1 July 2024, see ¶7-545.

## Amalgamations

Under the bright-line test there is rollover relief for amalgamations (former s FO 10(6)). This rollover relief extends the existing rollover relief for amalgamations to property that is revenue account property of an amalgamating company due to the application of the bright-line test (¶20-108).

## Family trusts

Rollover relief applies to transfers to and from a family trust that is considered a "rollover trust" on or after 1 April 2022, provided certain criteria are met (former s CB 6AB).

## Key definitions

The definition of "rollover trust" is, at the time of a relevant transfer (former s CB 6AB(5)):

## all principal settlors are beneficiaries

## all principal settlors are close family associates, and

## all beneficiaries are close family beneficiaries.

A "principal settlor" is defined under s YA 1 as a settlor whose settlements for a trust are the greatest by market value, or greatest in equal share where there is more than one settlor.

Two persons are "close family associates" if one or more of the following applies (former s CB 6AB(7)):

## they are within 4 degrees of blood relationship

## they are married, in a civil union, or in a de facto relationship, or

## one person is within 4 degrees of blood relationship to the other person's spouse, civil union partner,

or de facto partner.

For the relevant trust, "close family beneficiary" is defined as a beneficiary that is at least one of the following (former s CB 6AB(6)):

## a principal settlor

## a close family associated of another beneficiary who is also a principal settlor

## a trustee of another trust, if at least one beneficiary of the other trust is a close family associate of a

beneficiary of the relevant trust

## a charity registered under the Charities Act 2005

## any association, club, institution, society, organisation, or trust not carried on for the private profit

of any person whose funds are applied wholly or principally to any civic, community, charitable, philanthropic, religious, benevolent, or cultural purposes, whether in New Zealand or elsewhere, and in the case of it having one principal settlor only, the trust has one or more beneficiaries who are close family associates of the principal settlor

## from 1 March 2023, a company in which a 50% or more voting interest (or a market value interest of

at least 50%, if a market value circumstance exists) is owned by a beneficiary that is a principal settlor or a close family associate of another beneficiary that is a principal settlor.

## Transfers to family trusts

For transfers of residential land to a family trust on or after 1 April 2022, rollover relief applies when at the time of transfer, all of the following criteria are satisfied (former s CB 6AB(1)(a)):

## the relevant trust is a rollover trust

## each transferor is both a settlor and a beneficiary of the trust

## at least one of the transferors is a principal settlor of the trust.

## Transfers from family trusts

Rollover relief applies to a transfer of residential land, or disallowed residential property, out of a family trust either back to the principal settlor or the group of settlors that originally transferred the land to the trust (provided that, for a group, at least one of the settlors is a principal settlor), even if the settlor(s) is not an "original settlor" (as was required by the originally enacted provisions, but changed with retrospective effect).

For transfers of residential land from a family trust on or after 1 April 2022, there are 2 separate tests for determining whether rollover relief applies.

The first test is relevant when the transferees had originally transferred land to the family trust (former s CB 6AB(2)(a)). Under this test, rollover relief applies when at the time the trustee transfer the land to the transferees:

## the trust is a rollover trust

## all transferees are beneficiaries of the trust

## at least one of the transferees is a principal settlor of the trust, and

## if there is more than one transferee, the transferees acquired proportionally the same amount of land

they originally transferred to the trust.

From 29 March 2024, former s CB 6AB(2B) applies to modify the proportionality requirement in the first test above for when a settlor who had previously transferred the land to the trustee dies. This means the proportionality requirement is met and rollover relief still applies, provided that the transferees receive at least the same proportion of the land back from the trustee as they had previously transferred. See example in Tax Information Bulletin ¶364-102d Vol 36, No 4, May 2024 at 104 to demonstrate the application of the rules in a scenario involving land transferred back to surviving settlors.

Resettlements of family trusts

Rollover relief applies in resettlements where trustees transfer land to a new trust. Rollover relief is available when residential land or disallowed residential property held in a qualifying family trust is resettled onto another family trust, provided certain conditions were met.

To qualify for the rollover relief, both trusts must be rollover trusts, and either (former s CB 6AB(1)(b)):

## the trust receiving the residential land or disallowed residential property has the same beneficiaries

as the head trust (that is the trust from which the land is transferred as part of the resettlement), or

## all natural person beneficiaries are either the same, or close family associates of a principal settlor of

the head trust.

The rollover trust receiving the settlement of land may not necessarily have the same beneficiaries as the head trust. However, provided the above conditions are met, this would not preclude the resettlement from qualifying for rollover relief. For example, if an additional generation is added to the resettled trust's beneficiaries, rollover relief would still be available if the close family beneficiary requirements were satisfied.

Rollover relief can apply even if the principal settlor of the head trust is deceased.

## M#ori family trusts

Former s CB 6AC provides rollover relief under the bright-line test for transfers of residential land to or from a "M#ori rollover trust" occurring on or after 1 April 2022.

A "M#ori rollover trust" means at the time of a relevant transfer (former s CB 6AC(4)):

## all beneficiaries of the trust are members of the same iwi or hap#, or descendants of the same t#puna

(living or dead), and

## the land in question is subject to Te Ture Whenua Maori Act 1993.

A "M#ori trustee" is defined as a trustee of a trust that is either a M#ori authority or eligible to elect to be a M#ori authority (former s CB 6AC(5)).

## Transfers to M#ori family trusts

When residential land is transferred to the trustee of a M#ori rollover trust, rollover relief applies if (former s CB 6AC(1)(a)):

## the transferors are settlors of the trust, and

## at the time they transfer the land to the trustee, they are also beneficiaries of the trust.

## Transfers from M#ori family trusts

Rollover relief is available for when trustees of a M#ori family trust transfer residential land back to the settlors, provided that the settlors are beneficiaries of the trust. As with the rules for family trusts, the proportional ownership interest must be the same as prior to the original settlement. In this case, rollover relief applies when (former s CB 6AC(2)):

## the trust is a M#ori rollover trust

## the transferees acquire proportionally the same amount of land they had transferred to the trustees,

and

## all transferees are settlors of the trust.

Similar to the rollover relief for family trusts above (former s CB 6AB(2B)), from 29 March 2024, former s CB 6AC(2B) applies to modify the proportionality requirement for when a settlor who had previously transferred the land to the M#ori trustee dies. This means the proportionality requirement is met and rollover relief still applies, provided that the transferees receive at least the same proportion of the land back from the M#ori trustee as they had previously transferred.

Transfers to or from different capacity

For the purposes of the 2 types of transfers involving M#ori rollover trusts, former s CB 6AC(3) provides that the transferors and transferees may have different capacities. For example, a transferee may be a settlor of the trust in their personal capacity and be a beneficiary as an LTC owner, or they may have transferred the land to the trust in their personal capacity and acquire it back from the trust as a partner in a partnership.

## Resettlements

Former s CB 6AC(1)(b) provides similar rollover relief for resettlements of M#ori rollover trusts as those afforded to family trust resettlements. In this case, rollover relief applies if all the beneficiaries of the resettled trust are the same as for the first trust and this includes the situation where one of the settlors of the first trust is deceased.

## Look-through companies

Due to the look-through nature of LTCs, relief applies to transfers of residential land to or from an LTC (former s CB 6AB(4)). The rules are intended to apply where each person transferring residential land to the LTC or acquiring it from the LTC has the same ownership interest in the land before and after the transfer.

No intervening transfer to a third party may take place.

In these cases, the person's bright-line acquisition date for when the land is ultimately disposed of to a third party is the bright-line acquisition date they first had for the land.

Rollover relief applies to transfers that are effected on or after 1 April 2022.

Note that former s CB 6AB(8) provides that rollover relief does not apply when shares in an LTC are transferred to a rollover trust. Rollover relief also does not apply when LTC shares are transferred back to an original settlor.

## Partnerships

Rollover relief applies to transfers of residential land to or from a partnership (former s CB 6AB(4)). The relief applies where each partner transferring land to, or acquiring land from the partnership, has the same ownership interest proportion before and after the transfer.

The original bright-line acquisition date will apply when the residential land is eventually disposed of to a third party.

Rollover relief applies to transfers that are effected on or after 1 April 2022.

## Cost of land subject to rollover relief

Full rollover relief is only available for the transactions discussed above when the transfer is made for an amount of consideration that is less than or equal to the total cost of residential land to the transferor. When this is the case, rollover relief will deem the transfer to be at cost.

Partial rollover relief applies if the transfer is for an amount that is more than the transferor's cost but less than market value of the land at date of transfer. Partial relief deems the cost to be the amount of consideration paid, rather than deeming the transfer to take place at cost or market value. See former ss FC 9B and FC 9C.

Irrespective of whether the transaction qualifies for full or partial rollover relief, the recipient is treated as having the same bright-line acquisition date as the transferor. The effect is that the bright-line start date is not reset by the transfer.

Where partial relief applies and where more than cost is paid for the transfer, the excess gain is taxable income under the bright-line test.

## Companies within a consolidated group

Transfers of residential land between companies within a wholly-owned group of companies, consolidated for tax purposes, also qualify for rollover relief. The commencement of the bright-line period is not reset when residential land is transferred between consolidated companies. Rollover relief applies irrespective of consideration for the transfer.

## Changes in co-ownership

Remedial amendments ensured that transfers of residential land that effect changes in co-ownership do not reset the bright-line commencement date. The date of acquisition will remain the same as long as there is no change to the person's proportional, or notional proportional, ownership. The remedial amendments apply to all residential land transactions, not just those that qualify for rollover relief.

It is possible for a person to hold land jointly in several different ways, for example under joint tenancy, as tenants in common, in partnership with other persons or in capacity as a trustee. A person can transfer land to themselves by changing the form of co-ownership, or transferring the land from co-ownership to sole ownership, and vice versa. For a discussion on the effect of changes to co-ownership under the land disposal rules, see ¶7-300.

For the purposes of the bright-line rules, while registration of a transfer instrument has the effect of changing the legal form of co-ownership, it does not necessarily result in a disposal. Generally, registration triggers the start date of the bright-line period. To remedy this issue, former ss CB 6A(5B) and CB 6A(5C) provide that as long as the economic ownership of the land has not changed, the bright-line start date is not reset by the transfer.

When joint tenancy is converted to tenants in common, former s CB 6A(5B) provides that to the extent that the residential land is held as tenants in common in equal shares to the all joint owners, and previously the land was held in equal nominal shares, the bright-line test acquisition date is the date the joint tenancy was acquired.

Where the transfer results in a change in the co-ownership proportion the bright-line test acquisition date reset only applies to the ownership share that has changed hands, for example where the co-ownership changed from 50:50 to 75:25, the bright-line test reset would apply to the newly acquired 25% share transferred. See former s CB 6A(5D).

## Transfers involving a Treaty of Waitangi claim

Settlements under the Treaty of Waitangi can be a multi-staged process. The Crown transfers the land to a single governance entity that may act on behalf of several different claimant groups (usually different hap# or a collective of iwi groups). The governance entity then transfers the settlement assets, including residential land, to different members of the claimant group in accordance with the deed of settlement or the settlement legislation.

Previously there was reduced risk of the bright-line test being triggered under the 2-year bright-line test, or the 5-year bright-line test, but under the 10-year bright-line test there is a greater risk. Therefore, former s CB 6AE applies to provide rollover relief when:

## the land is subject to the Treaty of Waitangi, and

## is transferred to a trustee of a trust who:

## is a M#ori authority (or eligible to be a M#ori authority), and

## on behalf of the claimants, receives and manages assets that are transferred from the Crown as part

of the settlement of a claim under the Treaty of Waitangi, in accordance with the requirements of s HF 2(3)(e)(i).

The rollover relief applies to the transfer of the residential land to a member of the claimant group. The recipient trustee has the transferor's acquisition date.

The transfer of residential land in settlement of a Treaty of Waitangi claim may be treated by the transferee as an acquisition at market value for bright-line test purposes (former s FC 9D).

## Subsequent disposal of a Treaty of Waitangi settlement

A person who subsequently disposes of residential land the person acquired in settlement of a Treaty of Waitangi claim may have income under the bright-line test. To calculate the amount of the income, the cost of the residential land is taken to be market value of the land at the time it was transferred from the Crown to the person (former s FC 9D).
