# Exception for genuine contracts ¶23-144

On the face of it, s GB 23 would appear to grant to the Commissioner very broad powers to control the level of remuneration paid to relatives (¶23-142). However, there is an important exception to the effect that the Commissioner may not rely upon these powers when the parties are employed under a genuine contract of employment or there is a genuine contract of partnership. For these purposes, a genuine contract of employment or partnership is one in which:

## the contract is in writing and is signed by all of the parties to it

## all of the parties are aged 20 or more at the time of signing

## the contract is binding for at least 3 years (except for death, bankruptcy or dissolution by the court in

the case of a partnership contract)

## each party to the contract has real and effective control of the income to which they are entitled under

the contract

## each party to a partnership contract has real and effective control or liability for their share of profits

or losses respectively under the contract, and

## no part of the income or share of profits of the relative is a disposition for inadequate consideration.

Should the contract fail to be regarded as genuine, the Commissioner may reallocate the remuneration or share of profits paid under the agreement (¶23-142).
