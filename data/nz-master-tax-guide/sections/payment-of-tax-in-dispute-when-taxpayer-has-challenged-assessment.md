# Payment of tax in dispute when taxpayer has challenged assessment ¶4-230

Briefly, a taxpayer who has challenged an assessment is not required to pay:

## the deferrable tax

## a shortfall penalty in respect of any tax in dispute, or

## the interest accruing on the deferrable tax or shortfall penalty

until the due date for payment of that deferrable tax. Deferrable tax is the tax assessed as payable under a tax law, or the GST payable by the taxpayer on a due date, and for which the taxpayer has filed a challenge.

However, payment of all tax in dispute may be required if the Commissioner considers that, in the event the taxpayer's challenge does not succeed, there is a significant risk that the tax in dispute will not be paid.

If the taxpayer is successful, the Commissioner must refund (or pay to, or apply on behalf of, the taxpayer) the tax in dispute paid as required and any accrued interest. This refund, payment or application must be made on the day after the day that the final liability is determined.

See ss 128(2)–(4) and 138I(2) of the TAA.

Tax in dispute may be provided to the Commissioner by acceptable security if the taxpayer cannot pay the sum in cash (s 7A of the TAA). #Note: The previous rules for deferrable tax meant that when there were consequential amendments to related taxpayers, those taxpayers could not defer their tax. For example, a company may have part of its expenditure disallowed as a deduction, which alters the amount of a loss offset made to another company in the same group. In that situation, both entities must object to or challenge the assessments. This results in increased administrative and compliance costs for those taxpayers and lengthens the disputes process.

With effect from 30 March 2021, an amendment now includes consequential amendments resulting from an amended assessment that is in the disputes process within the definition of deferrable tax.

*Refs: ss 128(2), s 7A*

## Rate of interest

Interest continues to accrue on deferrable tax, even though a taxpayer has or may have no liability at the time to pay the deferrable tax (s 120T of the TAA).

Refundable tax in dispute is treated as overpaid tax for the purposes of the interest regime under Pt 7 (ss 128(3) and 138I(3) of the TAA). This means that the amount of interest payable by the Commissioner on overpaid tax and by a taxpayer on unpaid tax is calculated for each day in the interest period in accordance with the formula (s 120E of the TAA): t × r ÷ 365 where: t is the unpaid or overpaid tax on which interest is payable, and r is the Commissioner's paying rate or the taxpayer's paying rate applying on the day.

For the current interest rates, see CCH, New Zealand Tax Rates, Dates and Depreciation Rates 2026 (¶50-083).

Law: TAA ss 3(1) ("deferrable tax"), 7A, 120E, 120T, 128, 138I
