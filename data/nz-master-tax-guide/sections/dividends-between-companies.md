# Dividends between companies ¶16-800

The general proposition is that dividends are a class of assessable income in the hands of the shareholder who derives the dividends. There are some modifications if the shareholder is also a company.

## Wholly-owned group companies

Dividends paid between members of a wholly-owned group of companies (companies with 100% common voting interests and, where relevant, market value interests) are exempt income (s CW 10). Companies do not have to have the same tax balance date or satisfy the Commissioner that no timing advantage will occur.

For the exemption to apply, the dividend must also be:

## derived by a company that is not a foreign company, or a New Zealand resident company treated as

being non-resident under a DTA, and

## derived by a company that is part of the same wholly-owned group at the time the dividend is derived,

and

## derived from a company that is neither a foreign company nor a company that can derive only exempt

income and must not be:

## before 1 April 2022, derived by a local authority from any council-controlled organisation, energy

company or port company or subsidiary of a port company (see note below)

## derived by a friendly society from a company registered as an insurer under the Accident Insurance

Act 1998 that is under the friendly society's control, or

## derived by a trustee in trust for any sickness, accident or death benefit fund from a company

registered as an insurer under the Accident Insurance Act 1998 that is under the control of that trustee.

## Dual resident wholly-owned companies

For dual resident companies, where the dividend is declared within a wholly-owned group, the dividend is not allocated to the income year in which the recipient company receives the dividend. In these cases, the dividend income is deferred to the "DRCD deferral date" (which is the second anniversary of the date the dividend was paid). This gives the company the opportunity to correct their tax residence within 2 years to ensure the domestic dividend exemption applies. The amended deferral of income rule only applies to those dividends that meet the criteria of the domestic dividend exemption in s CW 10(1), (1B), (5) and (6).

For a detailed discussion of the amended dividend rules and corporate migration rules, see Tax Information Bulletin ¶356-102 Vol 35, No 6, July 2023 at 54.

## Close company beneficiary income

From 1 April 2024, s HC 38 provides that beneficiary income derived by certain close companies is to be treated as trustee income and taxed at 39% (¶25-120). The dividend exemption in s CW 10 overrides the corporate beneficiary rule in s HC 38 (s HC 38(3)(b)). This provision is relevant for a wholly-owned group of close companies owned by a trust. For example, company A and company B are both wholly-owned by a trust and both are beneficiaries of the trust. If company A pays a dividend to the trust and the trust subsequently distributes the dividend as beneficiary income to company B, this dividend income is exempt income for company B and s HC 38 does not apply.

Local authority dividends

For the 2022–23 and later income years, dividends derived by local authorities are exempt from income tax and are no longer excluded under s CW 10(3). Dividends derived by a local authority from a council- controlled organisation, port company, or energy company are exempt income under s CW 10B.

In addition, dividends derived by a holding company which is a council-controlled organisation from a council-controlled organisation with 100% public ownership are exempt income.

Before the amendments, the income of a local authority was exempt from income tax except for income earned from a CCO, a port company or an energy company. The amendments are put in place to ensure that the tax treatment of dividends for these entities is consistent with the tax treatment of dividends with other exempt entities and the integrity of local government taxation is improved.

## Dividend from foreign company

Dividends derived from a foreign company by a New Zealand company are also exempt income (s CW 9).

There are a number of exclusions to this general rule, including:

## dividends from a direct income interest in certain FIFs (comprising shares in ASX-listed Australian

companies, Australian unit trusts with adequate turnover or distributions, certain venture capital investments into New Zealand companies that have since migrated, and certain grey list companies)

## dividends from fixed-rate foreign equity

## dividends from deductible foreign equity

## dividends derived by a portfolio investment entity

## in certain circumstances, dividends derived by a person from a direct income interest of 10% or more

in an Australian resident FIF.

A "foreign company" is defined in s YA 1 as a company that is not resident in New Zealand, or a company that is treated under a DTA as not being resident in New Zealand.
