# FBT exemption — certain public transport fares paid by employer ¶21-655

Generally, public transport fares that are subsidised by an employer mainly for the purpose of their employees travelling between their home and place of work are subject to FBT (¶21-440).

However, in order to align the FBT outcomes of travelling to work via car and public transport, and in an effort to encourage a more environmentally friendly choice of transport for employees, an exemption from FBT has been introduced.

From 1 April 2023, an exemption applies to certain types of subsidised "public transport fares" provided by an employer to an employee. For the exemption to apply the following criteria must be met:

## the transport fare is paid by the employer

## the transport is for the purposes of getting to work, or going home from work, and

## the transportation mode is public transport (bus, train, ferry, tram or cable car).

## Interaction with s CX 9 (Subsidised transport)

Transport subsidised by an employer is still subject to FBT if the transport does not meet the requirements above.

Example: Sarah is an air hostess and works for Air New Zealand. Sarah gets a free flight from Auckland to Kerikeri. The flight does not fall within the list of transport services set out in s CX 19C. Therefore, the benefit will continue to be valued, for FBT purposes, at 25% of the highest fare available to the public for a flight from Auckland to Kerikeri.

For the FBT treatment of e-scooters and vehicle-share services, see ¶21-660.
