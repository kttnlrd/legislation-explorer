# Changing between standard deduction rules and mixed-use asset rules ¶10-037

When a taxpayer derives income from a residential property, they must determine which set of deduction rules to apply. The use of the property may change from residential rental (under a lease), to use as short- stay accommodation (¶10-113) or may even be party used as a private residence. It is important that a taxpayer knows which rules to apply and the inter-relationship of the various limitations which may apply.

To that end, a person must keep track of the use of residential property throughout a tax year to determine which rules apply.

## Interpretation statement

Interpretation statementIS 25/08 "Income tax – implications of a residential property moving between the standard tax rules and the mixed-use asset rules" considers situations where a person's use of their residential property has changed so the property moves from being under one set of income tax deduction rules to another. The statement explains how a person determines which income tax deduction rules apply and the consequences of moving between the standard tax rules (¶10-021 and ¶10-032) and the mixed-use asset rules (¶10-035). The statement only applies to persons owing residential properties and does not apply to property owned by companies (including close companies).

The interpretation statement is accompanied by 2 draft fact sheets. The first fact sheet covers situations where there is a change from the mixed-use asset rules to the standard tax rules. The second fact sheet considers the reverse — when there is a change from the standard tax rules to the mixed-use asset rules.

See Flowchart 1 in IS 25/08 which assists in determining which set of income tax deduction rules apply.

Each year a person must revisit the rules if there has been a change of use of the residential property in that income year. The mixed-use asset rules apply on an income year basis, so once a property falls withing those rules, they apply for the entire income year.

## Calculating depreciation deductions

If a residential property is being used to derive income, a person can claim depreciation in relation to depreciable property. The proportion of depreciation a person is able to claim may be impacted by the proportion of use that asset has in respect of the income earning activity. If the mixed-use rules apply, then a formula applies (s DG 9).

## Disposal of depreciable property

When an item of depreciable property is disposed of, depreciation recovery income or depreciation loss on sale must be calculated. The method for calculating depreciation recovery income/loss on sale is the same for both the standard tax rules and mixed-use asset rules.

However, a person cannot offset any prior-year mixed-use asset quarantined expenditure against depreciation recovery income. Depreciation recovery income is not asset income (that is, income derived from the use of the mixed-use asset).

If the residential property ring-fencing rules (¶7-365) apply to the property in the income year an item of depreciable property is sold, a person can offset unused excess deductions against depreciation recovery income they derive on the sale of the item. However, if the residential property ring-fencing rules do not apply in the income year of sale, a person cannot offset unused excess deductions against depreciation recovery income.

## Prior year quarantined expenditure or unused excess

deductions If the property has been subject to the missed-use asset rules, some expenditure may be quarantined.

Where the quarantined expenditure rules apply, the person may only deduct their expenses up to the amount of rental income they received from the mixed-use asset. Excess expenditure cannot be offset against other income, like salary and wages. The excess amount is quarantine and carried forward to use in future income years.

The item has detailed commentary on offsetting prior year quarantined expenditure against current year income. It also covers the application of the ring-fencing rules and the unused excess deductions that arise under those rules. These excess deductions are carried forward and their utilisation will depend on whether the ring-fencing rules were applied on a portfolio or a property-by-property basis (¶7-365).
