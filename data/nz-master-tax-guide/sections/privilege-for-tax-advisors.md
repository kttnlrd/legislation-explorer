# Privilege for tax advisors ¶1-600

The concept of solicitor–client privilege includes certain confidential communications between a tax advisor and a client (generally referred to as a "tax advice document"). Under the common law, accountants enjoyed no similar protection to solicitor–client privilege. However, tax advice documents are afforded protection if they meet strict statutory requirements. A document is eligible to be a tax advice document if the following criteria are satisfied:

## it is confidential, and

## it is created for the main purpose of instructing a tax advisor to act for a person by giving advice to

the person about the operation and effect of tax laws, or

## it is created by a tax advisor or, if the tax advisor is in public practice, an employee of the tax

advisor's firm, for the main purpose of recording research and analysis that is performed for the main purpose of enabling the tax advisor to give advice about the operation and effect of tax laws, or

## it is created by a tax advisor or, if the tax advisor is in public practice, an employee of the tax

advisor's firm, for the main purpose of giving tax advice or recording advice given about the operation and effect of tax laws.

A document that is created for the purposes of committing, promoting or assisting the committing of an illegal or wrongful act is excluded from the privilege for tax advisors.

A tax advisor must be a natural person who is subject to a professional code of conduct in giving tax advice and is subject to a disciplinary process that enforces compliance with the code of conduct. The person must also be a member of an "approved advisor group". An "approved advisor group" is a group approved by the Commissioner that includes natural persons who:

## have a significant function of giving advice on the operation and effect of tax laws

## are subject to a professional code of conduct in giving the advice, and

## are subject to a disciplinary process that enforces compliance with that code of conduct.

The following organisations have received advisor group status to date: The New Zealand Institute of Chartered Accountants, Accountants and Tax Agents Institute of New Zealand, CPA Australia and Chartered Accountants Australia and New Zealand. Therefore, tax advice given by a member of any of these Institutes may qualify for privilege.

In Blakeley v C of IR (2008) 23 NZTC 21,865, the High Court commented that the protection afforded by s 20B of the TAA is much more confined than legal professional privilege (¶1-595) both as to the information protected and the conditions attaching to its application. Unlike legal professional privilege, privilege for a tax advice document is not a response to public interest considerations. Instead, the privilege protects defined parts of a limited category of written communications. The High Court also held that a waiver of privilege could not arise under ss 20B–20G given that tax advice documents were not automatically protected from disclosure.

## Claim for privilege

The statutory privilege afforded under s 20B does not affect the common law legal privilege. Privilege for a tax advice document must be claimed (form IR 519) and include a brief description of the form and contents of the document, the name of the tax advisor for whom the document was intended and the date that the document was created. If privilege is being claimed in relation to a document created by a tax advisor or an employee of a tax advisor's firm, the claim for privilege must include a brief description of the document, the name of the tax advisor giving the tax advice in relation to which the document was created, the approved advisor group to which the tax advisor belonged when the document was created, the legislative authority and the type of revenue that was the subject of the tax advisor's advice and the date on which the document

was created. If the privilege is claimed by a tax advisor acting on behalf of a person, the claim must include notification by the tax advisor of their authority to act on behalf of the client in the matter of privilege.

The privilege extends to an information request made under a discovery obligation for a challenge, unless:

## a court or TRA has not held a case management conference or directions hearing, or directed that no

such hearing is to be held, and

## the issues raised by the challenge are not substantially similar to the issues in another challenge

before the court or TRA at that date, which has not had a case management conference or directions hearing, or given the direction that no case management conference or directions hearing will be held.

A discovery obligation is defined as an order of a court or TRA, or notice of discovery in proceedings before a court or TRA, requiring disclosure of information to the Commissioner in relation to proceedings before the court or TRA.

## Time limits

The claim for privilege must be made within a certain time. The time limits are summarised below:

## for an information request made under s 17 or under ss 17 and 17C of the TAA (¶1-590), on the day

the right of inspection or the right of removal of the document is exercised by the Commissioner or by the officer of IR or by a later date agreed with the Commissioner

## for an information request made under s 17B or 17GB (¶1-590), by the date given in the request or

the date that is 28 days after the date of the request, whichever is the later

## for an information request made under s 17H, 17J or 17K (¶1-590), by the date on which the court

requires the information to be produced

## for an information request made under s 17I (¶1-590), by the date on which the Commissioner

requires the information to be produced, and

## for an information request made under a discovery obligation, by the date by which the discovery

obligation requires the disclosure of information.

## Treatment of document

A document that is included in a request for information and that may possibly be subject to privilege as a tax advice document must be treated in a particular way. First, the document must be treated as being a tax advice document from the time of the request for, or discovery obligation for disclosure of, information until the time by which a claim for privilege must be made (see above) or until the time at which the Commissioner is notified that privilege will not be claimed, whichever is the earlier.

If privilege is asserted, the document must be treated as a tax advice document from the time of the claim until:

## the District Court, the High Court or TRA rules that the document is not a tax advice document

## the person notifies the Commissioner that the document is not eligible to be a tax advice document

for the person

## the person notifies the Commissioner that they withdraw the claim for privilege, or

## an approved advisor group informs the Commissioner that a tax advisor is not or was not a member

of the approved advisor group at a time at which the tax advisor is claimed to be a member of the group and at which the tax advisor would be required to be a member of the group for the document to be a tax advice document.

If a document must be treated as a tax advice document, a copy of the document must be held in a secure place by the tax advisor for the periods of time referred to above.

Attachments

A document may include an attachment for which privilege cannot be claimed. Any person who receives an information request to disclose information in relation to another person must provide a copy of the attachment. This also applies for parts of an attachment.

## Information excluded from privilege

Even if a tax advice document is privileged, certain information included in the document must still be disclosed in a statutory declaration in a prescribed form (form IR 520). This type of information is referred to as "tax contextual information" and, in broad terms, consists of:

## a fact or an assumption relating to a transaction that has occurred or is postulated by the person

creating the tax advice document

## a description of a step involved in the performance of a transaction that has occurred or is postulated

by the person creating the tax advice document

## non-tax advice (eg valuation and investment advice)

## advice that concerns the tax implications of the Commissioner collecting debts owing to the

Commissioner

## a fact or an assumption relating to advice on non-tax matters or collecting outstanding debts owed to

the Commissioner

## a fact or an assumption from, or relating to, the preparation of the person's financial statements, and

## a fact or an assumption from, or relating to, a document containing information that must be provided

under an IR Act.

The statutory declaration for the disclosure of tax contextual information must be made by a tax advisor who is not barred from making this type of statutory declaration (see below). Also, the declaration must state that the tax advisor is authorised to act on the relevant person's behalf for the purposes of claiming privilege for a tax advice document.

## Excluded tax advisors

A tax advisor can be barred from making a statutory declaration for the disclosure of tax contextual information if the advisor is convicted of an offence for:

## making a false statement or declaration under s 111 of the Crimes Act 1961

## failing to provide tax information under s 143(1)(b) of the TAA

## knowingly failing to provide tax information or knowingly providing altered, false, incomplete or

misleading tax information under s 143A(1)(b) or (c)

## knowingly failing to provide tax information or knowingly providing altered, false, incomplete or

misleading tax information, with the intention to evade income tax or to obtain a refund under s 143B(1) (b) or (c), or

## obstruction under s 143H.

## Time limit

Tax contextual information must be disclosed within a certain time. The time limits are summarised below:

## for an information request made under s 17 or under ss 17 and 17C of the TAA, by the date that is

determined by the Commissioner

## for an information request made under s 17B or 17GB and if the request is accompanied or followed

by a request for the disclosure of a description of tax contextual information, by the date given by the Commissioner in the requirement to disclose the description, or 28 days after the date of the requirement to disclose the description, whichever is the later

## for an information request made under s 17H, 17J or 17K, by the date that the court requires the

information to be produced

## for an information request made under s 17I, by the date that the Commissioner requires the

information to be produced, and

## for an information request made under a discovery obligation, by the date that the discovery

obligation requires disclosure of information.

## Challenge

The Commissioner or the person claiming privilege may apply to a District Court Judge, or to the court or TRA hearing the proceedings, for an order determining whether:

## a document is a tax advice document for the person

## information provided by the person is tax contextual information in relation to the document, and

## the person should provide a more detailed or better description of tax contextual information in

relation to the document.

## Operational statement

Operational statement OS 18/02, "Non-disclosure right for tax advice documents" sets out the process the Commissioner will follow when issuing a notice to a taxpayer, tax advisor and/or a third party requiring the disclosure of documents, which may contain tax advice and may be subject to the right to claim non- disclosure under ss 20B to 20G. It should be read in conjunction with operational statement OS 25/05, "Section 17B notices" (¶1-590). OS 18/02 replaced SPS 05/07.

Operational statement OS 18/02 applies to information demands or discovery obligations issued on or after 1 July 2018 and tax advisors who may or may not be chartered accountants, but are members of an approved advisory group.

Operational statement OS 25/04, "The Commissioner of Inland Revenue's search powers" sets out the procedures the Commissioner will follow when inspecting any documents, property, process or matter considered necessary or relevant to put into effect the collection of all taxes, duties and/or other functions imposed by the IR Acts and the Search and Surveillance Act 2012 (ss 16, 16B, 17, 17C and 17D of the TAA).
