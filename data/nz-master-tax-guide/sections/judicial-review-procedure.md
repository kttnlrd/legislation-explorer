# Judicial review procedure ¶4-275

Under s 109 of the TAA, a disputable decision made by the Commissioner cannot be disputed in any proceedings except the objection proceedings under Pt 8 or a challenge under Pt 8A of the TAA. (A disputable decision is an assessment or some other decision made by the Commissioner that is open to objection or challenge proceedings and includes certain discretionary powers available to the Commissioner in the disputes resolution process.) The section further provides that, with the exception of those procedures, a disputable decision and all its particulars are conclusively deemed to be correct. This has the consequence, for example, that in proceedings for the recovery of tax shown to be owing under an assessment it would be no defence to allege that the tax was not properly payable.

Judicial review is authorised in general terms by the Judicial Review Procedure Act 2016. That Act sets out procedural provisions for the judicial review of the exercise, failure to exercise, and proposed or purported exercise of a statutory power. It authorises the High Court to grant any relief that an applicant would have been entitled to in proceedings for a writ of mandamus, prohibition or certiorari, or for a declaration or injunction. This is when the relief would have been available in relation to the exercise, the refusal to exercise or the proposed or purported exercise by any person of a statutory power. If an application is successful, the court may not only declare that the impugned decision is unauthorised or otherwise lawful but also set aside the decision. Furthermore, the court may direct the decision-maker to reconsider any aspect of their decision. That correction may be accompanied by such other directions as the court thinks just. The matter is then to be reconsidered by the decision-maker.

The question of whether the judicial review procedure is available (despite the wide terms of s 109 of the TAA) to challenge tax assessments made by the Commissioner has been reviewed in many cases, for example:

## An application for judicial review of the decisions made by the TRA, the High Court and the Court

of Appeal in respect of the JG Russell scheme was dismissed by the Privy Council in O'Neil v C of IR (2001) 20 NZTC 17,051 (PC). The Privy Council noted that judicial review should be granted only in exceptional circumstances. Such exceptional circumstances may arise when there is abuse of power or when the error of law claimed was fatal to the exercise of the statutory power, and it would be wasteful to require recourse to the objection procedure. The Court of Appeal reaffirmed these principles in Westpac Banking Corporation v C of IR (2009) 24 NZTC 23,340 (CA) at [59].

## On application for judicial review, the High Court set aside the decision of the TRA in Case U35

(2000) 19 NZTC 9,330, and referred it back to the Authority for reconsideration. The High Court held that the Authority made errors of law in its judgment. See M & J Wetherill Co Ltd v Taxation Review Authority (2001) 20 NZTC 17,166 (HC). Application for judicial review was dismissed by the Court of Appeal in M & J Wetherill Co Ltd v Taxation Review Authority; C of IR v M & J Wetherill Co Ltd (2002) 20 NZTC 17,624 (CA). Application for leave to appeal to the Privy Council was dismissed in M & J Wetherill Co Ltd v Taxation Review Authority; C of IR v M & J Wetherill Co Ltd (2002) 20 NZTC 17,681 (CA). See also M & J Wetherill Co Ltd v Taxation Review Authority (2004) 21 NZTC 18,924 (CA).

## In a case in which a taxpayer sought judicial review of taxation assessments, the High Court

confirmed that judicial review is concerned not with the correctness of a decision but with the process or means by which it is made. See Douglas v C of IR (2000) 19 NZTC 15,971.

Other judicial review cases include Ch'elle Properties (NZ) Ltd v C of IR (2005) 22 NZTC 19,622; W v C of IR (2005) 22 NZTC 19,602; Hollis v C of IR (2005) 22 NZTC 19,570; Wire Supplies Ltd v TRA (2005) 22 NZTC 19,395; Wire Supplies Ltd v TRA (No 2) (2005) 22 NZTC 19,401; Allen v C of IR (2005) 22 NZTC 19,473 (CA); Allen v C of IR (2006) 22 NZTC 19,827 and C of IR v Chesterfield Preschools Ltd (No 2) (2010) 24 NZTC 24,500.
