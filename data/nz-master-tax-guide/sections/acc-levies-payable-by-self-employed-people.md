# ACC levies payable by self-employed people ¶31-055

Self-employed persons pay:

## the work account levy (¶31-015)

## the working safer levy (¶31-080), and

## the earners' levy (¶31-048).

See Example below.

Payment of the accident compensation levies must be made in accordance with the invoice.

Note: The ACC has been contacting people who are in the business of providing short-stay accommodation (eg AirBnB and BnB suppliers) regarding ACC levies payable as they are treated as self-employed people.

## Minimum and maximum income levels

The work account levy and the earners' levy are subject to minimum and maximum income levels. For the levy year beginning on 1 April 2025, the maximum income level for a self-employed person is $152,790. This increased to $156,641 for the 2026–27 tax year and to $160,244 in the 2027–28 tax year or later tax years.

For levy year beginning on or after 1 April 2025, the minimum amount of earnings on which the earners' levy is payable by a self-employed person who works, on average, for more than 30 hours per week (whether as an employee or not) is $49,365. The thresholds for the following tax years are $50,501 for the 2026–27 tax year and $51,632 for the 2027–28 tax year. See Examples below.

A self-employed person is not required to pay the work account levy or the earners' levy if the amount invoiced for that levy is less than $40. In addition, no penalties will be payable if an amount of the work account levy or the earners' levy invoiced under this threshold remains unpaid.

## Work account levy

Work account levies payable by self-employed people are based on the person's previous year's earnings from self-employment.

The work account levy rates for the 2025–26 tax year for self-employed persons, on an activity basis, were contained in Sch 1 of the Accident Compensation (Work Account Levies) Regulations 2025 and the rates for the 2026–27 tax year are contained in Sch 2. The rates for the 2027–28 tax year are contained in Sch 3 of those regulations.

No loss offset from earlier tax years is allowed in determining a person's earnings from self-employment (¶18-015) (s 169A of the AC).

If a person earns income both from self-employment and as an employee, ACC levies will be payable on the self-employed income. See Example, mixed income, below.

A newly self-employed person who is working an average of more than 30 hours per week and did not have earnings from self-employment in the previous tax year, pays the work account levy and earners' levy based on the minimum income levels. See Example, no previous self-employed earnings, below.

## Classification of activity

The work account levy rate is determined by the type of activity or activities carried on. For the definition of "activity" see ¶31-015 and ss 6 and 168B of the AC.

A self-employed person who is engaged in 2 or more activities must pay the work account levy at the highest applicable rate. However, if a particular activity accounts for 5% or less of the self-employed person's earnings for the income year, then that activity need not be considered when determining the correct rate, provided accurate records are kept regarding the apportionment of total earnings in the income year (s 171 of the AC).

## Reduction in levy

A self-employed person's work account levy may be reduced on the basis of that person's safety management capabilities. The ACC will decide whether an adjustment will be made and the level of the adjustment. See s 175 of the AC.

ACC has established workplace incentive programmes that allow adjustments to the work account levy. See ¶31-025.

## Note: The following examples are based on levy rates for the levy year commencing 1 April 2025.

## Example: No previous self-employed earnings

Toni starts her own market research business on 1 April 2025. Before that date, Toni worked as an employee. During the 2025–26 tax year Toni works an average of 35 hours per week and informs IR that she is self-employed. Toni has not purchased the right to receive a predetermined and guaranteed level of weekly compensation.

In 2025, ACC will invoice Toni for ACC CoverPlus based on the minimum liable earnings specified by regulation for the 2025–26 cover year, being $49,365:

## $

## ACC CoverPlus levy

## Classification unit: market research services (78530)

## Work account levy

($0.06 × $49,365/100) 29.62 Earners' levy ($1.67 × $49,365/100) 842.40

Subtotal 872.02 Add GST @ 15% 130.80 Total ACC levies invoiced (for 12 months) 1,002.82

The levies for the 12-month cover period would be pro-rated if Toni had started her business after the beginning of the cover period.

She receives an invoice once she has informed the ACC or IR of her employment status.

Self-employed persons may use the interactive calculators on the ACC website (www.acc.co.nz) to work out what their levies will be. However, where the self-employed person has mixed income, as in Example 3, care should be taken to ensure the correct result.

## Predetermined and guaranteed weekly compensation

A self-employed person and a non-PAYE shareholder-employee (¶31-060) can apply to ACC for the right to receive a predetermined and guaranteed level of weekly compensation for loss of earnings as a self- employed person or shareholder-employee for any personal injury covered under ACC. This type of policy is known as the CoverPlus Extra policy. If no such agreement has been entered into, compensation will be determined in accordance with Sch 1 of the AC.

The guaranteed level of weekly compensation must be more than a specified minimum and less than a specified maximum. The specified minimum amount is 80% of the minimum weekly earnings, adjusted annually in accordance with Sch 1 cl 42 of the AC.

From 1 July 2025, the specified maximum guaranteed level of weekly compensation is $2,418.55 per week and the specified minimum is $752.00. They are subject to adjustment for the movement in average weekly earnings (s 115 and Sch 1 cls 42(3) and 46 of the AC).

If a self-employed person or shareholder-employee enters into an agreement with the ACC for the right to receive guaranteed weekly compensation, the levy payable by the self-employed person or shareholder- employee is determined in accordance with regulations. The self-employed person or shareholder-employee must continue to pay the earners' levy (ss 211 and 212 of the AC).
