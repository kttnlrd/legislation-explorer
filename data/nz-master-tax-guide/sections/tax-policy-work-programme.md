# Tax policy work programme ¶1-025

In October 2025, the government set out the latest version of its overarching priorities in respect of the Work Programme: simplify tax, reduce compliance costs, address integrity risks, and improve fiscal sustainability, in order to rebuild the economy.

It committed to transparency through public engagement on the design of tax policy through the Generic Tax Policy Process, with public consultation playing an important role in the creation of a sustainable, durable and widely accepted tax system.

While the Work Programme is updated periodically to reflect the government's current priorities, the October 2025 version covers the following workstreams:

## Attracting and retaining capital and talent: aimed at making New Zealand a better place to invest

capital, work and do business by minimising biases on economic decisions, reducing international tax barriers and rewarding effort and individuals' investment in their own skills.

## Supporting small businesses: focuses on supporting small businesses by reducing costs and making

tax treatment simpler and fairer.

## Simplification and integrity of the tax system: aimed at reducing compliance costs for taxpayers and

protecting against tax avoidance and evasion to maintain a simple, stable and predictable tax system.

## Improve social policy: this workstream involves improving the delivery of income support payments

administered through the tax system and increasing work incentives.

## Other agency work: this workstream involves other agency work that aligns with government priorities

that may have tax consequences or IR delivery implications.

## The current public guidance work programme can be viewed on IR's Tax Policy website; Government

Tax and Social Policy Work Programme.

## Regulatory Stewardship Reviews

## The donations tax credit regime

In February 2025, the "Donation Tax Credit Regime: regulatory stewardship review" was released. The review found the regime to be performing adequately but with recommendations for structural and/or incremental reform. IR accepted the findings, committing to a medium-term implementation programme to address those recommendations it could resource and that did not require significant system changes.

These include:

## links with FamilyBoost

## review intermediary, AML and authority to act rules

## introduce a 3-month grace period for losing donee organisation status

## optimise myIR.

The recommendations IR was unable to prioritise are: delinking the credit from income tax, real-time payments and shift to payments-based system.
