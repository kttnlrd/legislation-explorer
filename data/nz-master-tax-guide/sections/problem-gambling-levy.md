# Problem gambling levy ¶30-367

The GA is generally a social policy measure to control the growth of gambling and to prevent and minimise the harm caused by gambling, including problem gambling. The legislation also aims to ensure that money from gambling is used to benefit the community.

To help recover the costs of problem gambling, a problem gambling levy is imposed on a gambling operator or a class of gambling operator in a specified sector of gambling. The levy is based on the estimated level of player expenditure.

The problem gambling levy is neither a tax nor a duty. However, for the purposes of its collection and recovery, it is treated as if it were a duty. The powers of collection, recovery and enforcement in the GD and the TAA also apply to the levy. Any unpaid levy is recoverable as a debt due to the Crown.

The amount of the problem gambling levy, set every 3 years, is calculated in accordance with a formula set out in s 320 of the GA. The formula takes into account the level of harm attributable to and the total revenue derived in each sector.

*Refs: s 320*

## Levy from 1 July 2022

The problem gambling levy rates for the period from 1 July 2022 are as follows:

Gambling sector Levy rate (GST excl) Casino operator 0.87% Gaming machine operator 1.08% Lotteries Commission 0.44% TAB NZ (before 1 August 2020, Racing Industry Transition 0.76% Agency)

## Levy from 1 July 2025

The problem gambling levy rates for the period from 1 July 2025 are as follows:

Gambling sector Levy rate (GST excl) Casino operator 0.89% Gaming machine operator 1.24% Lotteries Commission 0.69% TAB NZ (before 1 August 2020, Racing Industry Transition 0.74% Agency)

The problem gambling levy is treated as if it were a duty for the powers of collection, recovery and enforcement. Thus, the levy is payable monthly and payment, accompanied by the appropriate levy return, must be made by the same date as the relevant duty must be paid. In other words, for casino operators, gaming machine operators and the TAB NZ, the levy must be paid no later than the 20th day of the month following the month for which the return relates. For the Lotteries Commission, the levy must be paid no later than 14 days after the date of drawing of a lottery.

Goods and services tax is also payable on the problem gambling levy. IR issues a GST tax invoice showing the GST content of the problem gambling levy.

Note that where a refund of problem gambling levy is payable, from 25 June 2021 this amount may be refunded by direct credit (Gambling (Problem Gambling Levy) Regulations 2019).
