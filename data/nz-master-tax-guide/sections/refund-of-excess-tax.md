# Refund of excess tax ¶2-145

The Commissioner is required to refund tax if it represents an excess over the tax properly payable, the amount has arisen on an original assessment, and the Commissioner is satisfied of the entitlement to the refund. In the case of an amount that has arisen on an amended assessment, the Commissioner is only required to refund tax (which includes refundable tax credits) if the Commissioner is satisfied of the entitlement to the refund before the end of the relevant 4-year time bar in s 108 of the TAA (¶4-110), any extended period for applying for a tax credit allowed under s 78B of that Act, or the 7-year period for amendment of assessments under s GC 13(6) (Calculation of arm's length amounts).

Similarly, the Commissioner must refund tax paid by a person as a result of an amendment to an assessment that increased the amount of tax payable if the Commissioner is satisfied that the amount represents an excess over the tax properly payable and the relevant 4-year time bar period has not ended.

A taxpayer may claim a refund from an amended assessment outside the time bar period where the refund arises due to a past personal service rehabilitation payment.

All tax refunds are made by direct credit into a person's bank account (or an account nominated by the person). The only exception is if the Commissioner is satisfied that paying by direct credit would result in undue hardship to a person, or is not practicable.

*Refs: s 108, s 78B, s GC 13(6)*

## Offset against outstanding tax liabilities

After applying the provisions in the IT07 that limit the amount of refund, a refund payable to a person under ss RM 2 and RM 4, including a refundable tax credit under s LA 5(5), may be applied by the Commissioner in payment of an outstanding taxable amount payable under either the IT07 or any other IR Act. The refund may be applied in accordance with a tax transfer request made under the tax transfer rules (¶2-148) or, if no request has been made, in such order or manner as the Commissioner may determine. However, note that a refund relating to overpaid RWT or NRWT or WFF tax credits or arising from an election to carry back a loss may only be applied towards an outstanding tax liability if requested by the taxpayer.

A refund of a FamilyBoost tax credit (¶11-200) may be applied to satisfy a liability that a person has in respect of any significant over payment by the Commissioner of the credit.

Law: IT07 ss LA 5(5), RB 4, RM 2, RM 4, RM 10; TAA s 184A
