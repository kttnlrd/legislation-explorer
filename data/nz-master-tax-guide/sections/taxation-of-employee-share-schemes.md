# Taxation of employee share schemes ¶3-470

#Note: The Taxation (Annual Rates for 2025–26, Compliance Simplification, and Remedial Measures) Bill proposes the following amendments to the employee share scheme (ESS) rules:

## changes to allow unlisted companies to elect into a regime where the tax liability for employees who

receive shares or share options as part of an ESS can generally be deferred until the shares can be more easily valued and sold

## clarify that the share scheme taxing date can arise before shares are held by or for the benefit of an

ESS beneficiary

## clarify the timing of an employer's expenditure or loss under s DV 27(6) that is equal in amount to the

income derived by the employee under the ESS rules.

A change in core rules for determining the amount and time of derivation of income and incurring of expenditure under an ESS were introduced with effect from 29 September 2018. The objective of the provisions is neutral tax treatment of ESS benefits. That is, to the extent possible, the tax position of both the employer and the employee should be the same whether remuneration for labour is paid in cash or shares.

Generally, these rules apply to benefits where the taxing point occurs on or after 29 September 2018.

For a discussion of the taxation of benefits from an ESS before 29 September 2018, see CCH, 2019 New Zealand Master Tax Guide, ¶3-469.

Interpretation statement IS 25/04, "What an employee share scheme is, the taxing date and apportionment" provides detailed guidance on the definition of an "employee share scheme", when the share scheme taxing date arises and when shares are held by or for the benefit of an employee, as well as the circumstances when the share scheme taxing date may be deferred. The statement also addresses how benefits are apportioned when some of the employee's entitlement arises for non-residents.

*Refs: s DV 27(6)*

## Definition of employee share scheme

The rules apply to benefits provided under ESS. An "employee share scheme" is an arrangement with a purpose or effect of issuing or transferring shares in a company to a person who will be, is, or has been an employee (or shareholder–employee) of that company or of another company in the same group. It also includes the provision of shares to an associate of the employee or shareholder-employee. In all cases, the arrangement must be connected with the person's employment or service.

The use of the term "arrangement" covers all aspects of a scheme, for example, direct transfers of shares, loans to buy shares, bonuses, put and call options and transfers to trusts, etc. The definition also covers past, present and future employees, and includes shareholder–employees.

However, an ESS does not include an arrangement that requires an employee, shareholder–employee or associate to:

## pay market value for the shares on the "share scheme taxing date" (described in more detail below,

but generally the date on which the employee holds the shares like any other shareholder), or

## put at risk shares they acquired for market value consideration, where the scheme provides no

protection to the person against a fall in the value of the shares and none of the consideration for acquiring the shares is provided to the person under an agreement it is used for acquiring the shares.

See further below for detailed discussion on approved valuation methods for determining the market value of the shares.

The definition of "employee share scheme" also excludes an "exempt ESS" (¶3-471).

Example:

Jim is employed by ABC Co, a closely-held company. As part of his employment agreement, after he has worked for the company for three years, if the company's other shareholders are happy with his performance, they will let him buy 25% of the company's shares for their current market value at that time.

While this is an arrangement with a purpose or effect of issuing or transferring shares in a company to a person who will be, is, or has been an employee (or shareholder-employee), market value will be paid for the shares on the share scheme taxing date.

Accordingly, this arrangement is not an employee share scheme for the purposes of the new definition.

Example: Casey, Hamish and Steve get together and incorporate a company to develop some technology-related intellectual property (IP).

They are each employed by the company. When the shares are issued they are worth virtually nothing (on a balance sheet basis) and a nominal subscription price of $0.01 is paid by each shareholder-employee. The shareholders' agreement states that to ensure they commit to developing the IP over three years, if they leave within three years they forfeit their shares.

While this is an arrangement with a purpose or effect of issuing or transferring shares in a company to a person who will be, is, or has been an employee (or shareholder-employee), market value was paid for the shares, not using money provided to the employees for that purpose, and the employees have then chosen to put the shares at risk. Accordingly, this arrangement is not an employee share scheme for the purposes of the new definition.

Examples taken from Tax Information Bulletin ¶305-102 Vol 30, No 5, June 2018 at 53–54.

## Timing of employees' income — share scheme taxing date

The time when the employee is taxable is defined as the "share scheme taxing date" (s CE 7B). Unless an employee first transfers its share scheme benefits to a non-associate, or the company cancels them, the share scheme taxing date will be the first date when:

## there is no material risk that beneficial ownership of the shares will change, or that the shares will be

required to be transferred or cancelled

## the employee is not entitled to be compensated for a fall in the value of the shares, and

## there is no material risk there will be a change in the terms of the shares affecting their value.

If the benefits are cancelled or transferred to a non-associate before these events occur, then the share scheme taxing date is at the time of the cancellation or transfer.

In determining whether there is a risk of a change of ownership, transfer or cancellation, certain rights and requirements do not affect the employee's status as the economic owner of the shares under the scheme and are ignored. They are rights or requirements:

## for transfer for market value

## not contemplated by the ESS

## that have no material risk of occurring or are of no material commercial significance, or

## that also apply to shares not under the ESS.

In IS 25/04, the Commissioner acknowledged there may be a delay in the transfer of shares to an employee and the employer is unable to determine the exact date that shares are held by or for the benefit of the employee for example in the context of global schemes. The Commissioner states: 77. We understand that often the process to transfer shares to an ESS beneficiary begins when an option is exercised or share rights vest under the scheme – for example shares are issued by the company, transferred from a trust or purchased on market. However, the actual transfer is often not able to be completed on the same date due to processes required to issue and transfer shares.

Commentators have suggested it can be difficult to determine the exact date when those shares are held by the ESS beneficiary, or by someone for the benefit of the ESS beneficiary. We understand this is particularly an issue for global schemes where software calculates the benefit on the exercise date or vesting date (as applicable), and where payroll teams in New Zealand may not have access to any information other than the exercise date or vesting date. 78. The Commissioner will accept that the best evidence of when the shares are held by or for the benefit of the ESS beneficiary could be the vesting date or exercise date under the ESS (as

applicable) for the purposes of determining the SSTD under s CE 7B(1)(a) where the following requirements are met:

- the shares are expected to be held by or for the benefit of the ESS beneficiary within 10 working

  days after the exercise date or vesting date under the ESS; and

- the employer is unbale to determine the exact date by taking reasonable steps.

A number of examples can be found in Tax Information Bulletin ¶305-102 Vol 30, No 5, June 2018 at 57–62 and IS 25/04 that illustrate how these rules work in practice for some common types of ESS.

## Example: Simple vesting period

## Facts

A Co transfers shares worth $10,000 to a trustee on trust for an employee. If the employee leaves the company for any reason during the next 3 years, the shares are forfeited for no consideration. After 3 years, the shares are transferred to the employee.

Result The share scheme taxing date is when the 3 years is up and the employee is still employed.

Analysis The risk of loss of the shares for the first 3 years means there is a material risk that the beneficial ownership of the share will change under the terms of the scheme. None of the exceptions apply.

## Example: Vesting subject to misconduct

## Facts

A Co transfers shares worth $10,000 to a trustee on trust for an employee. The shares are transferred to the employee if they are still employed by the company after 3 years. Also, if the employee ceases employment for any reason other than being subject to disciplinary action or committing some form of employment-related misconduct during the 3-year period (ie being a "bad leaver") the employee is entitled to the shares at that time.

Result The share scheme taxing date is when the shares are initially transferred to the trust, and the income will be their value at that time.

Analysis The risk of the employee losing their job for these "bad" reasons during the 3-year period, and thus losing entitlement to the shares, is not sufficiently material to require deferral.

## Approved valuation methods for employee share scheme

benefits For the purposes of ESS, "market value" of shares is defined as (ss CE 7CB and YA 1):

## for a share or option quoted on an exchange—

## means an amount equal to the middle market quotation at the time for a similar share or option,

unless the quotation is not a fair reflection of the market value having regard to certain features of the exchange, and

## includes an amount equal to the 5-day volume weighted average price or any other method that is

accepted by the Commissioner or is comparable to the 5-day volume weighted average price, for such shares or options

## for a share or option not quoted on an exchange, means the amount that a willing purchaser would

pay to acquire the share or option in an arm's length acquisition and is determined using a method that —

## confirms with commercially acceptable practice

## may have regard to the present value of the company's anticipated income or cash flows and the

realisable value of the company's assets, and

## results in a valuation that is fair and reasonable.

Commissioner's statement CS 24/01, "Determining the 'market value' of shares that an employee receives under an employee share scheme" provides detailed guidance on the acceptable safe harbour valuation methods and the relevant documentation requirements (see paragraph 33, 40 and 50 of CS 24/01 for detailed guidance on the documentation required for different types of shares).

## Listed company

When a company listed on a "recognised exchange" issues shares to its employee under an ESS, the Commissioner will accept the share value determined using one of the following methods:

## Option A: The 5-day volume weighted average price ("VWAP") over the last 5 trading days including

the share scheme taxing date.

## Option B: The closing price of the listed share on the share scheme taxing date.

## Option C: If on the share scheme taxing date the employee disposes of any of the shares at market

value on a recognised exchange, the actual proceeds of sale for those shares on that date. If the proceeds of sale are in a foreign currency, the New Zealand dollar equivalent by applying the close of trading spot exchange rate on the sale date.

Section CE 7CB(b) provides a VWAP method which is independent of Option A above although the outcome is essentially the same.

For foreign-listed shares, the company should determine the foreign share value by using one of the options above and then convert to its New Zealand dollar equivalent by applying the close of trading spot exchange rate on the share scheme taxing date.

Shares issued by a newly listed company to employees as part of an initial public offering (IPO) should be valued using the published offer price for the IPO.

## Unlisted company

When a company issues unlisted shares to an employee (excluding a start-up company), the Commissioner will accept the share value determined using one of the following methods:

## Option A: An arm's length value determined by an independent and qualified valuer.

## Option B: A valuation involving the arm's length issue or sale of the same class of shares to a non-

associated third party in the 6 months before the share scheme taxing date where, if new shares are being issued to the employees, the valuation is adjusted for dilution of existing shares.

## Option C: A valuation prepared by an appropriate person in the company using an appropriated

method as described in the statement (see paragraph 44 of CS 24/01 for detailed guidance).

## Unlisted start-up company

For a start-up company, the Commissioner accepts one of the methods outlined above under unlisted company for determining the market value of the start-up company shares. The statement also sets out the Commissioner's view of the definition of a start-up company and this generally means the company:

## is in the first stage of its operations,

## is initially financed and operated by founding shareholders or investors,

## has not paid any dividends,

## has expenses that tend to exceed its revenues and may not yet be producing a profit,

## tends to have very low net tangible assets,

## may have a high level of expended research and development costs relative to its tangible assets

value, and

## does not yet have a stable market for its product or service.

This statement will not apply:

## to any arrangements that are subject to anti-avoidance provisions in the Act,

## where a company uses a previous valuation and the original share value is adjusted resulting in a

lower value or where the previous valuation relates to shares of a different class from the shares issued under the employee shares scheme, or

## to a company engaged in a current or proposed venture capital funding round which is intended

to take place within 6 months of the share scheme taxing date. In these cases, the company should obtain a separate arm's length valuation for the ESS shares issued.

CS 24/01 replaces the previous CS 17/01.

## Amount of income to employee

The amount of income to the employee is the value of the shares at the share scheme taxing date (or the transfer price if transferred to a non-associate, or the amount paid for cancellation, if cancelled by the employer) less the amount paid for them. Even where benefits are conferred on, or transferred to, an associate of an employee, it is the employee who is taxed on the income. If the amount paid exceeds the value of the shares, the difference is deductible to the employee (ss CE 2(3) and DV 27(3)).

## Apportionment for overseas service

The rules contain an income apportionment formula. The formula applies to all employees. It excludes from taxable income ESS benefits that accrue while a person is neither New Zealand resident nor deriving New Zealand source income. The extent of such accrual is determined by first establishing the entire period over which the benefit accrues, and then determining the proportion of that period during which the person is non-resident and not deriving New Zealand source income from their employment. The period of accrual ends once the rights vest, rather than when the income arises. So, for example, in the case of an option, the period of accrual will end once the options are exercisable rather than when they are actually exercised.

The ESS income is treated as non-residents' foreign source income (which is not taxable income) to the extent of this proportion (see Examples 14–16 in IS 25/04).

## Transfers to associates

Transfers to associates are ignored for the purposes of calculating the employee's share scheme income.

## Rollover relief for transfer to new scheme

If a person's ESS rights are cancelled and replaced with rights in a different scheme, the value of the replacement rights are not included in the person's income arising due to the cancellation of the original scheme: ss CE 2(2)(c) and CE 7D.

The benefit provided by the replacement scheme is taxed appropriately by applying the rules to that scheme.

## Timing and amount of employers' deduction

An employer is denied a deduction for provision of ESS benefits, except as provided for by s DV 27:

## Costs incurred in establishing or managing the scheme. These costs include legal and accounting

fees incurred in setting up the scheme, as well as ongoing management fees. Deductibility of these costs are left to the usual tests, eg the capital/revenue test. Costs incurred by an ESS trust are treated as incurred by the employer or issuing company, as a result of s CE 6 treating an ESS trust as a nominee.

## An amount equal to the employee's income, which is treated as a cost incurred at the same time

as the employee recognises the income (see discussion of QB 21/04 below). Deductibility of this cost depends on meeting the usual tests.

## Amounts that are taxable to the employee as employment income other than as ESS benefits. This is

intended to preserve a deduction for the cost of paying a bonus where the payment of the bonus is part of the terms of an ESS.

Accordingly:

## payments to fund an ESS trust to acquire shares, or to reimburse a parent for providing shares, are

not deductible, and

## as a practical matter, employers will need to either prohibit employees from transferring their rights to

a non-associate before the share scheme taxing date or place a requirement on employees to inform them of the time and amount paid in such a transfer, in order to correctly calculate their deduction.

QWBA QB 21/04, "When an employer is party to an employee share scheme, when does an employer's expenditure or loss under s DV 27(6) or income under s DV 27(9) arise?" considers the timing of deduction or income and confirms that the employer's expenditure or loss arises when the related "employee amount" is recognised for the employee. The "employee amount" is the amount calculated on the share scheme taxing date, and the item concludes where that amount is:

## negative, the employee has a deduction on the share scheme taxing date

## positive and the employer is required to provide employment income information in relation to a

benefit received under an ESS in respect of that employee, the employee derives the amount on the ESS deferral date (being the 20th day after the share scheme taxing date), or

## positive and the employer is not required to provide the employment income information in respect of

the employee, the employee derives the amount on the share scheme taxing date.

To prevent double deductions, when shares are provided to an employee and a deduction has been taken for that provision other than in accordance with s DV 27, the deduction under s DV 27 is reduced by the earlier deduction.

Interpretation statement IS 24/07, "Deductions for parties to employee share schemes" discussed in detail along with practical examples regarding what deductions employers can claim in relation to an ESS. Section DV 27 does not supplement the general permission (except s DV 27(4) as expressed in s DV 27(10)) or override any general limitations and therefore, the item emphasises the need to satisfy the general permission and when the capital limitation might apply.

## Effect on available subscribed capital

The rules seek to tax any benefit conferred on an employee by the issue of shares in an ESS in the same way as an equivalent cash payment followed by an acquisition of shares in the issuing company. Consistent with this principle, the rules provide for an increase in the employer's available subscribed capital (ASC) by the amount deemed to be paid (plus actually paid) for the shares. The rules also cater for the situation where the employer is not the company issuing the shares.

The amount of the deduction to the employer gives rise to additional ASC for both the employer and, if the shares issued are in the parent of the employer, the parent. In the latter case, any reimbursement paid to the parent reduces the subsidiary's ASC but does not increase the parent's ASC. If the employer has income from the issue of the shares, that reduces its ASC.

Example: Facts Employer Co issues 700 shares worth $3 each to an employee for $2 per share (that is, at a $1 per share discount). The scheme taxing date is the date of issue. The employee has $700 income and Employer Co has a $700 deduction.

ASC Result Employer Co's ASC increases by $2,100, being the total of the $1,400 received for the provision of the shares and its $700 expenditure incurred for providing the shares.

Example: Facts Employer Co issues 700 shares worth $3 each to an employee for $2 per share, funded by a loan from the employer. If the employee is still employed by the company after one year, the employee will receive a bonus of $1,400 grossed-up for PAYE, the net amount of which must be used to repay the loan. Otherwise, the employee must return the shares to the employer in full repayment of the loan at the time she leaves employment. After one year, the shares are worth $4 each.

ASC result Issuing the shares gives rise to ASC of $1,400. If the shares are repurchased because the employee does not remain employed, that will usually give rise to an ASC reduction of $1,400 (unless the shares are held by a trustee or as treasury stock).

Otherwise, at the share scheme taxing date, Employer Co's ASC will increase by a further $1,400. This is made up of the $1,400 that it received from the employee for the shares and $1,400 it incurred in providing the shares (the difference between the value of the shares on the vesting date and their cost to the employee).

The rules also ensure that the acquisition of shares as part of an ESS is able to be treated as an acquisition of treasury stock, regardless of whether the shares are acquired by the company itself and not cancelled, or are acquired by a trustee who is treated as a nominee of the company, provided the shares are allocated to an employee within a year of acquisition. This is then entirely disregarded for ASC purposes and no adjustments are necessary.

Example: Facts An employee share scheme trust for Employer Co acquires 700 Employer Co shares on market for $2.50 per share. Six months later, when they are worth $3 per share, it allocates them to an employee for $2 per share. The purchase price is funded by a loan from Employer Co. If the employee is still employed by Employer Co after one year, the employee will receive a $1,400 bonus from Employer Co (grossed-up for PAYE), which must be used to repay the loan. Otherwise, the employee must return the shares to the employer in full repayment of the loan at the time she leaves employment.

After one year, the shares are worth $5 each.

ASC result for Employer Co The trustee's acquisition of the shares is treated as an on-market acquisition of treasury stock by Employer Co. Because the shares are allocated to an employee within 12 months of acquisition, the acquisition is not treated as reverting to an on-market cancellation under section CD 25(2). Therefore the amount paid to acquire the shares does not reduce Employer Co's ASC and the $1,400 paid by the employee to acquire the shares does not increase Employer Co's ASC.

If the employee does not stay for 12 months, Employer Co will be treated as acquiring the shares at that time for $2.50 per share, the amount for which they were initially acquired on market. This acquisition will not reduce Parent Co's ASC if the shares are allocated to another employee within 12 months.

Example taken from Tax Information Bulletin ¶305-102 Vol 30, No 5, June 2018 at 70.

In addition, a company that issues shares to an employee (or trustee on their behalf) for market value can also elect not to apply the provisions and therefore no adjustments are necessary for ASC purposes.

Example: Facts B Co provides an employee with an interest-free full recourse loan of $10,000 to acquire newly issued shares in B Co for market value, on the basis that:

## the shares are held by a trustee for three years

## dividends are paid to the employee from the time the shares are acquired

## if the employee leaves within three years, the shares must be sold back to the trustee for $10,000, which must be used to

repay the loan

## if the employee is still employed by B Co after three years, the employee can either sell the shares to the trustee for the

loan amount, or choose to continue in the scheme, and

## if the employee chooses to continue, the loan is only repayable when the shares are sold.

The employee remains employed for three years, and chooses to continue in the scheme, as the shares are worth $18,000 at that time.

## Result

B Co's ASC will increase by $10,000 when it issues the shares. Prima facie it will also increase by $8,000 at the end of year 3, unless the company elects to the contrary.

Analysis The $10,000 received by B Co is ASC under s CD 43(2), because it is within the definition of "subscriptions". The $8,000 is also included in "subscriptions" under section CD 43(6E). However, B Co can elect out of section CD 43(6E), because it issued the shares for their market value (section CD 43(6F).

Example taken from Tax Information Bulletin ¶305-102 Vol 30, No 5, June 2018 at 70.

## Employee share scheme trust – IS 24/04

## Effect on ASC and application of the treasury stock rules

For tax purposes, the ESS trustee is treated as nominee of the company and the company is treated as holding shares, and issuing and buying back shares in itself (s CE 6). Any consideration received by the ESS trustee for the sale or transfer of the shares is treated as consideration received by the company and give rise to an increase in the company's ASC (subject to the treasury stock rules). Various subsections of s CD 43 specifically address the effect of ESS benefits on the ASC for different ESS arrangements.

The item further emphasises that s CD 25 addresses the application of the treasury stock rules for an ESS trustee (see ¶16-700 for detailed discussion on the treasury stock rules).

## Employer may account for tax on benefit

An employer who has established an ESS for a class of employees (or former employees) may elect to withhold and pay an amount on account of income tax payable on shares an employee acquires under the scheme. The benefit the employee receives then becomes part of their salary or wages for the relevant pay period, being treated as an extra pay. The objective of this treatment is to simplify an employee's year-end

tax process. The election is made by the employer calculating the amount of tax payable on the employee share benefit and accounting for it to IR. The amount must also be reported in the employer's employment income information (see below).

From 26 June 2019, employers can make an irrevocable election to withhold tax on ESS benefits to allow the use of equity-settled accounting treatment under financial reporting standards (s RD 7B(1)(a)). See Tax Information Bulletin ¶318-106 Vol 31, No 8, September 2019 at 85.

## Reporting by employer

Section 23K of the TAA specifically provides for the reporting of ESS benefit information by employers. The th recognition of benefits derived by an employee under an ESS is deferred to the 20 day after the share scheme taxing date for the employee (the ESS deferral date) in order to provide all employers with sufficient time to compile information to support the required disclosures and deduction of tax, if it is to be withheld.

Employers may provide the employment income information in relation to the ESS benefits:

## under the rules for the payday filing of information (¶3-023), with the ESS deferral date treated as the

payday, or

## twice-monthly, where for any benefit received where the ESS deferral date falls between the 1st and

15th of the month the benefit must be reported, at the latest, as if the benefit was received on the 15th, and where the ESS deferral date falls between the 16th and month end the benefit must be reported, at the latest, as if it was received at month end.

Employers are not required to provide IR with information on:

## ESS benefits received by former employees if they have not chosen to withhold tax from the benefit,

or

## benefits arising under tax-exempt, widely-offered ESS.

The particulars to be provided are specified in Sch 4 table 1 of the TAA (¶3-023).

Example: Under the new rules, if an employee received a benefit under an employee share scheme on 5 July 2019 (that is, the share scheme taxing date for the employee share scheme beneficiary is 5 July 2019) they would be treated as deriving income in relation to the benefit on 25 July 2019.

Their employer would have two options as to how they meet their obligation to provide information in relation to the benefit to Inland Revenue:

## Option 1: reporting on a payday basis

If their employer chooses to report information in relation to the benefit on a payday basis, 25 July 2019 would also be the relevant payday. This would mean that their employer would be required to report information about the value of the benefit nd received by the employee and any tax withheld in relation to the benefit by the 2 working day after 25 July 2019 if they are th an employer in the online group, or by the 10 working day after 25 July 2019 if they are an employer in the non-electronic group (or an employer in the new group who provides their employment income information on paper).

## Option 2: reporting on a twice-monthly basis

If their employer chooses to report information in relation to the benefit on a twice-monthly basis, 25 July 2019 would be treated as the "relevant day" for the purposes of new section 23C(4) of the Tax Administration Act 1994. This would mean that their employer would be required to report information about the value of the benefit received by the employee and any tax withheld in relation to the benefit by the 2nd working day after 31 July 2019 if they are an employer in the online group, th or by the 10 working day after 31 July 2019 if they are an employer in the non-electronic group (or an employer in the new group who provides their employment income information on paper). 25 July 2019 would also be the relevant date for determining the due date for the payment of tax withheld in relation to the benefit (assuming that the employer elected to withhold tax in relation to the benefit). In this example, the due date for paying the tax withheld to Inland Revenue would be 5 August 2019 if the employer was above the $500,000 per annum of PAYE and ESCT threshold, or 20 August 2019 if they are below the threshold.

## ESS benefits paid in cash

Interpretation statement IS 25/06, "Employer obligations for ESS benefits paid in cash" sets out an employer's PAYE, student loan and KiwiSaver obligations when an employee receives an benefit under an ESS that is paid in cash.

A "cash-settled ESS benefit" is not defined in the legislation. The Commissioner's view is it is a cash payment made to an employee where the employee receives the amount under an ESS and the amount gives rise to a benefit under an ESS (see Examples 1–4 in IS 25/06).

Once a cash-settled ESS benefit arises, an employer should be aware of the following tax obligations:

- The payment is treated as an extra pay and a PAYE income payment.

- From the 2024-25 income year, the payment is not subject to the ACC earner's levy.

- Student loan deduction from the payment is required if applicable.

- KiwiSaver deductions or employer contribution is not required in relation to the payment.

## Anti-avoidance provision

Section GB 49B allows the Commissioner to counteract any tax advantage gained from an arrangement that attempts to circumvent the intent and application of the share scheme taxing date by seeking to have shares taxed either earlier or later than is consistent with that intent.

## Employer-issued cryptoassets

In May 2023, IR released an updated public ruling in the cryptoassets series : BR Pub 23/07, "Income tax – application of the employee share scheme rules to employer issued crypto-assets provided to an employee" (see also BR Pub 23/04 and BR Pub 23/05, which replaced BR Pub 21/01 and BR Pub 21/02, at ¶3-198, and BR Pub 23/06 at ¶21-436). BR Pub 23/07 considers the income tax treatment of employer- issued cryptoassets provided to employees, where the cryptoasset issued is a share in the employer (ie provides an interest in the capital of the company), the employee is not required to pay market value for the cryptoasset and the cryptoasset is not provided under an exempt ESS.

The ruling concludes that the provision of the cryptoassets by the employer (or other group company) to the employee in the above circumstances is an ESS and the ESS rules will apply. The value of the taxable benefit received by the employee will be determined under s CE 2 and the amount of that benefit will be the employee's employment income.

The ruling applies to certain types of cryptoassets (commonly referred to as "equity" tokens) that provide ownership interests in entities. However, note that in relation to determining whether a cryptoasset is a share, the ruling states: "37. Crypto-assets that provide an interest in the capital of a company, such as the right to receive a share in the surplus assets on a winding up of the company and potentially other distributions from the company, will be a 'share' for income tax purposes. To qualify as a share, the interests need to be by way of an equity interest in the company's performance, and not simply a debt or other contractual right to receive payments. For completeness, the relevant interest must be in a company, so this would not include any interests in a decentralised autonomous organisation that operates over the blockchain, nor would it include a token that only provides interests in a platform. 38. Given that this will be highly fact specific and will depend on the terms of each particular crypto- asset issued, this Ruling does not rule on when a crypto-asset will be a 'share'. To determine whether a crypto-asset is a 'share' it will be necessary to carefully consider the terms and conditions on which the crypto-asset is issued. This commentary can be used as guidance." Note also that cryptoassets that are a "share" for tax purposes may not necessarily be a "share" for the purposes of the Companies Act 1993 or an "equity security" under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013.

The ruling provides the following examples:

## Example: Crypto-assets that provide interests in a platform

DigitalKeyChain Ltd, a New Zealand incorporated limited liability company, is developing a new blockchain-based platform for property sales. DigitalKeyChain Ltd issues its own crypto-asset, D-KEYs, by way of an ICO.

DigitalKeyChain Ltd employs 5 full-time employees. As DigitalKeyChain Ltd is unable to pay competitive rates, it proposes to provide each of the full-time employees with D-KEYs issued in the ICO to the value of NZ$5,000.

The D-KEY whitepaper provides that a certain number of D-KEYs are required for users to access the platform and provides holders with voting rights over the operation of the platform.

The D-KEYs are not "shares" as they do not provide an interest in the capital of DigitalKeyChain Ltd. Accordingly, the employee share scheme rules do not apply. However, other employment income rules are likely to apply to the benefit received by the employees (as set out in BR PUB 19/03: Income Tax — employer issued crypto-assets provided to an employee).

## Example: Crypto-assets that provide rights on a wind-up

Assume that, in addition to the facts set out above, the whitepaper and relevant terms and conditions of issue provide that D-KEY holders will receive (at the discretion of the directors) a right to distributions of DigitalKeyChain Ltd's profits.

The whitepaper and relevant terms and conditions also provide D-KEY holders with a right to a share in surplus assets on a liquidation of DigitalKeyChain Ltd after all creditors have been paid. In these circumstances, the D-KEYs are "shares" under s YA 1.

The employee share scheme rules will apply to the D-KEYs issued to the 5 employees. The employees did not pay anything for the receipt of the D-Keys, so the full market value will be employment income.
