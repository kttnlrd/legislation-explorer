# Reporting requirements for domestic trusts ¶25-560

The Commissioner issued an operational statement on reporting requirements for domestic trusts on 6 April 2022. This operational statement OS 22/02 sets out the Commissioner's approach to applying the trust information gathering powers contained in ss 59BA and 59BAB of the TAA. The statement applies from 6 April 2022.

## Note: The Taxation (Annual Rates for 2025–26, Compliance Simplification, and Remedial Measures) Bill

proposes to repeal the specific provisions for trust disclosure in ss 59BA and 59BAB of the TAA as these are not necessary for the Commissioner to collect information from trustees under ss 33 and 35 of the TAA.

*Refs: ss 59B, ss 33*

## Overview

The trust information gathering powers in s 59BA, by way of annual return for trusts, were introduced with effect for the 2021-22 and later income years. The retrospective trust information gathering power in s 59BAB was introduced with effect from 7 December 2020. The retrospective information gathering powers were introduced for periods beginning after the end of the 2013-2014 income year.

The purpose of the information gathering is to gain insight into the use of trusts for individuals who are, or would have been, on the top margin tax rate of 39%.

## Application

Trustees who derive assessable income are currently obliged to file a tax return in the form prescribed by the Commissioner, unless the trust is an excluded trust (see below). Trustees must file a return for all income derived in the corresponding income year.

Trustees who derive assessable income are obliged to prepare the financial statements required by Order in Council (see below), but may not necessarily be required to furnish these to the Commissioner. The obligation to maintain financial statements is a separate obligation (required by the OIC) to the obligation to provide further information.

The changes to the requirements for domestic trustees are:

## Preparation of financial statements, and filing a statement of profit or loss, and statement of financial

position

## Disclosure of prior settlor details

## Disclosure of appointee details

## Disclosure of monetary and non-monetary settlements and distributions

## Providing financial information, including financial information relating to non-business activity.

## Excluded trusts

Some trusts are excluded from the requirement to file an annual return. These include:

## Trustees of foreign trusts (they have their own requirements under s 59D)

## Trustees of a non-active complying trust.

The trustees must complete an IR633 Non-active trust declaration form. If at any time the trust no longer meets the requirements to be eligible for non-active status, the trustees must notify IR and furnish a return of income for the relevant income year.

Bare trust arrangements are circumstances where a trustee holds assets on trust for another without any other duties, other than to follow direction of the beneficiary. For reporting purposes, the bare trustee is not treated as deriving income on their own behalf but acts as a nominee for the beneficiary. Therefore, no obligation for the trustee to file an income tax return under s 59BA(1), or to comply with the additional disclosure requirements of s 59BA(2).

In March 2022, the Tax Administration (Financial Statements—Domestic Trusts) Order 2022 added specific trusts, in addition to the types discussed above, excluded from the disclosure rules. They are:

## charitable trusts registered under the Charities Act 2005

## trusts eligible to be M#ori authorities

## trusts that are widely-held superannuation funds

## trusts that are exempt employee share schemes

## trusts that are debt funding special purpose vehicles

## lines trusts established under the Energy Companies Act 1992

## Trustees to prepare financial statements

The Tax Administration (Financial Statements—Domestic Trusts) Order 2022 prescribes the minimum standards for financial statements for domestic trusts. Section 59BA(2)(a) requires that a trustee to provide a statement of profit or loss and a statement of financial position, including explanatory commentary (commonly referred to as financial statements). The financial statements do not need to be filed with the annual return but must be available if the Commissioner requests them at a later date.

The minimum standard for financial statements set by the Order in Council includes a requirement that trusts follow the principles of accrual accounting. In addition, trustees are required to disclose comparable figures for the previous income year.

The first year for which the financial statements are required to be prepared to the minimum standards is the 2021–22 income year. The Commissioner recognises that some trustees may not have previously prepared financial statements, or have prepared statements to a lower standard, therefore the requirement to disclose comparable figures relating to the 2021 income year is only required to the extent that the trustee has that information.

## Core requirements for all trusts

The following minimum standard are set out in the Order in Council:

## the financial statements must consist of a:

## statement of financial position, and

## statement of profit and loss

## financial statements must be prepared using double-entry method of recording financial transactions

## financial statements must use the prescribed valuation principles and disclose the valuation method

adopted for land, buildings and shares or ownership interests

## completion of an IR10, where required.

In addition, unless a trust is a "simplified reporting trust", the financial statements must include:

## a statement of accounting policies

## disclosure of the following specific items:

## a reconciliation between profit or loss in the statement of profit and loss to taxable income

## an appropriately detailed schedule of the trust's fixed assets and depreciable property

## for trusts with forestry businesses – information about the cost of timber as at year end and a

reconciliation of movements in the costs of timber during the income year

## if a trust is a specified livestock owner, details of livestock valuation methods, valuations and

calculations for tax purposes

## details of transactions between the trust and any associated person of the trustee, unless the

transaction is minor and incidental to the activities of the trust

## transaction details including the names of the associated persons, nature of the association, nature of

the transactions and the amounts involved

## disclosure is not required if the transaction is at a market rate, or if disclosed separately elsewhere.

Most trustees that derive 'business' income will already be providing a completed IR10 Financial statements summary (IR10) when they file their tax return. Going forward, to align the s 59BA(2)(a) disclosure requirements and the existing IR10 form, the IR10 should reflect the trust's total income, expenses and all assets, liabilities, and equity, not just the 'business' income, expenses, assets and liabilities. A trustee who does not derive 'business' income is not required to provide an IR10.

## Valuation

Assets and liabilities can be valued at market value, cost or tax adjusted value. The appropriate valuation method is chosen by the trustees. Tax value may only be used in relation to assets that produce assessable income.

## De minimus threshold for small trusts (simplified reporting

trusts) To reduce the compliance burden on small trusts, an exemption from using accrual accounting is available for trusts that:

## derive assessable income of less than $100,000, and

## incur deductible expenditure, or loss, of less than $100,000, and

## hold total assets less than $5 million at all times during the year to which the exemption applies.

The total assets threshold includes both private and income producing assets.

The assessable income and deductible expenditure thresholds do not include assessable income which arises under the bright-line test or related deductible expenditure (¶7-050).

The de minimis exemption does not exempt the trustees from preparing financial statements nor from their obligation to file a return and include the required information. The exemption modifies the reporting standard. The exemption:

## permits small trusts to use cash-basis accounting, and

## removes the requirement to include prior-year comparable figures in the financial statements.

In addition, simplified reporting trusts do not have to include:

## a statement of accounting policies

## a reconciliation of the profit and loss in the statement of profit and loss with the trustee's taxable

income for the income year

## a schedule of the trust's fixed assets and depreciable property

## information about the cost of timber as at the end of the income year (or accounting period if

permitted) or a reconciliation of movements in the cost of timber during the year, if the trustee derives income from forestry

## details of livestock valuation methods, valuations and calculations for tax purposes, if the trust

includes specified livestock

## for each transaction in connection with the trust that is entered into between the trustee and any

associated person, the name of the associated person, the nature of the association or the nature of the transaction including the amounts involved.

## Further information required

Unless excluded, the following information must be returned to the Commissioner:

## financial statements (discussed above)

## the nature and amount of each settlement on the trust during the income year

## settlor details which have not previously been reported to IR, including name, date of birth, tax

jurisdiction, and taxpayer identification number

## details of distributions made during the year, including the amount of the distribution and personal

details of the beneficiary (name, date of birth, tax jurisdiction, and taxpayer identification number)

## the details of each person having a power under the trust to appoint or dismiss a trustee, to add or

remove a beneficiary, or to amend the trust deed (name, date of birth, tax jurisdiction, and taxpayer identification number of each person)

## any other information the Commissioner may require.

For more detail on the above, see OS 22/02 and the Order in Council.

## Settlements

It is important to note that the amount of any settlement is determined in line with s HC 4(1B), and does not include the provision to the trustee, at less than market value, of minor services incidental to the activities of the trust. An additional item to note is when any beneficiary who is owed an amount by the trust that exceeds $25,000 at the end of an income year, and the beneficiary is not paid interest at the prescribed or market rate, the beneficiary is deemed to be a settlor (ss HC 27(2)(bb) and HC 27(6)).
