# Imposition of income tax ¶1-090

Section BB 1 is the charging provision that imposes income tax on a person's taxable income. This taxable income for any tax year is determined by offsetting any available net losses against the person's net income.

A person's net income is determined by reference to annual gross income less annual total deduction. In other words, individual sources of income can only be identified at the gross level of the tax calculation (¶2-105). The way in which s BB 1 applies may be illustrated by reference to a person who derived income from 2 sources: salary and New Zealand superannuation.

*Refs: Section BB 1, s BB 1*

Example: A person took the view that tax at the appropriate rate should be imposed on each item of income separately. This method produced a lower tax burden than if the tax payable had been calculated on the sum total of all items of income. The TRA rejected the person's method. Income tax is imposed on the year's income as an accumulated sum and not on each item of income separately.

See Case D45 (1980) 4 NZTC 60,763.

See also Tax Information Bulletin ¶89-115 Vol 8, No 9, November 1996 at 27. However, certain categories of income, called schedular income, are subject to separate income tax calculations to ensure conformity with the gross/global concept underlying the legislation (¶2-105).

Law: IT07 s BB 1
