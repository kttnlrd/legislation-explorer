# Goods liable to FBT ¶21-430

Free, subsidised or discounted goods provided by the employer may constitute unclassified benefits.

## Goods manufactured by the employer

Where goods are manufactured, produced or processed by the employer, the value of the benefit is equal to the lowest arm's length (GST-inclusive) price at which identical goods are sold by the employer to wholesalers, retailers or the general public (¶21-638). "Discount" sales to employees of the employer's goods will not give rise to a benefit if the amount paid by the employee does not fall below the lowest price charged on an arm's length sale. However, sales "at cost" will give rise to a benefit if the cost price is below the price charged to anyone at arm's length.

Example: Sales at wholesale would not give rise to a benefit. However, where the employer bulk-discounts on an arm's length sale, the reduced price per item on such sales could be the price charged to employees without a benefit arising even though the employees do not buy in the same bulk. It would still be, provided the goods were identical, the lowest arm's length price.

## Goods purchased or paid for by the employer

The benefit of free, subsidised or discounted goods is liable for FBT if it is provided by an employer in an arm's length transaction to an employee at less than the GST-inclusive cost to the employer. This valuation means that discount sales to employees of goods at cost will not give rise to any benefit. A retailer, for example, which permits its employees to purchase goods at a staff discount which does not fall below the cost of the goods to the employer will not be providing any benefit. Cost to the employer, in most cases, means the purchase price of those goods to the employer.

Discounted goods provided by a third party under an arrangement with an employer may be exempt from FBT (¶21-638).

## Questions we've been asked on gifts cards and products

QWBA QB 25/07: "What is the income tax treatment of gift cards and products provided as trade rebates or promotions?" explains that products or gift cards received as trade rebates by a business and provided to its employees (directly or indirectly) may give rise to employment income, a fringe benefit or a dividend in the case of shareholder-employees. The tax treatments are summarised below.

## Products provided directly or indirectly to employees

Products provided to employees who are not shareholder-employees will be unclassified fringe benefits and FBT will be payable on the market value of the product (subject to the de minimis threshold for unclassified benefits). For products provided to shareholder-employees, an unclassified benefit will arise unless the benefit is treated as a dividend under s CX 17.

## Open loop gift cards provided to employees

Open loop gift cards are prepaid cards co-branded with a credit card or other payment network processor (eg Prezzy cards). The Commissioner considers that open loop cards are e-money and money. Therefore, the provision of open loop cards by trade customer employers to non-shareholder-employees is employment income and subject to PAYE. Provision of these cards to shareholder-employees is assessable income and the tax treatment depends on the shareholder-employee's election under ss RD 3B and RD 3C.

## Implementation of the tax position in QB 25/07

In Operational Position 25/02: "Commissioner's operational position on the FBT treatment of open loop cards provided by employers to employees" the Commissioner recognises that immediate implementation of the tax position outlined in QB 25/07 would be difficult due to required changes for the taxpayer's accounting record system. Therefore, the Commissioner confirms that IR will not apply resources for the determination of whether an employer taxpayer has applied the position outlined in QB 25/07 correctly for the PAYE periods covering 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026. This position only applies when the employer has returned FBT on the provision of open loop cards during this period.

## Note: The Commissioner has acknowledged that, in practice, employers have treated the provision of open

loop cards to employees as subject to FBT rather than PAYE. To this end, the Taxation (Annual Rates for 2025–26, Compliance Simplification, and Remedial Measures) Bill contains a proposed amendment that will allow employers to treat "gift cards" provided to employees as fringe benefits unless the employer chooses to treat the gift card as subject to PAYE. The proposed amendment is retrospective and will apply from 16 April 2025.

## Closed loop gift cards provided to employees

Closed loop cards are prepaid cards that are specific to a particular merchant or multiple merchants at the same location, such as a shopping mall. The Commissioner's view is closed loop cards are neither e- money nor money; as such, the trade rebate closed loop cards provided by trade customer employers to their employees are not employment income of the employees and subject to the FBT rules. These type of gift cards give rise to an unclassified benefit and FBT liability arises unless the de minimis for unclassified benefits applies. Note where the employee is a shareholder-employee, an unclassified benefit will arise unless the benefit is treated as a dividend under s CX 17.

## Group companies

If a group company is providing the goods, that company may choose to value the benefit as if the group were one company.

## Commissioner may determine value of benefit

Where no value for a benefit from goods can be determined on any of the bases discussed above, then the value will be market value or a value determined by the Commissioner. In this case, market value is the price normally paid, at the time when the fringe benefit is received by the employee, for the fringe benefit in a sale in the open market, freely offered, made on ordinary trade terms and to a member of the public at arm's length.
