# Overview of the student loan scheme ¶8-010

The Student Loan Scheme Act 2011 (SLSA 2011) governs the administration of student loans and how borrowers manage and repay their loans. The student loan scheme provides for the payment of compulsory fees, course-related costs and living costs (¶8-015).

A student will be eligible for a student loan if they are a New Zealand citizen, a permanent resident or a refugee, and enrolled in a Tertiary Education Commission approved course (¶8-020).

Whether a borrower is based in New Zealand or overseas will determine the type of repayment obligations that apply (¶8-025 and ¶8-030).

Repayment obligations depend on the type of income the borrower has. Methods for repayment differ according to whether the borrower earns a salary or wages, pre-taxed income (eg dividends or interest) and/ or other income (eg rental income).

Loan repayments for salary and wages earners are deducted by the employer based on the pay period repayment threshold and paid to Inland Revenue (IR) on behalf of the borrower. Deductions are made at a rate of 12% of any income over the repayment threshold. Repayment is not required if income is below the threshold. The threshold for the 2025–26 income year is $24,128 (¶8-040).

Unless there is a significant over- or under-deduction, the borrower's repayment deductions are considered final and there is usually no need for an annual assessment. Annual assessments may be required if the borrower has income from other sources such as interest, dividends, rental or business income.

If there is a significant under-deduction, IR may require the employer to make "catch-up" deductions from the borrower's future salary or wages. For significant over-deductions, the borrower can either request a refund or have this applied to their loan balance (¶8-065).

A borrower that derives income from secondary employment may apply to transfer any unused repayment threshold from their primary employment to their secondary employment.

Borrowers who are full-time students and earn less than the annual repayment threshold can apply to be exempt from repayment deductions from their salary or wages and are not required to file an income tax return (¶8-060).

The New Zealand Customs Service and IR can exchange information under an information matching programme for the purposes of verifying whether borrowers are based overseas or in New Zealand (¶8-095).

Late payment interest is charged where a borrower has failed to pay an amount by the due date (¶8-100). If a borrower takes an incorrect tax position, they may be liable for a shortfall penalty (¶8-105). If a borrower wilfully or negligently fails to provide information to IR, penalties for criminal offences may apply (¶8-110).

*Refs: Student Loan Scheme Act 2011*
