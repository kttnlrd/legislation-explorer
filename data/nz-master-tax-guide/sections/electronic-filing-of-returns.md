# Electronic filing of returns ¶2-019

The Commissioner may give approval to any person or GST-registered person and/or the person's agent to file information electronically. The return must be filed in the prescribed format and approval may be subject to any conditions specified by the Commissioner.

Where the return has been filed electronically, a signed, hard-copy of the return does not need to be retained if the information is kept in an electronic form meeting the requirements of s 229 of the Contract and Commercial Law Act 2017. The information must be retained for at least 7 years.

An agent may sign any hard-copy form of a return where the return is filed electronically.

IR is able to accept most income tax returns, employment income information and correspondence by electronic means, including details required under the provisional tax pooling provisions or in relation to the operation of the person's environmental restoration account. The Commissioner requires electronic filing of GST returns (see below). FBT returns (¶21-030) can also be filed online. A return or information furnished by electronic means must be transmitted in an electronic format prescribed under s 33E, 35, 36, 36BB, 36BC, 36BD or 36BE of the TAA 1994 (s 36C).

Employers with a combined PAYE and employer's superannuation contributions tax bill of $50,000 or more for the preceding tax year, must submit their employment income information digitally unless they obtain a special dispensation from the Commissioner (¶3-023). There is a penalty for non-electronic filing (¶14-045).

Supplementary returns for the research and development credit must be filed through the taxpayer's MyIR (s 33E and ¶11-150).

*Refs: s 229, s 33E, s 36C*

## GST returns required electronically

With effect from 29 March 2018, s 36BD provides for the setting of a taxable supplies threshold above which GST-registered persons are required to file their GST return with IR electronically in a prescribed format. The threshold will be set separately by Order in Council, but this has not occurred yet.

GST-registered persons below the threshold will still be able to voluntarily file electronically.

The Commissioner may exempt a registered person, or class of registered persons, from the electronic filing requirements. The Commissioner must have regard to:

## the nature and availability of digital services to the person or persons in the class, including the

reliability of those services for their purposes

## the capability of the person or class of persons relating to the use of computers, and

## the costs they would incur in complying with the requirements if those costs would be unreasonable

in the circumstances.

The Commissioner must provide a statement of reasons for the exemption. Generally the exemption remains valid until the Commissioner notifies the registered person that it is to be cancelled, in which case it expires six months after the date given in the notice. However, the Commissioner may set a time limit on the exemption when it is made.

Although the provision applies from 29 March 2018, it will only affect taxpayers once a threshold has been set.
