# Taxation and Charities Review Authority general jurisdiction ¶4-235

The Taxation Review Authorities Regulations 1998 set down the procedure to be followed when pursuing a challenge before the Authority.

## Documents to be filed and served

The first step in challenging an assessment (or other disputable decision) is filing the notice of claim. The notice must be in the form approved by the chief executive of the Ministry of Justice. If the Commissioner has issued the taxpayer with a disclosure notice (¶4-185) relating to the assessment or disputable decision, the notice of claim must contain:

## the Commissioner's SOP (¶4-185)

## the taxpayer's SOP

## any written notice by the Commissioner of the disputable decision (eg the notice of assessment) and

the reasons for the disputable decision, and

## the taxpayer's physical address (to which documents can be officially served by the Commissioner).

If no disclosure notice has been issued to the taxpayer, they must include in the notice of claim the information that would have been in a SOP (in lieu of including the parties' actual statements). If no disclosure notice has been issued, the Commissioner must file and serve a notice of defence on the taxpayer within 25 days of being served with the notice of claim. The notice of defence must contain the information that the Commissioner would have had to include in the SOP.

Three copies of the notice of claim are to be filed with the Authority by delivering or posting them to the Authority at the Ministry of Justice. The notice can be delivered to: The Registrar Taxation and Charities Review Authority Tribunals Unit 86 Customhouse Quay Wellington 6011

Or posted to: The Registrar Taxation and Charities Review Authority Tribunals Unit SX 11159 Wellington.

The filing fee is $552. The Authority is able to waive the fee if it is satisfied that the disputant is unable to pay the fee or the proceeding concerns a matter of genuine public interest and is unlikely to be commenced or continued unless the fee is waived.

## Time limits

A taxpayer must file a notice of claim within 2 months of an assessment being issued or, when the taxpayer is challenging a disputable decision other than an assessment, within 2 months of the Commissioner issuing notice that the taxpayer's NOPA is rejected. In Case W53 (2004) 21 NZTC 11,493, the Authority held that filing notices of claim with the Authority was complete on posting.

The taxpayer must serve a copy of the notice of claim on the Commissioner within 10 working days of filing the notice with the Authority. Service of the notice (or any other document required to be served) must be served in one of the following ways: Delivery: Legal Services, Inland Revenue, Level 5, Asteron House, 55 Featherston Street, Wellington, 6011. Or postal: Legal Services, Inland Revenue, PO Box 2198, Wellington 6140.

## Directions hearings and interlocutory applications

Before the Authority hears a case, there must be a preliminary directions hearing at a place and time appointed by the Authority no sooner than 90 working days after filing the notice of claim (or a date agreed between the parties). At this hearing, the Authority will want to know how long the Commissioner and the taxpayer think the hearing will last. Both the taxpayer (or the taxpayer's agent) and the Commissioner must attend the directions hearing, otherwise the challenge is deemed to be withdrawn unless leave is obtained to proceed with the hearing. The Authority has a discretion to allow a taxpayer to attend a directions hearing by means of a telephone conference link-up or by audio-visual link. The Authority will decide who pays the cost of the telephone conference.

As soon as possible after the directions hearing, the Authority will send a notice recording its directions to the parties. Either the Commissioner or the taxpayer may then ask the Authority to set a date for the challenge to be heard. The Authority will then send the taxpayer a notice setting a time and date for the hearing.

In addition to the directions hearing, either the taxpayer or the Commissioner can make an interlocutory application to the Authority at any time before a hearing. Interlocutory applications are usually made to sort out a preliminary matter (of evidence, fact or law) before the substantive hearing is held. Unless the interlocutory application involves a notice to admit facts or the issue of a witness summons, the applicant must file the application in writing with the Registrar of the Authority and serve a copy on the other party.

## The hearing

At the hearing, the taxpayer may present their challenge personally or be represented by a solicitor, accountant or other person of the taxpayer's choice.

Taxation Review Authority hearings are not open to the public and, in most cases, costs are not awarded.

The exceptions to this are if either party does not appear at the hearing, or abandons or settles the dispute without giving adequate notice, the Authority strikes out a proceeding (see below), or the costs awarded relate to the filing fees paid by the objector or disputant. After the hearing, the Authority must give its decision in writing and state the reasons for the decision.

The decision does not contain the taxpayer's name or, as a general rule, any other information likely to identify the taxpayer.

The Authority has the power to determine a proceeding on the papers if the Authority considers it appropriate. However, before doing so, the parties must be given the opportunity to comment on whether the proceeding should be dealt with in that manner.

The hearing of a matter may be conducted by telephone, audio-visual link or other remote access facility if the Authority considers it appropriate and the necessary facilities are available.

An Authority may strike out a proceeding if satisfied that it:

## discloses no reasonable cause of action

## is likely to cause prejudice or delay

## is frivolous or vexatious, or

## is otherwise an abuse of process.

If a party is neither present nor represented at the hearing, an Authority may:

## if the party is required to be present, strike out the proceeding

## determine the proceeding in the absence of the party, or

## adjourn the hearing.

## Transfer to the High Court

The Commissioner can apply to have a challenge transferred to the High Court (s 138N(2) of the TAA). For a discussion of the exercise of the High Court's discretion when an application for transfer to the High Court is made by the Commissioner, see C of IR v Skudder (No 2) (2008) 23 NZTC 21,846. In that case the High Court declined the Commissioner's application for a challenge to be transferred from the TRA.
