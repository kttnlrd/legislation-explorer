# Specific anti-avoidance provisions ¶33-144

Subpart GB contains a number of specific anti-avoidance provisions which complement specific tax regimes contained elsewhere in the Act. A brief summary of each of the specific anti-avoidance provisions follows.

## Dividend stripping

Section GB 1 applies when certain share disposals are part of a dividend stripping arrangement (¶33-175).

Newton v FC of T (1958) 11 ATD 442; [1958] AC 450 (PC) is an example of a dividend stripping transaction.

## Transfer pricing

Section GB 2 extends the transfer pricing rule in s GC 6 (¶33-146) to a collateral arrangement such as a market sharing arrangement, an arrangement not to enter into a particular market, a back-to-back supply arrangement or an income-sharing arrangement (¶26-605).

## Loss carry-forward and carry-back

Sections GB 3 and GB 3B prevent the manipulation of rights attaching to shares in order to meet the terms of the net loss carry-forward or carry-back rules (¶18-035).

Sections GB 3BA, GB 3BAB and GB 3BAC prevent the business continuity test for carrying forward tax losses from being used for loss trading. For examples of where the sections would apply, see IS 25/14, "Income tax – arrangements involving tax losses carried forward under the business continuity rules", at [110], [112], [113] and reconstruction at [137].

## Group loss offset

Rules permitting grouping of company tax losses do not apply when shares in a company:

## have been subject to an arrangement (which may or may not alter the rights attached to the shares),

and

## the arrangement allows the company to satisfy the loss offset requirements in subparts IC and IP or s

IZ 8 for group companies, and

## a purpose of the arrangement is to defeat the intent and application of those subparts.

The same restriction applies when the arrangement applies to shares in a company other than the company that would otherwise be enabled to claim a group loss offset.

Section GB 4 prevents arrangements for the grouping of company tax losses. It uses the phrase "to defeat the intent and application of those provisions". This form of wording was also found in s 76 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 before the recasting of that provision. Consequently, the interpretation of that phrase by the Supreme Court in Glenharrow Holdings Ltd v C of IR (2009) 24 NZTC 23,236 (SC) will have some impact on the interpretation of s GB 4. Indeed, the use of the phrase "defeat the intent and application" appears in numerous provisions in subpart GB and hence Glenharrow will have an impact in the interpretation of each of the provisions in which the phrase appears.

## Trust beneficiaries

Section GB 5 prevents arrangements using trusts to defeat the loss continuity provisions.

provisions

Where there is a change in the beneficiaries of a trust under an arrangement which has a purpose or effect of defeating the intent and application of any of the continuity provisions, the trustee is treated as having disposed of the share or option to an unrelated third party and having immediately reacquired it (s GB 5 and ¶16-190).

## Qualifying companies

Section GB 6 is directed at arrangements involving qualifying companies.

Status as a qualifying company is denied if shares in the company, or another company, have been subject to an arrangement which has the purpose of defeating the intention and application of the qualifying company regime (s GB 6).

## International tax regime

Section GB 7 is directed at arrangements over the control of interests in foreign companies. If, as a result of an arrangement between 2 or more New Zealand residents, the control interests in a foreign company are held by another person or persons so as to prevent the foreign company from being a controlled foreign company (CFC), the interests are deemed to be held by the residents in equal shares.

Sections GB 9 to GB 14 contain provisions aimed at nullifying attempts by taxpayers to manipulate their control interests and income interests in CFCs by successive variations to those interests before and after a quarterly measurement day.

Sections GB 15 and GB 16 permit the Commissioner to disregard certain measurement elections in relation to CFC and foreign investment fund (FIF) interests if they involve an arrangement that defeats the intent and application of the international tax rules.

Section GB 15BA allows the Commissioner to treat an election to include or not to include a particular CFC in a test group as reversed if the person has entered into an arrangement to reduce the amount of net attributable CFC income or increase the amount of net attributable CFC losses.

Sections GB 15B and GB 15C apply to arrangements that may affect the application of the active business exemption as outlined at ¶26-062.

Section GB 15B provides that the income from a supply is included in the item gross adjustments when a CFC makes a supply to a person who would not meet the requirements of the default test for a non- attributing active CFC and the purpose behind the supply was to manipulate the formula by increasing the denominator. In other words, it applies when a CFC makes a supply with the purpose of increasing the tax measure of its total income in s EX 21D. Increasing total income lowers the ratio of attributable income to total income. This rule makes it clear that supplies made for this purpose are to be ignored. This rule is aimed at CFCs who repeatedly sell goods back and forth between each other or where a sale is put through an intermediary with the purpose of increasing the intermediate CFC's total income. The purpose of inflating the total incomes must be the main purpose of the arrangement.

If a person enters an arrangement with a purpose, that is more than incidental, of enabling a CFC to meet the requirements of s EX 21E (active business exemption based on accounting measures of income) when the CFC would not actually meet the requirements, s GB 15C will apply.

The use of accounting measures is allowed because it reduces the compliance costs for taxpayers.

However, accounting measures of income will inevitably differ from tax measures. Therefore, there is a risk of exploitation in order to benefit from the active business exemption if no effort has been made to align the accounting and tax measures of income by way of adjustments (eg s EX 21E).

If s GB 15C applies, the CFC does not qualify for the active business exemption and its income or loss must be attributed. In addition, if the arrangement involves a financial arrangement between a CFC and an associated CFC, it is possible that the associated CFC may also be treated as not qualifying for the active business exemption. This occurs if the arrangement involves a financial arrangement which produces

provisions

a foreign exchange loss for the first CFC, and it results in a decrease in the numerator of the formula (effectively reducing its accounting measure of attributable income).

Section GB 51 provides that an arrangement that has the purpose or effect of defeating the intention of the proportionality rule in the thin capitalisation rules can be disregarded.

Section GB 51B disregards increases or decreases in a value that affect a calculation under the thin capitalisation rules caused by an action or omission, or produced by an arrangement, with the purpose of effect of defeating the intent and application of those rules.

## Films

Sections GB 17–GB 19 contain provisions governing the timing of the deduction for deferred fees and other costs of acquiring and producing a film where their payment is deferred beyond the period in which they would normally have been payable, and giving the Commissioner certain powers to counteract arrangements which seek to artificially inflate the cost of producing a film or of acquiring rights in a film (¶10-446).

## Petroleum and mineral mining

Section GB 20 allows the Commissioner to adjust the taxable income of any person affected by an arrangement consisting of a disposal of any petroleum mining asset, the incurring of exploration expenditure or a farm-out arrangement which has the effect of, or which has been entered into for the purpose of, tax avoidance so as to counteract any tax advantage obtained by the person. What Parliament would expect to see in the arrangement is in fact present. It follows that the non-application of the substituting debenture rule would be within Parliament's purpose for that rule in this instance (s GB 20 and ¶28-110 and ¶28-125).

## Financial arrangements

Section GB 21 permits the Commissioner, if he is satisfied that a financial arrangement has been issued or transferred so as to defeat the application of the financial arrangements rules, to substitute an arm's-length consideration for the consideration used by the parties (¶6-560).

## Beneficiary income

Section GB 22 applies to any arrangement that operates to defeat the intent and application of ss HC 17–HC 23 (relating to income of beneficiaries) by transferring property or providing services or benefits via the trustee to a person other than a beneficiary of the trust. The property, services or benefits are treated as being received by the beneficiary. This requirement in s GB 22 does not apply to a Maori authority.

## Excessive remuneration

Section GB 23 provides the Commissioner with broad powers to treat excessive salary or wages or share of profits as income in the hands of the recipient and to treat certain payments as a dividend for tax purposes (¶16-590, ¶23-142 and ¶23-144).

No deduction is permitted for a payment for something other than services made by a taxpayer to their spouse, civil union partner or de facto partner unless the Commissioner consents to that deduction before the deduction is claimed (ss DB 57, DC 5 and ¶10-900).

When a close company pays or credits any sum as remuneration for services rendered by a shareholder, a director or one of their relatives, the Commissioner is authorised to limit the deduction available to a reasonable level of remuneration (s GB 25 and ¶16-070).

For a look-through company (LTC), the effective look-through interests and consequently the income and deductions allocated to an owner who is a minor (ie less than 20 years of age) may be adjusted by

provisions

the Commissioner, having regard to the services rendered by the minor to the company (s GB 25B and ¶19-210).

In QWBA QB 14/09: "Income Tax — Meaning of 'Excessive Remuneration' and 'Excessive Profits or Losses' Paid or Allocated to Relatives, Partners, Shareholders or Directors", IR responds to the question 'when is a payment of remuneration or allocation of profits or losses considered to be excessive for the purposes of ss GB 23 to GB 25B (¶16-070, ¶19-210 and ¶23-142).

## Commercial bills

Section GB 26 is aimed at preventing non-residents from avoiding non-resident withholding tax or approved issuer levy on a redemption payment by disposing of a commercial bill to a resident immediately before its maturity. Section GB 26 does this by including the redemption payment in the resident person's income if the sale or transfer from non-resident to resident "has the purpose of avoiding non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) or the approved issuer levy".

## Attribution rules

Sections GB 27–GB 29 contain rules for the attribution of personal services income when a working person has inserted an entity between themselves and the recipient of those services (¶33-325).

## Restrictive covenants

Section GB 30 allows the Commissioner to counteract the effect of arrangements which have an effect of avoiding the application of the restrictive covenant rule in s CE 9 (¶5-297).

## Fringe benefits

Section GB 31 also counteracts the effect of any arrangement that has a purpose or effect of defeating the intent and application of the FBT rules (¶21-060).

Section GB 31(4) and Sch 5 cl 4 operate to fix the tax value of a motor vehicle for FBT purposes at market value. This treatment applies when there is an arrangement between an employer and an associated person to defeat the intent and application of the FBT provisions, and the vehicle was owned by the employer or an associate in the 2 years before it was provided to the employee (¶21-265).

Section GB 32 concerns arrangements directed at the fringe benefit rules involving associates of employees.

In the case of a benefit provided to an associate of an employee, the benefit is deemed to be received by the employee and not the associate. There are limited exceptions for a benefit granted to a shareholder– employee, provided no arrangement exists between the parties to substitute employment income or avoid FBT. There is also an exception for a benefit provided by an LTC, partnership or limited partnership where the associate of the employee is an owner of the relevant LTC or a partner of the relevant partnership (¶21-080).

Section GA 2 allows the Commissioner to adjust any fringe benefit which a person obtains as a result of an arrangement that is void under s BG 1 (¶21-130).

## Depreciation

Section GB 33 allows the Commissioner to disallow any deduction for depreciation where it is considered there was an arrangement to enable additional depreciation to be claimed when it was not intended that it be available.

Imputation

provisions

Under s GB 34 a company is treated as not meeting the continuity requirements that apply for imputation credit account (ICA) purposes where any shares in that company or in any other company:

## have been subject to an arrangement or series of related or connected arrangements, or

## had any rights attaching to them extinguished or altered in any way

where the purpose, or one of the purposes, was to enable the company to meet the requirements (¶17-055).

Sections GB 35 and GB 36 operate to counteract the effect of credit-streaming arrangements in relation to imputation credits (¶17-120).

## "Stapled stock" arrangements

Section GB 37 counters the effect of "stapled stock" arrangements (in very general terms, where a company that cannot operate an ICA and pass credits to shareholders or does not have available imputation credits, arranges for shareholders to receive imputed dividends from another company), (¶17-115).

## Consolidated groups and imputation

Section GB 38 details how the specific anti-avoidance provisions in ss GB 35–GB 37 apply in the case of consolidated groups.

## M#ori authority credit accounts

Sections GB 42 and GB 43 operate to counteract the effect of credit-streaming arrangements in relation to M#ori authority credits (¶24-317).

## Tax credits for families

Section GB 44 is directed towards arrangements made with a view to increasing a person's entitlement to working for families tax credits under Pt M. The Taxation Review Authority's decision in Case Y1 (2007) 23 NZTC 13,001 is an example of the application of the section.

Arrangements which have the purpose or effect of defeating the intent and application of FamilyBoost tax credits will result in a reduction of the credit (s GB 44B).

## Limited recourse loans

Sections GB 45–GB 48 apply to certain arrangements involving limited recourse loans, providing for a deferral of deductions arising from those arrangements (¶33-285 and ¶10-061).

## Returning share transfers

Section GB 49 allows the Commissioner to counteract the effect of arrangements which have an effect of avoiding a requirement of the definition of a returning share transfer (¶17-116).

## Employee share schemes

Section GB 49B allows the Commissioner to counteract any tax advantage gained from an arrangement that attempts to circumvent the intent and application of the share scheme taxing date by seeking to have shares taxed either earlier or later than is consistent with that intent.

Partners and partnerships, owners of LTCs

provisions

Section GB 50 provides for situations where partners or owners of LTCs enter arrangements involving consideration that is not at market value (¶19-215 and ¶23-070).

## Land-rich companies and trusts

Sections GB 52 and GB 53 deem disposals of residential land to have occurred when companies and trusts are used to defeat the intent and application of the bright-line test (¶7-050).

## Interest arrangements related to land

## Permanent establishments

Section GB 54 deems a permanent establishment to exist in New Zealand for a non-resident if a number of criteria are met. The non-resident is deemed to make any relevant supplies through that permanent establishment (¶26-300).

## Charitable gifts

Section GB 55 provides for the Commissioner to reduce a tax credit for charitable or other public benefit gifts when an arrangement has a purpose or effect of defeating the intent and application of s LD 1 (¶11-055).

## Research and development tax credits

Section GB 56 provides for the Commissioner to reduce a research and development tax credit when an arrangement has a purpose or effect of defeating the intent and application of subpart LY (¶11-150).

Non-market transactions

Anti-avoidance provisions — Non-market transactions

Subpart GC contains provisions dealing with non-market transactions. A summary of the provisions follows.

## Trading stock

Under s GC 1, where trading stock is disposed of without consideration, or for an amount that is less than its market value at the date of sale, the trading stock is treated as having been sold for market value (¶9-087).

This section does not apply to certain identified disposals (including to shares transferred back to a share supplier under a share-lending arrangement (¶17-116)). From 1 April 2024, if a person disposes of, or donates, trading stock to an approved donee organisation at less than market value, the person can treat the disposal at its discounted value or as having zero value.

## Disposal of timber or timber rights

Section GC 2 requires certain disposals of timber or timber rights to be treated as disposals of trading stock for the purposes of s GC 1 (disposals at below market value).

## Life insurers

Section GC 3 requires certain property of a life insurer to be treated as trading stock for the purposes of s GC 1 (disposal below market value).

## Disposal of emissions units

Section GC 3B requires certain disposals of emissions units to be treated as disposals of trading stock for the purposes of s GC 1 (disposals at below market value) (¶9-063).

## Disposal of FIF interests

Under s GC 4, a person who disposes of an attributing interest in a FIF is treated as doing so at market value when:

## the consideration received is less than market value, and

## FIF income or loss has been calculated under the comparative value, deemed rate of return, cost or

fair dividend rate methods.

These 4 methods of calculating FIF income or loss also apply to a person acquiring an attributing interest in a FIF at other than market value.

## Leases for inadequate rent

Section GC 5 notionally increases the amount of rent received by a property owner from a relative or a related company where the rent is less than the market rate (¶10-830).

Transfer pricing

Non-market transactions

Sections GC 6–GC 19 set out the transfer pricing regime, which is directed at multinational companies that shift profits from New Zealand to countries with low tax costs (¶26-605–¶26-610).
