# Disposal of trading stock at below market value ¶9-087

Trading stock, or an interest in trading stock, that is sold or otherwise disposed of for no consideration or for less than market value is treated for tax purposes as having been sold at market value on the date of disposal. This deemed market value is income to the seller and is treated as expenditure to the purchaser (s GC 1).

From 1 April 2024, the deemed market value rule is limited to the following scenarios (s GC 1(1)(b)):

## the disposal is effected by a person taking the trading stock for their own use or consumption

## the disposal is not made by a person in the course of carrying on a business for the purpose of

deriving assessable income or excluded income, or a combination of both, or

## the disposal is made to an associated person.

In Foodstuffs (Wellington) Co-operative Society Ltd v C of IR (2010) 24 NZTC 23,959, the High Court confirmed that the market value of shares (held as trading stock) that were cancelled on amalgamation was income of the shareholder, and that s GC 1 applied to the transaction.

Section GC 2 provides that s GC 1 also applies to the following disposals as if they were disposals of trading stock:

## the grant (other than in favour of the grantor) of a right to take timber, or

## the sale or other disposal of land with standing timber on the land, other than a disposal subject to a

right in favour of the seller to take timber, unless the extent the standing timber is:

## trees that are ornamental or incidental

## trees in a crop subject to a forestry right registered under the Land Transfer Act, or

## trees subject to a profit à prendre granted before 1 January 1984.

In addition, the inclusion of an interest in trading stock within trading stock under s GC 1(4) does not apply if the disposal is of land with standing timber, subject to a right to take timber (s GC 2(3)).

It should be noted that the definition of "trading stock" is expanded for ss CG 6, FB 13, FC 1 and GC 1–GC 3 to include:

## anything produced or manufactured

## anything acquired for the purposes of manufacture or disposal

## livestock

## timber or a right to take timber

## land whose disposal would produce income under any of ss CB 6A–CB 15, and

## anything for which expenditure is incurred and that, if possession of that thing were taken, would be

trading stock.

The expanded definition of "trading stock" in s YA 1 specifically excludes financial arrangements to which the financial arrangements rules or the old financial arrangements rules apply.

*Refs: s GC 1, s GC 1(1)(b), Section GC 2, s GC 1(4), s GC 2(3), ss CG 6, ss CB 6A, s YA 1*

## Exclusion

The deemed market value rule does not apply to the following disposals (s GC 1(5)):

## A transfer of trading stock made to a person under a relationship agreement. In such a case, the

price is determined under s FB 13.

## A transfer of trading stock to a non-associated person for use by that person in a farming, agricultural,

or fishing business affected by a self-assessed adverse event.

## From 1 April 2024, a disposal of trading stock to a donee organisation.

## From 1 April 2024, a disposal of trading stock to which s GC 20(2) or GC 21(7) applies. These are

provisions under the purchase price allocation rules (¶33-200).

## Trading stock taken for own use or consumption

QWBA QB 14/01, "Income tax — adjustments for trading stock (including raw materials) taken for own use or consumption", considers the situation where trading stock is taken by a sole trader or a partner in a partnership for their own use or consumption. The item confirms that taking trading stock for own use or consumption is a disposal for s GC 1 and that the mutuality principle (that a man cannot make a profit by trading with himself) does not apply to override the clear words of the legislation that require an adjustment at market value.

Law: IT07 ss FB 13, FC 1, GC 1, GC 2, GC 20, GC 21, YA 1 ("trading stock")
