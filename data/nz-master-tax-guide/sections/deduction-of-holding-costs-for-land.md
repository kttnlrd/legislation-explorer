# Deduction of holding costs for land ¶7-371

## What are holding costs?

Holding costs are costs such as interest, rates, insurance, and repairs and maintenance expenditure that are incurred as a result of owning land. It is generally accepted that, to the extent that land is used to earn taxable income, holding costs are deductible. So, if a person owns a rental property, holding costs are deductible in full (subject to the rental ring-fencing rules – see ¶7-365) because those costs relate to the taxable rental income. See also the interest deductibility rules which disallow interest deductions for residential properties (¶10-814).

Equally, if land is held by a land dealer, holding costs are generally deductible in full because those costs relate to the taxable income from the sale of the land.

Prior to 27 March 2021, interest was allowed as a deduction for most companies, without the need to establish a connection with the production of assessable income. That principle would, for example, permit a company that is not a qualifying company to deduct the interest payable on a borrowing raised to purchase bare land that is retained for an extended period prior to committal to a development or division scheme. A non-corporate land owner would need to establish that any interest cost is expected to produce assessable income before a deduction for the expense would be allowed (¶10-650). However, since 27 March 2021, when subpt DH came into effect, some companies are disallowed from claiming interest. See ss DB 7(6C) and DH 3. See also ¶10-814.

## Privately used land taxable on sale

In October 2019, IR released a consultation document, "Holding costs for privately used land that is taxable on sale". The document considers the rules for the deductibility of holding costs for land that is taxable on sale and is used privately (in whole or in part) while it is held. For example, this will arise where a bach or second home is sold within 10 years (or 5 years if acquired before 27 March 2021) so that the gain from the sale is taxable under the bright-line test. This will also arise where a person regularly purchases properties to renovate and sell, and lives in the properties while they own them.

The document considers 3 options for the deductibility of holding costs for land subject to income tax on sale that is used privately. These options are:

## apportioning the holding costs between the taxable gain on sale and the private use of the land while

it is held

## allowing deductions for all holding costs, even though there is private use, and

## denying deductions for all holding costs for periods of private use.

While it is acknowledged that an apportionment approach would arguably be the most accurate option, the document notes that it would not be consistent with other areas of New Zealand's tax law where no apportionment is required. Therefore, officials' current view is that denying deductions for all holding costs for periods of private use would be the best option.

The document also considers the tax treatment of holding costs relating to land that is not actively used and is taxable on sale. If deductions are denied as a result of private use, it is necessary to determine whether unused or vacant land should be treated as being used privately or for income earning purposes.

The document proposes that the treatment of vacant or unused land as either private or income earning could depend on the other uses of the land during the period of ownership. This would mean that:

## where land is otherwise actively used either privately or for income-earning purposes, any vacant or

unused days could be treated similarly

## where land is used for both income-earning and private purposes and there are more than 62 days of

vacant or unused time, the current "mixed-use asset rules" could continue to apply.

Where land is wholly vacant or unused, the use could be considered income earning if:

## it is held for a business of dealing in, developing, or building on land

## it is held for another income-earning purpose (eg land purchased to expand a current business onto,

or to erect a rental property), or

## the taxpayer otherwise informs the Commissioner at the time of purchase that the land was acquired

solely with an intention of resale for profit.

In all other situations where land is wholly vacant or unused the use would be considered private.

This document should be read keeping in mind the updated comments in the interpretation statement discussed below.

## Interpretation statement

Interpretation statement IS 23/10 "Deductibility of holding costs for land" considers the deductibility of holding costs related to land in detail and covers all taxpayers, except companies. It discusses the key aspect of nexus between the expenditure incurred and income derived, and when apportionment of any allowable holding costs is required. Apportionment is required when the land is used privately by the owner at times.

## Land must be used in income-earning activity

A key aspect of the statement is that land must be used to derive assessable income. Use of land generally means that the land is physically used or occupied, like being rented out or used by the owner in some way. Where land is held on capital account, the view in the statement is that it cannot be said that the land is being held for income-earning sale as the property is not committed to an income-earning activity. This is the case even when the land disposal may result in tax being payable, for example when the bright-line test applies. In circumstances where the bright-line test applies to the disposal of land, holding costs will not be deductible. See [12] and [30]–[32] of the summary of the interpretation statement for discussion on land held on capital account and periods of private use and periods of rental use relating to the land. Where assessable rental income is derived, holding costs relating to those periods are deductible. However, where there has been a period of private use, holding costs are not deductible. If there is no current year income- earning use of land, the general permission under s DA 1 is not satisfied.

See below for commentary where the holding costs relate to mixed-use assets, for property used both privately and rented out from time to time.

## Interest deductibility

When holding land, taxpayers are limited by subpt DH (the interest limitation rules) when the interest expenditure satisfies the nexus test under the general permission. The disallowed interest expenditure may become deductible when the property is disposed of and the disposal is taxed. The amount that is allowed as a deduction on disposal is dependent on application of the general permission and apportionment (where applicable). If the interest is deductible, the previously disallowed interest expense is added to the cost of the land in the year of disposal (subject to any cost reduction under s DB 23C and also subject to s EL 20 under the loss ring-fencing rules). See [45] in the summary onwards and [172] onwards in the main statement.

## Cost of land on disposal

If the disposal of land is taxable, a deduction for the cost of land is allowed (s DB 23). The statement covers whether the holding costs related to the land forms part of the "cost" of land. The Commissioner's view is that holding costs do not form part of the costs intended to be deductible under s DB 23. Their view is that s DB 23 applies to costs incurred to acquire the land. This includes the initial outlay on acquisition as well as the cost of capital improvements (as these form part of the land). Holding costs do not form part of these initial acquisition costs, but rather they are incurred as part of ownership holding of the land.

## Deductibility where land is subject to bright-line test

Generally during the period of ownership if land is held on capital account (not held for taxable sale), then a taxpayer cannot deduct holding costs. Notably, there is an exception for land taxed under the bright-line test.

Where land is taxed under the bright-line test, interest expense which was disallowed due to the application of the interest limitation rules under subpt DH are allowed to be deducted as part of the cost of land under s DB 23. The total amount allowed is calculated in accordance with s DB 23C which reduces that allowed interest expense if the property has been used as a main home. In addition, the bright-line loss quarantine rule in s EL 20 must be taken into account. As a result, the final allowable interest deduction may be reduced under any of these provisions.

## Apportionment

Where land is used privately, holding costs are not deductible as there is no derivation of assessable income. Therefore, if land is used privately during an income year for periods of time, apportionment of the holding costs must take place. The method of apportionment will depend on whether the mixed-use asset rules apply or not (below and at ¶10-035). If they do not, then holding costs are subject to:

## apportionment for any period the property is not part of an income earning process, and

## where applicable, application of subpt DH denial of interest deductions.

Ring-fencing of rental losses may also apply (¶7-365).

## When to apply the mixed-use asset rules

The statement clarifies when the mixed-use asset rules should be used, as opposed to the general deductibility rules. These rules already apportion use in the formula. The mixed-use asset rules apply when the land is (¶10-035):

## used privately (including individuals associated with the owner, or if the property is rented out at less

than 80% of the market value rent)

## used for income-earning, and

## unused for at least 62 days.

The statement covers a summary of apportionment of holding costs at [15] to [37] in the summary.

The interpretation statement contains a flowchart which can assist in the determination of whether, and to what extent, holding costs are deductible.
