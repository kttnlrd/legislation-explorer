# Determining the amount of tax payable ¶1-380

Taxpayers must calculate and satisfy their income tax liabilities in accordance with the core provisions. The process that taxpayers other than non-filing taxpayers must follow is set out in ss BC 2–BC 8.

To arrive at the actual income tax payable for any year, certain amounts must be determined, namely the annual gross income derived by the taxpayer for the year and the annual total deduction for that year.

This includes general business and income-related deductions, as well as deductions for depreciation and incentives. Subject to any timing rules, these deductions are taken from the gross income figure to determine the net income or net loss for the year. Any net losses from previous years available for carry-forward are deducted from the net income to give rise to taxable income.

The actual calculation can be shown as follows: [annual gross income]  [annual total deduction] = [net income or net loss] [net income or net loss]  [available tax loss] = [taxable income] To calculate the gross tax payable (referred to as the unadjusted income tax liability), a taxpayer applies the basic tax rate for the particular tax year to the taxpayer's taxable income (s BC 6(1)). A taxpayer's final tax liability is satisfied by first applying any available tax credits (for tax paid or withheld) and then through the payment of terminal tax (s BC 8).

Any tax credits remaining after offset against the income tax liability are first set off against other income tax obligations in accordance with Pts L and M (¶1-400). Any remaining excess credits, subject to s LA 5, may be refunded by the Commissioner (¶2-110).

For non-filing taxpayers, the total tax already deducted at source is the year's tax liability (¶2-105).

*Refs: ss BC 2, s BC 6(1), s BC 8, s LA 5*
