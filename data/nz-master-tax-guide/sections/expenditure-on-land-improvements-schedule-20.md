# Expenditure on land improvements — Schedule 20 ¶50-166

The formula calculating the amount of the deduction allowed for land improvements of a kind listed in sch 20 is: Schedule 20 percentage × diminished value where:

Schedule 20 is the percentage set out opposite to the description of the improvement listed in sch 20, and percentage diminished value is the diminished value of the improvement.

The kinds of expenditure (ie farming, freshwater fish farming, mussel farming, rock oyster farming, scallop farming, sea-cage salmon farming and forestry) eligible for the progressive deduction, and the appropriate percentage allowable as a deduction in each income year, set out in sch 20, are listed below.

Note that in the Income Tax Act 2004 the equivalent of cl 2 above gave a percentage of 45.

The deduction is available irrespective of whether the person owns the land. A person farming land owned by the person may claim a deduction for development expenses incurred either by the person or any other person. This allows a deduction for expenses incurred by a previous owner. But a person farming land that the person does not own may only claim for expenses incurred by the person.

In each case, the expenditure must be incurred in developing the land on which the farming or agricultural business is carried on. It must also be expenditure that is of benefit to the business. No deduction is allowable in calculating the income derived in the income year in which the person disposes of the land. See s DO 4.

In each case, the eligible expenditure must be in respect of improvements and be incurred in developing the aquaculture business. It must also be expenditure that is of benefit to the aquaculture business.

A person working with aquaculture improvements that the person owns may claim a deduction for development expenses incurred either by the person or by any other person. This allows a deduction for expenses incurred by previous owners. However, a current lessee may not claim a deduction if the expenditure was incurred by a previous owner or lessee. A person working with improvements that the person does not own may only claim for expenses incurred by the person alone.

No deduction is allowable in calculating the assessable income derived by the aquaculturist in the income year in which the aquaculturist ceases to carry on the business. See s DO 6.

The eligible expenditure must be incurred in developing the land. It must also be expenditure that is of benefit to the forestry business. In the case where the person owns the forest land, the qualifying expenditure need not be incurred by the person. A deduction is still available if the expenditure is incurred by some other person such as the previous owner of the land. This enables a purchaser of a forestry business to continue with the deduction for the cost of land improvements.

No deduction is allowed for the income year in which the person disposes of the forest land or ceases to carry on a forestry business on the land. Expenses not included in the above table must be capitalised to a cost of forest account and deducted from the proceeds of sale as part of the cost of the timber. See s DP 3.
