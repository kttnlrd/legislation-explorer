# GST and finance leases ¶32-648

Interpretation statement IS 22/02, "GST and finance leases" explains how to classify finance leases for the time of supply and value of supply rules under the GST Act. The statement also explains how to account for GST on a finance lease when applying any special time and value of supplies.

## Note: For taxable periods beginning on or after 1 April 2023, "tax invoice" includes taxable supply

information.

A finance lease is not defined for GST purposes. A "finance lease", in general commercial terms, is a lease of an asset for a fixed term when the amounts payable by the lessee relate to the value of the leased goods, and not the value of their use to the lessee. The terms of a finance lease can differ and therefore analysis of a specific finance lease is required to determine the application of the GST rules. Classification of the agreement takes place at the time the agreement is entered into.

## Time of supply

As discussed in ¶32-026, the general time of supply rule is modified for certain supplies. In order to apply the correct time of supply rules, a finance lease needs to be correctly classified. A finance lease will fall into one of the following categories:

## Agreement to hire: goods are treated as being as being successive supplies and each supply is

treated as taking place at the earlier of when payment is due, or when payment is received.

## Hire purchase agreement: time of supply is when the agreement is entered into by the parties.

## Third category agreement: the agreement is neither an agreement to hire nor a hire purchase

agreement. In these cases, the general time of supply rules apply. Therefore, the time of supply is the earlier of the time the supplier issues an invoice or receives payment for the supply.

## Hire purchase agreement defined

A "hire purchase agreement" is defined in s YA1 as an agreement:

## under which goods are let or hired with an option or purchase, or

## for the purchase of goods by instalment payments; however the agreement describes the payments

under which the person who agrees to purchase the goods is given possession of them before the total amount payable has been paid.

The definition has a number of paragraphs defining a hire purchase agreement so it is important to compare the agreement against the entire definition. The definition expressly excludes an agreement "made otherwise than at retail". IS 22/02 discussed what is meant by this phrase. This is important because a finance lease can be a hire purchase agreement only if it is made at retail. If the agreement is not made at retail, it cannot be a hire purchase agreement. The term "at retail" is not defined for tax purposes. Generally, an agreement is made at retail if it is made otherwise than at wholesale (sales to person who buy to sell again) or privately.

Commonly a finance lease is an agreement for hire for GST purposes. However, the agreement will not be an agreement for hire if:

## the lessee is the end user of the goods and the lessor give the lessee an option to purchase the

asset, or

## the title to the property passes upfront or will definitely pass under the terms of the agreement.

Flowchart 1 in IS 22/02 at 3 summarises the process for classifying finance leases for time of supply.

Value of supply

Application of the value of supply rules depends on whether the finance lease is a credit contract or not.

When it is a credit contract, GST is calculated on the "cash price" of the leased goods. The balance of the payments under a credit contract will usually be interest and finance charges which are classified as financial services (which are exempt from GST under ss 3(1)(f) and 14(1)(a), unless zero-rated under s 11A(1)(q)).

When a finance lease is not a credit contract, GST is calculated on the total amount payable under the lease.

IS 22/02 has detailed commentary on finance leases that are credit contracts. In short, this will depend on whether it is a credit contract under the Credit Contracts Act 1981. However, the lessor and lessee can elect to apply the definition in the Credit Contracts and Consumer Finance Act 2003 (CCCFA) to a particular arrangement if this provides a different outcome. The lease agreement and the tax invoices should reflect the agreed position.

## Accounting for GST on finance leases

How a finance lease is accounted for will depend on whether it is:

## a hire purchase agreement, an agreement to hire, or a third category agreement, and

## a credit contract.

The table below summarises how GST relating to a finance lease is accounted for:

Category of lease Credit contract GST calculation Timing Hire purchase or third Yes GST is 3/23 of the cash GST payable upfront category agreement price of the leased goods Hire purchase or third No GST is 3/23 of the full GST payable upfront category agreement consideration of the leased goods Agreement to hire Yes GST is 3/23 of the cash GST spread over the term price of the leased goods of the lease.

Lessors may adopt straight line method and must return on the basis set out in the tax invoice.

Lessees must adopt the method set out in the tax invoice Agreement to hire No GST is 3/23 of each lease GST paid on each lease payment payment over the term of the lease

## Agency issues and on selling goods

Where a lessee sells goods as agent for the lessor, the supply is treated as being made by the lessor as they are the principal. In these cases, the lessor must account for the GST. Even if the lessee issues the tax invoice, the onus to return the GST is on the lessor. The lessee is required to notify the lessor of the sale and furnish them with sufficient information for the lessor to account for the GST correctly. An exception applies where the lessor and the lessee agree to treat the supply as 2 separate supplies.

This is a complex area of GST and the interpretation statement goes into more detail on the following points:

## changes to the definition of "hire purchase agreement"

## issues concerning the treatment of options to purchase

## whether business to business leases are made "at retail"

## uncertainty about agreement that expressly contemplate ownership passing to the lessee

## the new definition of "credit contract" under the CCCFA, and

## agency issues on selling goods at the end of the lease.

GST and hire firm security bonds and late return charges

## Hire firm security bonds

The GST implications of a customer paying a bond to a GST registered hirer of goods as security when the goods are hired are considered in QWBA, QB 14/06: "GST — Hire firm security bonds". QB 14/06 concludes that the paying of the bond has no GST effect because there are no services supplied in return for the bond.

If the bond is repaid in full on the return of the goods, the answer remains the same. However, where, or to the extent, the bond is forfeited because it is applied in accordance with the agreement between the parties:

## as payment of an extra hire charge because the goods are returned late, it is consideration for a

supply and is subject to GST

## as compensation for damage to, or loss of, the goods, it is not consideration for a supply and is not

subject to GST

## for the purchase of goods or services, it is consideration for a supply and is subject to GST, or

## for a breach of a condition (eg operating the goods in a way not permitted under the hire agreement),

it is not consideration for a supply and it is not subject to GST.

## Late return charges

QWBA, QB 14/14, "Goods and services tax — Late return charges (including library fines and parking overstay charges)" concerns charges imposed for the late return of a borrowed item, referred to collectively as "late return charges". The Commissioner considers the law in this area to be well settled. The principles are as set out in QB 14/06 above and the interpretation statement on court awards and out-of-court settlements (¶32-624).

The item concludes that a late return charge will be subject to GST if it is consideration for a taxable supply. Whether the charge is consideration for a taxable supply will depend on whether a sufficient nexus or reciprocity exists between the charge and the supply. This will be determined from the legal arrangements entered into. In most cases, a late return charge will be subject to GST. This is because the legal arrangements between the parties will characterise the charge as additional consideration for the supply of the borrowed item. However, in some cases, the legal arrangements between the parties may characterise the late return charge as a payment of damages or a penalty for a breach of the legal arrangements. In these cases, the charge will not be subject to GST because it will not be consideration for a taxable supply.
