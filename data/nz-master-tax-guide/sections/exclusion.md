# exclusion ¶10-819

The general rule against the deductibility or residential rental interest (¶10-814) does not apply to build- to rent land. For build-to-rent dwellings that meet the asset class definition below, there is an in-perpetuity exclusion from the interest limitation rules . However, if the land fails to meet the definition at any point, the interest limitation rules apply, and interest is no longer able to be deducted in relation to that residential rental property.

The requirement is that the land must continually meet the requirements. If there was a period of time where the land failed to meet the requirements and then met the requirements at some point in the future, the build- to-rent exemption cannot apply as the land must continuously meet the statutory requirements.

For the exclusion to apply, land must meet the following build-to-rent asset class definition:

## the land must be development or subdivision land (as described in ss CB 12 and CB 13)

## tenants must be offered a fixed-term tenancy of at least 10 years with the ability to give 56 days'

notice of termination but they may agree to or request other tenancy offers (Note that in order to qualify as build-to-rent, a tenant does not have to accept a 10-year tenancy offer. A build-to-rent development will satisfy this requirement as long as a 10-year tenancy term is offered.)

## at least 20 dwellings in one or more buildings that comprise a single development, on either a single

parcel of land or multiple contiguous parcels

## the dwellings and any common land or facilities for those dwellings have a single owner

## dwellings can be held in one or more titles

## the building that a build-to-rent dwelling is in can include other dwellings or commercial premises that

do not form part of the build-to-rent development (for example, an apartment block that has shops on the ground floor)

## the dwellings are used or available for rent under the Residential Tenancies Act 1986

## explicit personalisation policies must be offered, over and above the Residential Tenancies Act 1986,

and

## approval must be granted by Te T##papa Kura K#inga (Ministry of Housing and Urban Development)

that the development has met the asset class definition.

Although the exclusion was introduced in March 2023, the exclusion is effective from 1 October 2021 when the interest limitation rules for residential rental property was enacted. Therefore, taxpayers who held existing build-to-rent assets are not be subject to the interest limitation rules for the short period of 1 October 2021 to 31 March 2022 and will continue to be exempt in perpetuity. For new build-to-rent assets, initial investors are able to deduct interest for as long as they hold an asset and operate it as a build-to-rent development. Any subsequent investors can deduct interest for as long as the asset is held and operated as a build-to-rent development.

*Refs: ss CB 12*

## Exemption applies to existing dwellings that meet the

## definition by 1 July 2023

The policy is intended to apply to both new and existing build-to-rent developments. However, in reality, landlords are unlikely to have any existing developments would have offered 10-year tenancies to all tenants. Therefore, existing build-to-rent assets have until 1 July 2023 to comply with the tenure and personalisation policy requirements included in the above definition of "build-to-rent land" and qualify for the exemption.

Configuration of land

The definition is structured in a way that allows the 20 or more dwellings to be held in one or more titles.

The parcels of land could be adjoining each other. As the rationale is to increase community living spaces, it is envisioned that mixed property types, like commercial property, may be part of the development. If commercial property forms part of the development, then an apportionment must be applied between the residential land and the commercial land.

## Deductibility "to the extent"

The exemption will apply to the extent that the land meets the definition. Determination must be made of the extent that each individual dwelling meets the definition as well as the overall development. Only interest which relates to the portion of the development that meets the build-to-rent definition can claim a deduction under this exemption. The balance of the development may or may not be able to deduct interest under another provision.

To illustrate when a development qualifies "to the extent" it meets the definition, see examples in the "Special Report on build-to-rent exclusion from interest limitation rules", April 2023.
