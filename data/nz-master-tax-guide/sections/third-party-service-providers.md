# Third party service providers ¶2-038

There are different types of third party service providers (in addition to tax agents) that IR may offer "special" or extended service offerings to in order to assist with their clients' tax and social policy obligations or entitlements. These include representatives or nominated persons. The legislation sets out the eligibility requirements that a person must satisfy to be approved by IR as a representative or nominated person. IR may also withhold approval or disallow a person as a representative or nominated person when this action is necessary to protect the integrity of the tax system.

## Representatives

Section 124D sets out that a person is eligible to be a representative if they:

## have signed authorities to act for 10 or more other persons in relation to their tax affairs, or in relation

to their entitlements and obligations arising under social policy that is administered by IR, and

## are in a business, occupation or employment in which they act on behalf of others in relation to their

tax affairs or social policy entitlements and obligations

## are carrying on a professional public practice dealing in matters relating to tax and social policy

assistance, or

## are in a business, occupation or employment in which they provide budget advisory services to other

persons or claim entitlements to social policy assistance on behalf of other persons.

IR must approve the person as a representative if it considers that the person meets the above requirements and that approving the person as a representative would not adversely affect the integrity of the tax system. Approval can be revoked if it is considered that the person does not meet the relevant eligibility requirements, or that continuing to allow the person to act as a representative for others would adversely affect the integrity of the tax system.

If the person applying to be a representative is not a natural person, they must provide the Commissioner with the names of the following:

## for companies, other than a closely held company, each person who has the duties of tax manager,

chief financial officer, chief executive officer, or director

## for closely held companies, each shareholder

## for partnerships, each partner

## for unincorporated bodies, each member.

If these names change, the person must notify the Commissioner within 12 months of the change.

The Commissioner may also request further information in relation to an application from any person applying and may obtain information relating to them from other persons.

## Nominated person

A person may also nominate another person to act for them in relation to their tax affairs or social policy entitlements and obligations by informing the Commissioner and providing the following information:

## their name, contact address and tax file number

## the name of the nominated person and any other information that may lead the Commissioner to be

satisfied about the identity of the nominated person

## the relevant tax types of social policy entitlements and obligations for which the nominated person

intends to act for them, and

## the relevant start and end dates.

If the person making the nomination is not a natural person, they must also provide the name and position of a natural person for them.

IR may disallow a person's status as another's nominated person where continuing to allow the person to be a nominated person would adversely affect the integrity of the tax system. In addition, the Commissioner may disallow a person's status as a nominated person if the person is acting in a fee-earning or other professional capacity (ie as a tax agent) and continuing to allow the person to act as nominated person for another person would adversely affect the integrity of the tax system. However, this does not apply if the person is acting as a nominated person on behalf of their spouse, civil union partner, de facto partner, or a relative within two degrees of relationship.
