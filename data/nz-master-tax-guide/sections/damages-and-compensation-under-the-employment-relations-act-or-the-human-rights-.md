# Damages and compensation under the Employment Relations Act or the Human Rights Act ¶3-468

Payments ordered by a court or authority to be made to a former employee to cover loss of wages or allowances are taxable to the recipient. PAYE must be deducted from such payments (¶3-050).

Compensation payments under the Employment Relations Act

Compensation payments made for humiliation, loss of dignity or injury to feelings are treated differently.

Public binding ruling BR Pub 06/05 confirms that:

## Payments that are genuinely and entirely for compensation for humiliation, loss of dignity or injury to

feelings under s 123(c)(i) of the Employment Relations Act 2000 are not employment income (monetary remuneration). Consequently, such payments do not form part of the income of the employee under s CE 1 or under ordinary concepts of income (s CA 1(2)).

## Specifically excluded from the ruling are payments that, in reality, are for lost wages or other income

but that are merely characterised by the parties as being for humiliation, loss of dignity or injury to feelings. This is irrespective of whether such an agreement is signed by a mediator under the Employment Relations Act.

## There is no liability under subpart RD for employers or former employers to deduct PAYE from these

payments.

The ruling applies for an indefinite period.

The deductibility of damages and compensation payments is discussed at ¶10-212.

*Refs: s 123(c)(i), s CE 1, s CA 1(2)*

Compensation payments under the Human Rights Act 1993 Public binding ruling BR Pub 05/12 concerns payments for damages to a complainant or aggrieved person for humiliation, loss of dignity, and injury to feelings under s 92M(1)(c) of the Human Rights Act 1993 (for breaches of Pt 1A or Pt 2 of that Act awarded by the Human Rights Review Tribunal) or under an out-of- court settlement where the complaint involves an employer–employee relationship.

The ruling provides that:

## payments for damages or out of court settlements that are genuinely and entirely awarded for

humiliation, loss of dignity, and injury to feelings under s 92M(1)(c) of the Human Rights Act are not income under s CE 1 of the IT07 (amounts derived in connection with employment), and

## such payments for damages are not income under ordinary concepts under s CA 1(2).

*Refs: s 92M(1)(c), s CE 1, s CA 1(2)*

Example: An ultimatum to an employee that he work at a lower grading or be dismissed resulted in the Employment Court finding that he had suffered a personal grievance. Tax consequences led to the taxpayer contending before the Taxation Review Authority that part of the payment awarded to him by the Employment Court was not assessable income. The Authority found, however, that the payment was for a combination of loss of wages and benefits relating to the taxpayer's employment and was assessable as employment income (monetary remuneration). See Case U39 (2000) 19 NZTC 9,369. The Authority's decision was affirmed by the High Court.

See Cleland v C of IR (2001) 20 NZTC 17,086.

This ruling applies for an indefinite period.
