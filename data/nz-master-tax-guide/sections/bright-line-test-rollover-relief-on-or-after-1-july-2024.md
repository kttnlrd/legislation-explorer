# Bright-line test — rollover relief on or after 1 July 2024 ¶7-545

## Note: This commentary covers the position relating to the rollover relief under the bright-line test applicable

to residential land transfers occurring on or after 1 July 2024.

In 2024, the bright-line test has been simplified by returning to the 2-year bright-line test for disposal of residential land occurring on or after 1 July 2024. The scope of the rollover relief under the bright-line test has also been simplified and widened for transfers of residential land on or after 1 July 2024.

Where rollover relief applies, the transfer is treated as being at cost to the transferor (or at the amount paid by the transferee, if that is greater). The transferor is treated as having disposed of the residential land for that consideration and the transferee is treated as having acquired the residential land for the same amount.

If a rollover relief provision applies to a transfer of residential land, the transferee's bright-line date is deemed to be the same as that of the transferor (s FD 1(3)). However, a transitional rule applies if the transferor acquired the land before 1 July 2024 and the transferee's bright-line start date is the transferor's bright-line date if the transferor acquired the land on or after 27 March 2021 but before 1 July 2024, or 27 March 2021 if the transferor acquired the land prior to 27 March 2021.

The new rollover relief applies to transfers of residential land on or after 1 July 2024.

*Refs: s FD 1(3)*

## Transfers between associated persons

From 1 July 2024, rollover relief applies to:

## all transfers between persons associated under any of ss YB 2 to YB 13, provided they have been

associated for at least 2 years prior to the transfer (s FD 1(a)), or

## a transfer to a trustee of a newly established trust if all the beneficiaries are (s FD 1(b)):

## persons that have been associated with the transferor for at least 2 years (other than infants less than

2 years old and persons that are associated due to a recent marriage, civil union, defacto relationship or adoption), or

## charities, meaning organisations including trusts not carried on for the private profit and whose funds

are applied principally for civic, philanthropic, charitable, religious, community or like purposes in New Zealand or elsewhere.

If rollover relief applies, the transferor is treated as transferring the residential land at cost meaning the transferee takes on the transferor's bright-line acquisition date and cost base (ss FD 1(2) and FD 1(3)).

For the purposes of the main home exemption under s CB 16A, the transferor's use of the residential land is attributed to the transferee (s FD 1(4)). For example, if the transferor used the land as a main home for one year, this is attributed to the transferee and can be taken into account when the transferee subsequently sells the land.

A discussion on the persons associated under the land provisions can be found at ¶7-433.

## Limitation on rollover relief

There is a limitation on the number of times rollover relief under s FD 1 can be applied to a property, that is, for the relevant residential land, rollover relief can only be claimed under the associated person provision once in any 2-year period (s FD 1(5)).

M#ori family trusts

Section FD 2 provides rollover relief under the bright-line test for transfers of residential land to or from a "M#ori rollover trust" occurring on or after 1 July 2024.

A "M#ori rollover trust" means at the time of a relevant transfer (s FD 2(5)):

## a trustee of the trust is a M#ori authority or eligible to elect to be a M#ori authority

## all beneficiaries of the trust are members of the same iwi or hap#, or descendants of the same t#puna

(living or dead), and

## the land in question is subject to Te Ture Whenua Maori Act 1993.

Where rollover relief applies, the transferor is treated as transferring the residential land at the greater of cost or the actual amount paid by the transferee, and the transferee takes on the transferor's bright-line acquisition date (ss FD 2(3) and FD 2(4)).

The table under s FD 2(1) summarises when rollover relief applies for transfers involving M#ori rollover trusts. Further, s FD 2(2) provides that the transferors and transferees may have different capacities.

## Transfers involving a Treaty of Waitangi claim

Settlements under the Treaty of Waitangi can be a multi-staged process. The Crown transfers the land to a single governance entity that may act on behalf of several different claimant groups (usually different hap# or a collective of iwi groups). The governance entity then transfers the settlement assets, including residential land, to different members of the claimant group in accordance with the deed of settlement or the settlement legislation.

Section FD 3 applies to provide rollover relief for transfers of residential land when:

## the land is subject to Te Ture Whenua Maori Act 1993

## the land is subject to the Treaty of Waitangi, and

## is transferred to a trustee of a trust who:

## is a M#ori authority (or eligible to be a M#ori authority), and

## on behalf of the claimants, receives and manages assets that are transferred from the Crown as part

of the settlement of a claim under the Treaty of Waitangi, in accordance with the requirements of s HF 2(3)(e)(i).

The rollover relief applies to the transfer of the residential land to a member of the claimant group. The recipient trustee has the transferor's acquisition date. The transferor is treated as transferring the land at the greater of the cost or the actual consideration derived (s FD 3(2)). The transfer may be treated by the transferee as an acquisition at market value for bright-line test purposes (s FD 3(3)).

## Subsequent disposal of a Treaty of Waitangi settlement

A person who subsequently disposes of residential land the person acquired in settlement of a Treaty of Waitangi claim may have income under the bright-line test. To calculate the amount of the income, the cost of the residential land is taken to be market value of the land at the time it was transferred from the Crown to the person.

## Cost of land subject to rollover relief

Full rollover relief is only available for the transactions discussed above when the transfer is made for an amount of consideration that is less than or equal to the total cost of residential land to the transferor. When this is the case, rollover relief will deem the transfer to be at cost.

Partial rollover relief applies if the transfer is for an amount that is more than the transferor's cost but less than market value of the land at date of transfer. Partial relief deems the cost to be the amount of consideration paid, rather than deeming the transfer to take place at cost or market value. See ss FC 9B and FC 9C.

Irrespective of whether the transaction qualifies for full or partial rollover relief, the recipient is treated as having the same bright-line acquisition date as the transferor. The effect is that the bright-line start date is not reset by the transfer.

Where partial relief applies and where more than cost is paid for the transfer, the excess gain is taxable income under the bright-line test.

## Companies within a consolidated group

Transfers of residential land between companies within a wholly-owned group of companies consolidated for tax purposes also qualify for rollover relief. The commencement of the bright-line period is not reset when residential land is transferred between consolidated companies (s FM 15(2B)). Rollover relief applies irrespective of consideration for the transfer.

## Amalgamations

Under the bright-line test there is rollover relief for amalgamations (s FO 10(6)). This rollover relief extends the existing rollover relief for amalgamations to property that is revenue account property of an amalgamating company due to the application of the bright-line test (¶20-108).

## Changes in co-ownership

Remedial amendments ensured that transfers of residential land that effect changes in co-ownership do not reset the bright-line commencement date. The date of acquisition will remain the same as long as there is no change to the person's proportional, or notional proportional, ownership. The remedial amendments apply to all residential land transactions, not just those that qualify for rollover relief.

It is possible for a person to hold land jointly in several different ways, for example under joint tenancy, as tenants in common, in partnership with other persons or in capacity as a trustee. A person can transfer land to themselves by changing the form of co-ownership, or transferring the land from co-ownership to sole ownership, and vice versa. For a discussion on the effect of changes to co-ownership under the land disposal rules, see ¶7-300.

Where the transfer results in a change in the co-ownership proportion, the bright-line test acquisition date reset only applies to the ownership share that has changed hands, for example where the co-ownership changed from 50:50 to 75:25, the bright-line test reset would apply to the newly acquired 25% share transferred (s CB 6A(3)).

## Questions we've been asked issued

QWBA QB 25/15 "How do the bright-line rollover relief provisions apply to transfers of residential land between associated persons?" covers the rules relating to transfers of land after 1 July 2024 and sets out the Commissioner's view of how the new rules apply. Two categories of transfers are discussed:

## transfers of residential land between persons who have been associated for at least 2 years before

the transfer date and at the date of transfer (s FD 1(1)(a))

## transfers of residential land to a trustee of which all beneficiaries are either:

## associated at date of transfer (and have been associated in the 2 years prior to the transfer date), or

## a charitable organisation.

If the above criteria are met, rollover relief applies for the purpose of the bright-line test and:

## the transfer is treated as a disposal and acquisition for an amount equal to the transferor's cost

## the transferee's bright-line start date for the land is the transferor's bright-line start date, and

## the transferor's use of the residential land is attributed to the transferee in determining whether the

main home exclusion applies.
