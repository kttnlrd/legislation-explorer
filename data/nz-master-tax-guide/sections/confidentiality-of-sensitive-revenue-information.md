# Confidentiality of sensitive revenue information ¶1-604

## Introduction

The law authorising the Commissioner to collect, inspect and use information relating to the affairs of taxpayers is contained in subpart 3A and Sch 7 of the TAA. Broadly, the structure of subpart 3A is:

## purposes, principles and key terms of the subpart: ss 16–16C (¶1-590)

## collection and use of information by Commissioner: ss 17–17M (¶1-590)

## confidentiality of sensitive revenue information: ss 18 and 18B

## permitted disclosures of sensitive revenue information: ss 18C–18J.

The Commissioner's power to collect and use information relating to taxpayers is discussed at ¶1-590.

The main confidentiality rules binding IR officers now appear in ss 18–18J, with more detailed provisions (concerning exceptions to secrecy) located in Sch 7.

The redrafted confidentiality rules are discussed in the commentary that follows. The relevant provisions are:

## s 18D (disclosure carrying into effect a revenue law) — ¶1-606

## s 18E (information-sharing arrangements) — ¶1-609

## s 18F (regulations for sharing information for public services purpose) — ¶1-615

## s 18G (disclosure to a person or their representative) — ¶1-618

## s 18H (disclosure to other agencies) — ¶1-622–¶1-634

## s 18I (disclosure for international purposes) — ¶1-636

## s 18J (disclosure for risk of harm purposes) — ¶1-638

## s 18K (disclosure when funds of gift-exempt body are misapplied) — ¶1-640.

For a discussion of the provisions applying before 18 March 2019, see CCH, 2019 New Zealand Master Tax Guide.

## Confidentiality rule

Information is critical to IR's ability to deliver services. Much of that information is provided by taxpayers.

This may be information about themselves (eg in an individual or business income tax return) or about other taxpayers (eg employee income information). For taxpayers to be comfortable providing their information, they need to feel the information requested is reasonable and is treated appropriately by IR. This assurance is given by the confidentiality rule, often referred to as the "tax secrecy" rule, set out in s 18 of the TAA (previously in former s 81).

As stated above, a key policy change with the redrafted provisions is that s 18 has narrowed the confidentiality rule from its previous coverage of all matters relating to the IR Acts, to more clearly target information about taxpayers by referring to "sensitive revenue information" (see below).

A revenue officer must keep all sensitive revenue information confidential and must not disclose it unless the disclosure is a permitted disclosure under ss 18D–18J: s 18(1). Permitted disclosures are exceptions to the confidentiality rule in s 18: s 18C.

However, note that despite the permitted disclosures, the Commissioner is not required to disclose any item of revenue information if its release would adversely affect the integrity of the tax system or would prejudice the maintenance of the law: s 18(3).

A person, other than a revenue officer, must keep sensitive revenue information confidential unless the disclosure is:

## a permitted disclosure

## # authorised by the Commissioner for a purpose, or

## permitted under the agreement by which the information is accessed or obtained: s 18(2).

A number of offences apply to those revenue officers or other persons breaching the confidentiality rule (ss 143C–143EB and ¶14-300).

## Key terms

"Revenue information" is defined as information that is acquired, obtained, accessed, received by, disclosed to, or held by the Commissioner under or for the purposes of a revenue law or under an information-sharing agreement: s 16C(2). "Sensitive revenue information" is defined in s 16C(3) as meaning:

## revenue information that relates to the affairs of a person or entity—

## that identifies, or is reasonably capable of being used to identify, the person or entity, whether directly

or indirectly

## that might reasonably be regarded as private, commercially sensitive, or otherwise confidential, or

## the release of which could result in loss, harm or prejudice to a person to whom or to which it relates

## but does not include aggregate or statistical data that may contain information about the person or

entity to the extent to which the information does not meet the requirements of the above.

Amendments made in March 2022 narrowed the interpretation of the definitions of "revenue information" and "sensitive revenue information" as the previously broad definition could have conceptually meant all information held by the Commissioner (including non-tax-related information) needed to be treated as sensitive revenue information. This would have negatively affected the Commissioner's ability to release certain information to other government agencies. The amendment is back dated to align with the re-write of the subpart and is effective from 18 March 2019.

A "revenue officer" is a person employed in or seconded to IR and any person formerly so employed or seconded. The definition also includes a person employed by the government of an overseas country or territory and who is seconded to IR.

## Confidentiality declaration

Revenue officers must complete a declaration of confidentiality before they begin their official duties. The declaration may be made before the Commissioner, another revenue officer or a person authorised to take statutory declarations.

Similarly, other persons who acquire, obtain or have access to sensitive revenue information must complete a certificate of confidentiality prescribed by the Commissioner. The certificate must be kept by the person, entity or agency that employs or engages the services of the person holding the information in a form acceptable to the Commissioner.

Revenue officers and other persons bound by undertakings given under earlier confidentiality provisions corresponding to s 18B are treated as having made a declaration, or given a certificate, under that section.
