# Sports clubs ¶24-130

Amounts derived by any club, society, association or trustee or trustees of a trust (the promoter) established mainly to promote any amateur game or sport, for the recreation or entertainment of the general public, are treated as exempt income. The requirements that must be satisfied before the exemption is available are that the promoter must be established mainly for the purpose of promoting any amateur game or sport and that the game or sport must be conducted for the recreation or entertainment of the general public and no part of the income or funds of the promoter may be available to be used for the private pecuniary profit of any proprietor, member, shareholder or beneficiary. In reviewing what the main purpose of a sports club might be, the Commissioner would look to all the surrounding circumstances, including the promoter's constitution or trust deed and activities carried on for a particular tax year.

## Qualifying pastimes

Virtually any pastime will qualify providing all the conditions listed above are met. District and national wrestling/boxing associations, clubs and associations connected with amateur cricket, tennis, golf, rugby, rugby league and association football would all qualify, as would dance, cultural, car, chess, gun, art and craft and music clubs. A club that also has professional activities could still qualify so long as IR is satisfied the club is promoting amateur games or sport and the club's share of any professional event is used to promote the amateur games or sport.

## Fringe benefit tax

Even though a sports club enjoys a concession for income tax purposes, it is still required to file FBT returns and account for FBT if it provides benefits for employees. This would be the case, for instance, if an employee of a sports club used the club's motor vehicle for private purposes (¶21-010).
