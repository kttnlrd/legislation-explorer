# Transfer scheme withholding tax ¶29-460

A person may derive income when funds are transferred from a foreign superannuation scheme to a New Zealand superannuation scheme (¶5-317). The objective of the transfer scheme withholding tax (TSWT) is to allow a person who is transferring their UK pension fund to a New Zealand QROPS (or non-UK pension fund to a participating KiwiSaver scheme) to elect to have the New Zealand scheme (the "transfer scheme") pay the New Zealand tax due on transfer on the person's behalf.

The transfer scheme must either be a QROPS or a KiwiSaver scheme that elects to pay TSWT under subpart RI.

A QROPS is a superannuation scheme in New Zealand that meets the definition of a qualifying recognised overseas pension scheme under the Finance Act 2004 (UK).

TSWT commenced on 1 April 2026. The tax is paid out of the transferred funds (the "scheme pays" method) at a flat rate of 28%. It is a final tax that applies when:

## a person derives income in the form of a foreign superannuation withdrawal that is income under s

CF 3(2)(b) (¶5-317)

## the income is from an interest in a foreign superannuation scheme that is withdrawn and reinvested in

a superannuation scheme in New Zealand

## the scheme in New Zealand is a transfer scheme, and

## all or part of the foreign superannuation withdrawal is an assessable withdrawal amount for the

person.

The person deriving the foreign superannuation withdrawal has 10 working days in which to notify the transfer scheme that TSWT is to be withheld from the assessable withdrawal amount. An election so made cannot be revoked. The assessable withdrawal amount is the foreign superannuation withdrawal a person derives that is not treated as exempt income under s CF 3.

The transfer scheme must withhold TSWT from the assessable withdrawal amount at the rate of 28% and pay that amount to the Commissioner on a monthly basis. The due date for payment is the 20th day of the month after the month in which TSWT is withheld.

The Commissioner must refund any TSWT that a transfer scheme overpays. The 4-year time bar in s 108 of the TAA can apply when a transfer scheme has filed a return for TSWT (s 108(1C) of the TAA).

It is an offence for a person to knowingly apply TSWT for a purpose other than payment to the Commissioner. No offence is committed if the deduction or withholding is accounted for and the failure to account for it within the prescribed time was caused by certain factors outside the person's control. TSWT is a deduction or withholding of tax for this purpose.

When a person has elected not to have a transfer scheme withhold TSWT from an assessable withdrawal amount and will pay tax on their own behalf, that amount is reportable income. The Commissioner will take the amount into account when calculating the person's taxable income.

See Tax Information Bulletin ¶375-102 Vol 37, No 5, June 2025 at p 40 for further discussion of the TSWT.

*Refs: s CF 3(2)(b), s CF 3, s 108, s 108(1C)*

## Transfer scheme reporting requirements

A transfer scheme paying a foreign superannuation withdrawal must provide investment income information to the Commissioner. The information is to be sent in electronic form by the 20th day of the month following a transfer of funds. Liability for giving the correct assessable withdrawal amount and personal information to the scheme rests with the person for whom the funds are transferred.

When a transfer scheme provides the Commissioner with information showing TSWT has been withheld from a person's assessable withdrawal amount, a tax credit arises for the tax year equal to the TSWT. The credit can be used only to satisfy the person's income tax liability on the assessable withdrawal amount.
