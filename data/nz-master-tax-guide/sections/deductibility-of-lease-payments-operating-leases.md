# Deductibility of lease payments — operating leases ¶10-675

Leases entered into on or after 20 May 1999 that are not finance leases are known as operating leases.

## Deduction to lessee under operating lease

The lessee under a lease for personal property entered into on or after 20 May 1999 that is not a finance lease, a specified lease or an operating lease to which s EJ 10B applies is entitled to a deduction for lease payments made under the lease. The allowable deduction is calculated in accordance with s EJ 10.

Example: On 1 October 2012, TP Ltd entered into a 5-year lease agreement for an item of plant. The lease provided for an immediate deposit of $10,000 and thereafter monthly payments of $420.36, so that total rent payments under the lease were $35,222. The deduction available for the lease payments during the term of the lease would be calculated applying the following formula: (part of lease term within income year ÷ lease term) × sum of lease payment

## IFRS leases

For income years starting on or after 1 January 2019, the previous accounting standard for leases, New Zealand Equivalent to International Accounting Standard 17 Leases (NZ IAS 17) has been replaced with New Zealand Equivalent to International Financial Reporting Standard 16 Leases (NZ IFRS 16). As a result, in March 2021 retrospective changes were made to the IT07 to allow taxpayers who apply NZ IFRS 16 to more closely follow their accounting treatment of leases for tax purposes.

Under NZ IAS 17, there was a difference in the accounting treatment for operating and finance leases for both lessors and lessees. For lessees, NZ IFRS 16 removes the distinction between operating and finance leases for accounting purposes. Under NZ IFRS 16, lessees are required to include all leases on their balance sheet by recognising both an asset, being the right to use the leased asset for the lease term, and a lease liability, representing the obligation to pay rentals. NZ IFRS 16 also changes the timing of accounting for expenditure for lessees compared to the previous treatment, but total deductions are unchanged over the life of the lease.

The changes made to the IT07 allow lessees who use NZ IFRS 16 to follow their accounting treatment for tax. Taxpayers can choose to apply the new rules on a lease-by-lease basis. However, once a taxpayer chooses to follow the NZ IFRS 16 tax approach for a particular lease, they must continue to do so for the life of the lease.

Income and expenditure arising from an IFRS lease is calculated under s EJ 10B. Expenditure is deductible under s DB 51C and income is assessable under s CC 14. Most amounts calculated under s EJ 10B are expected to be expenditure, but income can arise in some circumstances, such as when an impairment adjustment from a previous year is reversed through profit and loss for accounting purposes. Note that a de minimis provision applies in certain circumstances.

Section EJ 10B applies to a person's operating lease of a personal property lease asset (the IFRS lease), if:

## the person, as lessee, uses NZ IFRS 16 in their financial statements for the IFRS lease

## the person irrevocably chooses to use the section for the IFRS lease

## the lessor and the person are not associated, and

## the asset is not subleased to another person.

## Wash-up calculation when IFRS lease ends

When an IFRS lease ends, either because the lease itself ends or because it no longer qualifies to be an IFRS lease, ss EJ 10B(6) and (7) require a wash-up to ensure that total deductions over the term of the lease match those that would have been deductible for a taxpayer not applying NZ IFRS 16 for tax. The formula for the wash-up is set out in s EJ 10B(6) as follows: IFRS deductions – IFRS income – expenditure where:

IFRS deductions is the total amount deducted for the IFRS lease for all income years, including when the person has not applied s EJ 10B IFRS income is the total amount of income for the IFRS lease for all income years, including when the person has not applied s EJ 10B expenditure is the amount of expenditure for the IFRS lease for all income years, ignoring s EJ 10B.

The person, as lessee, has a deduction for a positive amount under the formula or income for a negative amount. The deduction or income is for the income year in which the IFRS lease ends or fails to qualify as an IFRS lease.

## Transitional adjustment

Sections EJ 10B(8) and (9) include transitional adjustments for certain leases when NZ IFRS 16 applies for tax after the original start of the lease. This can arise in the year a taxpayer first chooses to retrospectively apply NZ IFRS 16 for tax, or in a later year for an individual lease that did not meet the IFRS lease requirements but subsequently does. The person's deduction (for a positive amount) or income (for a negative amount) calculated under the following formula is spread in equal proportions over the income year and the following 4 income years: retrospective accounting expenditure – retrospective tax adjustments – previous tax deductions where: retrospective accounting expenditure is the total amount of expenditure or loss recognised under NZ IFRS 16 for the IFRS lease, if the amount is in accordance with NZ IFRS 16 retrospective tax adjustments is the total amount of the add-back adjustment, impairment and revaluation adjustment, make-good and direct costs adjustment, and deductions for make-good costs and direct costs (as discussed above), treating the adjustments and deductions as if they applied for the income years previous tax deductions is the total amount of deductions not in accordance with NZ IFRS 16 and not provided by s EJ 10B, for the income years that the person has applied NZ IFRS 16 retrospectively for the IFRS lease or has not applied this section for the IFRS lease while they have applied NZ IFRS 16 for it.

## De minimis

To reduce compliance costs, the adjustments calculation in s EJ 10B(3) is not required for certain shorter- term and lower-value leases.

The de minimis rule applies if the initial right of use asset under NZ IFRS 16 is $100,000 or less and the remaining term of the IFRS lease under NZ IFRS 16 is 4 years or less initially and immediately after any extension starts. In that case, as lessee, the person is allowed a deduction (for a positive amount) or has income (for a negative amount) for the total amount recognised by the person through their profit and loss account for the IFRS lease for the income year. This is provided the amount is in accordance with NZ IFRS 16.
