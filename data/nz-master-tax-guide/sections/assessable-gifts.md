# Assessable gifts ¶5-430

## Interpretation statement on gifts

As a general rule, gifts are non-taxable receipts (¶5-026). There are circumstances where this general rule may not apply. Interpretation statement, IS 23/11, "Income tax: Income – when gifts are assessable income" and accompanying fact sheet IS 23/11 FS provide guidance on the circumstances in which gifts are assessable income.

While a gift is not usually subject to income tax, there are circumstances in which a gift is taxable income to the recipient. Specific examples of when a gift may be subject to income tax include those received:

## from a business (s CB 1)

## as a result of carrying on or carrying out a profit-making activity (s CB 3)

## in connection with employment (s CE 1), and

## when undertaking a voluntary activity (s CO 1).

A gift may also be income under ordinary concepts and therefore subject to income tax under s CA 1(2). The statement applies both to monetary gifts and to non-monetary gifts that are convertible to money.

The statement does not apply to gifts that may be subject to FBT (¶21-425) or gifts that are not subject to income tax but may be family scheme income for the purposes of Working for Families tax credits (¶12-125).

## Business or profit-making activity

A gift that a person makes as a mark of affection, esteem or respect for an individual (even if actually received by a non-individual) is a "mere gift" and is not business income or income from a profit-making activity. Gifts are business income or income from a profit-making activity where the payment can be attributed to the activities or specific work the recipient has carried out.

A gift that is unexpected or unsolicited supports a conclusion that a gift is not business income.

## Employment income

Case law indicates that the following features may indicate a gift is employment income:

## The amount of the payment reflects the extent of any services provided.

## The payment is made in the hope of future services or to encourage further efforts by employees.

## The recipient's employment agreement contemplates the payment.

## The payment is, or may be in the future, recurring rather than a one-off.

## The payment is related to duties that are expected of the employee (although if an employee willingly

does something beyond their usual duties and is paid for that, the range of duties is enlarged to encompass it).

## The payments are commonplace as a matter of practice in the occupation, profession or industry.

## The gift is one commonly provided to the holder of a position or office that has a degree of

continuance and independence from the current incumbent.

For commentary on gifts, tips and gratuities made to employees, see ¶3-450.

## Voluntary activities

A gift is treated as income under s CO 1 when the gift and the volunteer's activity have a sufficient connection. That is when the gift is "a product of" or "in connection with" the person's voluntary activity.

Similar considerations as those discussed under "employment income" apply to voluntary activities.

## Income under ordinary concepts

A gift that would be assessable income under ordinary concepts is subject to income tax.
