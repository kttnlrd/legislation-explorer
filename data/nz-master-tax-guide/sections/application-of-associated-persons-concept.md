# Application of associated persons concept ¶16-225

The associated persons concept applies in a wide variety of contexts. The concept may have application to assist in the creation of a tax liability. Alternatively, the presence of association may give rise to the imposition of an arm's length dealing for tax purposes, on the assumption that association prevented the parties reaching that standard. The areas of application include the following:

## the land disposal provisions (¶7-430)

## to limit a deduction for employee misappropriation when the employee and the employer are

associated (¶10-935)

## to assess as employment income to an employee the value of the benefit arising from an employee

share scheme when the benefit is received by an associated person of the employee

## to apply anti-avoidance adjustments in respect of financial arrangements when persons are

associated persons (¶6-550)

## to apply fringe benefit tax (FBT) when a benefit is provided to an associated person of an employee

(¶21-080)

## to deny bad debt deductions under a financial arrangement when the parties are associated

(¶10-152)

## to restrict the deduction allowed to a surety when the loss has been caused by an associate

(¶10-452)

## to require a base price adjustment (BPA) upon transfer of a debt to an associate under the debt

parking rules (¶6-410)

## to restrict the purchaser's cost for depreciation purposes when depreciable property is purchased

from an associate (¶13-400)

## to exclude the business income exemption for a charity when a benefit or advantage can be provided

to associated persons (¶24-210)

## to make a realised capital gain a taxable distribution in transactions between associated persons

concerning a foreign trust (¶25-530)

## to limit the scope of a capital gain amount arising when a company disposes of property under an

arrangement with an associated person (¶16-720)

## to support application of the international tax rules (Chapter 26), and

## to deem shares to have been acquired by a company so as to result in an on-market acquisition

where there is an arrangement between the company and the associate for the associate to make the acquisition in lieu of the company (¶16-710).
