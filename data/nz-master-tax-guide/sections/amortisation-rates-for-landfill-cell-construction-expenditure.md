# Amortisation rates for landfill cell construction expenditure ¶50-169

Persons who meet the criteria under s DB 46 of the Income Tax Act 2007 and have incurred landfill cell construction expenditure in an income year beginning on or after 10 June 2005 may claim a deduction for that expenditure of an amount equal to 63.5% (straight-line equivalent) or 63.5% (diminishing value) of the expenditure.

The types of landfill cell construction expenditure to which these rates apply are:

## excavation

## cell lining, and

## leachate drainage.

Excavation means costs relating to:

## earthworks design

## preparatory works (such as clearing the cell site)

## earthworks, including excavation, borrowing and filling

## erosion control measures and remediation of borrowed areas associated with cell construction

## contractors' overheads related to cell construction, and

## cell-specific resource consents other than those which qualify as fixed life intangible property.

Cell lining means costs relating to:

## the design, construction and quality assurance of cell liners (clay, geosynthetic, flexible membrane,

concrete or bitumen), and

## protection and separation layers, including reworking and sub-excavation to support subsequent

compaction or to provide slope stability.

Leachate drainage means the provision of drainage material to assist in drainage of leachate from the base and sidewalls of a landfill cell. This excludes drainage pipes and systems designed for the passage and extraction of leachate from the landfill.

For further explanation of the rates, see the commentary to Determination DET 05/02 in Tax Information Bulletin ¶1710-107 Vol 17, No 10, December 2005 at 51–53.

*Refs: s DB 46, Income Tax Act 2007*

## PENDING LEGISLATION

Taxation (Annual Rates for 2025–26, Compliance Simplification, and Remedial Measures) Bill The Minister of Revenue introduced a new taxation Bill into the House on 26 August 2025. Taxation (Annual Rates for 2025–26, Compliance Simplification, and Remedial Measures) Bill contains a range of proposed measures, remedials and maintenance measures to boost and grow New Zealand's economy. The Bill introduces amendments to the following Acts:

## Income Tax Act 2007 (IT07)

## Tax Administration Act 1994 (TAA)

## Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 (GST Act)

## KiwiSaver Act 2006

## Unclaimed Money Act 1971

## Student Loan Scheme Act 2011

## Child Support Act 1991

## Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009

## Taxation (Annual Rates for 2024–25, Emergency Response, and Remedial Measures) Act 2025

## Taxation (Budget Measures) Act 2025, and

## Tax Administration (Financial Statements—Domestic Trusts) Order 2022

The following is a brief summary of the specific policy measures contained in the Bill:

## Confirmation of annual rates of income tax for the 2025–26 income year: The Bill proposes that the

annual rates of income tax for the 2025–26 income year be set at the rates currently specified in Sch 1 pt A of the IT07 — see CCH, New Zealand Tax Rates, Dates and Depreciation Rates 2026, ¶50-040.

## Introducing a new foreign investment fund (FIF) calculation method: The Bill proposes to introduce

a new calculation method, the revenue account method (RAM), which would tax eligible FIF interests on a realisation basis, ie dividends derived and 70% of gains or losses. The proposal would allow a taxpayer to apply the RAM to qualifying foreign shares when the taxpayer became a New Zealand resident and was not a transitional resident on or after 1 April 2024 and was a New Zealand non- resident for at least 5 years before becoming resident. The new RAM method is targeted towards migrants and returning New Zealanders and addresses the double taxation issue for taxpayers who are subject to tax in another country on the disposal of their foreign shares that are FIF interests.

## Certain visitors to be treated as non-resident: The Bill proposes to allow certain visitors to New

Zealand ("non-resident visitors") to be present in New Zealand for 275 days in a given 18-month period without becoming a New Zealand tax resident. The proposed changes are aimed at visitors who engage in remote work while present in New Zealand and would otherwise be subject to New Zealand tax obligations.

## Amendments to the employee shares scheme (ESS): The Bill proposes to introduce changes that

allow unlisted companies to elect into a regime where the tax liability for employees who receive shares or share options as part of an ESS can, generally, be deferred until a liquidity event, such as the sale of shares. The Bill also proposes changes to clarify that the share scheme taxing date can arise before shares are held by or for the benefit of an ESS beneficiary and to clarify the timing of employers' deductions.

## GST and unincorporated joint ventures: The Bill proposes to allow the members of a joint venture

to choose to individually account for GST on supplies made or received in the course of the venture under their own GST registrations rather than registering the joint venture separately, consistent with common practices in some industries. This would resolve issues with some joint ventures being unable to register and minimise compliance costs associated with certain joint venture activities.

## Income from residential supply of excess electricity: The Bill proposes to introduce an income tax

exemption for income derived by an individual from the sale of excess electricity from a residential property to the network. Under the exemption, individuals would not need to pay tax on, or file a tax return for, income derived from the sale of excess electricity. However, they would no longer be entitled to deductions relating to this activity.

## Simplification of the fringe benefit tax (FBT) rules: The Bill proposes amendments which will allow

employers the option to pool insurance benefits provided to employees under a global insurance policy.

The Bill proposes a retrospective amendment which will change the threshold for all-inclusive pay from $129,681 to $130,724. A proposed amendment in the Bill will clarify that the cost or adjusted tax value of a vehicle will not be impacted by the amount of any Investment Boost claimed in respect of the vehicle. The Bill contains a proposed amendment that will allow employers to treat "gift cards" provided to employees as fringe benefits unless the employer chooses to treat the gift card as subject to pay as you earn (PAYE). The Bill also proposes to exempt certain fringe benefits received by non-resident visitors who meet certain conditions from FBT.

## Repeal of legislative provisions for trust disclosures: The Bill proposes to repeal the specific

legislative provisions for trust disclosures in ss 59BA and 59BAB of the TAA to improve legislative clarity. Amendments are also proposed to be made to the Order setting minimum requirements for preparing financial statements. This would ensure that the Order continues to apply to trustees currently filing returns under the specific disclosure provisions if those provisions are repealed.

## Powers for the Commissioner of Inland Revenue (Commissioner) to set certain rates: The Bill

proposes to streamline the process of setting use of money interest rates, the FBT prescribed rate, and deemed rate of return for FIFs by statutorily vesting these rate-setting powers in the Commissioner as delegated secondary legislation. The determinations made must be published and presented to Parliament and are subject to review.

## Updated information sharing for proceeds of crime: The Bill proposes allowing the Commissioner to

disclose updated information to an authorised person of the New Zealand Police, after a prima facie case has been established, to maintain the accuracy of the Inland Revenue (IR) information already held by the Police (that was obtained under existing information disclosure rules).

## Information sharing by way of Ministerial agreement: The Bill introduces a new provision to

enable the Commissioner to disclose information to another government agency under a Ministerial agreement. These agreements give the Minister of Revenue and the Minister in charge of the other agency the power to agree to the disclosure of information to determine entitlement to, or eligibility for, government assistance, for the detection, investigation, prosecution, or punishment of suspected or actual crimes punishable by terms of imprisonment of 2 years or more or to remove the financial benefit of crime.

The Bill also contains a significant number of amendments of a remedial and technical nature to GST, KiwiSaver scheme settings, Investment Boost scheme and non-resident contractors' tax rules.
