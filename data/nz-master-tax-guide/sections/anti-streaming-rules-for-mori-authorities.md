# Anti-streaming rules for M#ori authorities ¶24-317

To prevent M#ori authorities streaming non-taxable distributions (ie distributions other than taxable M#ori authority distributions) to members best able to use them, s HF 8 provides that where a distribution consists of a taxable M#ori authority distribution and non-taxable amounts, an equal portion of the taxable and non- taxable portions must be allocated among all members to whom the distribution is made.

Section OK 20 requires every taxable M#ori authority distribution made by a M#ori authority in a tax year to be attributed with M#ori authority credits at the same level. The first distribution made by a M#ori authority in a tax year is referred to as the "benchmark distribution". The maximum ratio at which credits can be allocated to distributions is 17.5:82.5. A benchmark distribution with a M#ori authority credit ratio exceeding the maximum is treated as having a base ratio equal to 17.5:82.5. The M#ori authority credit ratio is the amount of M#ori authority credits attached to a distribution divided by the amount of the distribution. All subsequent distributions paid during the year must carry credits at the same ratio as the benchmark distribution. Credits may be attached at a different ratio if a ratio change declaration is made and the M#ori authority declares that the subsequent distribution is not being made as part of an arrangement to obtain a tax advantage.

In the absence of a ratio change declaration, a distribution with a M#ori authority credit ratio that differs from that of the benchmark distribution will give rise to a debit in the M#ori authority credit account (MACA) (¶24-327).

Section GB 42 provides an anti-avoidance rule to deal with the streaming of M#ori authority credits forming part of an arrangement to obtain a tax advantage. Here, the term "tax advantage" includes the arising of a credit to a MACA. This rule is similar to the anti-avoidance rule that applies to imputation credit streaming (¶17-120).

The rules governing the allocation of imputation credits to beneficiaries of trusts are extended to M#ori authority credits to prevent trustees of trusts that are M#ori authorities from allocating M#ori authority credits to beneficiaries best able to use them. Generally, these rules require that the proportion of credits allocated to a beneficiary reflects the proportion of total distributions received by the beneficiary (¶25-260).
