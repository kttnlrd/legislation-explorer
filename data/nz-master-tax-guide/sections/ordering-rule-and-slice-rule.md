# Ordering rule and slice rule ¶16-770

The ordering rule and the slice rule apply to ascertain the amount of ASC that may be attributed to a share.

The attribution is relevant to the amount of a return of ASC that is not a dividend. The ordering rule and the slice rule are applicable to unlisted unit trusts and group investment funds. The ordering rule is also applied to the ASC reduction for treasury stock and to calculate the debit to a company's ICA that may arise following an on-market share repurchase and cancellation.

## Ordering rule

The ordering rule for the calculation of the ASC per share is applied to all cancelled shares of the same class as the share cancelled. The ordering rule is incorporated in the following formula: available subscribed capital of class ÷ shares being cancelled of class where: available subscribed capital of class is the ASC of all shares of the same class at the time of calculation, and shares being cancelled of class is the number of shares of the class being cancelled.

The ordering rule effectively allocates subscribed capital on a "first come, first served" basis. The ASC of the company is treated as having been distributed to those eligible to receive it, rather than to those shareholders who may have contributed it. In addition to applying to a conventional company, the ordering rule applies to listed trusts and group investment funds.

Example: VM Ltd issued a total of 20 million shares in consideration of the receipt of $1 per share. Following the sale of the company's principal investment for a profit of $8 million, VM Ltd proposes a pro rata share cancellation. VM Ltd proposes to cancel 5 million shares upon payment of $1.60 per share cancelled. The ordering rule would be applied as: 20,000,000 ÷ 5,000,000 = $4.00 per share.

If the shareholder cannot obtain sufficient information to make the calculation, the ASC per share is zero.

This applies when the company in question is not resident in New Zealand.

## Slice rule

The slice rule for the calculation of the ASC per share is applied to shares of the same class on issue at the time of calculation. The formula for the calculation is as follows: available subscribed capital of class ÷ shares of class where: available subscribed capital of class is the ASC of all shares of the same class at the time of calculation, and shares of class is the number of shares of the class on issue at that time.

The effect of the formula is to allocate ASC evenly over shares of the class.

Example: WP Ltd had issued a total of 5 million shares for a consideration of $1 per share. Successful trading has resulted in the addition to equity of $2.5 million in retained earnings. WP Ltd contemplates a return of capital. The ASC of WP Ltd calculated under the slice rule would be: 5,000,000 ÷ 5,000,000 = $1.00 per share.

The slice rule comes to have application to an unlisted trust or unlisted group investment fund that issues units on the basis that the slice rule applies on their redemption (or, as an alternative formulation, that the ordering rule does not apply). When units in the trust are redeemed, there is no dividend to the extent of their redemption proceeds that equal the ASC per share as calculated under the formula.
