# foreign trust disclosure rules ¶14-056

Under the penalties regime, failure to register or provide information amounts to an absolute liability offence, which is criminal in nature. Until 1 April 2023, the Commissioner had no discretion to treat non-compliance with the foreign trust disclosure rules in any other way. In practice, the primary consequence for a foreign trust that does not comply with the disclosure requirements in an income year is that it cannot use the foreign-sourced income exemption that income year. This resulted in inequitable outcomes in many cases – either the penalty was too harsh (if the non-compliance was relatively minor) or too lenient (if the trust had no foreign-sourced income). Consequently, from 1 April 2023, s 139AC is introduced into the penalty rules.

The provision allows the Commissioner greater discretion to allow a trust to use the foreign-sourced income exemption in cases of minor non-compliance. A trustee is not liable to pay the penalty if the Commissioner is satisfied that the trustee makes reasonable efforts to comply with the requirements and to remedy the non- compliance.

The penalty is no more than $1,000.
