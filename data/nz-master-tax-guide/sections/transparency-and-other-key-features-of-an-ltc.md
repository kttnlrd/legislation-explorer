# Transparency and other key features of an LTC ¶19-160

The key features of a LTC are as follows:

## An LTC retains all the obligations and benefits, like limited liability, of a corporate entity under

company law. However, for tax purposes, income, expenses, gains, losses and tax credits are passed on to shareholders in proportion to their shareholding.

## The company must have 5 or less look-through counted owners (¶19-170). The ownership interests

of relatives are combined. From 1 April 2017, all shareholders must simply have the same rights, proportionally, to distributions by the LTC (¶19-165). Before 1 April 2017, all shares had to be of the same class and all owners had to have the same rights and obligations.

## Only the following persons can hold shares in an LTC (¶19-170):

## natural persons

## trustees, or

## another LTC.

## All owners must elect for the LTC rules to apply (¶19-175). The LTC then retains its status until an

election is revoked (¶19-180).

## Owners are subject to tax on their share of the LTC's profit at the owner's marginal rate (¶19-200).

## LTC losses flow through to the owners who can apply those losses to their other income (subject to

the loss limitation rule — see ¶19-215).

## Owners who sell their shares in the LTC are treated as disposing of their share of the LTC's property

(¶19-225).

## Owners are subject to income tax on deemed disposal of the LTC's property, unless an exclusion

applies (¶19-225).

Transparency only applies for income tax purposes. The limited liability afforded by the company structure is not affected under corporate law when a company elects to be an LTC. In addition, the LTC is treated as separate from its owners for various withholding regimes and other tax purposes, such as goods and services tax, pay as you earn (PAYE), fringe benefit tax (FBT), employer superannuation contribution tax, retirement scheme contribution tax, resident withholding tax and non-resident withholding tax.

Transparency is achieved by imputing the income and expenditure of the LTC to the holders of the look- through interests in the company in proportion to those interests.

Owners are treated as holding the LTC's property in proportion to their effective look-through interests.

Owners are treated as carrying on the LTC's activities and having the LTC's status, intention and purpose.

All owners are treated as being party to any arrangement to which the LTC is a party. Owners are treated as doing a thing and being entitled to a thing that the LTC does or is entitled to do.

Elections under any Inland Revenue Act concerning the tax treatment of an LTC's income or property, or any valuation or timing methods adopted in relation to an LTC's income or property are initially made or established by the LTC but are then treated as being the election or method of the owners for their effective look-through interests.

Law: IT07 ss HB 1, YA 1 ("look-through company", "look-through counted owner, "look-through interest")
