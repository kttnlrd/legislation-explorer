# Permitted disclosures made in carrying into effect revenue laws ¶1-606

## Carrying into effect a revenue law

Section 18D(1) of the TAA permits the disclosure of "sensitive revenue information" (¶1-604) if the disclosure is made for the purpose of carrying into effect a revenue law as set out in Sch 7 pt A.

The categories included in Sch 7 pt A can be summarised as follows:

## Tax advisors, tax agents and representatives — Sch 7 cl 3

The Commissioner may supply information to an approved advisor group about an action or omission by a person who is, or purports to be, a member of that group and that the Commissioner considers to be a breach of a member's responsibilities under the privilege rules for tax advisers (¶1-600).

The Commissioner may also supply information about a person to an association or group if the person is, or purports to be, a member of the association or group as a person in the business of preparing tax returns for other people and the information is, or would be, relevant to the Commissioner's decision to remove the person from, or refuse to add the person to, the list of tax agents. This applies when the members of the association or group are subject to a professional code of conduct and a disciplinary process to enforce that code. From 23 March 2020, this clause is also extended to representatives (s 124D), or people purporting to be representatives.

Further, operational statement 25/03 "Authority to Act for Tax Agents, Representatives and Nominated Persons: Access to a Client's Inland Revenue Information" prescribes how a tax agent, or a representative, can obtain the authority to act from their clients and the process for a person to get another person to act for them in relation to their tax affairs and/or their social policy entitlements and obligations. Referencing that such people will be collectively referred to as "tax agents", "representatives", "nominated persons" or "agents" as the context may require. OS 25/03 replaces OS 22/03.

## GST registration status — Sch 7 cl 3B

The Commissioner may supply information to an operator of an electronic marketplace in respect of the GST registration status of a person for the purpose of that operator determining whether they are required to make a deduction for a flat-rate credit (under s 20(3)(de)) be passed on to an underlying supplier of listed services in terms of that section.

## Misappropriation of money — Sch 7 cl 4

The Commissioner may communicate such information as:

## is necessary for the purpose of any prosecution under the laws of New Zealand or of any other

country, or

## the Commissioner considers desirable for the purpose of any investigation into any suspected

offence being a prosecution or investigation in relation to any actual or attempted misappropriation by any person in respect of money:

## payable by IR to another person, or

## entrusted to that person for payment to IR.

However, no such communication can be made in respect of any person to whom such money was payable or entrusted unless that person or that person's legal representative consents to the communication. In the case of a company, an authorised officer must consent to the communication.

## Offences under the Crimes Act — Sch 7, cl 5

The Commissioner may divulge or communicate any matter or thing or produce in any court any document the Commissioner considers desirable for the purposes of any investigation into any suspected offence or prosecution under the Crimes Act 1961 in relation to any tax imposed or payable or any refund made or claimed under the GST Act.

## Civil recovery action — Sch 7 cl 6

The Commissioner may communicate to any authorised person (as defined in s 98(1) of the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009) any information required for the purpose of establishing whether a prima facie case exists for taking civil recovery action.

## Offences under revenue laws — Sch 7 cl 7

The Commissioner may:

## divulge or communicate any matter or thing to the Director of the Serious Fraud Office, or

## produce in any court any document

that the Commissioner considers desirable for the purposes of any investigation or prosecution in relation to any suspected offence against the IR Acts.

## Child support: arrival and departure information — Sch 7 cl 8

The Commissioner may communicate to a member, employer or agent of the New Zealand Customs Service authorised to receive it, information that relates to a person liable to pay financial support under the Child Support Act 1991 and that the Commissioner considers is not undesirable to disclose and is reasonably necessary for the purposes of ss 307 and 313 of the Customs and Excise Act 2018 (ie to assist the Commissioner in locating persons in serious default of child support).

## Student loans — Sch 7 cl 9

Nothing prevents the Commissioner from disclosing to another authorised person any information for the purpose of verifying any declaration made by an applicant for a student loan as to any unpaid amounts owed by the applicant where the declaration is a condition of obtaining the loan. For these purposes, an "authorised person" means:

## the Commissioner, or revenue officer authorised by the Commissioner

## the chief executive of the Ministry of Social Development, or an officer of the Ministry authorised by

the chief executive

## an employee of a tertiary institution authorised by the chief executive

## the Secretary of Education, or an officer of the Ministry of Education authorised by the Secretary, or

## the loan manager, or an employee of the loan manager authorised by the chief executive.

The Commissioner may provide certain information on student loan borrowers to the chief executive of the New Zealand Customs Service to verify their status and to locate borrowers who are in serious default on their student loans when they enter or leave New Zealand (¶8-095).

The Commissioner may also provide information to a contact person for a borrower if required to enable the contact person to provide current address details for the borrower.

The Commissioner may provide information on borrowers to the Australian Taxation Office to verify details of overseas-based borrowers and administer the student loan scheme for those borrowers (¶8-097).

## Data processing — Sch 7 cl 10

The Commissioner may communicate to any officer, employee or agent of any of the State Services any information relating to the processing of data or any other matter necessary for the effective administration of the IR Acts.

Services necessary for effective administration of revenue laws — Sch 7 cl 11 The Commissioner may provide necessary information to persons contracted to perform services, eg consultants.

## Residential land withholding tax — Sch 7 cl 12

The Commissioner may provide to a relevant professional body appropriate details of a failure by one of its members to satisfy, as agent, a person's liability to pay Resident Land Withholding Tax (RLWT) in accordance with the RLWT rules.

## Publishing certain items — Sch 7 cl 13

The confidentiality rule in s 18 does not prevent the Commissioner from publishing a product ruling.

## Publication of information relating to unclaimed money — Sch 7 cl 13C

The Commissioner may publish information relating to unclaimed money held by the Commissioner under the Unclaimed Money Act 1971 that is intended to enable the owner of the unclaimed money to claim it.

## Carrying out a function conferred on the Commissioner

There is a permitted disclosure under s 18D(2) when disclosure of sensitive revenue information is made in carrying out or supporting a function lawfully conferred on the Commissioner to:

## administer the tax system

## implement the tax system, or

## improve, research or reform the tax system, and

the Commissioner considers the disclosure is reasonable for these purposes, having regard to:

## the Commissioner's obligation at all times to use best endeavours to protect the integrity of the tax

system

## the importance of promoting compliance with the law, especially voluntary compliance

## the impact of the disclosure, personally or commercially or in some other way

## the resources available to the Commissioner, and

## the public availability of the information.

Standard practice statement SPS 11/07, "Application of discretion in s 81(1B) of the Tax Administration Act 1994 — the secrecy provisions" provides guidance to IR staff as to when the former s 81(1B) (now s 18D(2)), might result in IR disclosing such information, and outlines the steps IR will take before making that disclosure to ensure maximum consistency. The statement also briefly examines how the provision interacts with the other exceptions to the secrecy provisions.

## Note: IR released a draft standard practice statement, ED0210, "Application of discretion in section 18D(2)

of the Tax Administration Act 1994 — an exception to confidentiality", which sets out the Commissioner's practice regarding the confidentiality rule in s 18D(2), previously contained in former s 81(1B) of the TAA.

The draft statement updates the earlier statement for the legislative change and replaces SPS 11/07.

Disclosures in co-located environments

Under s 18D(3), a permitted disclosure exists when a revenue officer discloses sensitive revenue information:

## to another revenue officer, or a person who is not a revenue officer but who acquires revenue

information and who has completed a certificate of confidentiality

## disclosure is made at a place, and in conditions relating to the confidentiality of information, where

revenue officers are expected to perform their duties, and

## the revenue officer does not intend the person receiving the information to receive it and has taken

care that is reasonable in the circumstances to prevent the disclosure.

The objective is to relieve revenue officers (and others) working in close proximity to persons employed by other government agencies from liability for the inadvertent disclosure of taxpayer information. The conditions of the co-location work environment will need to be taken into account in determining what level of care is reasonable.

## Disclosures for court proceedings

Sensitive revenue information may be disclosed to a court or tribunal if disclosure is necessary for the purpose of carrying into effect a revenue law: s 18D(4)(a).

Secondly, the confidentiality rule in s 18 does not require a revenue officer to produce a document in a court or tribunal, or disclose to a court or tribunal any matter or thing coming to the officer's notice in the performance of their duties: s 18D(4)(b).

The question of disclosure in court proceedings may arise in relation to the discovery of documents by the Commissioner under the High Court Rules; see Westpac Banking Corp Ltd v C of IR; ANZ National Bank Ltd v C of IR (2008) 23 NZTC 21,896 (SC).

The Supreme Court noted that the exception to the Commissioner's obligation of secrecy applies where the Commissioner chooses to use secret tax material to support a case in litigation. However, the secret material must be reasonably necessary for the purposes of the litigation in which the Commissioner proposes its disclosure. If the threshold is met, techniques of editing and redaction should be pursued where that does not impair the utility of the material concerned.

It would seem that disclosure is prohibited in proceedings that do not directly involve IR (R v Saint-Merat [1958] NZLR 1147). It would also seem that information that has been requested by an accused in criminal proceedings is privileged if the disclosure is not necessary for carrying out the duties of IR (Daemar v Gilliand [1979] 2 NZLR 7 and Burt v Darwin (1986) 8 NZTC 5,026).

In R v Morris (2005) 22 NZTC 19,217, the first witness for the prosecution was an investigations officer employed by IR. Counsel for the respondent objected to the proposed evidence on the grounds that the officer giving it would infringe the confidentiality rule. The Court of Appeal held that provided that the IR officer's proposed evidence was admissible under the general law, he was not prevented from giving that evidence by the confidentiality rule, because his purpose in giving the evidence was to carry into effect the TAA. In addition, it was irrelevant that in that case the alleged fraud on the Revenue had been charged under the Crimes Act rather than an IR Act.

## Litigation against officers

The Court of Appeal has ordered IR to disclose information it was seeking to keep confidential under the secrecy provisions of the Inland Revenue Department Act 1974 (now found in the TAA). In Knight v Barnett (1991) 13 NZTC 8,014, the court noted it seemed quite wrong for IR, in litigation against its officers for misconduct and abuse of power, to claim the benefit of secrecy provisions. The Court of Appeal also noted the New Zealand Bill of Rights Act 1990 strengthened this conclusion that, subject to the question of public interest immunity, the Commissioner should comply with the ordinary obligations of a litigant to make discovery of relevant documents. Therefore, it would seem the question of whether disclosure is required will turn on the necessity for the production of the document as defined in s 3(1) of the TAA, for the purposes of carrying into effect any of the IR Acts.
