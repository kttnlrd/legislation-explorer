# Effect of assessment ¶4-080

The assessment process does not itself impose the liability to pay tax; as McCarthy J pointed out in Reckitt and Colman (New Zealand) Ltd v Taxation Board of Review [1966] NZLR 1032, it is the Income Tax Act itself that imposes, independently, the obligation to pay tax, the assessment process being merely the machinery for quantifying the amount of tax due.

Section 109 of the TAA provides that, except in objection proceedings under Pt 8 or a challenge under Pt 8A, a disputable decision shall be conclusively deemed to be correct and the taxpayer's liability determined accordingly. (A disputable decision is an assessment or some other decision made by the Commissioner which is open to objection or challenge proceedings and includes certain discretionary powers available to the Commissioner in the disputes resolution process — see definition of "disputable decision" in s 3(1).) The general proposition is that a taxpayer may challenge the correctness of an assessment only by invoking the appropriate challenge procedures provided for under the TAA. The same proposition applies to one of the Commissioner's determinations. The possibility of a challenge to an assessment outside the scope of the statutory procedures, by way of judicial review, is discussed at ¶4-275.

An assessment can also affect the Commissioner's powers of recovery. Hieber v C of IR (2000) 19 NZTC 15,716 held that the Commissioner can apply s 157 of the TAA (¶2-190) to require deductions to be made from amounts payable to a person owing tax, once an assessment triggering a liability to pay non-deferrable tax has been issued. (Note that there is now no definition of "non-deferrable tax" accompanying the definition of "deferrable tax".)

*Refs: Section 109, s 3(1), s 157*
