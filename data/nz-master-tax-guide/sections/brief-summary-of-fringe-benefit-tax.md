# Brief summary of fringe benefit tax ¶1-450

Remuneration received by an employee from their employer in a cash form is subject to income tax in the hands of the employee. Generally, in the past, remuneration in a non-cash form was not subject to income tax. This led to widespread provision of non-cash benefits such as the use of a motor vehicle, low-interest loans and discounted merchandise. FBT is intended to reduce this practice by requiring an employer to pay the tax on the value of the fringe benefits enjoyed by their employees (¶21-010 onwards).

Generally, FBT is deductible to the employer. However, if the employer has tax losses, the provision of fringe benefits represents a cash cost without the immediate benefit of a deduction (¶21-010).
