# Disclosure by foreign exemption trusts ¶25-550

New Zealand's foreign trust disclosure rules were further strengthened by the Taxation (Annual Rates for 2022-23, Platform Economy, and Remedial Matters) Act 2023, which introduced the term "foreign exemption trusts", which means a trust with a resident trustee whether no election has been made and the trust has or does make use of the foreign-sourced income exemption in s HC 26.

If an effective election has been made under s HC 33, and the trustee is taxed on the trust's worldwide income, and the trust is subject to domestic trust reporting, then the foreign trust disclosure rules do not apply. A foreign exemption trust must register with IR (TAA, s 59B(2)).

Importantly, the definition of "foreign exemption trust" applies for the purposes of the foreign trust disclosure rules while the definition of "foreign trust" applies for determining the income tax treatment of distributions.

IS 24/01 explains the parameters of the definition of "foreign exemption trusts" including the reporting obligations at [13.10] to [13.27].

Disclosure requirements will apply in relation to certain foreign trusts that are also complying trusts — see discussion on dual status trusts at ¶25-300.

*Refs: s HC 26, s HC 33, s 59B(2)*

## Registration

The IR forms for registration of a foreign trust are: IR607 Foreign trust registration form (February 2023), IR607A Foreign trust connected person schedule (February 2023), and IR900A Foreign trust settlements and distributions schedule (February 2023).

## Fees

The prescribed fee for an application for registration of a foreign trust is $270 (incl GST). The fee for an annual return is $50 (incl GST). Provision is made for regulations to be made to change the fees. An exemption from the fee is allowed if all the resident foreign trustees of the foreign trust are natural persons not in the business of providing trustee services.

## Loss of tax exemption

A foreign trust with a resident foreign trustee loses its tax exemption for foreign-sourced amounts unless the trustee has registered the trust and fulfilled the associated disclosure obligations at the time the foreign- sourced amount is derived. An appropriate transitional period was provided for existing foreign trusts to register following enactment of the new rules without running the risk of losing the exemption (¶25-100).

The eligibility for an exemption on foreign-sourced income of the trust is assessed on a year-by-year basis.

Accordingly, a foreign trust that fails to register on time will lose its exemption for that year. However, if it later registers and fulfils its annual reporting obligations it will be able to get the exemption for those years.

Law: TAA ss 3(1) ("resident foreign trustee"), 18H, 59B, 59C, 59D, 59DB, 59E, Sch 7 cl 28
