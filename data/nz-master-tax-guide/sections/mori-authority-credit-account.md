# M#ori authority credit account ¶24-327

Most M#ori authorities are required to establish and maintain a M#ori authority credit account (MACA).

A M#ori authority that is a member of a consolidated imputation group is exempt from filing a credit account return if the account balance is nil at all times during the relevant tax year (s 69B(3)).

Two kinds of M#ori authority must not operate a MACA: M#ori authorities whose constitution prohibits the making of distributions to members, and M#ori authorities that derive only exempt income (other than exempt dividend income under s CW 10).

A MACA operates in a similar way to an imputation account operated by a company and is subject to similar rules.

*Refs: s 69B(3), s CW 10*

## Credits to record

The following amounts arise as credits to the MACA:

## a payment of income tax or provisional tax, with the exception of:

## income tax paid in respect of the 2003–04 or earlier tax year

## where a M#ori authority becomes a M#ori authority during the imputation year, income tax relating to

that part of the income year before the M#ori authority became a M#ori authority

## income tax paid by way of imputation credits or further income tax, or

## income tax paid by crediting a R&D tax credit against income tax liability

## provisional tax paid to another M#ori authority under s RC 32 (payments transferred within a wholly-

owned group of companies)

## further income tax paid where the MACA is in debt at the end of the tax year or when the entity

ceases to be a M#ori authority (see below)

## M#ori authority credits attached to distributions made to the M#ori authority during the tax year

## imputation credits attached to dividends paid to the M#ori authority during the tax year

## imputation credits attributed to a M#ori authority, being an investor in a multi-rate PIE, under s HM 54

## the amount of R&D tax credit the M#ori authority is entitled to

## an amount to offset a debit for M#ori authority credits determined to have been the subject of an

arrangement to obtain a tax advantage to the extent that it is subsequently established that the M#ori authority credits were not the subject of any such arrangement

## the amount of any RWT deduction deemed to have been derived by the M#ori authority in terms of s

RA 9.

The timing of when each of these credits arises is also prescribed in ss OK 2–OK 9.

Amendments were made to the rules around memorandum accounts when making transfers from previous years with effect from 30 March 2022. Changes to ss OK 2, OK 3, OK 11 and OK 12 permit MACA entries resulting from a transfer of tax from a previous period to be made on the date the taxpayer requests the transfer, rather than the effective date chosen by the taxpayer. Transfers made before the end of an income year will continue to be made in the current year (in the as yet unfiled MACA return).

## Debits to record

Debits to the MACA during an imputation year arise from the following:

## M#ori authority credits attached to distributions made by the M#ori authority

## the amount of any provisional tax allocated by a M#ori authority to another M#ori authority that is an

"underpaid company" in terms of s RC 32 (payments to be transferred within a wholly-owned group of companies)

## refunds of income tax paid to a M#ori authority except to the extent that the refund:

## relates to the 2003–04 or earlier tax year, or

## relates to a tax year or part of a tax year during which the M#ori authority did not maintain a MACA

(where the refund relates to part of a tax year during which the M#ori authority did not maintain a MACA, the debit is apportioned to the number of days in which the M#ori authority maintained a MACA), or

## is not more than the amount of the debit for a breach of shareholding — see below

## making a distribution with a M#ori authority credit ratio that differs from that of the benchmark

distribution in contravention of s OK 20

## transfers of refundable credits under s LA 6(2)(d) where the transfer does not lead to a refund and

refunds of credits under s LA 6(2)(e)

## failure to meet the continuity of shareholding requirements — see below

## the allocation of credits in pursuance of a tax advantage arrangement in terms of s GB 43

## an amount equivalent to the credit balance in the MACA when a M#ori authority ceases to be a M#ori

authority, and

## any overpaid income tax applied by the Commissioner in satisfaction of tax liabilities (other than

income tax or provisional tax instalments), except to the extent the amount applied is:

## for income tax paid for the 2003–04 or earlier tax year, or

## for income tax paid before the date that a debit for a breach of shareholder continuity has arisen and

is for not more than the amount of the debit for the breach of continuity.

The timing of when each of these debits arises is also prescribed in ss OK 10–OK 18.

The Commissioner has the power to correct credits or debits incorrectly recorded in a MACA.

## End-of-year requirements

M#ori authority credit accounts are subject to end-of-year requirements and general penalty provisions similar to those that apply in respect of ICAs. If a MACA has a debit balance at 31 March, further income tax is payable by the following 20 June. Further income tax must also be paid where a MACA has a debit balance immediately before a M#ori authority ceases to be a M#ori authority. Payments of further income tax may be applied to an income tax or a provisional tax liability that arises at any time when the M#ori authority is required to establish and maintain a MACA. Income tax paid in relation to a tax year in which a M#ori authority is required to establish and maintain a MACA may be applied to a further income tax liability caused by a MACA debit balance. A M#ori authority that is liable to pay further income tax for an end-of-year debit balance is also liable to pay a special tax known as M#ori authority distribution penalty tax of an amount equal to 10% of the amount of the further income tax. The general compliance and penalty rules apply if the M#ori authority fails to pay the further income tax and penalty tax by the due date. Provision is made for the remission of M#ori authority distribution penalty tax and interest and late payment penalties relating to further income tax in ss 181B and 183A of the TAA (¶14-090 and ¶14-180).

## Continuity requirements

Corporate M#ori authorities are required to maintain 66% continuity of shareholding for the purpose of carrying forward M#ori authority credits. If 66% continuity of shareholding is not met, M#ori authority credits are cancelled. Non-corporate M#ori authorities, such as M#ori trust boards who do not issue shares, should qualify for the "special corporate entity relief", whereby the special corporate entity is treated as always being 100% owned by the same single person so that no breach of continuity is possible (¶16-155).

## Limits on refund of tax

A refund of income tax to a M#ori authority is limited to the credit balance in its MACA at the later of the end of the tax year most recently ended, the last day of any period for which the M#ori authority has filed a

M#ori authority credit account return under s 70B(3) of the TAA and the last day of a period for which the Commissioner has required such a return to be filed under s 70B(1). For M#ori authorities that have an extension of time for filing returns, so long as the MACA return for a tax year is filed under the extension of time before the end of the next tax year, the refund is limited to the credit balance in the M#ori authority's MACA on the last day of the tax year for which the return is furnished, not the most recently ending tax year.

These rules are similar to the rules that limit a refund or allocation of income tax to an ICA company to the balance in its ICA (¶17-105).

Income tax paid in excess that is not refunded to a M#ori authority because its MACA has insufficient credits is retained and applied in payment of income tax or provisional tax payable by the M#ori authority in the tax year in which the entitlement arose or an earlier or later tax year. The excess can be credited as at a date on which there is no liability to pay provisional tax but from which use of money interest applies in relation to underpaid residual income tax. To the extent that income tax not refunded cannot be so applied, it is retained by the Commissioner.
