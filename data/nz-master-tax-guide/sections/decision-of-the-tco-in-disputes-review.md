# Decision of the TCO in disputes review ¶4-205

The IR officer in charge of the dispute is advised of the decision of the Tax Counsel Office (TCO). If the decision is in favour of the Commissioner, the officer will do what is required to give effect to the decision, including issuing a notice of assessment. If the decision is against the Commissioner, the dispute resolution process comes to an end. The Commissioner has no right of challenge. The Commissioner issues any assessments that may be necessary to reflect the decision in favour of the taxpayer.

If the decision made by the TCO is against the taxpayer, the taxpayer may challenge it. The taxpayer may file proceedings in the TRA or in the High Court.

If the dispute resolution process was commenced by the taxpayer, proceedings may be filed by the taxpayer in the following circumstances:

## the assessment was the subject of an adjustment proposed by the taxpayer that the Commissioner

has rejected, or

## the assessment was subject to a NOPA issued by the taxpayer, which the Commissioner has

rejected, and the Commissioner has issued a challenge notice. This defers the commencement of challenge proceedings as the challenge notice can only generally be issued after the Commissioner has issued a SOP, or

## the Commissioner or taxpayer has issued an assessment that is the subject of an adjustment notified

to the Commissioner, where:

## the adjustment relates to a matter for which the material facts and relevant law are identical to

another assessment, for a different period, for the taxpayer which are subject to court proceedings, or

## the adjustment corrects a tax position taken by the taxpayer, or a person associated with the

taxpayer, as a consequence of an incorrect tax position taken by another taxpayer, which is or was subject to court proceedings, and

## the Commissioner has issued a challenge notice (¶4-192), or

## a disputable decision, not being an assessment, was the subject of an adjustment proposed by the

taxpayer that the Commissioner has rejected.

If the dispute resolution process was commenced by the Commissioner, proceedings may be filed by the taxpayer if the assessment includes an adjustment proposed by the Commissioner, which the taxpayer has rejected within 2 months of the date of the NOPA. Proceedings may also be filed by the taxpayer if the assessment meets the following criteria:

## it is an amended assessment, and

## a fresh liability is imposed or an existing liability is increased.

The taxpayer must file proceedings with the TRA or the High Court within 2 months from the date the assessment is issued after the adjudication process. This time limit applies to all disputes, whether commenced by the taxpayer or the Commissioner.
