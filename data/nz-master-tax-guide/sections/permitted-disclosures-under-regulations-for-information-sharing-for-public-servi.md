# Permitted disclosures under regulations for information- sharing for public services purposes ¶1-615

Section 18F of the TAA allows regulations to be made by Order in Council authorising revenue information and other information to be shared in the context of providing public services. Section 18F applies when:

## sharing of the information is intended to improve the government's ability to deliver efficient and

effective services or enforce the law

## the information is more easily or more efficiently obtained from, or verified by, the Commissioner

## it is not unreasonable or impractical to require the Commissioner to deliver the information

## the nature of the sharing is proportionate, taking into account the purpose of the proposed sharing

## the agency or other recipient of the information has adequate protection in place for it, and

## sharing of the information will not unduly inhibit the future provision of information to the

Commissioner.

An Order in Council may be made issuing regulations for the sharing of revenue information that the Commissioner holds with an agency. The regulations may prescribe the types of revenue information to be shared, how it is to be provided, accessed and used, and how it is to be stored, protected or disposed of.

The regulations must also provide for monitoring by the Privacy Commissioner and specify whether further disclosure of the information is permitted and whether review requirements are to be stipulated.

As a further safeguard, before recommending that the regulations should be made, the Minister of Revenue must be satisfied that:

## the regulations are necessary to achieve the criteria listed above

## the type and quantity of information is no more than is necessary to facilitate the objective of

efficiency or law enforcement

## the privacy of individuals is not unreasonably impinged and adequate privacy safeguards will apply

## the information-sharing mechanism proposed is the most appropriate one, and

## there has been a consultative process (with a minimum of 4 weeks consultation period).

The former s 81BA of the TAA contained similar provisions (although those provisions only authorised the Commissioner to disclose information to government agencies, rather than the new wider sharing for public services). An Order in Council made under s 81BA is treated as a regulation made under s 18F, to the extent that the order remains in force.

Permitted disclosures to persons and their representatives

The disclosure of sensitive revenue information to a person in relation to whom the information is held (or to that person's representative) is a permitted disclosure and so not subject to the confidentiality requirement in s 18: s 18G. Detailed rules for this form of disclosure appear in Sch 7 pt B.

The categories of disclosure in Sch 7 pt B have been summarised below.

*Refs: s 18, s 18G*

## Persons in relation to whom information is held and their

## representatives — Sch 5 cl 15

The Commissioner may permit copies of, or details of or from, any document or information in the possession of, or obtained by or on behalf of, the Commissioner to be given to the person from whom, or on behalf of whom, or in relation to whom, such document or information is held or was obtained, or the legal personal representative or agent of such person. The Commissioner must be satisfied that the information is readily available in IR and must consider it reasonable and practicable to give the information.

For these purposes, "legal personal representative" means the executor or administrator of a deceased person, or any person who, by court order, has been appointed guardian or manager of the estate of any person or administers such an estate.

See Sch 7, cl 15.

## Third-party providers — Sch 7 cl 16

The Commissioner may provide information received from, or in relation to, a third-party provider to the person to whom the information relates in the following situations:

## Tax agent

The Commissioner may communicate to a taxpayer who has chosen to have their returns prepared by a tax agent:

## whether the tax agent is a listed tax agent (¶2-018)

## any decision of the Commissioner refusing to list the tax agent or removing the tax agent from the list.

## Tax pooling intermediary

Where a tax pooling intermediary has made a deposit to a tax pooling account on behalf of a person, the Commissioner may give the details of the deposit to that person.

## Representative or nominated person

When a person has another person acting on their behalf as either a representative or nominated person, the Commissioner may communicate to the person:

## whether approval of the other person's status as a representative is disallowed

## whether the other person's status as a nominated person is disallowed

## a decision of the Commissioner refusing to approve, or disallowing, the other person's status.

PAYE intermediary

The Commissioner may communicate the revocation of a PAYE intermediary's approval to an employer for whom the PAYE intermediary is acting.

## Approved AIM provider

The Commissioner may communicate the revocation of an approved AIM provider's approval to a person who uses the software provided by that provider.

## RWT proxy

The Commissioner may communicate the breach of the requirements to be an RWT proxy to a person for whom the RWT proxy is acting.

## Software clients — Sch 7 cl 17

The Commissioner may communicate information relating to a person (the software client) who uses an accepted software package to communicate information to, and receive information from, the Commissioner:

## to a person, or an employee or agent of a person, who maintains the accepted software package for

the software client, and

## as a consequence of communicating the information to the software client using the accepted

software package.

## Digital services providers — Sch 7 cl 18

The Commissioner may communicate information about a person to an accepted provider of digital services when the person uses the digital services to communicate with the Commissioner and the disclosure is a consequence of that communication.

Permitted disclosure: approved credit reporting agency

The Commissioner may disclose details of a taxpayer's unpaid tax to an approved credit reporting agency.

In broad terms, disclosure is permitted when the tax debt exceeds $150,000, it is not the subject of a tax dispute and the Commissioner has given the taxpayer 30 days' notice of the proposed disclosure.

Tax information that the Commissioner will disclose comprises:

## the tax type — GST, income tax and employer deductions required to be made under the PAYE rules

## the total dollar amount of debt, per tax type, and

## the oldest debt period, per tax type.

The only credit reporting agency currently approved is Centrix.

The existence of any instalment arrangement reached with IR will also be disclosed. Information disclosure will continue until the tax debt is resolved in full.

While the provision includes all taxpayers, currently the practical effect is that it is limited to non-natural persons, such as registered New Zealand companies. This is because personal information about individuals is regulated by the Credit Reporting Privacy Code of Practice (enabled by the Privacy Act). This code currently prevents credit reporters from receiving tax information about individuals. The government intends to apply the provision to all taxpayers and officials will work with the Office of the Privacy Commissioner on the interaction of the provision and the Credit Reporting Privacy Code of Practice.

Details of a taxpayer's unpaid tax may be communicated to allow a credit reporting agency to include information in the taxpayer's credit report. Disclosure is also permitted to evidence and maintain the accuracy of the report (eg updates on changes in the amount of unpaid tax may be given).

## Disclosure criteria

The Commissioner may disclose taxpayer information to an approved credit reporting agency when:

## the taxpayer has an amount of reportable unpaid tax

## the Commissioner has formally notified the taxpayer of the unpaid tax and of the intention to effect

disclosure

## the Commissioner has made reasonable efforts to recover the reportable unpaid tax, and

## 30 days after the date formal notification is given to the taxpayer:

## the reportable unpaid tax is greater than $150,000 (being the amount fixed by the Taxation

(Disclosure of Information to Approved Credit Reporting Agencies) Regulations 2017), or

## reportable unpaid tax has been outstanding for a year and, in the Commissioner's judgment, the

proportion of the unpaid amount to the taxpayer's assessable income for the year is 30% or more.

Reportable unpaid tax is unpaid tax resulting from liability for (or excess refunds of) income tax, GST, deductions required by the PAYE rules, student loan scheme deductions, child support deductions, ESCT, RSCT, tax credits under Pt L (other than working for families tax credits), and use of money interest and civil penalties on such reportable unpaid tax. It does not include unpaid tax subject to a dispute or challenge under the TAA or tax for which the Commissioner has entered into an instalment arrangement after a request by the taxpayer. (Note that it is not sufficient for the taxpayer to merely request such an arrangement.)

12-month rule The Commissioner may also disclose details of a taxpayer's unpaid tax to a credit reporting agency if:

## the Commissioner gives formal notification to the taxpayer that disclosure of unpaid tax may occur

("the current notification")

## the current notification is the third formal notification given to the taxpayer in 12 months

## neither of the 2 earlier notifications resulted in a disclosure to a credit reporting agency because the

taxpayer paid a portion of the unpaid tax, and

## the Commissioner has made reasonable efforts to recover unpaid tax from the taxpayer before the

current notification.

In this situation, disclosure may be made even if the amount of unpaid tax is less than the prescribed financial thresholds.

## Instalment arrangements

Some provision is made for taxpayers seeking to make instalment arrangements. Information concerning a taxpayer's unpaid tax cannot be disclosed to a credit reporting agency until the Commissioner has completed considering an application to remit tax or a penalty under s 177 or 183H of the TAA. This restriction applies to applications for relief made in the 30-day period after formal notification of the intention to disclose has been given to the taxpayer.

## Publication

The Commissioner must publish the following details on an annual basis for the previous tax year:

## the number of taxpayers formally notified

## the number of taxpayers whose information was disclosed to an approved credit reporting agency,

and

## the number of taxpayers who paid their reportable unpaid tax in full after being formally notified and

having their tax data disclosed.
