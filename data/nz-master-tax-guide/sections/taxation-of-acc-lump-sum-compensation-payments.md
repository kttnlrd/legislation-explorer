# Taxation of ACC lump sum compensation payments ¶31-076

## Lump sum support payments

An issue arose for recipients of lump sum compensation payments as application of the general derivation of income rule provides that income is taxed in the year it is received for most individuals. This could result in an unfair position for a taxpayer as the lump sum amount may push an individual into a higher tax bracket for the tax year in which it is received. If this happened, the individual is taxed at a higher marginal tax rate and receives less than they would have been entitled to if the amounts had been paid in previous years, or over a number of income years.

From 1 April 2024 payments of backdated accident compensation earnings-related payments, personal services rehabilitation payments and support payments made under the Veteran's Support Act 2014, which are paid out in a lump sum, are able to be taxed in a different way.

In effect, the recipient's tax liability is approximated to ensure that the lower amount of tax is applied as if it had been paid in earlier years. The tax rate that applies to these backdated ACC compensation payments that consist of a lump sum and related to more than one income year (a multi-year support payment) is either:

## 10.5% if the average basic tax rate is less than 10.5%, or

## the person's average basic tax rate, or

## the person's basic tax rate for the income year they receive the multi-year compensation payment if

the person's basic tax rate is less than the average basic tax rate.

The "average basic tax rate" is calculated over the period of the 4 income years (the rate averaging period) ending before the income year in which the person derives the multi-year support payment. The formula for the average basic rate is: total basic rates ÷ number of income years.

Where:

## total basic rates is the sum of the person's basic tax rate calculated under Schedule 1, part A,

clause 1 for each of the income years in the rate averaging period for which that rate is known by the Commissioner:

## number of income years is the number of income years within the rate averaging period for which the

person's basic tax rate is known by the Commissioner and included in the total basic rates.

A minimum withholding rate of 10.5% has been set. The provision introduces a 'lower of' test to ensure that recipients are not disadvantaged under the alternative tax treatment compared to application of the general derivation rule. If the recipient has had a higher tax rate in the 4 years prior to receiving the backdated lump sum but has a lower tax rate in the year the backdated lump sum payment is paid, the previous tax treatment would apply and the lump sum will be taxed according to the rate applicable in the year in which it is received. ACC can request the recipient's tax rate before the backdated lump sum is made and apply the rate as the withholding rate on the payment. Consequently, no additional tax should be payable for the backdated lump sum as long as the recipient's circumstances do not change. Note that the alternative tax rate applies to the backdated lump sum payment separately from the individual's other income in the year they receive the payment.

There are examples that illustrate how the alternative tax treatment works in Tax Information Bulletin ¶364-102a Vol 36, No. 4, May 2024

Attendant care payments

Attendant care payments receive a different tax treatment to support payments. Attendant care payments are also referred to as personal services rehabilitation payments. These payments are sometimes passed on to third parties. If attendant care payments are passed on to third parties, the existing tax treatment remains unchanged, and the income will be exempt income to the recipient to the extent that income is passed on.

Those amounts are then taxable to the person providing those care services. Attendant care payments have a standard rate of 10.5% withheld under part I of sch 4.

If the amount is not passed on it will be taxable to the recipient under the rules for multi-year support payments. The majority of backdated lump sum payments that relate to attendant care are likely to be retained by the recipient.

The withholding tax treatment for backdated lump sum attendant care payments differs from support payments (discussed above). As these payments have a standard withholding tax rate of 10.5%, the same withholding tax rate will be applied to attendant care backdated lump sums. However, the recipient may want tax on their payment to be withheld at their average tax rate for the prior 4 years. This would amount to a tailored tax rate; however, it should be noted that average tax rates inevitably result in a higher rate of withholding than the standard withholding rate. For attendant care payments, there is a high likelihood that the applicable tax rate will be 10.5% and therefore the standard rate is likely to be the most appropriate rate for these types of payments.
