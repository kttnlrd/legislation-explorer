# Exclusion from FBT — employee share loans ¶21-568

An employee share loan is excluded from the definition of fringe benefit.

An employee share loan meets the following criteria:

## the purpose of the loan must be to enable the employee (and not an associate) to acquire shares in

the employer (or a company associated with the employer)

## the loan must be used for that purpose only

## the shares must be beneficially owned by the employee at all times during the currency of the loan

## the loan must include a condition that the employee must immediately repay the loan in full if the

employee stops being the beneficial owner of the shares

## the company issuing the shares maintains a dividend paying policy

## the company issuing the shares is not a qualifying company throughout the term of the loan

## the employer and employee are not associated persons, and

## the employee share loan is not made under an exempt employee share scheme (ESS).

An employee share loan also applies to a loan for the purchase of rights or options in the company.

## Employee share loans exclusion and associates of the

employee QWBA QB 24/03: "Fringe benefit tax — employee share loans and associates" considered whether a fringe benefit arises where a trustee of a family trust that is associated with an employee is provided a loan to acquire shares under an ESS. The Commissioner's view is that the employee share loans exclusion can apply provided a fringe benefit would not arise if the employee were provided the loan to acquire the shares under the ESS in the same circumstances.

Law: IT07 ss CX 10(2)(a), CX 35

Exclusion from FBT — superannuation cash contributions

Employer's superannuation cash contributions are excluded from the FBT regime. Employer's superannuation cash contributions are contributions paid in money by an employer for the benefit of their employees to a "superannuation fund" (ie a retirement scheme (within the meaning of s 6(1) of the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013), and for a retirement scheme that is a trust, the trustees of the retirement scheme) or a KiwiSaver scheme registered under the KiwiSaver Act 2006.

Also excluded from the definition of fringe benefit are benefits in the form of the provision of services to a superannuation fund to the extent that those would have been allowed as a deduction if the expenditure had been incurred by the fund.

From 1 April 2024, employer contributions to foreign superannuation schemes for cross-border employees have been effectively removed from the ambit of FBT. The way the legislation is worded, these cash contributions may be treated as superannuation cash contributions if the employer of a cross-border employee chooses to treat the payment as such. Therefore, employers of cross-border employees have 2 options when making a superannuation cash contribution. These are to either choose to be subject to FBT or employer's superannuation contribution tax (ESCT). Then if ESCT treatment is chosen, the employer can choose to treat it as salary or wages under s RD 68.

Law: IT07 ss CX 13(2), CX 32, RD 65(1)(b), YA 1 ("employer's superannuation contribution")

Last reviewed on 31 December 2025

Exemption from FBT — employee tax return preparation

Assistance in preparing an employee's tax return is exempt from FBT. The exemption applies to the extent that the expenditure incurred in providing the assistance would have been deductible if incurred by the employee under s DB 3 (¶10-920). An employee's return includes the income information included in a pre- populated account under s 22D of the TAA.

Example: A company pays its tax advisers to prepare the tax returns of its senior managers. The expenditure is not subject to FBT.

Law: IT07 s CX 27
