# Income from unclaimed money ¶5-225

Many businesses hold money that is owing to another person. In New Zealand, the Unclaimed Money Act 1971 sets out rules that apply to holders of unclaimed money. The rules underwent a general reform in March 2021.

Generally, after a certain period, unclaimed money becomes payable to the Commissioner. However, the amount ceases to be unclaimed money (although the owner of the money may still be entitled to repayment) where the amount of unclaimed money does not exceed $100 per owner (the person entitled to the money).

Holders have a choice between transferring amounts of $100 or less to the Commissioner or putting them to another purpose (such as donating them to charity). Money applied for the benefit of the holder, or another use, will cease to be unclaimed money. However, this does not affect any claim that the owner of the money may have against the holder for repayment. Amounts retained by a holder that are not unclaimed money will be income in the hands of the fund holder.

Previously, the length of time required to pass before money was deemed unclaimed was either 6 or 25 years from the date of the owner's last interaction with the holder, depending on the type of funds. From 30 March 2021, a standard deeming period of 5 years applies to all amounts of money. To avoid engaging the deeming period, an owner must request information or provide instructions to the holder about the money, or another matter, within 5 years. To recognise modern forms of account interaction, "requesting information" or "providing instructions" will include, for example, customer interaction through internet banking (eg where a customer views their account balance online). An institutional approach is applied to account interaction, so that activity on one account is applied to all accounts the customer holds with the same institution. If the owner does not request information or provide instructions on any of their accounts, the money is deemed unclaimed and the deeming period commences from the date of the owner's last interaction with the money.

An exception applies to the standard deeming period for term deposits that are to be automatically reinvested on maturity. Where the deposit is the only investment a customer has with an institution, the 5-year deeming period will not commence until the second term (ie first rollover) of the renewing term arrangement. Once the deeming period starts and 5 years have passed with no interaction, the money will be deemed unclaimed money. However, to avoid breaking a deposit mid-term, a deposit is prevented from becoming unclaimed money before the end of a term.

Holders who have made reasonable efforts but have been unsuccessful in locating an owner may choose to pay money that has not yet become unclaimed to the Commissioner. Holders are encouraged to use their resources and the information they hold efficiently and to pursue avenues of contact they consider will be most productive. IR has stated it expects most holders would, in providing good customer service, make multiple attempts to contact the customer before the deeming period ends. The holder may, at its discretion, send a final notice to the customer once the funds become unclaimed, but this is not required.

Holders must provide the Commissioner with relevant information in their possession or control relating to the owner and the money within one month and 20 days of the end of their quarterly reporting period.

Holders were previously required to maintain a publicly available register of unclaimed money, but this requirement has been removed. Holders are instead encouraged to maintain sufficient internal records.

The reforms have made 3 categories of money received or held by the Commissioner unclaimable. These are amounts that:

## have been unclaimed money for 25 years or more

## have no identifying information, or

## are $100 or less.

Amounts that fall within these categories are removed from the list of unclaimed money by the Commissioner and may not be claimed by their owners. In each case, these amounts cease to be unclaimed money when they meet the requirements for delisting by the Commissioner. The Crown becomes the owner of this money.

Detailed transitional provisions apply for holders to take account of the change in timelines from the old deeming periods to the new 5-year deeming period.

## Rulings

BR Pub 17/01 and BR Pub 17/02, provide guidance on when amounts of unclaimed money of less than $100, received in the course of business, are derived as income. The rulings conclude that while money is held on trust and cannot be (and has not been) applied by the holder for their own (or anyone else's) benefit, it cannot be income of the holder. However, an unclaimed amount not held on trust will be derived when:

## it has been applied by the holder for its own (or anyone else's) benefit, and

## it is probable the amount will not have to be repaid (including situations where the amount is not

legally recoverable).

Whether it is probable that an amount will not be repaid will depend on the particular business. Business records and accounting treatment are likely to be relevant to determining this.

If a holder has recognised a previously unclaimed amount as income and subsequently returns that amount to the owner, the holder will be entitled to a deduction under s DA 1(b).

The rulings apply indefinitely from the beginning of the 2017–18 income year.
