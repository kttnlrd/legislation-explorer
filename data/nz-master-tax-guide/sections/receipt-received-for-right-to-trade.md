# Receipt received for right to trade ¶5-187

A distinction can be made between a person parting with some of the person's property and the use of that property as a method of trading. When a person does in fact part with an asset, such as the right to trade, the receipt is normally capital. However, a distinction is to be drawn between that type of case and the case in which a person acquires a right with the intention of reselling it.

Example: Profits arising from the sale of motel franchises were assessable as profit derived from the carrying on of a business. The selling or promoting of the franchise system was the business carried on and accordingly the income was assessable. See Consolidated Motels Ltd v C of IR; Trailways Motel Systems Ltd v C of IR (1976) 2 NZTC 61,097.
