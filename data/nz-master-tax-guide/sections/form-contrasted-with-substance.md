# Form contrasted with substance ¶33-040

The legal rights and obligations created by the contracts establishing the transaction are called the legal form. That can be contrasted with what is called the economic substance of the transaction. The case generally cited as explicitly endorsing the form over substance approach is IR Commrs v Duke of Westminster [1936] AC 1.

There are various constructional tools available to assist in identifying the rights and obligations created by a contract. These tools have been developed by the common law and in summary are:

## Where the transaction is contained in more than one contract both or all of the contracts can be read

together so as to understand the rights and obligations created: Buckley & Young Ltd v C of IR (1978) 3 NZTC 61,271 at 61,276; [1978] 2 NZLR 485 at 490, C of IR v Europa Oil (NZ) Ltd 70 ATC 6012 at 6,019; [1971] NZLR 641 at 649, Marac Finance Ltd v Virtue [1981] 1 NZLR 586 at 587.

## Regard may be had to the surrounding circumstances to assist in understanding the terms of the

contract but not so as to contradict or override the terms of the contract: Marac Finance Ltd v Virtue [1981] 1 NZLR 586 at 587, Buckley & Young Ltd v C of IR (1978) 3 NZTC 61,271 at 61,276; [1978] 2 NZLR 485 at 490.

## The courts are not concerned with the motives or intentions of the parties: Buckley & Young Ltd v C

of IR (1978) 3 NZTC 61,271 at 61,276; [1978] 2 NZLR 485 at 490.

## The nomenclature or labels used in the contract or contracts are not decisive. What is decisive is

the actual character of the rights and obligations actually created by the clauses of the instrument: see Buckley & Young Ltd v C of IR (1978) 3 NZTC 61,271 at 61,276; [1978] 2 NZLR 485 at 490.

An example of litigation concerning the correct characterisation of the legal rights and obligations, despite their label, can be found in Accent Management Ltd v C of IR (2005) 22 NZTC 19,027 (HC) at [133]–[168], overturned by the Court of Appeal in Accent Management Ltd v C of IR (2007) 23 NZTC 21,323 (CA) at [78]–[102], Europa Oil (NZ) Ltd v C of IR (No 2); C of IR v Europa Oil (NZ) Ltd (No 2) (1976) 2 NZTC 61,066 at 61,072; [1976] 1 NZLR 546 at 553, Street v Mountford [1985] 1 AC 814, Re Securitibank Ltd (No 2) [1978] 2 NZLR 136 and Ensign Tankers (Leasing) Ltd v Stokes [1992] BTC 110; [1992] AC 655.

## When trying to ascertain what rights and obligations have been created, the court will use the same

approach it would have used when trying a dispute between the parties as to the meaning of their documents: see C of IR v Renouf Corporation Ltd (1998) 18 NZTC 13,914 (CA).
