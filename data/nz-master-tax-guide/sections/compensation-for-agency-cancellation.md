# Compensation for agency cancellation ¶5-414

Compensation for the loss of an agency is a capital receipt if the agency constitutes the substratum of the recipient's business.

Example: A company repairing ships of a shipping company received a payment regarded as a capital receipt when the shipping company was liquidated. See Barr Crombie & Co Ltd v IR Commrs (1945) 26 TC 406.

On the other hand, compensation is income when the recipient holds several agencies and the loss of one is a normal trading risk. See IR Commrs v Fleming & Co (Machinery) Ltd (1952) 33 TC 57. A payment to a company as consideration for the variation and partial surrender of its rights under a long-term supply and marketing contract was held to be a payment of capital and not assessable.

See also C of IR v Thomas Borthwick & Sons (Australasia) Ltd (1992) 14 NZTC 9,101.
