# Geothermal wells and depreciation ¶13-465

A geothermal well is defined as "a bore or well solely for the purposes of investigating or exploiting geothermal energy in New Zealand".

The rules recognise that the capital costs associated with failed wells should be deductible. If a failed well is subsequently sold or brought back into production, the amount previously allowed as a deduction (or the sale proceeds, whichever is lower) is written back. In other cases, geothermal wells are to be depreciated over their useful life, from the date the well is completed. Geothermal wells owned during the "proving period" are treated as depreciable property (Tax Information Bulletin ¶193-114 Vol 19, No 3, April 2007 at 62).

Law: IT07 ss EE 6(4), EE 44(2)(c), EE 45(7), EE 47(6), YA 1 ("geothermal well")
