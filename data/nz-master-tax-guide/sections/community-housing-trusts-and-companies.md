# Community housing trusts and companies ¶24-132

A tax exemption exists for income derived by community housing entities (trustees or companies) whose activities involve the provision of housing or housing assistance. The exemption for an eligible community housing entity is intended to apply to all income derived by the entity, not simply the income derived from the entity's housing activity.

## Requirements

To benefit from the exemption, the community housing entity must be a registered community housing provider under the Public and Community Housing Management Act 1992, and it must ensure that its activities are not carried on for the private pecuniary profit of any individual, and all profits are retained, or distributed or applied to specific classes of recipients. The class of recipients includes other community housing entities, beneficiaries or clients of the entity, tax charities and persons to whom distributions would be in accordance with charitable purposes.

## Control test

In addition to the above, no person with some control over the activities must be able to divert amounts for the benefit or advantage of:

## if the entity (the operating entity) is carrying on the activities for or for the benefit of a community

housing entity or charity (the controlling entity), a person other than the operating entity or the controlling entity except for a purpose of either entity or for a charitable purpose, or

## in any other case, a person other than the entity except for a purpose of the entity or a charitable

purpose.

A person is treated as having some control and being able to divert amounts if they satisfy the criteria in s CW 42(5) and (6), discussed in ¶24-210.

Note that issues with the application of a previous control test were identified where wholly-owned subsidiaries carried out the activities (¶24-210), but retrospective amendments were made to ensure that when a community housing entity is itself a wholly-owned subsidiary of a charitable trust or another community housing entity, the wholly-owned subsidiary is eligible for the community housing entity tax exemption. A similar amendment was made for subsidiaries of other charitable entities (¶24-210) and tertiary education institutions (¶24-275).

## Exclusions

An entity will not be a community housing entity if:

## more than 15% of the people who become beneficiaries or clients of the entity on or after 14 April

2014 have income or assets exceeding a value (the entry threshold) set out in Sch 34, or

## the provision of housing or housing assistance to those people with income or assets not exceeding

the entry threshold is substantially different from that provided to those who do exceed the threshold.

Whether a person has income or assets that exceed the entry threshold is determined on the date they become a beneficiary or client of the entity.

## Income threshold

Schedule 34 provides that the income threshold is set at $85,000 for an individual and $130,000 for a group of applicants, eg a couple. If a person carries on a business, their income will be their net income from their

business for the previous 12 months. In other cases, it will be the assessable income derived in the previous 12 months.

## Asset threshold

There is no asset test for first home buyers. For others, the threshold asset value is set at $120,000 if the proposed purchase is of land in Auckland, $100,000 if the purchase is in Hamilton City, Tauranga City, Western Bay of Plenty District, Kapiti Coast District, Porirua City, Upper Hutt City, Hutt City, Wellington City, Nelson City, Tasman District, Waimakariri District, Christchurch City, Selwyn District or Queenstown Lakes District, and $80,000 for any other area in New Zealand.

A person's asset value is defined as the total value of property owned by the person that is available to the person for the purchase of land and is:

## money in a bank account, including a fixed or term deposit

## a share, stock or bond

## an investment in a bank or financial institution

## money paid to, or held by, a real estate agent or solicitor as a deposit in the purchase of a home

## a boat or caravan with a value of more than $5,000

## a vehicle not being used as a usual method of transport

## an asset of another type, with a value of more than $5,000.

Section 225D of the Tax Administration Act 1994 (TAA) provides for the Governor-General to make regulations, on the combined recommendation of the Minister of Revenue and the Minister of Housing, amending the income and assets threshold values set out in Sch 34.

## Goods and Services Tax

Three public binding rulings, BR Pub 24/01–24/03 "GST – Supplies of properties used for transitional housing" consider the GST treatment of supplies of properties by landlords to organisations for use in the Ministry of Housing and Urban Development's Transitional Housing Programme (¶32-052).

BR Pub 24/01 clarifies that the supply of a property by a landlord will be exempt from GST on the basis that it is supplied with the principal purpose of accommodation in a dwelling. However, certain supplies will not fall within the exemption. BR Pub 24/02 confirms a supply of a property where there is no right to quite enjoyment under the head-lease is a taxable supply and the value of the supply of the head-lease cannot be reduced under s 10(6) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. BR Pub 24/03 clarifies that a supply of a "commercial dwelling" under the head-lease is a taxable supply and the value of the supply of the head-lease cannot be reduced under s 10(6).
