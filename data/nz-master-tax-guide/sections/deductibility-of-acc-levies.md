# Deductibility of ACC levies ¶31-065

Deductibility of accident compensation levies are subject to the general permission (¶10-021) being satisfied and application of any of the general limitations (¶10-071). ACC levies will generally meet the test for deductibility under s DA 1 as they are part of the costs of employing people.

IR have confirmed that the private limitation under s DA 2(2) will not apply to self-employed persons in respect of the ACC earners' levy (despite the fact that the earner's levy paid by self-employed persons is used to fund accidents that do not occur at work). The Commissioner's view on the deductibility of ACC earners levy remains as published in Tax Information Bulletin ¶111-107 Vol 11 No 1 January 1999, which confirmed that the earners' account levy is tax deductible for income tax purposes. This applies to both self- employed persons and partners.

Earners' levy is deductible to a company paying on behalf of a shareholder-employee, because it is expenditure on account of an employee. The deduction for the company is based on the ability to claim a deduction for employee remuneration (including the ACC levies) rather than because it is earners' levy.

The other ACC levies, like the work account levy and working safer levy, are fully deductible as they are used to fund work related accidents for all employees.

The ACC Cover Plus Extra premiums paid by the employer company as expenditure on account of an employee is deductible for the company, however the premiums will be assessable income to the shareholder-employee and non-deductible to that shareholder-employee. Conversely, if the premium is simply debited to the shareholder-employee current account, there is no deduction to the employer company.

This is because the Cover Plus Extra policy cover the shareholder-employee and compensation under the policy is treated as income from employment. Therefore, the employment limitation under s DA 2(4) applies and these levies are non-deductible to the shareholder-employee.

Accident compensation levies are generally deductible in the year in which they are due (¶31-070).

*Refs: s DA 1, s DA 2(2), s DA 2(4)*

## ACC levies and GST

Generally, GST input tax credit can only be claimed when the ACC levy is deductible to the taxpayer.
