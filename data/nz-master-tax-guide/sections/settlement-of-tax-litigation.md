# Settlement of tax litigation ¶4-232

As a result of comments made by the Court of Appeal in Auckland Gas Co Ltd v C of IR (1999) 19 NZTC 15,027 (CA), IR revised its approach to reaching settlements in tax litigation. Previously, IR considered that its care and management responsibilities contained in s 6A of the TAA severely impeded its ability to settle tax matters. In Auckland Gas, the Court of Appeal did not accept the Commissioner's "restricted view" of his ability to settle tax disputes.

The Court of Appeal also discussed the ability of the Commissioner to compromise in order to settle tax disputes in A-G v Steelfort Engineering Co Ltd (1999) 1 NZCC ¶55-005 (CA) (a Customs Act case). The Court noted that the Commissioner has broad powers to enter into compromises where that is consistent with his duty under ss 6 and 6A of the TAA. Accent Management Ltd v C of IR (No 2) (2007) 23 NZTC 21,366 (CA) also confirms the Commissioner's power to settle cases commercially. See ¶1-520 for further discussion concerning the Commissioner's powers.

The Commissioner's duty to collect the highest net revenue over time is contained in s 6A (discussed above). In the context of settling tax disputes based on the perceived likelihood of success (litigation risk), IR considers that it can identify whether a proposed settlement meets this criterion only once the exact basis of dispute is identified and the "legitimate factual differences" are clear.

In addition to the above limitations, IR will only settle cases in which litigation has commenced, a SOP has been filed by the taxpayer and a specialist panel within IR has approved the settlement.

In Bage Investments Ltd v C of IR (2003) 21 NZTC 18,294, the High Court upheld a settlement agreement made between the parties which depended on the final outcome of the Court of Appeal decision in C of IR v BNZ Investments Ltd (2001) 20 NZTC 17,103 (CA). The taxpayer and the Commissioner were engaged in a long-running tax dispute. The facts and circumstances involved in the dispute were similar to those in the BNZ case. Following the High Court decision in the BNZ case ((2000) 19 NZTC 15,732, in which judgment was given for the taxpayer), the parties in Bage agreed that the Commissioner would withdraw the objection against the taxpayer "in the event the BNZ case is upheld on final appeal". The Court of Appeal decision in the BNZ case confirmed the High Court decision. The Commissioner and the taxpayer in Bage agreed that, for the purposes of their agreement, the Court of Appeal decision "upheld" the High Court's decision. The Commissioner and the BNZ settled their dispute out of court. An appeal by the Commissioner to the Privy Council regarding the BNZ case was therefore abandoned. In these circumstances, Bage Investments Ltd called on the Commissioner to honour the settlement agreement. The Commissioner refused, arguing that the BNZ case had not been resolved by the courts but rather by private settlement; therefore, the BNZ case was not "upheld on final appeal". The Commissioner's argument was rejected as it neither accorded with the plain words of the agreement nor made commercial sense.

Interpretation statement IS 25/26, "The Commissioner's duty of care and management – section 6A of the Tax Administration Act 1994" confirms that the Commissioner can enter into:

## settlements where taxpayers dispute the interpretation of the law or facts on which their liability

has been assessed. See Accent Management Ltd v C of IR (2006) 22 NZTC 19,758 (HC); Accent Management Ltd v C of IR (No 2) (2007) 23 NZTC 21,366 (CA), and

## agreements as to the payment of outstanding tax, penalties and interest. See Raynel v C of IR (2004)

21 NZTC 18,583.

The Commissioner may consider the litigation risk and decide that the resources required to undertake litigation could be better used elsewhere to maximise the new revenue collected. In Accent Management Ltd v C of IR (No 2) (2007) 23 NZTC 21,366 (CA), William Young P held (at [15]): The change in policy is justified by recognition that the Commissioner has limited resources and the function of collecting "over time the highest net revenue that is practicable within the law".

Major tax litigation is expensive and places a heavy strain on the human resources available to the

Commissioner. The Commissioner must be permitted to make rational decisions as to how those resources can best be deployed. Further, "sensible litigation, including settlement, decisions" must necessarily allow for litigation risk.

The courts have recognised the Commissioner's power to settle and thus given effect to a key outcome intended to be achieved by enacting s 6A (IS 25/26 at [122]–[131]).

IR's policy on settling tax disputes before an assessment has been issued is discussed at ¶4-118.
