# Notice of assessment ¶4-040

A return of income filed by a taxpayer must contain notice of the self-assessment made by the taxpayer.

The finalisation of certain pre-populated accounts are deemed to be assessments (¶4-044).

As soon as it is convenient after an assessment has been made by the Commissioner, the Commissioner must give notice of the assessment to the taxpayer. The Commissioner need notify the taxpayer of only the amount of tax payable or the tax refund (as appropriate) if:

## the taxpayer's self-assessment is correct

## the assessment is a default assessment

## the assessment is a deemed assessment on the finalisation of the taxpayer's pre-populated account

## the assessment is made as a result of a taxpayer's failure to inform the Commissioner that details in

a pre-populated account are incorrect or incomplete, or

## the Commissioner issues a separate statement setting out the matters required to be specified in the

assessment.

Any failure on the part of the Commissioner to give to the taxpayer a notice of assessment does not invalidate that assessment (s 111 of the TAA).

The rules for giving notice are discussed in ¶1-645.

If the Commissioner makes an assessment in relation to GST payable for goods sold in satisfaction of debt, the Commissioner is required to send a copy of the assessment to the person whose goods were sold or the person selling the goods, whoever was not assessed.

The assessment process performed by the Commissioner is distinct from the requirement to give the taxpayer a notice of assessment. In C of IR v Hyslop (2000) 19 NZTC 15,560 (reversing Case T49 (1998) 18 NZTC 8,335), the High Court held that it is not a fundamental part of the assessment process that the objector be notified of the reasons for the assessment. The assessment process is complete before the obligation to give notice arises. Even if a notice of assessment should contain reasons, a failure to give proper reasons does not invalidate the assessment. This decision was affirmed on appeal in Hyslop v C of IR (2001) 20 NZTC 17,031 (CA). See also Dandelion Investments Ltd v C of IR (2003) 21 NZTC 18,010 (CA).

Assessments and determinations made by a computer or other electronic means as a result of information entered or held in the computer are treated as made by or under the properly delegated authority of the Commissioner (s 105 of the TAA).

*Refs: s 111, s 105*

## Validity of assessment

The validity of an assessment made by the Commissioner is not affected by any non-compliance with the TAA or any of the Inland Revenue Acts (listed in Sch 1 to the TAA).

An assessment made by the Commissioner is also not invalidated if it is made wholly or partially in compliance with (s 114):

## a direction or recommendation made by an authorised officer on matters relating to the assessment,

or

## an approved current policy or practice that is applicable to the assessment.

Law: TAA ss 33, 105, 111, 114
