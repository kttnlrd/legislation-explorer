# Commissioner not required to issue notice of proposed adjustment (NOPA) ¶4-145

In some limited circumstances, the Commissioner can make an assessment of a taxpayer's tax position without initiating the dispute resolution process (ie without issuing a NOPA). Those circumstances are:

## the assessment corresponds with a tax return that has been provided by the taxpayer (s 89C(a) of

the TAA)

## the taxpayer has provided a tax return that, in the Commissioner's opinion, contains a simple or

obvious mistake or oversight, and the assessment merely corrects the mistake or oversight (s 89C(b))

## the assessment corrects a tax position previously taken by the taxpayer in a way or manner agreed

by the Commissioner and the taxpayer (s 89C(c))

## the assessment reflects an agreement reached between the Commissioner and the taxpayer (s

89C(d))

## the assessment is made in relation to a matter for which the material facts and the relevant law are

identical to an assessment of the taxpayer for another period that is at the time the subject of court proceedings (s 89C(db))

## the Commissioner has reasonable grounds to believe a notice may cause the taxpayer or associate

to either (s 89C(e)(i), (ii)):

## leave New Zealand, or

## take steps to remove assets from New Zealand, thus making it harder for the Commissioner to collect

the tax

## the Commissioner has reasonable grounds to believe that the taxpayer has been involved in

fraudulent activity (s 89C(eb))

## the assessment corrects a tax position previously taken by the taxpayer which is, in the

Commissioner's opinion, the result of a vexatious or frivolous act or failure to act by the taxpayer (s 89C(f))

## the assessment is made as a result of a direction or determination by a court or the TRA (s 89C(g))

## the taxpayer has not provided a tax return as required by law (s 89C(h))

## the assessment is made following the failure by a taxpayer to make a deduction required to be made

by a tax law or to account for a deduction in the manner required by a tax law (s 89C(i))

## the taxpayer is entitled to issue a NOPA in respect of a tax return provided by the taxpayer and has

done so (s 89C(j))

## the assessment corrects a tax position taken by the taxpayer or an associate as a consequence or

result of an incorrect tax position taken by another taxpayer and, at the time the Commissioner makes an assessment, the Commissioner has made or is able to make an assessment to that other taxpayer for the correct amount of tax payable by that other taxpayer (s 89C(k))

## the assessment corrects a tax position taken by a taxpayer in relation to a tax position taken by an

LTC in a return of income by that company, and the disputes resolution process has been completed in respect of that return and that tax position (s 89C(ka))

## the assessment results from an amendment made to a qualifying individual's final account under s

22G(6) (s 89C(l))

## the Commissioner is making an amendment before finalising a qualifying individual's account under s

22H (s 89C(lbaa))

## the assessment is of a penalty under ss 139AAB, 139AB, 139ABB (from 1 January 2025), 139AC,

142H or 142I, 142J or 142K (s 89C(lba)),

## the assessment extinguishes all or part of the taxpayer's tax loss in accordance with the

Commissioner's power to write-off tax (s 89C(lb)),

## the assessment extinguishes all or part of the taxpayer's excess deductions under the residential

rental ring-fencing rules (¶7-365) in accordance with s 177C(5BA) (s 89C(lbb)), or

## the assessment includes a calculation, by the Commissioner, of a WFF tax credit (s 89C(m)).

Action on receipt of notice of proposed adjustment (NOPA)

The recipient of a NOPA has 3 possible courses of action. The recipient may:

## accept the proposed adjustments (¶4-155)

## reject the adjustments (¶4-160), or

## do nothing (¶4-155).
