# Tax treatment of gifts received by volunteer workers ¶5-189

IR's policy on the tax treatment of gifts received by volunteer workers in New Zealand is set out in interpretation statement, IS 23/11 "Income tax: Income – when gifts are assessable income".

Generally, gifts will be income when they are a product of the person's activities. A gift will not be income if it is a personal gift made purely as a mark of affection, esteem or respect (G v C of IR [1961] NZLR 994).

Although there are no firm rules as to whether a particular gift is income, gifts to a volunteer worker will be included in income if they are a relevant product of the volunteer's activities. In IS 23/11 at [145] – [159] the Commissioner states the following factors support a conclusion that a gift is income under s CO 1:

## the amount of the gift reflects the amount of the recipient's personal exertion in undertaking the

voluntary activity

## the payer makes the gift in the hope that the recipient will undertake future activities or to encourage

them to make further efforts

## the gift has recurred or has a foreseeable element of recurrence rather than being a one-off payment

## these sorts of gifts are expected, requested or are commonplace as a matter of practice by

volunteers in the area of activity undertaken.

For commentary on voluntary activities and honoraria, see ¶3-455.

However, each case must be judged on its own merits. In the statement, IS 23/11, at [159], the following factors are considered to support the conclusion that a gift is not assessable income:

## the receipt is expressed in terms of being a personal gift inspired by personal goodwill rather than as

consideration for the activities of the volunteer.

## the recipient is not undertaking the voluntary activity at the time the gift is made.

*Refs: s CO 1*
