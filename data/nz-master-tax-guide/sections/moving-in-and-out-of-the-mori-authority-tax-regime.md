# Moving in and out of the M#ori authority tax regime ¶24-331

A person who has elected to become a M#ori authority may cancel that election by giving notice to the Commissioner. If eligible, that person may elect to re-enter the M#ori authority tax regime. However, to ensure that M#ori authorities do not move in and out of the rules to obtain tax advantages, a M#ori authority re-entering the M#ori authority tax rules is treated as if it had disposed of its property for market value immediately before re-entering the regime and then re-acquired it on the same date for the same value. For depreciation purposes, however, the cost of the property is limited to the lower of the market value of the property on the date of acquisition and the original cost of the property.

Other transitional rules apply to ensure that no additional tax consequences arise for entities moving in and out of the regime. Companies entering the M#ori authority tax rules lose their imputation credits (the rules relating to a company ceasing to be an ICA company apply) and any retained earnings, accumulated profits and capital reserves may be distributed to members tax-free. A company exiting the M#ori authority rules may transfer a credit balance in its M#ori authority credit account to its ICA. Income derived by the company in or before the 2003–04 tax year is converted to available subscribed capital, the subsequent distribution of which is exempt from tax. Income derived after the 2003–04 tax year is treated as being from a taxable source to which imputation credits may be attached.

For a trust entering the rules, trustee income derived before the trust became a M#ori authority may be distributed to members tax-free. For a trust exiting the rules, taxable income derived by the M#ori authority is treated as trustee income, the subsequent distribution of which is exempt from tax.

Filing and notification requirements for M#ori authorities

M#ori authorities must file returns of income (IR8), unless they have an exemption. The authorities are also required to provide members with information about distributions they receive or retirement scheme contributions made for their benefit so that those members can comply with their tax obligations. M#ori authorities are also required to provide IR with a M#ori authority credit account return (IR8J), and, if appropriate, an annual reconciliation statement for retirement scheme contribution tax. No credit of tax is allowed or refund paid to members unless the Commissioner has received a distribution statement relating to the credit.

A M#ori authority that is a member of a consolidated imputation group is exempt from filing a credit account return if the account balance is nil at all times during the relevant tax year (s 69B(3)).

The record-keeping requirements in s 22(2) of the TAA are extended to include records relating to M#ori authority credit accounts and retirement scheme contributions.

Before 1 April 2020, M#ori authorities had to provide IR with a distribution statement containing details of all distributions made for the year. From 1 April 2020, this information is required to be provided under the new investment income information provisions. The required information is set out in s 25I and Sch 6 and must be provided by the 20th of the month following the month in which the distribution was paid to the member. All investment income payers must file their information electronically, unless they receive an exemption from the Commissioner (¶15-215).
