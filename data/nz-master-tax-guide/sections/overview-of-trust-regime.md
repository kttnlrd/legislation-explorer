# Overview of trust regime ¶25-025

## Distributions from trusts

At the time any distribution is made, a trust is classified (for tax purposes only) as either a complying trust, a foreign trust or a non-complying trust. This classification determines the tax treatment of any distribution other than a distribution of beneficiary income, and the applicable tax rate (¶25-300).

Beneficiaries of foreign trusts and non-complying trusts may also be taxed on distributions of accumulated income and, in some cases, capital gains. These kinds of distributions are known as "taxable distributions" (¶25-360).

## Trustee income and beneficiary income

Regardless of trust classification, income derived by a trustee in an income year is taxed as either:

## "trustee income" at the flat rate of 39%, unless an exception applies in which case the trustee tax rate

remains at 33% (other than for legacy superannuation funds, which are taxed at 28%) (¶25-080), or

## "beneficiary income" at the beneficiary's marginal rate of tax.

The taxation of trustee and beneficiary income is discussed at ¶25-100 and ¶25-200 respectively.

## Special rules that apply to trusts

In addition to the specific regime that applies to trustee and beneficiary income and other distributions from trusts, there are other specific rules throughout the IT07 that have specific application to trusts.

## Modifications to continuity of shareholding rules

A company cannot carry forward imputation credits unless a continuity of shareholding test is met. However, these rules are modified in relation to trustees so a change of trustee will not result in a loss of continuity (ss OA 8, YC 9 and ¶16-190).

## Rules that apply to qualifying companies are modified for trustee shareholders

If a trustee is a shareholder in a qualifying company, all dividends must ordinarily be paid out to natural person beneficiaries. Where a trustee is a shareholder in a qualifying company, there are limitations as to who can benefit from the trust (s HA 7 and ¶19-010). These limitations do not apply in the same way to look- through companies.

## Anti-streaming rule applies to imputation credits distributed to beneficiaries

Imputation credits distributed to beneficiaries are distributed in proportion to total distributions to prevent streaming imputation credits to the beneficiaries who are most able to use the credit (¶25-260).

## Rules of association

Associated person provisions apply to associate persons, companies, trusts and partnerships. The effect of the rules of association is to alter the incidence of tax due to certain relationships. Under these rules:

## the trustee of a trust is associated with the settlor and any person with the power to appoint or

remove trustees (s YB 11)

Note that there is no association between the settlor and an appointor who is a professional trustee.

For the exemption for professional trustees to apply the appointor must be a provider of professional services who has not and cannot benefit from the trust, and who is subject to a professional code of conduct, and related disciplinary process, of an "approved organisation" as defined by the Tax Administration Act 1994 (TAA) (eg a member of Chartered Accountants Australia and New Zealand, the New Zealand Law Society or the Society of Trust and Estate Practitioners (STEP)). See Tax Information Bulletin ¶259-103 Vol 25, No 9, October 2013 at 40 and Tax Information Bulletin ¶267-121 Vol 26, No 7, August 2014 at 128.

## two trusts are associated if they have a common settlor

## a settlor is associated with a beneficiary

## a trustee is associated with a beneficiary

## a person is associated with a relative's trustee, and

## a trustee can be associated with a company.

In addition, a tripartite test provides that any two persons associated with the same third person are also associated (s YB 1(3)(k) and ¶16-245).

## Transfers of property from trusts

Rules in subpart FC provide for the income tax treatment of transfers of property including transfers following a person's death and distributions from trusts. Among other things, the rules provide a disposal value for trustees that make distributions in kind and a cost price for beneficiaries (or trustees) receiving property in kind (¶25-660).

## Change of trustees and disposal for land sales rules

Certain disposals of land are subject to tax (see the Land transactions chapter). A change of trustees of a trust and the consequential transfer of the land to new trustees does not amount to a disposal of land under trust law. The Commissioner's view contained in interpretation statement IS 22/03, "Income Tax – application of the land sales rules to co-ownership changes and changes of trustees" aligns with general trust law.

## Trustee capacity

Section YA 5 of the IT07 provides a general rule that when a person is acting in the capacity of trustee of a trust, they are treated, for income tax purposes, as acting in that capacity and not in their personal, body corporate, or other capacities.

Before the introduction of s YA 5 on 29 March 2018, there was uncertainty surrounding the application of the voting interest test for corporate trustees as a result of 2 High Court decisions, Concepts 124 Ltd v C of IR (2014) 26 NZTC ¶21-100 and Staithes Drive Development Ltd v C of IR (2015) 27 NZTC ¶22-028. These decisions changed how the voting interest test, which is used to measure the ownership of companies, including their association, was applied to corporate trustees. In both cases, the High Court held that the voting interests in the relevant companies were held by the legal owner of shares and effectively ignored the capacity in which those shares were held. This meant that the voting rights attached to shares owned by a corporate trustee were attributed to that trustee's natural person shareholders in their personal capacity.

Amendments to the definitions of "company" and "natural person", clarify that:

## any reference to "company" in the IT07 does not include a corporate trustee (subject to any identified

exceptions), and

## any reference to "natural person" in the IT07 does not include a natural person trustee (subject to any

identified exceptions).

Section YA 5 ensures that if a person is the trustee of more than one trust, the person is acting in a different capacity for each trust. In particular, a corporate trustee is not looked-through, including for the purpose of association. The exceptions to this general rule include the following:

## The definition of "close company" includes trustees (natural person or corporate) as many close

companies are owned in family trust structures.

## Section HD 15 applies to a company acting in its capacity as trustee. This gives the Commissioner

power to recover income tax from the directors and shareholders of a company where an arrangement or transaction depletes the company's assets so that it is unable to satisfy its tax liabilities. See ¶16-880.

## The residence rules in subpart YD clarify that the natural person residence test in s YD 1, and the

company residence test in s YD 2, continue to apply to a person or a company acting as a trustee.

## Interpretation statement "Taxation of Trusts"

Interpretation statement IS 24/01, "Taxation of trusts" is a general guide as to how income derived by trustees and beneficiaries of a trust is taxed. IS 24/01 also overviews the various compliance obligations imposed on settlors, trustees and beneficiaries under tax law. The statement does not consider the foreign tax credit regime as it applies to trusts, the application of double tax agreements to trusts, or the application of s BG 1, as these are beyond the scope of a general guide. IS 24/01 replaces interpretation statement IS 18/01.
