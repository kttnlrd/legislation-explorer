# Fixing the tax rates ¶1-040

Income tax rates are fixed annually. This is because of the constitutional convention that income tax should be imposed only on a yearly basis and that the Crown's ability to collect revenue be subject to Parliament's consent. The rates are fixed by an Act referred to as the annual taxing Act. In practice, such an Act simply adopts the tax rates set out in Sch 1 to the IT07. To remove the necessity of passing a separate Act each year for the sole purpose of fixing the tax rates, the rates may be set as part of a wider amending Act.

Taxation (Annual Rates for 2024–25, Multinational Tax, and Remedial Matters) Act 2025 set the rates for the 2024–25 year. Confirmation of the annual income tax rates for the 2025–26 year are set in the Taxation (Annual Rates for 2025–26, Emergency Response, and Remedial Measures) Bill. If an annual taxing Act is not passed, there is a savings provision in s 92A of the TAA which enables the assessment for a tax year at the basic rates set out in Sch 1 to the IT07. Such an assessment is not invalidated by the fact that it may have been made before the passing of an Act setting rates for the year concerned. Even without that provision, revenue would continue to flow to the Crown if Parliament did not sit or did not pass an annual taxing Act. This is because the pay as you earn (PAYE) tax deduction system under the principal Act would continue to operate.
