# Changes to co-ownership, subdivisions, and changes of trustees ¶7-300

Land may be held jointly in a number of different ways, for example tenants in common, joint tenancy, in partnership or in a capacity as trustee. Some changes to co-ownership may be a disposal for the purposes of the land taxing provisions. A good understanding of the legal relationship underlying the co-ownership is important when determining if a change in the co-ownership will be deemed to be a disposal for tax purposes. This is because different forms of co-ownership are, in law, different "interests in land".

Land can be owned by more than one person or entity. When this is the case, they are referred to as co- owners. Joint tenancy and tenants in common are common examples of co-ownership. Co-owners of land are joint tenants if there is no indication in the document creating their co-ownership that they are to own separate or distinct shares. When a joint owner dies, their interest passes to the survivor(s). In contrast, tenants in common is where each person has a distinct, undivided share in the land (either in equal or unequal shares). If a tenant in common dies, their share of the land is dealt with under the terms of their will, or in accordance with intestacy rules if there is no will.

Interpretation statement IS 22/03 "Income tax – application of the land sale rules to changes to co- ownership, subdivisions, and changes of trustees" clarifies the Commissioner's position on whether, and if so to what extent, the land sale rules in ss CB 6A to CB 15 and CZ 39 apply to changes to co-ownership, subdivisions of land, and changes of trustees.

The statement concludes that the ordinary meaning, case law and legislative history and context indicate that "disposal" in the land sale rules:

## requires complete alienation of the land by the disposer – the land must be "got rid of" by the person

## requires dealing with the land – so that one person loses ownership of the land and another gains it

(or gains a corresponding interest in respect of the same underlying land).

In the Commissioner's view, "disposal" in the land sale rules does not include:

## transfers to self (in the same capacity), or

## extinguishment of an estate or interest in land to the extent that does not result in some other person

acquiring an interest that corresponds (in whole or in part) to the extinguished interest.

In terms of the transactions considered in this statement, this means that:

## A change to the form of co-ownership, where the proportional shares or notional shares do not

change, will not be a "disposal" for the purposes of the land sale rules.

## If there is a transfer between co-owners where neither's interest is fully alienated but the proportional

share or notional share of a co-owner is reduced, there would be a "disposal" for the purposes of the land sale rules by that person to the extent their interest is reduced. This is because while they have not fully alienated the whole estate or interest they had in the land, they have fully alienated part of their interest in land.

## If there is a transfer that adds a new co-owner, there would be a "disposal" for the purposes of the

land sale rules to the extent the share (or notional share) of the original owner(s) in the land is reduced.

## If there is a transfer that removes a co-owner, there would be a "disposal" by the departing co-owner

of their share (or notional share) in the land.

## A transfer of land on a change of trustees of a trust will not be a disposal for the purposes of the land

sale rules. The IT07 treats all of the trustees of a trust as essentially a single person, and "disposal" in the land sale rules does not include transfers to self (in the same capacity).

See also Table 1 in IS 22/03 at paragraph 4 which summarises the conclusions discussed above.

*Refs: ss CB 6A*

In conclusion, if there is a "disposal", and it is for less than market value consideration, the IT07 may deem the person who disposed of the land to have derived an amount equal to the market value of the land at the time of the disposal.

There are specific provisions to ensure the registration of a transfer to change the proportionality of co- ownership will only result in the bright-line clock restarting to the extent a particular party's interest has increased. To the extent they already owned the land, the bright-line clock does not reset. See ss CB 6A(5D) and CZ 39(5D).

IS 22/03 contains a number of examples to illustrate the tax treatment of the above changes in co- ownership.

For transfers of residential land and application of the bright-line test, rollover relief may be available to certain transfers. See ¶7-540.

*Refs: ss CB 6A(5D)*

## Exempt income – certain subdivisions of land

Land is sometimes purchased by 2 or more persons together as co-owners to pool resources. It is not uncommon for the co-owners to then subdivide the land and allocate the subdivided parcels to each of the co-owners based on their ownership interests in the original parcel. This is known as partitioning. Under s GC 1 the tax law deems the partitioning to occur at market value and this may give rise to a tax liability where the value of the land has increased, even where there is no effective change in economic ownership, or where the eventual sale is not subject to tax (eg exceptions to the bright-line test because the property is the person's main home, or they have held the residential land for longer than the brightline period and no other land taxing provision applies).

For persons who hold land on revenue account, who are taxed on the eventual disposal of the land, the tax law prior to the introduction of s CW 3C meant there were 2 taxing points. The first was at the time of the partition or allocation, and then on final sale. While s GC 1 provides an uplift in the cost base ensuring no double taxation of the same income, taxpayers may have faced additional compliance costs and timing issues if the partition and eventual sale occur in different income years.

Section CW 3C ensures that the allocation of subdivided land among the co-owners of the original undivided land does not constitute a disposal for the land sales provisions. It applies to taxpayers who hold the land on capital account and on revenue account. This provision applies when a co-owner's proportion of the value of the land they receive on the partition or subdivision is no less than 95% of their contribution to the cost of the land. The result is that the amount derived is exempt income in proportion to the formula contained in s CW 3C(2).

For the purposes of the threshold of 95%, the cost of the land includes costs to subdivide, develop, and build on the land. When looking at the land ownership of the co-owners, the provision includes land held alone, jointly or in common with another person (s CW 3C(1)). Co-ownership is also defined broadly to include various capacities in co-owning entities like companies, trusts and look-through companies.

In 2025 s CW 3C was amended retrospectively to:

## include express references in s CW 3C(1) to the definitions of a person's end value proportion and

their acquisition proportion (as before, there is an exemption if the person's end value proportion is not less than 95% of their acquisition proportion), and

## provide that the bright-line period does not restart when land is allocated to each co-owner as part of

a partition or subdivision.

See Tax Information Bulletin ¶375-102 Vol 37, No 5, June 2025 at 127 for detailed commentary on the 2025 amendments and examples. Also see a number of examples in Inland Revenue, "Commentary on the Taxation (Annual Rates for 2022–23, Platform Economy, and Remedial Matters) Bill (No 2)" demonstrating the application of the rules before the retrospective amendments in 2025.

## Exclusion for land acquired from a co-owner on a partition of

subdivision An unintended consequence of land partitioning is that a co-owner may be subject to tax if the land disposed of was acquired from an associated entity set up to carry out the partition and this is because ss CB 10(2) and CB 15(1) impose tax on land acquired from an associated person in certain circumstances. To remedy this, s CB 15E applies to exempt a person's income on disposal of the land if the person had exempt income under s CW 3C and a full exemption is available if the person's end value proportion under s CW 3C is no more than 105% of their acquisition proportion under s CW 3C. Section CB 15E applies from 27 March 2021, the date on which s CW 3C was introduced.

This exclusion under CB 15E applies to a person for a disposal of land acquired from a co-owner on a partition or subdivision if the person:

## derives income from the disposal under s CB 10(2) or CB 15(1), and

## the person was not associated with a land developer when they originally acquired the land (ie before

it was partitioned or subdivided).

If the person's ownership proportion changed by more than the safe harbour amount (of 5%) on the subdivision or partition such that s CW 3C(2) applied to the land, then the income the person derives under s CB 10(2) or CB 15(1) from subsequently disposing of the land is exempt income to the extent calculated by the formula under s CB 15E(3): amount derived × (acquisition proportion ÷ end value proportion) Section CB 15E(3) was retrospectively amended (effective on 27 March 2021) to ensure it applied as intended by permitting an exemption instead of a reduction in a person's income (Tax Information Bulletin ¶375-102 Vol 37, No 5, June 2025 at 135). Also see examples from Tax Information Bulletin Vol 36, No 4, May 2024 at 95 demonstrating the application of this exclusion.
