# customisation costs ¶10-887

SaaS is a cloud-based software licensing and delivery model giving the customer a right to access the provider's software that runs on the provider's cloud infrastructure. Examples of SaaS applications are payroll processing, customer relationship management (CRM), enterprise resource planning (ERP), human resources management, content management and database management systems.

Configuration and customisation costs (C&C costs) incurred with SaaS arrangements are typically incurred by a customer to:

## assist integrating the application with their existing system, or

## add extra functionality or features not included in the application.

Interpretation guideline IG 23/01 "Deductibility of software as a service (SaaS) configuration and customisation costs" concludes that depending on the circumstances, C&C costs may be deductible:

## under the general permission under s DA 1 (¶10-021)

## as development expenditure under s DB 34 (¶10-021), or

## as depreciable intangible property (¶13-440).

The guideline covers the following main points:

## The income tax treatment of SaaS C&C costs is complex due to the many variations in contractual

arrangements and the nature of the services provided. Therefore, the tax outcomes depend on the specific facts of each situation.

## While it is generally the case that there is a nexus between incurring C&C costs while carrying on

a business or deriving income, the capital limitation usually operates to deny a deduction. This is because taxpayers incur C&C costs to transform or enhance their business structure and therefore receive an enduring benefit.

## However, in cases in which configuration and customisation activities occur continuously during

the term of the SaaS arrangement and the taxpayer incurs regular costs to cover these, the recurrent nature of the payments suggests they are revenue in nature.

## In some cases, the configuration or customisation activities may be in-house "development" (as

defined in s DB 35) activities, in which case some IFRS reporting taxpayers may be able to claim an immediate deduction for C&C costs under s DB 34. This provision allows a taxpayer to make a deduction for "internally generated" research or development costs recognised as an expense for financial reporting purposes under paragraph 68(a) of NZ IAS 38.

## A deprecation deduction may be available to the extent that C&C costs are not deductible under s

DA 1 or DB 34. The right to use software under a SaaS arrangement is depreciable intangible property.

The terms of each SaaS agreement will determine whether the taxpayer should depreciate this right under the depreciable property (s EE 62) or the "fixed-life intangible property" rules (s EE 67).

*Refs: s DA 1, s DB 34, s DB 35, s EE 62, s EE 67*
