# Overtime and work-related meal payments ¶3-448

## Overtime meal payments

Amounts paid to an employee, or on behalf of an employee, for a meal when the employee is working overtime is exempt income to that employee. This applies irrespective of whether the payment is made directly to the employee or on the employee's behalf.

For overtime meal allowances to be tax-exempt, all of the following criteria must be met:

## either the employee's employment contract specifies that they are eligible for a payment for overtime

hours worked, or an employer has a policy or practice of paying an overtime meal allowance, and

## the allowance reflects the actual expenditure incurred by the employee, or is a reasonable estimate of

the expected costs likely to be incurred by the employee or group of employees, and

## the employee has worked at least 2 hours overtime on the day of the meal.

The meaning of overtime is defined as the time worked for the employer on the day beyond the person's ordinary hours set out in their employment contract.

## Work-related meals

There are 2 exemptions from income tax in relation to "work-related meals".

The first exemption is for a period of up to 3 months and covers meal payments made to employees when the employee is required to work away from their employment base. It also covers food and drink consumed when the employee's duties require them to travel.

The 3-month time period runs from the date the employee starts working away from their normal workplace and extends for as long as the employee works continuously at that location. If an employee does not have a fixed workplace, the 3-month time period commences on the date they arrive at their accommodation base.

Example: George is a retail assistant for a large company that has branches in Auckland and Wellington. As part of his employment duties, he is required to travel to the Wellington branch for a period of 4 months due to a shortage of staff. The company reimburses George for meal expenditure that he incurs while in Wellington. The meal reimbursement payments will be tax free to George for the first 3 months and taxable for the last month.

Example: Ronald is an electrician who is sent by his Auckland employer to Taupo to complete a number of jobs in Taupo and the neighbouring towns. The work will take 4 months. Ronald rents accommodation in Taupo but also travels to Rotorua and Tokoroa to work at various sites. The 3-month period for which the exemption applies will commence on the date that Ronald arrives in Taupo at his rented accommodation.

The 3-month period includes the employee's time away from the location on leave or other breaks for personal reasons, weekend breaks, required rest periods and other similar periods. The period of exemption can be extended if exceptional circumstances arise beyond the control of the employer and employee and require the employee to continue to work away from their normal workplace. However, the exemption only continues to apply for the period for which the employee is unable to return to their normal workplace because of the exceptional circumstances.

The second exemption covers payments for working meals and light refreshments when the employee is required to be working away from their normal employment base for most of the day. This includes payments

to cover meals consumed at conferences or training courses situated close to the employee's normal work location. It does not include light refreshments if the employer would not normally provide such refreshments to the employee.

Example: Melissa is a beauty therapist who works for a busy salon. Her employer sends her on a 2-day course which will cover the latest developments on anti-aging treatments. Melissa pays for her meals while away on the course and is later reimbursed by her employer. The reimbursements will not be taxable to Melissa.

Example: Mark works in an office where tea and coffee are provided for employees while they are working. Mark is required to attend a local expo, where he staffs a stand promoting the firm's products. During his attendance at the expo, it is not practical for Mark's employer to provide him with tea or coffee. A payment made by Mark's employer to cover tea or coffee in these circumstances will not be taxable to Mark.

In both circumstances, when the exemption applies, the full amount of any meal payment is exempt. The exemption includes reimbursement payments and meal allowances. Neither exemption applies if the reimbursement is a substitute for salary or wages.
