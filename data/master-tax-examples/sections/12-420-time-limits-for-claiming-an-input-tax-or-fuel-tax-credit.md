# 12-420 - Time limits for claiming an input tax or fuel tax credit

tax credit Worked example: Time limits for claiming an input tax or fuel tax credit Issue Roger Lemon carries on a house cleaning business as a sole trader. He is registered for GST and lodges quarterly BASs. On 10 October 2020, he purchases a new carpet steam cleaner for $3,000, which he uses in his cleaning business.

Because of the stress of the Christmas period, Roger takes a longer than normal holiday which lasts throughout January and February 2021 and therefore he forgets to lodge his BAS for the December 2020 quarterly tax period. He is subsequently assessed by the ATO but forgets to advise them about the $300 input tax credit (ITC) to which he is entitled for the carpet steam cleaner, with the result that the ITC is not taken into account in his assessment for the December quarter.

In February 2025, Roger finally identifies that he did not claim ITCs to which he was entitled. On 27 February 2025, he applies to the ATO for it to amend his assessment to include the ITC.

Will the amendment request be accepted? If not, does Roger have any alternative means to force the ATO to take the missing ITC into account? Would the situation be any different if he had lodged the December 2020 quarter BAS himself, on time, but still forgot to claim the ITC for the cleaner?

Solution Section 93-5(1) of the A New Tax System (Goods and Services Tax) Act 1999 (GST Act) and s 47-5(1) of the Fuel Tax Act 2006 set out rules:

- explaining when ITCs and fuel tax credits (FTCs) can be attributed to a tax period, and

- limiting the time in which the credits can be claimed.

The limiting provisions state that an entity loses its entitlement to a tax credit if it is not included in an assessment within 4 years from the day that the entity was required to lodge its return for the first tax period to which the credits would have been attributable.

Miscellaneous Taxation Ruling MT 2024/1 sets out the Commissioner's views on the application of the 4- year time limit, including:

- when, and the extent to which, a tax credit has been "taken into account" in an assessment

- when the 4-year entitlement period ends

- exceptions to the limiting provisions

- when an objection to an assessment may preserve a taxpayer's entitlement to a tax credit

- the interaction with private ruling and amendment requests.

The Ruling makes it clear that the 4-year entitlement period is the period of 4 years commencing after the due date for the BAS lodgment for the quarter to which the tax credit would be attributable.

If the credit claim is not expressly included as a line item or "integer" in the BAS workings leading to an assessment (eg on lodgment), then it has not been "taken into account".

The 4-year period is only altered if, prior to the end of the period, the Commissioner formally grants an extension to the due date for the relevant return. There is no statutory discretion that may be exercised to extend or waive the 4-year entitlement period. tax credit A taxpayer's entitlement to tax credits ceases immediately on the expiry of the 4-year period. If the taxpayer subsequently realises (after the 4-year period has ended) that a tax credit was due for a particular tax period and the 4-year period has expired, there is nothing the taxpayer can do.

A tax credit is not taken into account in an assessment by lodging an objection, requesting an amendment (including in the course of making a voluntary disclosure), or applying for a private ruling.

However, the limiting provisions do not apply to the extent that an entitlement to a tax credit for a tax period is specified in the grounds of a valid objection lodged within the 4-year entitlement period. To the extent that the objection decision or any subsequent review or appeal process finds that the taxpayer was entitled to the tax credit and it is attributable to the period in dispute, the taxpayer will still be entitled to the tax credit.

This means that, in effect, a valid objection made before the end of the 4-year entitlement period can preserve a taxpayer's entitlement to tax credits that are the subject of the objection for the period in dispute, even if they have not been taken into account in an assessment.

Accordingly, dealing with the facts in the question, Roger's amendment request, despite being made within the 4-year period, does not result in the ITC being taken into account. As the original assessment was prepared by the ATO, and did not take into account the input credit, it is not possible to amend the assessment merely by requesting an amendment to the assessment (see para 69 of MT 2024/1). An in-time amendment request does not form part of the calculation that produced the assessed amount and it will not in itself result in any amount being taken into account.

Roger's entitlement to the ITC will cease unless, on or before 28 February 2025, either: (a) he objects to his assessment for the December 2020 tax period on the ground that he is entitled to the ITC in relation to the tax period, or (b) the ITC is taken into account in an assessment.

Option (b) is closed; however, he can still (if he hurries) take advantage of option (a).

Therefore, he could, on 28 February 2025, object to his assessment for the December 2020 quarterly tax period on the grounds that it did not include the ITC for the steam cleaner. A valid and correct objection made before the 4-year entitlement period ends will preserve his entitlement to tax credits that are the subject of the objection for the period in dispute, even if they have not been taken into account in an assessment.

Therefore, given that the steam cleaner was a creditable acquisition and that the ITC would be attributable to the December 2020 tax period, as Roger made a valid objection within the 4-year entitlement period, the limiting provisions do not apply to the $300 tax credit. The Commissioner should allow the objection.

If the objection was made after 28 February 2025 (ie from 1 March 2025 onwards) it would be outside the 4- year limit and it would be rejected, with the effect that Roger would be permanently unable to claim the ITC.

In the alternative scenario, where the BAS was lodged by Roger on time, but the steam cleaner was omitted, he would be entitled to claim the tax credit before 28 February 2025 (the expiry of the 4-year time limit). He would not be entitled to claim the ITC after this date (see Example 1 in MT 2024/1).

Note that the entitlement period commences from the due date of the GST return, not the lodgment date/ date of assessment. This is important where returns have been lodged late and sets up a different 4-year period to that in which objections/requests for amended assessments can be made.

The same 4-year time limit also applies to FTCs (see ¶12-300).
