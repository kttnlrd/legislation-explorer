# 1-380 - Employee share scheme

Worked example: Employee share scheme Issue Luxury Vehicles Limited (LVL) sells luxury motor vehicles through its Australia-wide dealership network. In January 2023, the company instituted an employee share acquisition scheme in which all employees were given the opportunity to acquire shares in the company at a discount of 10% on the market value of the company's shares. Stan Moore, one of the company's car salesmen, accepted the offer on 15 January 2023 and acquired 5,000 shares when the share price was $4 per share.

In September 2024, LVL introduced another employee share acquisition scheme offering shares in the company at a 20% discount but restricted to directors and senior executives of the company and subject to forfeiture provisions. Employees acquiring the shares were not permitted to dispose of the shares within 5 years of acquisition. Stephanie Wu, the company's finance director, accepted the offer and acquired 500 shares on 1 October 2024 when the share price was $5 per share.

Advise Stan Moore and Stephanie Wu on the tax implications of their share acquisitions and the tax implications of any future disposal of their shares.

Solution Since 1 July 2009, the taxation of employee share scheme (ESS) interests has been governed by ITAA97 Div 83A which applies where an employee (which includes a director, an officer and an associate of an employee) acquires an ESS interest in an ESS at a discount.

An ESS is defined as a scheme under which ESS interests in a company are provided to employees (s 83A-10(2)). An ESS interest is defined as a beneficial interest in a share in a company (s 83A-10(1)).

There are 2 alternative taxing rules for ESSs that might be offered at a discount by employers to employees:

- a scheme where the discount is taxed up front, and

- a scheme where taxation of the discount is deferred.

Where an employee is assessable on a discount received on an ESS interest upfront, a $1,000 exemption applies, provided the conditions in ss 83A-35 and 83A-45 are satisfied, including:

- the taxpayer's income is $180,000 or less

- the scheme is offered on a non-discriminatory basis

- the shares are not at real risk of forfeiture, and

- the shares are held for 3 years or until the employee ceases employment, subject to a minimum

  holding period.

The deferred taxing provisions apply where the ESS interests are subject to a real risk of forfeiture. In that case, the tax on the ESS interest is deferred until the earliest taxing point occurs, according to the rules in s 83A-115.

Because the ESS rules in Div 83A are intended to be the primary taxing regime for ESS interests, the discounts are exempt from being taxed as a fringe benefit (s 83A-5; FBTAA s 136(1)) or under the CGT provisions (ITAA97 s 130-75). However, once the shares are disposed of any gain or loss may attract the CGT provisions.

Stan Moore: acquisition of shares in the ESS Stan acquired 5,000 shares at a discount of 10% on the market value of $4 per share. The discount of $0.40 on 5,000 shares amounts to $2,000. This may be included in Stan's assessable income for 2022–23 (s 83A-25(1)) but he will be entitled to an exemption of $1,000 if the conditions in ss 83A-35 and 83A-45 are satisfied. In that case, $1,000 of the $2,000 discount will be included in his assessable income in 2022–23 and the $1,000 balance will be exempt. The conditions for the $1,000 exemption include:

- the sum of the taxpayer's taxable income (this includes the discount other than the exempt amount

  of the discount), reportable fringe benefits total, reportable superannuation contributions and total

  investment loss for the income year is $180,000 or less

- the scheme is offered in a non-discriminatory way to at least 75% of Australian resident permanent

  employees of the company with 3 or more years of service

- the shares are not at risk of forfeiture, and

- the shares are required to be held by the employee for 3 years (or until such earlier time as the

  Commissioner allows) or until the employee ceases employment.

Stan Moore: disposal of shares in the ESS If Stan disposed of the shares within 3 years of acquisition, he would not qualify for the $1,000 exemption on acquisition. At whatever time Stan disposes of his shares, he will be subject to CGT on the difference between the sale proceeds and the market value of the shares at the date of acquisition. If he holds the shares for more than 12 months, he will be entitled to the 50% CGT discount. Only 50% of the capital gain would be added to his assessable income (ITAA97 ss 115-5 and 115-25).

If Stan disposes of the shares after 15 January 2025 at the market value of $7 per share, the capital gain addition to his assessable income in 2024–25 would be $7,500 calculated as follows: Capital proceeds                                                                 5,000 shares at $7 each = $35,000 Less cost base                                                                   5,000 shares at $4 each = $20,000 Capital gain                                                                     $35,000 − $20,000 = $15,000 Applying the 50% CGT discount                                                    50% × $15,000 = $7,500 Addition to assessable income                                                    $7,500 Stephanie Wu: acquisition and disposal of shares in the ESS As there is a restriction on the right to dispose of the shares within 5 years of acquisition, there is a real risk of forfeiture in that Stephanie may forfeit or lose her interest in the shares (other than by disposing of them).

The deferred taxation provisions will therefore apply to Stephanie (Subdiv 83A-C) and she will not be entitled to the $1,000 exemption on her acquisition of the shares.

The taxation on the acquisition of the shares by Stephanie is deferred until the taxing point occurs. The earliest time that this taxing point could occur is (s 83A-120):

- when there is no real risk that Stephanie will forfeit or lose the shares (ie 5 years after acquisition of

  the shares), or

- 15 years after Stephanie acquires the shares.

The taxing point for Stephanie will be 1 October 2029, which is 5 years after acquisition of the shares, when the forfeiture condition will be lifted. Assume the value of the shares at that date is $12. Further, assume Stephanie disposes of the shares on 1 November 2030 at a price of $13 per share.

Under the deferred taxation provision (ITAA97 s 83A-110(1)), the amount to be added to her assessable income in the 2029–30 year would be the market value of $12 per share, reduced by the cost base of the ESS interest, that is, $4 per share on 500 shares. This amount would be calculated as follows: Proceeds at taxing point (1/10/29)                                               $12 per share × 500 shares = $6,000 Cost at time of acquisition (1/10/24)                                            $4 per share (ie $5 per share × 20% discount) × 500 shares = $2,000 Addition to assessable income in 2029–30                                           $6,000 − $2,000 = $4,000 A capital gain will arise in the year 2030–31 as follows: Capital proceeds                                                                 $13 per share x 500 shares = $6,500 Cost base at date of disposal                                                    $12 per share x 500 shares = $6,000 Gross capital gain                                                               $500 Application of 50% discount                                                      $500 x 50% = $250 Net capital gain                                                                 $250
