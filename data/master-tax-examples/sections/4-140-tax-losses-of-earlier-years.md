# 4-140 - Tax losses of earlier years

Worked example: Tax losses of earlier years Issue Fred Pattel is self-employed and turned 58 in 2024. Fred has owned and operated a landscape gardening business trading as "Keep Australia Beautiful" for the past 25 years.

Jim Gee, an employee of Fred's for 20 years, decided to retire in August 2024. Fred paid Jim a gratuity of $15,000 on the basis of Jim's past service and dedication to the business.

Fred has his own complying self-managed superannuation fund and made personal concessional (deducted) contributions of $25,000, in both October 2023 and October 2024.

Fred's landscape gardening business income for 2023–24 was $34,000 and his losses and outgoings incurred in conducting the business amounted to $48,000. For 2024–25, the figures were $43,000 and $49,000 respectively.

As a member of the Australian army reserve, Fred received $3,000 in pay and allowances on 1 July 2023 and on 1 July 2024.

Advise Fred as to his taxable income for both the 2023–24 and 2024–25 income years.

Solution A loss is incurred if the taxpayer's allowable deductions exceed the sum of their assessable income and net exempt income in a given year (ITAA97 s 36-1). The steps in calculating a tax loss are outlined in ITAA97 s 36-10. Such losses can be carried forward indefinitely and offset against net exempt income and assessable income in future years.

The rules for the deduction of tax losses from earlier income years contain certain limitations, for example:

- if the taxpayer has net exempt income for the later year, the tax loss is offset first against the net

  exempt income and then against assessable income (ITAA97 s 36-15(3), (4))

- the taxpayer cannot choose the year in which to claim the tax losses, in an attempt to avoid wasting

  tax losses when the taxpayer derives net exempt income

- if there are 2 or more losses, they are deductible in the order in which they were incurred (ITAA97 s

  36-15(5)), and

- deductions for certain outgoings are not allowed to the extent that they would create or add to a tax

  loss (ITAA97 s 26-55), namely:

– payments of pensions, gratuities or retiring allowances (ITAA97 s 26-50) – gifts (ITAA97 Div 30) – conservation covenants (ITAA97 Div 31) – personal superannuation contributions (ITAA97 s 290-150).

Australian army reserve pay The Australian army reserve pay and allowances of $3,000 received by Fred in both 2023–24 and 2024–25 are exempt income under ITAA97 s 51-5. The exempt income is added to Fred's business income in calculating the tax loss for the relevant income year. The effect is to reduce the carried forward loss from $14,000 to $11,000 in 2023–24 and from $6,000 to $3,000 in 2024–25. 2. Personal concessional superannuation contribution Fred can make personal concessional contributions to his complying superannuation fund of $27,500 in 2023–24 and $30,000 in 2024–25. Fred is therefore entitled to claim a deduction for contributions of $25,000 in both years to his superannuation fund under ITAA97 s 290-150. However, he is denied the deduction in both income years because they will give rise to, or add to, a tax loss (ITAA97 s 26-55). 3. Tax loss for 2023–24 carried forward to be offset against future income Fred's tax loss of $11,000 can be carried forward indefinitely to offset against future income. 4. Gratuity The gratuity paid to Jim, a former employee, would be deductible provided the requirements of s 25-50 are satisfied. However, the deduction is denied because it would give rise to, or add to, a tax loss (s 26-55). 5. Tax loss for 2024–25 carried forward for offset against future income Fred's tax loss of $11,000 for 2023–24 cannot be offset against income in 2024–25 because there is a tax loss in that year as well.

Fred's 2024–25 tax loss of $3,000 is added to his $11,000 loss from the previous year and is available to be offset against future income. The 2 losses are deductible in the order in which they were incurred (ITAA97 s 36-15(5)).
