# 4-280 - Deductions for employer and personal superannuation contributions

Worked example: Deductions for employer and personal superannuation contributions Issue Dr Su, aged 62 years, is a specialist medical practitioner conducting a private practice in which he employs a receptionist. Dr Su is also an adjunct professor at the Royal Prince Charles (RPC) teaching hospital. Dr Su's receptionist is Carol. She is 45 years of age and is paid $60,000 per annum. Dr Su contributes the 2024–25 mandatory 11.5% superannuation guarantee amount to the complying Health Employees Superannuation Fund (HESF). Carol also enters into a salary sacrifice arrangement contributing $14,300 from her salary into her superannuation fund.

As adjunct professor, Dr Su trains medical students at the RPC for which he is paid $50,000 per annum as a salary package with a salary of $30,000 and grossed-up fringe benefits amounting to $20,000. The hospital also makes the 11.5% superannuation guarantee contribution to Dr Su's complying self-managed superannuation fund (SMSF).

Dr Su's patient fees income for 2024–25 totalled $300,000 out of which Dr Su made a concessional contribution to his SMSF of $30,000. Dr Su claimed a deduction for the $30,000 contribution. During the year Dr Su made a capital gain of $15,000 from the sale of shares in Medibank Private, which he had held for 6 months.

Dr Su seeks your advice as to whether: 1. he can claim a tax deduction for the contributions made on behalf of Carol to the HESF, and 2. he can claim a tax deduction for his $30,000 contribution to his SMSF.

Solution The deductibility of superannuation contributions by an employer on behalf of employees or by individuals making personal contributions is governed by ITAA97 Subdivs 290-B and 290-C, respectively. The general deduction provision, ITAA97 s 8-1, has no role to play. The Commissioner's views on the deductibility of superannuation contributions are set out in Taxation Ruling TR 2010/1.

Section 290-60 allows an employer a deduction for a contribution made for the purpose of providing superannuation benefits for another person who is an employee of the employer. There is no limit to the amount that an employer can contribute. However, amounts in excess of the employee's concessional contribution cap have tax consequences for the employee.

The requirements in s 290-60 that need to be satisfied for the employer to claim a deduction are:

- the contribution is made to a complying superannuation fund

- the contribution is made for the purpose of providing superannuation benefits (Raymor Contractors

  Pty Ltd v FC of T 91 ATC 4259)

- the contribution is made for another person who is an employee of the employer, that is, the employee

  and the taxpayer claiming the deduction must be different people (Harris v FC of T 2002 ATC 4659),

  and

- the contribution is made for an employee whose age is within the prescribed limits.

If an employee enters into an effective salary sacrifice arrangement with their employer, the superannuation contributions for the employee are treated as employer contributions and are deductible to the employer.

Under s 290-150, personal concessional contributions to superannuation are deductible provided the requirements in ss 290-155 to 290-170 are met, namely:

- the contribution is made to a complying superannuation fund

- the contribution is made not later than 28 days after the end of the month in which the contributor

  turns 75 years (s 290-165(1A))

- the contributor notifies the fund of the intention to claim a deduction for the contribution (s 290-170(1))

- the contribution is not an amount previously released under the First Home Super Saver scheme (s

  290-168) or the COVID-19 early release rules (s 290-169), and

- the contribution is not a downsizer contribution (s 290-167).

  Tax deduction for contributions on behalf of Carol to the HESF

Carol has a concessional contributions cap of $30,000 for the 2024–25 year. Her employer, Dr Su, has made the mandatory 11.5% superannuation guarantee contribution to Carol's complying superannuation fund of $6,900 ($60,000 × 11.5%). Carol has entered into an effective salary sacrifice arrangement with her employer contributing a further $14,300 on Carol's behalf. Her employer is entitled to claim a tax deduction for both the superannuation guarantee and salary sacrifice contribution amounts.

Tax deduction for $30,000 contribution to Dr Su's SMSF As an adjunct professor at the RPC, Dr Su is an employee with a salary package of $50,000 comprising salary of $30,000 and grossed-up fringe benefits of $20,000. The hospital, as his employer, is required to make superannuation guarantee contributions on behalf of Dr Su based on his salary of $30,000 amounting to $3,450 to his complying SMSF.

Dr Su has patient fee income of $300,000 which is derived from being self-employed in private practice. Dr Su made a concessional contribution to his complying SMSF of $30,000.

Assuming Dr Su has given the trustee of his SMSF notice of his intention to claim a tax deduction for his $30,000 concessional contribution, he is entitled to claim a tax deduction for his $30,000 concessional contribution to his complying SMSF.

An individual's concessional contributions to a superannuation fund are subject to a concessional contributions cap of $30,000 in 2024–25. Combining his own contribution to his SMSF of $30,000 with the $3,450 paid by RPC, this cap has been exceeded in the 2024–25 year. Exceeding the cap generally results in additional tax payable by the individual on whose behalf the contributions are made, but this does not, by itself, affect the deductibility of the contributions.
