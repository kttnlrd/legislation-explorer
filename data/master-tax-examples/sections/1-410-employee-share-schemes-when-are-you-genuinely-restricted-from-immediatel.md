# 1-410 - Employee share schemes when are you genuinely restricted from immediately disposing of a share interest

disposing of a share interest?
Worked example: Employee share schemes: when are you genuinely restricted from immediately disposing of a share interest? Issue Marinus Ltd, an Australian-resident company, establishes an employee share scheme (ESS) to incentivise its senior employees by providing them with the opportunity to acquire shares in the company at a discount. Dolores is an executive employee of Marinus Ltd, and she is offered the chance to participate in the ESS by being granted the right to buy 1,000 shares at a nil exercise price. On 30 September 2021, Dolores accepts the offer and exercises the option to acquire the shares for nil consideration. The shares, however, are subject to certain vesting conditions, which if not met mean that Dolores will forfeit them. According to the ESS plan documentation, Dolores' shares will vest subject to satisfaction of all the following conditions:
• Performance Vesting Condition — Dolores must meet certain key performance indicators over the first
3 years from the date of grant of the shares.
• Continued Employment Condition — Dolores must remain in continuous employment with Marinus Ltd
for at least 3 years, otherwise the shares will be forfeited.
• Board Approval Condition — Marinus Ltd's Board may, at their discretion, grant their permission to
allow Dolores to dispose of the shares.
The shares are held by an independent plan administrator, who manages any requirements relating to an
employee's disposal of the shares during and after the period when the shares are restricted. This means
that even where an employee wants or attempts to sell their shares, they are unable to do so until the
restriction ends, and the ownership passes unconditionally to the employee.
The Board Approval process is subjective, and the Board has a broad discretion, with no clear, fixed,
objectively measured criteria to be applied. Furthermore, Dolores can choose whether or when to apply
for approval and Marinus Ltd's Board routinely approves requests to trade shares. When this happens
(assuming the other performance conditions have also been met), the shares move into an account with the
plan administrator specifically in the employee's name and can be sold.
What is the date on which Dolores will be taxed on her shares? Is there a deferred taxing point in place? If
so, what is the date on which that the restriction falls away?
Solution
Employees who receive ESS interests (shares or rights to acquire shares) at a discount are generally
assessed on the amount of the discount at the time they acquire their ESS interest, which is often the date
on which they choose to exercise their option to acquire shares.
However, ITAA97 s 83A-110 allows for this taxing point to be deferred in some instances, including when the
employee is genuinely restricted under the scheme from immediately disposing of their ESS interest (ITAA97
s 83A-115).
A genuine disposal restriction is commonly imposed as part of an ESS so that employee participants are
not taxed in the year of acquiring shares or rights to shares. If this happens, the deferred taxing point arises
when this restriction is lifted, potentially several years later.
disposing of a share interest?
Taxation Determination TD 2022/4 outlines the ATO's views on when an employee is "genuinely restricted"
from immediately disposing of an interest under an ESS. It is the first time that the ATO has expressed a
view on the meaning of "genuine restriction".
Genuine restrictions preventing disposal of an ESS interest include:
• a rule of the ESS that contractually prevents disposal of the share
• a law that makes disposal of the share a criminal offence, such as a law against insider trading, or
• an internal share trading policy of the employer company, provided that there is a meaningful penalty
for breaching the company policy that is strictly enforced.
TD 2022/4 goes on to state:
• a scheme "genuinely restricts" an employee from "disposing" of their shares or rights if the scheme
imposes serious and enforced consequences when the restriction is breached
• a disposal restriction is not genuine if it does not, in a real practical sense, limit the disposal, or if the
restriction is open to manipulation, and
• if a board of a company that has a discretionary power to approve requests to trade (based on
subjective criteria) routinely approves requests, the discretionary power is not a genuine restriction.
In relation to that last point, whether internal share trading company policies will be regarded as "genuine
restrictions" depends on both their form and substance. The documented policy and the practice and
implementation of that policy are all relevant factors. The ATO says, for example, where an employee is
required to get approval from a Board of Directors before disposing of the ESS interest, that in itself may
not qualify as "genuine" if the board routinely exercises its discretion to permit disposal/trading and the
employee is free to seek the exercise of such discretion at the time of their choosing. In such instances, the
ATO requires "clear, fixed and objectively measured criteria" be demonstrably applied when exercising the
discretion.
A company policy may also restrict an employee from disposing of an ESS interest because they possess
price-sensitive information. To be a genuine restriction, the employee must be able to demonstrate with
objective evidence how the information they held prevented immediate disposal (the employee's position in
the company alone is not evidence), and that they possessed such information at all times when no other
restrictions on disposal were imposed on them.
A restriction that meets the above criteria, but which can be lifted in cases of severe hardship, is still
considered to be a genuine restriction.
TD 2022/4 says that a restriction is considered to be lifted at the first time an opportunity arises for the
taxpayer to dispose of the share. In the case of a trading window, or another restriction that is temporarily
lifted and later re-imposed, the restriction is taken to have been lifted at the first opportunity for the taxpayer
to dispose of the share. In other words, the ESS deferred taxing point is not further delayed until the taxpayer
actually takes up an opportunity.
If there are multiple genuine disposal restrictions imposed at the time the ESS interest was acquired, all
these restrictions must be lifted for the deferred taxing point to arise.
If a participant in the scheme, due to being in a position of power in the employer (such as a director), is able
to control the decisions of the scheme administrator (eg because the administrator is an associate of the
director), the scheme's disposal restriction is considered "illusory".
In this case, there is a deferred taxing point in place because there are "genuine restrictions" on Dolores's
ability to sell the shares, being the Performance Vesting Condition and the Continued Employment
Condition. These "genuine restrictions" both fall away after 3 years. Dolores' ESS deferred taxing point will
be the first trading day after the third anniversary of her acquisition of the 1,000 shares (ie 1 October 2024).
The plan no longer genuinely restricts Dolores from disposing of the shares on that date.
The third condition (the Board Approval Condition) is not a genuine disposal restriction because there
are no "clear, fixed and objectively measured criteria" in place which dictate whether and when the Board
exercises it discretion and the Board routinely approves requests to trade shares. This condition is ignored in
determining the date on which Dolores can dispose of her shares.
