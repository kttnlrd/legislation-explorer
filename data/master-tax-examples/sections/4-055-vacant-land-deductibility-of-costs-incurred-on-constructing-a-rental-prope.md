# 4-055 - Vacant land deductibility of costs incurred on constructing a rental property

Worked example: Vacant land; deductibility of costs incurred on constructing a rental property Issue Richard purchased a residential block of land in July 2023. He took out a mortgage which resulted in him incurring borrowing costs and interest with respect to the purchase of the land. Richard's intention is to build a residential rental property on the land and, to help facilitate that, he took out a second loan to fund the construction of the house. As at 30 June 2025, the house is only partially complete with the frame completed but all internal work still outstanding. Richard does not carry on a business.

Advise Richard whether he can claim a deduction for costs associated with the first loan (to purchase the land) and the second loan (to fund the building of the house).

Solution ITAA97 s 26-102 denies certain deductions for losses and outgoings associated with holding vacant land that are incurred on or after 1 July 2019. The provisions apply to individuals, self- managed superannuation fund (SMSF) trustees, private trusts and partnerships comprised of any of these, but do not apply to companies.

There are key exclusions which include expenses incurred in connection with carrying on a business such as property development or a primary production business. There are also exemptions for structures affected by natural disasters or other exceptional circumstances.

Where s 26-102 operates to deny a deduction for an amount, the amount can be included in the calculation of the cost base of the land for capital gains tax (CGT) purposes.

Section 26-102(4) applies to disregard certain substantial and permanent structures from qualifying for holding cost deductions where they are residential properties and they do not meet certain criteria. Such properties are treated as "vacant land". The section says that, for residential properties, the premises are deemed not to be a substantial and permanent structure while the taxpayer holds the land unless:

- the premises are legally available for occupation, and

- the premises are rented, hired or licensed or available for lease, hire or licence.

This means that newly constructed or substantially renovated residential premises must be lawfully able to be occupied, and either leased, hired or licensed, or available for lease, hire or licence, before they are no longer treated as "vacant land" (at which point holding costs will no longer be denied).

The Commissioner's preliminary view on the application and exclusions of s 26-102 are set out in Taxation Ruling TR 2023/3.

Section 26-102(1) states that any interest or borrowing costs to acquire land are included as a cost of holding land. Examples of other costs of holding land include council rates, land taxes and maintenance costs. Where s 26-102 prevents a deduction for holding costs, the expenses may form part of the third element of the cost base of owning the asset for CGT purposes.

Paragraph 26 of the ruling states that, in the context of s 26-102, the ATO does not consider the costs of constructing a substantial and permanent structure on the land, or any interest or borrowing costs (to the extent they are associated with construction), to be a loss or outgoing related to holding land (see Example 6 in para 27 of TR 2023/3).

Accordingly, taxpayers incurring interest and borrowing costs directly related to the construction of a rental property can still claim deductions for such expenses. This assumes the taxpayer clearly intends that, once constructed, the property will be used for income-producing purposes (eg as a rental property) and as such the expense is otherwise deductible under ITAA97 s 8-1.

Richard cannot claim the borrowing costs and interest expenses with respect to the loan to purchase the vacant block of land until a certificate of occupancy is obtained and the property is available for lease.

However, TR 2023/3 makes clear that Richard will be able to claim a deduction for loan interest and borrowing costs in relation to the second loan as it relates to the construction of an investment property.

Richard will be able to deduct these costs as they are not considered to relate to holding the land.

In this case, there are 2 separate loans and therefore clearly separate loan interest and other borrowing costs. However, the same principles apply where only one loan is taken out for the dual purposes of buying the land and constructing the house. A deduction will be available for the element of the loan interest and other borrowing costs that relate to the construction of the residence. So, if the construction loan and land loan are combined, the interest will need to be apportioned. The difficulty for the taxpayer in such a case will be to split out (and substantiate) the deductible element.

*Refs: ITAA97 s 26-102, s 26-102, ITAA97 s 8-1*
