# 10-255 - Division 7A when will an unpaid present entitlement become subject to Division 7A

to Division 7A?

Worked example: Division 7A: when will an unpaid present entitlement become subject to Division 7A?

Issue John Drake is the trustee of the Astley Family Trust, a family discretionary trust. Smart Pty Ltd is a related bucket company that is controlled by John. On 15 June 2025, John resolves that $100,000 of the Astley Family Trust's income for the year ended 30 June 2025 should be distributed to Smart Pty Ltd. The Astley Family Trust however requires the funds to finance its own working capital. Accordingly, there is no intention to actually pay the funds across to Smart Pty Ltd.

The accounts for the Astley Family Trust are prepared by 31 October 2025.

Explain the tax implications of the above transactions to John, as trustee. In particular, is there a ITAA36 Div 7A liability for Smart Pty Ltd and, if so, when does it arise?

Solution Within private groups, it is common practice for trustees to appoint trust income to a related private company beneficiary. The appointed trust income is included in the profits of the corporate beneficiary, which is then assessed on its share of the trust's net income at the rate then in force (25% or 30%).

However, often — as here — the entitlement remains unpaid. This is an unpaid present entitlement (UPE).

The advantage of this arrangement is that the tax paid by the company on its entitlement to income is capped at 30%; whereas, if the funds are retained in the trust (or paid out to individual beneficiaries), the tax liability can be as high as 45%.

The ATO considers such a UPE can be a financial accommodation provided by the private company to the trust, and therefore the UPE can be deemed a ITAA36 Div 7A loan.

In Taxation Determination TD 2022/11, the ATO sets out its view that a private company beneficiary with a UPE to the trustee will give rise to a deemed Div 7A loan. This will happen when the company has "knowledge" of an amount that it can demand immediate payment of from the trustee, but it does not do so.

Therefore, at that date, the company has consented to the trustee retaining that amount for the continued use by the trust. This applies to trust entitlements arising on or after 1 July 2022.

TD 2022/11 states that the company will be deemed to have the requisite knowledge if the company and trustee "have the same directing mind and will", which is the case in this situation where John Drake is both trustee of the Astley Trust and also controls Smart Pty Ltd. This means the financial accommodation — deemed Div 7A loan — is given when the same directing mind (John) has calculated the trusts "trust law income" and knows the amount to be distributed to the beneficiary. This will usually be when the accounts for the year have been prepared (31 October 2025, for the year ended 30 June 2025).

The ATO considers that this applies unless the unpaid distributed funds are put under a sub-trust in circumstances where the sub-trust funds are not mixed with the main trust funds, and are invested solely for the benefit of the company, in which case Div 7A does not apply because there is no financial accommodation. In this case, however, the funds are not put under a sub-trust for the benefit of the company as they are required to finance the day-to-day working capital of the trust.

In the earlier draft determination, TD 2022/D1, the timing of when the UPE becomes a loan was more complex than in the final determination, TD 2022/11. Under the draft determination, the timing of when the UPE becomes a loan could have been 30 June of the year the UPE was created, or when the trust accounts were prepared, which usually occurs in the following year. This timing depended on the way the trustee expressed the present entitlement in the trustee resolution. For example, where the trustee resolves that to Division 7A? the bucket company is entitled to a fixed amount of trust income, the Div 7A liability would arise in the year the UPE was created (30 June 2025 in this case). Where, alternatively, the trustee resolves that the bucket company is entitled to a percentage share of trust income, the Div 7A liability would instead arise when the trust accounts were prepared, being 30 October 2025, which is in the year ended 30 June 2026. This clearly meant that depending upon the wording of the trust resolution, a different timing outcome could be achieved. In TD 2022/11, however, the ATO revised its view, in response to comments from the tax and legal professions, to treat all relevant corporate beneficiary UPEs as loans at the time when the accounts are prepared, usually in the following year.

The accounts for the Astley Family Trust are finalised on 30 October 2025. Accordingly, that is the date that Smart Pty Ltd provides financial accommodation to Astley Family Trust. This falls within the company year ended 30 June 2026. Therefore, Smart Pty Ltd and John (as trustee of the Astley Trust) can avoid a Div 7A deemed dividend on the UPE by entering into a complying loan agreement by 15 May 2027, being the lodgment date for the company in relation to the year ended 30 June 2026. The first minimum yearly repayment must be made by 30 June 2027 and further repayments are then required over the life of the loan (whether 7 or 25 years) to prevent deemed dividends arising under ITAA36 s 109E in subsequent income years.

Footnotes 1       In the recent case of FC of T v Bendel & Anor (2025 ATC ¶20-946; [2025] FCAFC 15), the Federal Court upheld the AAT view that a private company beneficiary with a UPE was not a loan within the scope of ITAA36 s 109D. The AAT had previously stated: "…the necessary conclusion is that a loan within the meaning of s 109D(3) does not reach so far as to embrace the rights in equity created when entitlements to trust income (or capital) are created but not satisfied and remain unpaid. The balance of an outstanding or unpaid entitlement of a corporate beneficiary of a trust, whether held on a separate trust or otherwise, is not a loan to the trustee of that trust." The AAT went on to observe that UPEs are more specifically dealt with by Subdiv EA of Div 7A, not s 109D. Subdivision EA applies to circumstances where a trustee creates a UPE in a private company and transfers the underlying cash (or property) to a shareholder or an associate of a shareholder by way of certain payments or loans, or by the forgiveness of such loans. However, in the absence of those circumstances (eg, when the trustee retains the cash for use in the trust, as here) Subdiv EA has no application.

The ATO noted that it disagreed with the Federal Court's findings (Interim Decision Impact Statement, 19 March 2025). The Commissioner has therefore appealed the judgment to the High Court. Therefore, no weight can be placed on the Federal Court's findings, pending the outcome of this further hearing.

Accordingly, this answer is prepared on the basis that the ATO's legal interpretation in TD 2022/11 is correct.
