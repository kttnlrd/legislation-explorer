# 6-320 - Small business entity assets temporary changes to immediate deduction

Worked example: Small business entity assets; temporary changes to immediate deduction Issue Matt Eames is a sole proprietor manufacturing furniture and trading as Furniture Edge. The annual turnover of his business is $1.4 million. To take advantage of the more generous accelerated depreciation provisions, Furniture Edge acquired new assets during 2024–25. On 1 August 2024, Furniture Edge purchased a furniture delivery van for $19,250 and a timber cutting and shaping machine for $44,000. On 29 May 2025, Furniture Edge purchased a furniture assembly machine for $48,000. On 24 August 2024, Furniture Edge commissioned an IT company to develop in-house software to program the machines, cutting and shaping the timber and constructing the furniture. This development project cost Furniture Edge $17,400.

Advise Matt on the taxation consequences of Furniture Edge's asset acquisitions over the course of 2024– 25.

Solution The small business entity (SBE) regime in ITAA97 Subdiv 328-D provides concessional and simpler depreciation rules for SBEs. The current requirements to qualify as an SBE are that a business's aggregate turnover must be less than $10 million (ITAA97 s 328-110). The provisions provide for immediate write- offs, a general small business pooling arrangement and simplified balancing adjustments on the disposal of assets.

Temporary changes to accelerated depreciation for small business assets came into effect on 1 June 2023 and continue until 30 June 2025. The changes necessitate alterations to Subdiv 328-D. In particular, the addition of s 328-180 and additions to s 328-175(1), 328-180(1)(b), 328-180(2)(a) and 328-180(3)(a), 328-210(1), 328-250(1) and (4) and 328-253(4).

The substance of the changes allows for an immediate deduction for assets, acquired or installed ready for use, costing less than $20,000, from 1 July 2023 through until 30 June 2025.

Assets costing more than the relevant instant write-off amount can be allocated to a general small business pool. Assets allocated to the pool are depreciated at 15% during the year they are allocated to the pool. The general small business opening pool balance is depreciated at 30% using the diminishing value method.

Once the pool balance falls below $20,000, it can be written-off in that year. The changes also apply to software costing less than the instant asset write-off amount, which attracts an immediate write-off. Software costing the instant asset write-off amount or more may be allocated to a software development pool.

The 5-year lock-out rule does not apply to the temporary changed accelerated depreciation provisions (ITAA97 s 328-175(10)).

Immediate write-off/deduction for depreciating assets for 2024–25 Furniture Edge is an SBE with an annual turnover of $1.4 million. The business, therefore, qualifies for the accelerated depreciation provisions and can immediately claim a deduction for the $19,250 furniture delivery van in the 2024–25 year.

The increased threshold for immediate write-off of assets is a temporary measure, although it is proposed to be extended at least until 30 June 2026 (after which — based on the law as it currently stands — the threshold will revert to $1,000).

Establishment of a general small business depreciation pool The cost of the timber cutting and shaping machine ($44,000) exceeds the immediate write-off threshold and can be allocated to a general small business depreciation pool. The asset allocated to the pool during 2024– 25 can be depreciated at 15%, using the diminishing value method, for the 2024–25 tax year. In addition, the business can also add the $48,000 furniture assembly machine to the general small business depreciation pool for the year, depreciated at 15%. For 2025–26, the opening value of the pool is depreciated at 30% and assets acquired during 2025–26 and allocated to the pool are depreciated at 15%. "In-house software" commissioned by Furniture Edge Under the new temporary arrangements, an immediate deduction is available to Furniture Edge for the $17,400 paid for the development of computer software to co-ordinate the operation of their machines, because the amount is below the instant asset write-off threshold ($20,000 at the date the software was commissioned). That is provided Furniture Edge is not currently operating a software development pool, in which case the $17,400 software would have to be added to the pool.

*Refs: ITAA97 s 328-110), s 328-180, s 328-175(1), ITAA97 s 328-175(10))*
