# 3-055 - Car parking fringe benefits

Worked example: Car parking fringe benefits Issue Jagaroth Pty Ltd is an engineering company located in the south-west suburbs of Sydney. Its annual turnover for the past 3 years has been approximately $120 million. It provides free on-site car parking outside its factory for its employees, who are all based there as their permanent place of employment. The parking is in a secure area to the side of the factory, not accessible to members of the public.

Employees typically park in the factory car park for the whole of the business day, that is, 8:30 am to 4:30 pm.

The only commercial parking facility within a one-kilometre radius of Jagaroth's premises is a nearby Westfield shopping centre, which provides dedicated car parking spaces in a multi-level concrete structure attached to the shopping centre. The car park is operated by the landlord of the shopping centre.

There is licence plate recognition functionality at each entrance and boom gates at each exit to the shopping centre parking facility. There is signage visible from the street and at each entrance advertising that car parking is available.

Parking is primarily intended to be for short-term shoppers and longer-term parking is discouraged. No fee is charged for the first 2 hours, after which parking costs $7 per hour. All-day parking is therefore significantly more expensive than the current FBT "lowest representative fee" of $10.77 for the 2025 FBT year.

Jagaroth Pty Ltd's Finance Director comes to you to understand the company's potential FBT liability. In particular, he is keen to understand whether the company has an FBT liability with regard to the provision of free parking in the 2025 FBT year.

Solution A car parking benefit is provided on a particular day when, between 7 am–7 pm:

- a car is parked at a work car park for a period of more than 4 hours

- an employee uses the car in connection with travel between their place of residence and primary

  place of employment at least once on that day

- the work car park is located at or "in the vicinity of" the primary place of employment, on that day

- a commercial parking station, which is a permanent facility, is located within a one-kilometre radius of

  the work car park used by the employee

- the "lowest representative fee" charged by any commercial parking station for all-day parking within a

  one-kilometre radius of the work car park exceeds the relevant car parking threshold for the FBT year

  in question

- the parking is provided to the employee in respect of their employment, and

- the parking is not excluded by the regulations. For example, a car space is excluded if it is provided

  to a disabled employee who holds and displays a valid disabled person's parking permit and the permit

  allows the employee to park in spaces that are designated for the exclusive use of a disabled person.

Jagaroth Pty Ltd provides free parking for its employees. It is a requirement for an FBT liability to arise that cars are routinely parked here for more than 4 hours between 7 am and 7 pm on each day. In fact, employees typically park here for the whole of the business day, that is, 8:30 am to 4:30 pm.

However, an FBT liability can only arise if there is a "commercial parking station" located within a one- kilometre radius of Jagaroth's premises, and the lowest fee charged to members of the public for all-day parking exceeds the car parking threshold, $10.77 for the 2025 FBT year. This latter point is clearly met.

The question, then, is whether the shopping centre car park is a "commercial parking station".

A "commercial parking station" is defined as a permanent parking facility that provides all-day parking to members of the public, in the ordinary course of business, for payment of a fee.

It should generally be operated by a car parking operator, including a parking facility that is within another complex (such as an office, shopping centre or hospital) where the owner or lessor of the complex outsources the management of the parking facility to a car parking operator.

However, a facility may still be a commercial car parking facility where it is not managed by a car parking operator if it:

- has clear signage visible from the street advertising that paid parking is available

- has mechanisms to control who can enter and/or exit the parking facility, or park at the facility (this

  may include boom gates, or "pay and display" ticket machines)

- charges more than a nominal fee (usually a significantly lower rate than the local market rate) for paid

  parking. This includes charging a user for parking which is not all-day parking (such as parking at an

  hourly rate).

Note that para 81 of the now withdrawn Taxation Ruling TR 96/26 exempts from the definition of commercial parking stations those facilities which have "a primary purpose other than providing all-day parking, that usually charge penalty rates significantly higher than the rates chargeable for all-day parking at commercial all-day parking facilities (such as parking provided for short term shoppers or hotel guests)". Under this view, the shopping centre car park is not a commercial parking station because the primary purpose of the shopping centre car park is not to provide all-day parking. In fact, the pricing structure of the car parking facility is specifically designed to discourage it.

However, the ATO has reinterpreted this view in Taxation Ruling TR 2021/2 (as modified by TR 2021/2dc1).

Paragraph 36 of that ruling states that "all-day parking may still be provided in the ordinary course of business, even if a parking facility's fee structure appears to encourage short-term parking and discourage all-day parking. The fact that all-day parking charges are expensive will not, on its own, mean all-day parking is outside the ordinary course of business". In this respect, the new ruling applies in respect of car parking benefits provided on or after 1 April 2022. Therefore, for the FBT year ending 31 March 2025, the nearby shopping centre car park is regarded as a commercial parking station, notwithstanding that it actively discourages all-day parking. As a result, the provision of on-site parking by Jagaroth Pty Ltd to its employees will give rise to car parking fringe benefits in the 2025 FBT year.

The small business car parking exemption in FBTAA s 58GA does not apply as the company's turnover exceeded the relevant threshold ($50 million).

Footnotes 1       The ATO's view was challenged in the Federal Court in the recent case of Toowoomba Regional Council v FC of T 2025 ATC ¶20-950; [2025] FCA 161, but in a decision impact statement released after the case went against it, the ATO said that pending the outcome of a future appeal, it will continue to administer the law in accordance with the view set out in TR 2021/2.
