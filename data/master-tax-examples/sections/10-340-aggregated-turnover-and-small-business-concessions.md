# 10-340 - Aggregated turnover and small business concessions

Worked example: Aggregated turnover and small business concessions Issue Alpha Pty Ltd is a Melbourne based food manufacturer, which is a 100% owned subsidiary of a New Zealand parent. There are no other companies in the group.

In the year ended 30 June 2024, the company had a turnover of $4.6 million. Its overseas parent company had a turnover of $5.2 million.

Included in the turnover of $4.6 million is bank interest of $50,000, proceeds from the sale of machinery amounting to $120,000 and insurance proceeds for the loss of a food processing machine of $114,000.

In the year ended 30 June 2025, the company expects to have a turnover of $4.8 million, while its overseas parent is expected to have a turnover of $5.4 million.

Included in the turnover of $4.8 million is bank interest amounting to $64,000 and the proceeds from the sale of machinery of $48,000.

Alpha Pty Ltd does not sell goods to its New Zealand parent.

Advise Alpha Pty Ltd whether it is a small business entity (SBE) for the year ended 30 June 2025 and what tax concessions it will be entitled to.

Solution Alpha Pty Ltd will be a SBE if it satisfies the $10 million aggregated turnover test, which will be the case where it carries on a business and meets one of the following: (a) Aggregated turnover for the previous income year was less than $10 million. So, if the company has aggregated turnover of less than $10 million (and carries on a business) in the 2023–24 year, it will be a SBE in the 2024–25 year. (b) Aggregated turnover for the current income year is "likely" to be less than $10 million. (c) Aggregated turnover for the current income year is "actually" less than $10 million.

Aggregated turnover is the sum of the following:

- annual turnover for the income year

- the annual turnover of a connected entity for that part of the income year that the entity is connected

  with the other entity

- the annual turnover of an entity that is an affiliate, for that part of the income year that the entity is

  affiliated with the other entity.

Both Australian and foreign entities can be connected or affiliated, meaning both Australian and foreign owned entities must be included in aggregated turnover for the period during which they are connected or affiliated.

Annual turnover includes all ordinary income earned in the ordinary course of business for the income year, including:

- trading stock sales

- fees for services

- interest from business bank accounts

- amounts received to replace something that would have had the character of business income.

  The following amounts are excluded from annual turnover:

- amounts borrowed for the business

- proceeds from selling business capital assets

- insurance proceeds for the loss or destruction of a business asset

- amounts of non-assessable, non-exempt income.

Therefore, the aggregated turnover of Alpha Pty Ltd for the year ended 30 June 2024 is $9,566,000 (being the total of turnover for the Australian business and the parent company, less $120,000 proceeds from the sale of machinery and $114,000 insurance proceeds).

For the year ended 30 June 2025, expected aggregate turnover is $10,152,000 (being the total of turnover for the Australian business and the parent company, less $48,000 proceeds from the sale of machinery).

The company is unable to state what its actual 2024–25 turnover will be and its "expected" turnover for the year is too large to satisfy the test. However, the 2023–24 turnover is less than $10 million and therefore it passes the SBE turnover test for the 2024–25 year.

The company will not be able to access the CGT small business concessions contained in Div 152, as the aggregated turnover threshold for those concessions is $2 million (unless the company can satisfy the alternative maximum net asset value test of $6 million).

A range of income tax concessions are available to Alpha Pty Ltd in 2024–25, all of which require a $10 million turnover threshold, including:

- access to the SBE simplified depreciation rules in Subdiv 328-D

- access to the small business restructure roll-over relief in Subdiv 328-G

- access to the option to account for GST on a cash basis and pay GST instalments as calculated by

  the ATO.

In addition, a number of concessions will be available to Alpha Pty Ltd that are available to companies with aggregated turnover of less than $50 million:

- Access to the 25% corporate tax rate where it is a "base rate entity" (which means a company with

  aggregated turnover of less than $50 million with no more than 80% "base rate entity passive income").

- Deductions for certain prepaid business expenditure (ITAA36 ss 82KZM and 82KZL).

- Ability to claim an immediate deduction for start-up expenses of a small business taxpayer (ITAA97 s

  40-880(2A)) (from 1 July 2020).

- Access to the simplified trading stock rules in Subdiv 328-E.

- Access to the simplified method for paying PAYG instalments calculated by the ATO.

- Access to the 4-year amendment period for assessments.

- Access to the FBT car parking exemption for small business employers in FBTAA s 58GA (from 1

  April 2021).

- Expanded access to the FBT exemption for portable electronic devices in FBTAA s 58X (from 1 April

  2021).
