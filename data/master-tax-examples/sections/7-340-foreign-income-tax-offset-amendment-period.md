# 7-340 - Foreign income tax offset amendment period

Worked example: Foreign income tax offset amendment period Issue Cameron Davis is an Australian tax resident who resides in Sydney. He owned a commercial building in Canada which he decided to sell. The contract date for the sale of the property was 1 June 2022 but, because of financing delays, settlement did not take place until April 2024.

Under the double tax agreement between Australia and Canada, Cameron is subject to taxation in both countries in relation to any capital gain arising from the sale of the Canadian property. For Australian tax purposes, CGT event A1 happened on 1 June 2022. No other CGT event happened to Cameron during 2021–22 and a net capital gain of $60,000 was included in Cameron's 2021–22 tax return.

Cameron lodged his own 2021–22 tax return in October 2022. He could not claim any foreign income tax to offset the Australian tax liability as he had not yet paid Canadian tax on the capital gain. It was not until December 2024 that Cameron lodged a Canadian tax return for the 2022 Canadian tax year. The return disclosed a taxable capital gain of $80,000 (in Australian dollars). Cameron paid the Canadian tax on the capital gain in January 2025.

Is Cameron entitled to claim a foreign income tax offset (FITO) in relation to the Canadian tax paid on the capital gain even though his amendment period expired in November 2024?

Solution As at January 2025, Cameron is entitled to claim a FITO in relation to 75% of the Canadian tax paid on the capital gain, notwithstanding that his standard amendment period of 2 years expired in November 2024.

ITAA97 s 770-190(1) allows taxpayers a special amendment period of 4 years after an "amendment event" happens. Relevant to Cameron, an "amendment event" includes the payment of foreign tax (Canadian tax in this case) that counts towards the FITO for the year (s 770-190(2)).

This amendment period of 4 years overrides the standard amendment periods in ITAA36 s 170.

An amount of foreign tax counts towards the FITO for the year to the extent that it was paid in respect of an amount included in assessable income that year (ITAA97 s 770-10(1)). In Cameron's case, the $60,000 capital gain that was assessable in Australia in 2021–22 equals 75% of the $80,000 capital gain that was taxed in Canada. Therefore, only 75% of the Canadian tax paid in respect of the gain can count towards the FITO for 2021–22.

Cameron has until January 2029 to amend his 2021–22 tax return to claim a FITO in relation to the Canadian tax paid on the capital gain.

The FITO claimable will equal the lesser of:

- 75% of the amount of Canadian tax paid, and

- the FITO limit calculated under ITAA97 s 770-75.

The FITO limit calculated under s 770-75 very broadly represents the Australian tax that would be payable on double taxed foreign source income in the absence of the FITO.

Cameron will calculate his FITO limit as follows: Step 1: Calculate his tax payable for the 2021–22 year, including Medicare levy and Medicare levy surcharge, but excluding any tax offsets.

Step 2: Calculate his tax payable for the 2021–22 year using the following assumptions. (a) Assessable income does not include:

- any assessable amount (whether Australian or foreign sourced) on which foreign income tax has been

  paid that counts towards his FITO, or

- any other foreign source income.

  (b) The following deductions were not allowable:

- debt deductions attributable to an overseas permanent establishment

- any other deductions (other than debt deductions) that are reasonably related to any amount covered

  by (a), or

- an amount of the foreign loss component of one or more tax losses deducted in the income year.

  Step 3:

Subtract the result of Step 2 from the result of Step 1. This is Cameron's FITO limit for 2021–22.

Note, a taxpayer may claim up to $1,000 of foreign tax paid as a FITO without needing to calculate the limit and comparing it to the foreign tax paid on the double taxed income. In Cameron's case, his FITO limit is likely to be much higher than $1,000 given the size of the capital gain taxed in Canada. However, for taxpayers with smaller foreign tax amounts, the $1,000 limit offers an administrative concession.

*Refs: ITAA97 s 770-190(1), s 770-190(2)), ITAA97 s 770-10(1)), ITAA97 s 770-75, s 770-75*
