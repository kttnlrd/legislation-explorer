# 1-560 - Non-arms length expenses of a superannuation fund

Worked example: Non-arm's length expenses of a superannuation fund Issue David Rogers is the principal of Rogers and Co, a firm of accountants which provides general accounting services to several unrelated self-managed superannuation funds (SMSFs) on an arm's length basis. He is also the director of Rogers Trustees Pty Ltd, which is the corporate trustee of an SMSF of which David is the sole member.

David's firm charges his clients $3,000 for his normal SMSF package however his own SMSF acquires the same services free of charge.

The services provided were for fund accounting and were general in nature. They did not relate to any particular asset or assets.

In addition, David's life partner, Phillip, is a licensed builder who undertakes renovation work on the SMSF's rental property worth $3,000, for which he charges nothing.

The total income of David's SMSF in 2024–25 was $20,000 in rent from the rental property, with eligible deductions of $5,000, resulting in taxable income in 2024–25 of $15,000. No assessable contributions were made in that income year.

What is the tax liability of the SMSF?

Solutions Non-arm's length income (NALI) is income generated through a non-arm's length arrangement by a superannuation fund. Income is classified as NALI if the income is more than the fund would have received if it was dealing at arm's length.

Non-arm's length expenses (NALE) arise where the fund incurs expenses that are lower than the expenses that would have been incurred if the fund was dealing at arm's length.

For NALE, an expense can be specific or general in nature. Where an expense is incurred in relation to a particular asset or assets of the fund, it is a specific expense. Some examples of specific expenses include:

- maintenance expenses for a rental property

- investment advice fees for a particular pool of investments, and

- a limited recourse borrowing arrangement (LRBA) for the purchase of a specific asset.

A general expense is an expense that relates to all the income of the fund. Examples include:

- actuarial costs

- accountant fees

- fees to an auditor

- administrative costs in managing the fund

- trustee fees

- costs of complying with the regulatory obligations of the fund, and

- investment adviser fees, where those fees relate generally to the operation of the fund and not to a

  specific investment or a particular pool of investments.

Where a specific expense is NALE, the income of the asset that the specific expense relates to will be deemed to be NALI and taxed at the highest marginal rate (s 295-550(1)(b) and (c)).

Where a general expense is NALE, the taxation treatment is different. Instead, the amount of NALI taxed at the highest marginal tax rate is twice the difference between the amount that would have been expected to have been incurred for a general expense at arm's length, and the amount actually incurred, with no deductions applying against that amount (s 295-550(8) and 295-550(9) ITAA97).

The fund's total non-arm's length component cannot exceed the fund's assessable income minus deductions, excluding assessable contributions and deductions against them (s 295-545(2A) ITAA97).

These rules only apply to SMSFs and small APRA-regulated funds (ie any regulated superannuation fund that has 6 or fewer members, other than an SMSF).

The new law applies to income derived in the 2018–19 income year or later and to expenses incurred on or after 1 July 2018. This new legislation supersedes the existing ATO guidance on this matter, set out in LCR 2021/2. In addition, in PCG 2020/5 the ATO announced it would not allocate compliance resources to determine whether the NALI provisions applied to a complying superannuation fund for the 2021–22 income year and earlier income years, where the fund incurred NALE of a general nature that has a sufficient nexus to all ordinary and/or statutory income derived by the fund.

Applying the above to the question, the renovations are provided by an entity (Phillip) who is separate from the SMSF and as such, should have been on arm's length terms. The renovations were an expense incurred in deriving income from a particular asset (the property) and are therefore a specific non-arm's length expense to which para 295-550(1)(c) applies.

The acquisition of free accounting services by the SMSF constitutes non-arm's length dealing between David and his SMSF. No expense was incurred when the SMSF would have been expected to have incurred an expense in respect of the accounting fees had the parties been dealing at arm's length, so the NALE provisions apply. The accounting services were general in nature and did not relate to any particular asset or assets so are captured under s 295-550(9).

In relation to the specific expense, the amount under subparagraph 295-545(2A)(a)(i) is the NALI arising from the specific non-arm's length expense under s 295-550(1) minus attributable deductions, which is the $20,000 in rental income minus $5,000 in deductions, resulting in $15,000.

In relation to the general expense, the amount under subparagraph 295-545(2A)(a)(ii) is the NALI arising from the general expense that is a non-arm's length expense under s 295-550(9), which in this case is 2 × $3,000 which equals $6,000.

The sum of the amounts under subparagraphs 295-545(2A)(a)(i) and (ii) is $15,000 + $6,000 which equals $21,000.

The cap on the non-arm's length component under para 295-545(2A)(b) is the entity's taxable income excluding assessable contributions and any deductions against assessable contributions. In this case, this is $15,000 as there are no assessable contributions or deductions against them.

The amount of the non-arm's component is the lesser of the amount under para 295-545(2A)(a) and the amount under para 295-545(2A)(b). As $15,000 is less than $21,000, the total non-arm's length component is $15,000. This amount (being the entire taxable income of the fund) is taxed at the highest marginal rate (45%). This compares to a usual tax rate of 15% and represents a punitive level of taxation to David's fund because of the NALE.

According to LCR 2021/2, the non-arm's length expenditure will also result in any capital gain that might arise from the subsequent disposal of the rental property being NALI (taxed at 45%).

There is a low-tax component of $0, which would — were it not zero — be taxed at the normal rate for SMSFs (15%). The low tax component is any remaining taxable income after calculating the non-arm's length component.
