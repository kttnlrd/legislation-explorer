# 2-290 - Small business concessions and death

Worked example: Small business concessions and death Issue William owned a commercial property which was solely used in his engineering model-making business, Toy Soldiers Pty Ltd. William owned 100% of the shares in Toy Soldiers Ltd. The property was acquired for a base cost of $1.5 million on 1 July 2012 and immediately became the base for the business.

William died on 26 March 2024 and the property was left under his Will to his sister, Annabelle. She did not wish to continue the business and the property was left empty after his death, with Annabelle ultimately entering into a contract to sell the land on 15 April 2025 for $3 million.

William previously had another business which was sold in March 2023, which involved dealing in rare model figures. The capital gain arising on the goodwill of this business was $80,000. William claimed the CGT small business rollover in relation to this amount as he was intending to purchase a new business within 2 years of the date of the gain, however his death prevented this from happening.

William meets the basic conditions for the CGT small business concessions to apply. William had not used up any of the CGT retirement limit.

Annabelle inherited all of William's assets and has no current year or prior year capital losses.

Explain the CGT implications of the above transaction.

Solution Section 152-80 ITAA97 allows a deceased person's legal personal representative (LPR) or beneficiary to access the small business concessions, to the extent that the deceased would have been able to access them just before they died, if a CGT event happens to the assets in the hands of the LPR or beneficiary within 2 years of the death of the individual.

Section 152-80 applies if: (a) a CGT asset forms part of the estate of a deceased individual or was owned jointly by joint tenants and one of them dies (b) one of the following happens: (i) the asset devolves to the individual's LPR (ii) the asset passes to a beneficiary (iii) an interest in the asset is acquired by the surviving joint tenant or tenants (iv) the asset devolves to a trustee of a trust established by the will of the deceased individual (c) the individual would have been entitled to reduce or disregard a capital gain under Div 152 if a CGT event had happened in relation to the CGT asset immediately before their death, and (d) a CGT event happens in relation to the CGT asset within 2 years of the individual's death. The Commissioner has the power to extend the 2-year period under s 152-80(3).

The LPR, beneficiary, surviving joint tenants, or trustee or beneficiary of the trust is entitled to reduce or disregard a capital gain under Div 152 in the same ways as the individual would have been entitled, except: (a) in relation to the 15-year exemption, the requirement that the CGT event happens in relation to the retirement of the individual does not need to be met, on the basis that it is impossible to achieve this condition because the individual has already died (s 152-80(2)(a)). The other conditions — that the individual was 55 years of age or over, or permanently incapacitated at the time of the CGT event referred to in (c) above — apply as normal, and (b) in relation to the small business retirement exemption, there is no need for the CGT exempt amount to be contributed to a complying superannuation fund or an RSA (s 152-80(2)), even if the deceased was less than 55 years of age just before their death.

Where an LPR or beneficiary is protected by the 2-year extension upon death, there is no restriction on the use of the asset within the 2-year period (or further time allowed) after death. For example, the LPR or a beneficiary of the deceased can continue to run a business in their own right before selling it, lease business premises to a third party or even allow business assets to sit dormant until they are sold.

If, however, the 2-year period is exceeded (and the Commissioner does not exercise his discretion to extend the time frame), then the taxpayer (eg the LPR or beneficiary) will need to satisfy the conditions of the small business concessions independently.

In this situation, if the sale of the property had been completed immediately before William's death, he would have been entitled to apply the small business CGT concessions as all the basic conditions would have been satisfied, with the exception of the 15-year exemption, which does not apply because the asset was not held for 15 years.

Therefore, Annabelle is also entitled to apply the small business 50% reduction, the retirement exemption and the CGT small business roll-over relief to the capital gain which arises as:

- the property formed part of William's deceased estate

- the property passed to Annabelle in accordance with William's Will

- William would have been entitled to the small business 50% reduction, the retirement exemption and

  the CGT small business rollover if the sale occurred before his death

- CGT event A1, which arose from the sale of the property, happened within 2 years of the date of

  William's death.

The capital gain will be equal to the capital proceeds of $3 million less the cost base of the land in Annabelle's hands, being $1.5 million. The first element of Annabelle's cost base is the cost base of the land in the hands of William at the time of his death (s 128-15(4)).

Annabelle is treated as having acquired the land on the date of William's death, being 26 March 2024.

Annabelle is entitled to the 50% general discount as the property was held by her for at least 12 months (the sale occurred on 15 April 2025). However, it would appear that this cannot arise as the "taxpayer" (ie William) was dead by this point. Accordingly, any deferred capital gain would be disregarded permanently. There is no specific provision within ITAA97 which would allow the capital gain to be passed on to Annabelle.
