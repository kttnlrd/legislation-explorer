# 10-380 - Digital games tax offset

Worked example: Digital games tax offset Issue Meep Games Pty Ltd is an Australian resident company which specialises in digital games. Meep has been developing 2 new video games over the past several years and they are now almost complete. Details of the games are as follows: 1. A game that requires players to bet money in a simulated version of poker. 2. An adventure game that requires players to drive across a post-apocalyptic landscape, battling zombies and ruthless humans who wish to take over the player's car and kill them.

Both games can be played on mobile phones, via app, and are to be launched onto the market before 30 June 2025.

In relation to the poker game, development expenditure of $600,000 in the years ended 30 June 2024 and 30 June 2025 (combined) was incurred on overseas resident employees and independent contractor costs.

The work on this particular game was outsourced to Indian game developers meaning that about 90% of the work has been carried out by Indian-resident independent contractors, working either independently or for sub-contracted development firms. In addition, a further $50,000 in sales, marketing and social media costs has been incurred on the advance promotion and impending launch of the game.

In relation to the adventure game, it incurred direct development costs of $1.2 million across both years, spent entirely on Australian resident employees and independent sole-trader contractors, plus a further $200,000 of sales, marketing and social media costs that were incurred to promote the impending launch of the game.

Advise Meep whether it is eligible to apply for the digital games tax offset, the process for making a claim, and how much it is entitled to claim under the offset.

Solution The Digital Games Tax Offset (DGTO) is a measure which provides eligible game developers with a 30% refundable tax offset for qualifying Australian development expenditure from 1 July 2022 as determined by the Arts Minister. The legislation governing the DGTO is included in Div 378 ITAA97.

To be eligible for the offset, a company must spend a minimum of $500,000 on Australian development expenditure related to the development of digital games.

The DGTO sits on top of normal tax deductions, unlike other programs such as the R&D tax incentive.

For the purposes of the DGTO, a digital game is defined as a:

- game in electronic form which can be played on a portable electronic device or any other form of

  electronic display medium that allows interactive gaming, or

- component of a digital game if a foreign resident company, with no permanent establishment in

Australia, that owns or controls the rights to develop the game, engages an Australian developer that satisfies the conditions listed in ITAA97 s 378-10(1)(c).

Div 378 ITAA 1997 sets out the criteria to be eligible for the offset, as follows:

- The company must be an Australian resident for tax purposes or a foreign resident with a permanent

  establishment in Australia.

- To qualify for the offset, Australian gaming development expenditure must include:

  – completing a new digital game

– porting a completed digital game to a new platform, or – ongoing development work on completed digital games in an income year.

- The company must seek a completion certificate, a porting certificate, or an ongoing development

  certificate from the Minister for the Arts which states the amount of qualifying Australian development

  expenditure on which the offset will be determined.

- The digital game must be developed for entertainment and educational purposes for the general

  public and be primarily operated and played through the internet.

- Games with gambling, advertising or promotional elements will be ineligible for the offset. Games that

  contain elements that are likely to lead to the game being refused classification under the Classification

(Publications, Films and Computer Games) Act 1995 are also ineligible (s 378-25(7)(c)(ii)).

The amount of the DGTO will be 30% of the sum of all amounts as determined in the certificates issued by the Arts Minister. The maximum tax offset that can be claimed by a group of entities is $20 million per income year. The ATO guidance is that $66.7 million is the approximate limit of a game's budget that can be claimed, ie $20 million/0.3 = approx. $66.7 million.

However, the company can claim all qualifying Australian development expenditure in relation to that certificate across multiple income years prior to the game being completed or ported. For example, if the company applies for a completion certificate for the year ended 30 June 2025, the company can claim the DGTO on eligible expenditure from 1 July 2022–30 June 2025.

The company must apply for ongoing development certificates each income year, meaning the company can only claim the eligible expenditure for that income year. However, unlike the other 2 certificates, the ongoing development certificate allows companies to:

- Claim ongoing development costs for games completed or ported before 1 July 2022 (with the eligible

  expenditure consisting of costs incurred after that date), and

- Claim ongoing development costs for multiple digital games (completion and porting certificates limit

  the expenditure to one digital game only).

Can Meep make a claim and, if so, how much will it be entitled to?

Meep will be entitled to apply for a completion certificate for the adventure game and should be able to claim a tax offset (at 30%) in respect of the direct development costs incurred in 2023–24 and 2024–25, amounting to $1.2 million, provided the game has been classified or is likely to receive classification. This will produce a tax offset to Meep of $360,000 ($1.2 million x 30%). This is because the direct development costs were spent on Australian resident employees and independent sole-trader contractors. The additional expenditure of $200,000 does not qualify as eligible expenditure because it relates to sales, marketing and advertising costs, not games development expenditure.

The costs of development of the poker game do not qualify for the offset as this game is based on gambling and therefore excluded. In any case, the development expenditure was incurred on overseas employees and contractors, which does not meet the definition of qualifying Australian expenditure (and the $50,000 promotional expenditure is also excluded, see above). There is no point in applying for a completion certificate in respect of this game.

How does Meep make the claim?

To handle applications for the DGTO, a Digital Games Tax Offset Advisory Board (the Board) has been established to advise the Arts Minister about whether a certificate should be issued in relation to a game or slate of games and give its opinion on the amount of qualifying Australian development expenditure that will qualify for the DGTO.

Therefore, Meep will be able to apply for a certificate through the Board, either upon completion of the game or a provisional certificate. A provisional certificate is designed to provide an indication of whether the game is likely to be eligible for the offset but does not bind the Arts Minister to that decision until the game is completed.

The information to be included in an application is as follows:

- Particulars of the applicant (ie name, address etc).

- Particulars about the game (ie title, platform, genre).

- The primary purpose for the game being developed.

- Information on the game's connectivity with the internet.

- Outline of any elements of the game that could result in the game being refused classification or

  which come within the meaning of the Interactive Gambling Act 2001.

- Details of how and where the game is, or will be, available to the public.

- Details of the qualifying Australian development expenditure.

- Estimated dates of commencement and completion of Australian development expenditure.

- Details of any other grant that has been claimed in relation to the game.

- A comprehensive expenditure statement.

Once a certificate is obtained from the Minister for the Arts, Meep will be able to claim the DGTO in its 2024– 25 company tax return at item 24: Digital Games Tax Offset, with label A being for current year refundable DGTO amounts being claimed and Label B being the amount of total current year DGTO already being claimed or being claimed by related companies. The company will also need to include the amount from label A in the calculation statement at label E – "refundable tax offsets".
