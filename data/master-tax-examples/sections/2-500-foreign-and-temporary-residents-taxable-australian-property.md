# 2-500 - Foreign and temporary residents taxable Australian property

Worked example: Foreign and temporary residents: taxable Australian property Issue Dougal Kennedy is a resident of Scotland. Dougal and his wife relocated permanently to Western Australia from 1 October 2024, on a permanent residency visa. Dougal owns the following assets (all valued in A$) as at 1 October 2024:

- the family home in Scotland, which he intends to keep as an investment property. The property was

  originally acquired on 1 June 2010 at a cost equal to $500,000. As at 1 October 2024, it has a market

  value of $2 million

- shares in NatWest Plc (listed on the London Stock Exchange) worth $25,000

- 10% of the ordinary shares in Kennedy Construction Ltd, the family business, which specialises in

  home building throughout Scotland. The latest accounts (for the year ended 30 September 2024)

  suggest a market valuation of $200,000

- a further investment property in Western Australia, which he acquired in 2022 for $750,000, which is

  worth $850,000 on 1 October 2024

- a block of land in Perth, which he bought in 2023 for $400,000 and on which he is planning to build

  the family home, valued at $450,000 on 1 October 2024

- 8% of the shares in Kennedy Property Group Pty Ltd, an Australian property company which was set

  up in 2017 with his 3 brothers, who migrated to Perth at that time. This company owns assets worth

$4 million, of which $3.75 million are attributed to land which has been acquired in the Perth area. He acquired the shares in 2017 for $800 and their current market value is $240,000.

Which of the above assets are taxable Australian property and what are the implications for Dougal when he moves to Australia?

Solution An Australian resident is subject to CGT in respect of all their worldwide assets, regardless of where the assets are located, whereas a foreign resident is only subject to CGT if a CGT event happens to a CGT asset that is taxable Australian property (TAP).

Under s 855-45 ITAA97, when a taxpayer becomes an Australian resident (other than a temporary resident), each CGT asset they own is deemed to be acquired at its market value on that day, except for assets that:

- are TAP at that time

- were acquired before 20 September 1985 (ie pre-CGT assets).

This means that foreign residents who become residents will need to get a market valuation of any non-TAP assets that may be subject to CGT — assets such as foreign property, foreign shares, business interests in overseas businesses and personal items acquired overseas like jewellery — which were owned on the date that they became resident.

As a result, the taxpayer will only be eligible for the CGT discount if the asset is held for at least 12 months from the time of becoming a resident, even if it was actually owned before that time.

Where a taxpayer was previously an Australian resident and made the election under s 104-165(2) to treat an asset as being TAP, the date of acquisition of the asset will be its original date of acquisition, not the date of recommencing Australian residency (see ATO ID 2009/148).

Section 855-45 does not apply to people who come to Australia and become resident but are deemed to be temporary residents, as temporary residents are treated as non-residents for CGT purposes.

There are 5 categories of CGT assets that are TAP (s 855-15). These are: 1. "Taxable Australian real property" (TARP) (s 855-20) which includes:

- real property situated in Australia (including a lease of land situated in Australia), or

- a mining, quarrying or prospecting right to minerals, petroleum or quarry materials situated in

  Australia.

2. An "indirect Australian real property interest", which is basically an interest in Australian real property that is held indirectly through one or more interposed entities (s 855-25). More specifically, an indirect Australian real property interest exists where a foreign resident has an interest in an entity that satisfies both of the following 2 tests:

- the non-portfolio interest test — this test is satisfied where the direct interests held by the foreign

  resident (and their associates) in the entity are 10% or more (s 960-195)

- the principal asset test — this test is satisfied if the market value of the entity's TARP assets exceeds

  the entity's non-TARP assets. If the foreign resident's "total participation interest" in the entity is less

  than 10%, then the market value of the deemed TARP asset is taken to be nil for these purposes and

  the principal asset test would not be satisfied. As a result, the foreign resident's interest in the entity

  would not be TAP, which means it is not subject to CGT in Australia.

The concept of an "indirect" interest in Australian real property ensures that the disposal of an interest in Australian real property is subject to Australian CGT regardless of whether the interest is held directly or indirectly by the foreign resident. A typical example would be a company or unit trust in which a foreign resident and their associates own more than 10% of the shares or units and whose assets consist of at least 50% Australian land and buildings. 3. A CGT asset that a taxpayer has used at any time in carrying on a business through a permanent establishment in Australia (not already covered by Items 1, 2 or 5). 4. An option or right to acquire an asset that is covered by Items 1, 2 or 3. 5. A CGT asset that is covered by s 104-165(3), which is about choosing to disregard a capital gain or loss made on ceasing to be an Australian resident (see ¶2-480).

Applying the above to Dougal, the following can be concluded:

- The family home in Scotland is not TAP and therefore it is deemed to be acquired for CGT purposes

  at its market value on 1 October 2024 ($2 million). This becomes the base cost of the family home

  in any future capital gains computation, although no capital gain arises merely by virtue of becoming

  resident. The property must be retained for 12 months after the date of arrival in order to qualify for the

CGT 50% general discount. Both the absence rule and the main residence exemption can theoretically apply to an overseas property sold after becoming a resident (see Taxation Determination TD 95/7).

- The shares in NatWest Plc are not TAP and are deemed to be acquired for their market value on 1

  October 2024 ($25,000).

- The shares in Kennedy Construction Ltd are not TAP and are deemed to be acquired for their market

  value on 1 October 2024. A market valuation is therefore necessary. Using the values as per the latest

  set of accounts as a guide suggests that the shares are currently worth $200,000.

- The investment property in Western Australia will always be TAP, going back to the date of original

  acquisition. The cost base of the property is therefore $750,000 and the date of acquisition is the date it

  was originally acquired in 2022.

- The block of land in Perth will always be TAP, going back to the date of original acquisition in 2023.

  Therefore, its cost base is $400,000.

- The shares in Kennedy Property Group Pty Ltd are TAP. Although Dougal owns only 8% of the

  shares (which appears to be less than the 10% which is the threshold for the non-portfolio test), the

  interest owned by associates needs to be taken into account. Therefore, including the shares owned

  by his brothers, Dougal owns (with his associates) all 100% of the shares. The company satisfies the

  principal assets test as the market value of its TARP assets ($3.75 million) exceeds the value of its

  non-TARP assets ($250,000). As both tests are satisfied, the value of Dougal's shares are TAP, with a

  cost base (at the date of original acquisition in 2017) of $800.

*Refs: s 855-45, s 104-165(2), s 855-15), s 855-20), s 855-25), s 960-195), s 104-165(3)*
