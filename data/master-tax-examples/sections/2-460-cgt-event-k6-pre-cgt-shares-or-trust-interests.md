# 2-460 - CGT event K6 Pre-CGT shares or trust interests

Worked example: CGT event K6: Pre-CGT shares or trust interests Issue William Owens owns all the shares in Rubble Pty Ltd, a mining exploration company. He acquired all the shares pre-CGT. All of the assets of Rubble Pty Ltd were post-CGT, except for a mining tenement in Queensland which was pre-CGT. On 1 June 2024, William disposed of his entire shareholding for $1,070,000. Rubble Pty Ltd owned the following assets and had liabilities of $50,000 (which related solely to the post-CGT property): Post-CGT ($)                                          Pre-CGT ($) Market value                                            820,000                                               200,000 Cost base                                               400,000                                               100,000 Market value excess                                     420,000                                               100,000 The capital proceeds of $1,020,000 represented a premium of $70,000 over the net value of Rubble Pty Ltd and reflected the premium paid by the purchaser to remove a competitor from the market.

Assess whether CGT event K6 arises on the disposal of Willliam's shares and, if so, work out the capital gain.

Solution The usual situation with a CGT event involving assets acquired before 20 September 1985 (pre-CGT assets) is that any capital gain or loss is disregarded.

However, a taxable capital gain can arise under CGT event K6 if certain CGT events happen in relation to a pre-CGT interest in shares in a company or an interest in a trust where that company or trust owns post-CGT assets (for example, where a pre-CGT company later acquired a post-CGT building).

For CGT event K6 to happen, the following factors must be present (s 104-230(1) ITAA97):

- the taxpayer must own shares in a company or an interest in a trust acquired before 20 September

  1985 (the pre-CGT interests)

- one of CGT events A1, C2, E1, E2, E3, E5, E6, E7, E8, J1 and K3 must happen in relation to the

  shares or trust interest, and

- there must not be a roll-over in relation to the other (non-K6) CGT event.

The time of CGT event K6 is when the relevant CGT event happens in relation to the shares or interest (s 104-230(5)).

However, s 104-230(2) states that CGT event K6 only happens if:

- in the case of directly held property, the market value of post-CGT property (other than trading stock)

  is at least 75% of the net value of the entity, just before the CGT event happened, or

- in the case of indirectly held property, the market value of post-CGT interests in property owned

  through interposed companies or trusts (other than trading stock) is at least 75% of the net value of the

  entity.

The "net value" of an entity (s 995-1(1) ITAA97) is the amount by which the sum of the market values of the assets of the entity exceeds the sum of its liabilities. Paragraph 94 of Taxation Ruling TR 2004/18 states that the term "assets" refers to "property according to ordinary concepts as well as the other economic resources of the company that it is capable of turning to account, even if they are not property".

Similarly, "liabilities" include legally enforceable debts due for payment and presently existing obligations to pay either a certain or ascertainable sum, but do not include contingent liabilities, future obligations or expectancies.

William determines that CGT event K6 applies because the market value of the post-CGT property (ie $820,000) is at least 75% of the net value of the company of $970,000 (ie $820,000 post-CGT assets + $200,000 pre-CGT assets – liabilities of $50,000).

In this case, the market value of the post-CGT property is approximately 85% of the net value of the company (ie $820,000 ÷ $970,000).

When CGT event K6 occurs, the capital gain is equal to that part of the capital proceeds that is reasonably attributable to the amount by which the market value of the post-CGT property (other than trading stock) is more than the cost base of that property (s 104-230(6)).

A capital loss cannot arise from a K6 event.

Taxation Ruling TR 2004/18 provides a procedure for working out the capital gain. This document was recently revised and is available in draft form as TR 2004/18DC.

In the case of a single tier company structure (such as this), it involves a 2-step process.

Step 1 involves determining how much of the capital proceeds actually relates to the post-CGT property. This can be determined as: market value of post-CGT property Step 1 amount                      =capital proceeds                            x market value of all property where:

- market value of post-CGT property equals the sum of the market value of the post-CGT property

  taken into account under s 104-230(2)(a)

- market value of all property equals the sum of the market value of all property (including pre-CGT

  acquired property and trading stock) owned by the company.

Step 2 involves determining how much of the Step 1 amount relates to the amount by which the market value of the post-CGT property exceeds the cost bases of that property. The amount of the CGT event K6 capital gain is determined under Step 2 as: market value excess Step 1 amount                      × market value of post-CGT property where market value excess means the excess of the market value of property taken into account under s 104-230(6) over the sum of the cost bases of that property.

In a situation where a capital gain calculated under Step 2 exceeds the market value excess, the capital gain would be limited to the market value excess.

Therefore, William can use the ATO's 2-step approach as stipulated in TR 2004/18 to calculate his capital gain under CGT event K6, as follows: Step 1 — Capital proceeds relating to post-CGT property In this case, William can demonstrate that the liabilities relate solely to the post-CGT property and, therefore, can determine the capital proceeds relating to the post-CGT property as: Capital proceeds relating to post-CGT property less liabilities = ($1,070,000 x $820,000 / $1,020,000) – $50,000 = $810,196 Step 2 — Allocating the Step 1 amount to the market value excess The capital proceeds relating to the post-CGT property (as worked out in Step 1) must now be allocated between the cost base of the property and the market value excess of that property on a reasonable basis.

This can be determined as follows: Step 1 amount x market value excess of post-CGT property / Market value of post-CGT property = $810,196 x $420,000 / $820,000 = $414,978 As the capital gain does not exceed the market value excess ($420,000), the capital gain under CGT event K6 is $414,978.
