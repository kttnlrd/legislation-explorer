# 2-540 - Interaction of the non-arms length income and CGT provisions in an SMSF

Worked example: Interaction of the non-arm's length income and CGT provisions in an SMSF Issue The Hinchcliffe Family SMSF disposes of certain assets during the year ended 30 June 2025.

It sells a warehouse which it has used to generate rental income for proceeds of $2 million, having acquired it for $1 million 5 years ago. Both the sale and the acquisition involved unrelated third parties.

It also sold a small office block to a relative of a member of the fund for $6 million. However, the market value of the property at the date of disposal was only $2 million. The cost base of the office block, which was originally acquired 6 years ago, was $1 million. The SMSF acquired the office block from an unrelated third party.

There are no other assessable contributions during the year.

The SMSF had a brought forward capital loss of $300,000, arising from the arm's length disposal of listed company shares in an earlier year.

Explain the CGT implications of the disposals made in the year ended 30 June 2025.

Solution The disposal of the warehouse results in a capital gain of $1 million.

The disposal of the office block is at a non-arm's length (NAL) value and therefore it might be assumed that Hinchcliffe is forced to substitute the arm's length market value for sales proceeds (s 116-30 ITAA97). This would produce a gain of $1,000,000 for the office block, with an additional $1,000,000 capital gain for the warehouse. The resulting $2,000,000 capital gain could then be reduced by the brought forward capital losses and then discounted by the general discount as it applies to SMSFs (1/3rd), to give a net capital gain of $1,133,333 (and a tax liability at 15% of $170,000).

However, where there is non-arm's length income (NALI) in an SMSF, this rule has been modified. Under s 116-30(2C), there is no substitution of the market value of the asset and therefore no reduction in the capital proceeds.

This is clarified in Taxation Determination TD 2024/5, which outlines the ATO's view on the interaction between the non-arm's length income and the CGT provisions.

Section 295-550(1) ITAA97 defines NALI of a complying superannuation fund to include an amount of ordinary or statutory income obtained because of a scheme in which the parties were not dealing at arm's length. The statutory income of a superannuation fund includes capital gains made by a fund, and this is calculated under the method statement in s 102-5(1).

The ATO states in para 7 of TD 2024/5 that a capital gain resulting from a NAL scheme is considered NALI under s 295-550(1) where either of the following apply:

- the amount of the capital gain is more than the amount the fund might have been expected to derive if

  the parties had been acting at arm's length in relation to the scheme, or

- for SMSFs or small APRA funds — in gaining or producing the capital gain, non-arm's length

  expenditure (NALE) is incurred (including nil expenditure) in respect of a CGT asset that is less than

  the amount of a loss, outgoing or expenditure that the fund might have been expected to incur if those

  parties were dealing with each other at arm's length in relation to the scheme.

Therefore, s 295-550(1)(a) applies to the $5 million capital gain as this is more than might have been expected to have been derived if the parties had been dealing with each other at arm's length in relation to the scheme.

The ATO notes in para 9 of TD 2024/5 that the amount of statutory income from capital gains that is NALI cannot exceed the superannuation fund's net capital gain calculated under the method statement in s 102-5(1) for the relevant income year, meaning that where the amount of NALI capital gains exceeds the net capital gain (ie once losses and discounts are applied in accordance with the method statement), the amount of NALI from capital gains is equal to the net capital gain of the superannuation fund.

TD 2024/5 also confirms that the interaction between the NALI and CGT provisions leads to a NAL capital gain "tainting" an arm's length capital gain.
