# 3-052 - FBT and dual cab utes

Worked example: FBT and dual cab utes Issue Dan Walker is provided with the use of a brand new Toyota HiLux SR5 from his employer, Hillside Pty Ltd.

The vehicle has a seating capacity of 5 and has a payload of 995 kg. Dan advises that he does not use the vehicle privately — he has his own vehicle for private use — however, he does take the vehicle home at night, where it is garaged, and he drives to and from work in it each day.

The vehicle has the company logo marked on the side and the company also has strict policies in place to enforce the "no private use" rule amongst employees.

One of Dan's colleagues, David Hewlett, is provided with a Toyota HiLux Workmate (4x4) by Hillside. This has seating capacity of just 2 (and is therefore a single cab) and a payload of 1,240 kg. Everything else about the vehicle is identical to Dan's situation.

Will Hillside be liable for FBT in relation to Dan's dual cab or David's single cab or both?

Solution Generally, an employer will be liable for FBT where an employee uses an employer provided car for private purposes or the car is available for the employee's private use. However, under ss 8(2) and 47(6) FBTAA, a liability for FBT will not arise where the private use of certain vehicles by employees is limited to certain work-related travel and non-work- related use is minor, infrequent and irregular, including the occasional use of the vehicle to remove domestic rubbish (see also ¶3-050). Work-related travel is travel between the employee's residence and place of work or other place at which employment duties are performed and any travel that is incidental to travel in the course of that employment.

Guidance on the operation of these rules is included in MT 2024, Fringe benefits tax: dual cab vehicles eligibility for exemption where private use is limited to certain work-related travel.

Vehicles that qualify for the work-related use exemption are those that are not classified as a car, ie vehicles other than those which are designed to carry a load of less than one tonne and fewer than 9 passengers.

Alternatively, a vehicle may qualify for the exemption if, while classified as a car, it is a taxi, panel van, utility truck or any other road vehicle that, while designed to carry a load of less than one tonne, is not designed for the principal purpose of carrying passengers.

The rules regarding the exemption are focussed on the vehicle's load carrying capacity and its use.

Therefore, the signage on the vehicle is not relevant and nor is the company's policy regarding private use (although the latter is relevant to whether or not the ATO applies compliance resources, see previous example).

So, dual cabs will qualify for the work-related use exemption only if either of the following tests are satisfied: a) they are designed to carry a load of one tonne or more, or more than 8 passengers (s 47(6)), or b) while having a designed load capacity of less than one tonne, they are not designed for the principal purpose of carrying passengers (s 8(2)).

Current model dual cabs can have a maximum seating capacity of 7 (although it is typically 5) and, on the face of it, could not qualify under the second limb, at b) above.

Whether a particular vehicle is designed to carry a load of one tonne or more is worked out using the same methodology as applied in determining the applicability of the expensive car depreciation limit for income tax purposes. Therefore, the load capacity of a motor vehicle is its gross vehicle weight as specified on the compliance plate by the manufacturer, minus the basic kerb weight of the vehicle. The basic kerb weight is the weight of the vehicle with a full tank of fuel, oil and coolant together with spare wheel, tools (including jack) and installed options but excluding the weight of goods or occupants.

Where a particular dual cab model has a load capacity of one tonne or more, it will be a commercial vehicle and the FBT exemption will apply if the employee's use of the vehicle during a particular FBT year consists solely of eligible work-related travel or private travel which is minor, infrequent and irregular.

A dual cab that has a designed load carrying capacity of less than one tonne may still qualify for the work- related use exemption, under s 8(2) above, if the vehicle is not designed for the principal purpose of carrying passengers, ie the majority of the load capacity is not attributable to passenger carrying capacity.

The designed passenger carrying capacity is determined by multiplying the designed seating capacity (including the driver's) by 68 kg, which is the figure used in the Australian Design Rules.

If the total passenger weight exceeds the remaining "load" capacity, the vehicle is treated as being designed for the principal purpose of carrying passengers and as such is ineligible for the work-related use exemption.

In this case, the Toyota HiLux has a payload capacity of 995 kgs, 340 kgs (5 x 68 kgs) of which is set aside by its designed passenger carrying capacity, leaving 665 kgs available for the transport of goods or other freight (this is similar to the methodology used to determine the applicability of LCT to utes, explained in ¶12-340). Therefore, the vehicle is not designed for the principal purpose of carrying passengers and it can – potentially – qualify for the exemption.

There used to be a list of eligible dual cabs which qualified for the exemption attached to MT 2024 but this was removed on 22 October 2008. The ATO advised at that time that a list of eligible dual cabs would be provided on their website but no such list is currently available.

Single cab utility trucks, such as the Toyota HiLux Workmate (4x4), qualify for the exemption as they are not designed for the principal purpose of carrying passengers (they have only 2 seats). In addition, this particular vehicle has a carrying capacity of over 1 tonne. Therefore, provided the employee's private use of the vehicle is limited to certain work-related travel and non-work- related use that is minor, infrequent and irregular (including the occasional use of the vehicle to remove domestic rubbish), they will be exempt from FBT.
