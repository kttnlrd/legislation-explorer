# 2-520 - Removal of the CGT general discount for foreign and temporary residents

Worked example: Removal of the CGT general discount for foreign and temporary residents Issue Anna Johnson is an Australian citizen who moved to London to take up a job with a large investment bank on 1 July 2019. She remains in London with her husband, Paul, who is also an Australian citizen. She met him while working at the bank but Paul has lived in London for longer — since 1 July 2010.

Anna owns an investment property back in Australia, on the Mornington Peninsula. This property was acquired on 1 July 2015 for $400,000 and is now valued at $1.3 million.

Paul also owns an investment property in Sydney, acquired on 1 July 2008 for $600,000. The market value of the property is currently $2.1 million. The property was independently valued as at 8 May 2012 at $1 million.

They have recently reappraised their finances and, having no intention of returning to Australia, have decided to sell both properties in order to buy a better family home in London. The date of contract of disposal for both properties is 30 June 2025.

Explain the Australian CGT consequences of both transactions.

Solution As the properties are located in Australia (and are therefore taxable Australian property (TAP)), they will be subject to CGT on their disposal, even though both Anna and Paul are non-residents for Australian tax purposes.

From 8 May 2012, non-resident individuals are denied access to the CGT general discount, to the extent that part (or all) of their capital gains have accrued while they were foreign residents for tax purposes or temporary residents.

For individuals with a direct capital gain, the rules apply where both of the following conditions are met (s 115-105(2) ITAA97):

- the CGT event (resulting in the direct capital gain) happens after 8 May 2012

- the individual is a foreign (or temporary) resident during some, or all of, so much of the "discount

  testing period" (ie the period starting on the day the asset was acquired and ending on the day of the

CGT event) as falls after 8 May 2012.

Where the rules apply for an individual with a direct capital gain (eg where the individual was a foreign or temporary resident with a capital gain that happened after 8 May 2012), s 115-115 sets out 4 different methods to work out the CGT general discount percentage, as follows:

- Method 1: Where the asset is acquired after 8 May 2012.

- Method 2: Where the asset was acquired on or before 8 May 2012, and the individual was an

  Australian resident (but not a temporary resident) on 8 May 2012.

- Method 3: Where the asset was acquired on or before 8 May 2012, the individual was a foreign (or

  temporary) resident on 8 May 2012, and the market value method is chosen.

- Method 4: Where the asset was acquired on or before 8 May 2012, the individual was a foreign (or

  temporary) resident on 8 May 2012, and the market value method is not chosen.

In this case, Anna must consider Method 1 (in relation to the property on the Mornington Peninsula) and Paul must consider Method 3 (in relation to the apartment in Sydney).

Method 1: Where the asset is acquired after 8 May 2012 Where the relevant CGT asset (eg real property) is acquired by an individual after 8 May 2012, the 50% discount percentage must be reduced to the extent the individual was a foreign (or temporary) resident during the period they held the asset (s 115-115(2)). In other words, the discount percentage is recalculated to allow the individual to receive the CGT discount for the number of days they were an Australian tax resident (denying the CGT discount for the number of days they were a foreign or temporary resident), as follows: Days individual was a resident during discount testing period (but not a temporary resident) / 2 x Days in discount testing period For example, this means that:

- if the individual was an Australian resident (but not a temporary resident) for the entire period they

  held the relevant asset, the discount percentage will remain at 50%

- if the individual was an Australian resident (but not a temporary resident) for half the period they held

  the relevant asset, the discount percentage will be reduced to 25%, and

- if the individual was a foreign resident (or a temporary resident) for the entire period they held the

  relevant asset, the discount percentage will be 0%.

- the individual was a foreign resident or temporary resident on 8 May 2012

- the asset's market value on 8 May 2012 exceeded the asset's cost base at this time (ie there was an

  accrued capital gain in respect of the asset as at 8 May 2012), and

- the individual chooses to apply this method (referred to as the "market value" method).

In this case, the individual's discount percentage for the discount capital gain is calculated as follows (s 115-115(4) or (5)):

- Where the accrued capital gain as at 8 May 2012 is equal to or greater than the discount capital

  gain from the asset — the discount percentage is 50%. This ensures that there is no reduction in the

  discount percentage where the value of the asset has fallen or remained the same since 8 May 2012

(ie between 8 May 2012 and the end of the discount testing period or when the CGT event occurs in respect of the asset).

- Where, as here, the accrued capital gain as at 8 May 2012 is less than the discount capital gain from

  the asset (ie there has been a further net increase in the value of the asset after 8 May 2012) — the

  discount percentage is calculated by reference to the following formula:

(Excess + [Shortfall x Number of apportionable days that you were an eligible resident/number of apportionable days]) / (2 x Amount of the discount capital gain) where: – the excess means the capital gain that accrued up until 8 May 2012 (ie the accrued capital gain as at 8 May 2012) – the shortfall means the capital gain that is attributable to the period after 8 May 2012 (ie discount capital gain less the accrued capital gain as at 8 May 2012) – the number of apportionable days are the number of days after 8 May 2012 during the discount testing period (ie up until when the CGT event occurs in relation to the asset) – eligible resident means an Australian resident who is not a temporary resident.

Accordingly, Paul's discount percentage would be calculated under s 115-115(5) as follows: ($400,000 + [$1.1m x 0/4,801]) / (2 x 1.5m) = 13.33% where:

- the excess is $400,000 (ie the accrued capital gain as at 8 May 2012)

- the shortfall is $1.1 million (ie $1.5 million total capital gain less $400,000 accrued capital gain as at 8

  May 2012)

- the number of apportionable days that Paul was an eligible resident after 8 May 2012 is zero

- the number of apportionable days (ie days after 8 May 2012 during which Paul held/owned the

  property — 8 May 2012 to 30 June 2025) is 4,801.

Note that the upshot of this very complex calculation is that Paul is entitled to a full 50% discount for the gain that accrued till 8 May 2012 ($200,000) and no discount at all for the increase in value that accrued after that date (as he was a foreign resident). As the total gain is $1,500,000, applying this discount (13.33% or $200,000) results in a net capital gain of $1,300,000.

Note that in order to use this method, the taxpayer must have a record of the market valuation of the asset as at 8 May 2012. If the taxpayer does not have one (and does not want to obtain one, even a historical valuation), then Method 4 must be used, which means that no CGT discount is available for any of the capital gain that accrues up to 8 May 2012. Consequently, the CGT discount percentage is apportioned by reference to the number of days the individual was an Australian resident after 8 May 2012 as a proportion of the total time that the asset was held by the individual (which will almost certainly lead to a lower discount percentage). Paul is advised not to adopt this method.
