# 7-278 - Total superannuation balance and the bring forward of non-concessional contributions

Worked example: Total superannuation balance and the bring forward of non-concessional contributions Issue Jane is 55 years old. On 1 March 2025, she sold an investment property she owned in South Africa. She now wishes to use some of the proceeds of that sale to boost her superannuation. She would like to make a non-concessional contribution of $240,000 into her superannuation fund before 30 June 2025 and to make a further non-concessional contribution of $120,000 in the year ended 30 June 2026. She has not previously determined her non-concessional contributions cap for a year by bringing forward non-concessional contributions from a later year. At 30 June 2024, her total superannuation balance was $1.5 million and, at 30 June 2025, it stood at $1.9 million. Advise Jane on the feasibility of her plan. Solution The concept of a "total superannuation balance" applies from 1 July 2017 as a method for valuing an individual's total superannuation interests at a particular time and for limiting the amount of superannuation contributions that can be made. The concept was introduced by the Treasury Laws Amendment (Fair and Sustainable Superannuation) Act 2016 (Act No 81 of 2016). An individual's total superannuation balance (ITAA97 s 307-230) at a particular time is calculated as the sum of:
• the accumulation phase value of their superannuation interests that are not in the retirement phase
• if the individual has a superannuation income stream that is in the retirement phase, their transfer
balance or modified transfer balance, and
• the amount of any roll-over superannuation benefit not already reflected in the accumulation phase
value of their superannuation interests or their transfer balance.
The bring forward to a year (the first year) of the non-concessional contributions cap from the next 2 years
(the second and third years) is available for an individual if the following conditions are satisfied (ITAA97 s
292-85(3)).
• Their non-concessional contributions for the first year exceed the general non-concessional
contributions cap for that year ($120,000 for 2024–25 and $110,000 for 2023–24).
• Their total superannuation balance immediately before the start of the first year is less than the
general transfer balance cap for the year ($1.9 million for both 2024–25 and 2023–24).
• They are under 75 years (67 years until 30 June 2022) at any time in the first year.
Eligible individuals may be able to bring forward 2 years' worth of entitlements to make non-concessional
contributions, and so can make 3 years' non-concessional contributions in one year without exceeding their
non-concessional contributions cap.
For 2024–25, an individual who is eligible to bring forward 2 years of entitlements would normally have a
non-concessional contributions cap of $360,000, subject to their total superannuation balance not being too
high. For those whose total superannuation balance is approaching the $1.9 million cap, special rules apply
whereby the amount of the cap an individual may bring forward is:
• 3 times the annual cap, that is $360,000, over 3 years if their total superannuation balance is less
than $1.68 million
• 2 times the annual cap, that is $240,000, over 2 years if their total superannuation balance is above
$1.68 million and less than $1.79 million, and
• nil, if their superannuation balance is $1.79 million or above — this would mean no bring-forward
period and the general non-concessional contributions cap would apply (ITAA97 s 292-85(5)).
In this case, as Jane has not previously determined her non-concessional contributions cap by bringing
forward non-concessional contributions from later years, she has a $360,000 (3 times the annual figure) cap
available for 2024–25. This is because:
• she satisfies the age requirement, and
• her total superannuation balance, which is $1.5 million as at 30 June 2024, is not too high.
Jane can therefore do what she wants, that is, to bring forward $240,000 (being 2 years' worth of
entitlements to make non-concessional contributions) for the year ended 30 June 2025.
The question states that, immediately before the start of the 2025–26 income year (as at 30 June 2025),
Jane's total superannuation balance was $1.9 million. In that case, her cap for 2025–26 would be
reduced to nil and she could not make any non-concessional contributions for the year. If the value of her
total superannuation balance were to fall during 2025–26, she may be able to make non-concessional
contributions in 2026–27 depending on her balance at 30 June 2026.
