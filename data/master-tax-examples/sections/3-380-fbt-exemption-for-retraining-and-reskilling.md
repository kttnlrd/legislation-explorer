# 3-380 - FBT exemption for retraining and reskilling

Worked Example: FBT exemption for retraining and reskilling Issue Ruby Pty Ltd (Ruby) is a company based in Australia, with retail stores selling office supplies and stationery across Australia.

On 20 March 2025, due to a decline in foot traffic and sales from its retail shopfronts, Ruby announced a series of store closures over the next year as it transitions its business model to online sales. Employees across the company were advised their positions would be made redundant within this period.

In relation to these redundancies, Ruby is complying with all relevant obligations under industrial relations law and is offering affected employees the opportunity to reskill for future employment.

The following employees are affected by the announcements: 1. Roger is a store manager in Sydney who wishes to undertake a gardening course to gain future employment as a horticulturalist. Roger also plans to use the skills and knowledge acquired to cultivate a vegetable patch at his home. However, this is not the main reason why he wishes to undertake this training. Ruby agrees to support Roger's studies. Roger accepts a full fee-paying place at the Australian National University to undertake a Science of Gardening degree. Ruby covers the costs associated with the degree by reimbursing Roger for the tuition fees. Roger is made redundant from his position at Ruby. After his departure, he struggles to find employment as a horticulturalist as planned and instead, after the course ends, he takes up a new job at a different retailer that is not related to his studies. 2. Philip is a store manager in Melbourne who wishes to undertake a diploma in information technology at RMIT for the purpose of gaining future employment as a web developer. Upon hearing about Philip's study plans, Ruby offers to redeploy Philip to its web development team after he has completed his studies to work on the development and maintenance of its online sales platform. Philip accepts. Ruby covers the costs associated with the diploma by paying the tuition fees directly to RMIT. Ruby also reimburses Philip for the cost of the textbooks he requires for the course. On completion of his studies in December 2025, Philip is redeployed to his new position within Ruby in its web development team. 3. Deidre is a buyer with the company based in Brisbane who wishes to undertake a short course in illustration to gain future employment as an illustrator for children's books. The course involves an intensive workshop over one weekend in Melbourne in late October. Ruby is satisfied that the course is for the primary purpose of gaining future employment as an illustrator and covers the costs associated with the short course by reimbursing Deidre for the tuition fees, as well as reasonable travel and accommodation expenses for the intensive workshop in Melbourne. In November, after Deidre has completed the short course, Ruby decides to retain a limited buying team to service its new online hub, and rescinds Deidre's redundancy. She agrees to remain employed as a buyer.

Advise Ruby Pty Ltd of the FBT implications of these transactions.

Solution Section 58ZE of the FBTAA provides employers with an exemption from FBT if they provide training or education to a redundant, or soon to be redundant, employee for the purpose of assisting that employee to gain new employment.

Employers can apply the exemption to retraining and reskilling benefits provided on or after 2 October 2020.

A benefit is exempt from FBT if all of the following conditions are satisfied:

- the benefit is provided in, or in respect of, the tax year for education or training undertaken by an

  employee of an employer

- the employee is redundant

- the employer has complied with any obligations under the Fair Work Act 2009 that applies in relation

  to the redundancy (such as any requirements to consult about the redundancy), and

- the education or training is for the primary purpose of enabling the employee to gain or produce salary

  or wages in respect of any employment to which the education or training relates.

The benefit must be provided in respect of education or training undertaken by an employee of the employer.

This covers the actual provision of the education or training (such as reimbursing or paying the course fees or giving training) as well as associated costs (such as related course materials, textbooks, travel and accommodation).

For the purposes of the FBT exemption, an employee is redundant if the employee's employer no longer requires, or reasonably expects to no longer require, the employee's job to be performed by anyone because of changes in the operational requirements of the employer's business or undertaking (FBTAA s 58ZE(2)).

However, the concept of redundancy for the purposes of the FBT exemption is broad. It covers circumstances where an employee is made redundant in one part of the employer's business but is able to be redeployed to another part of its business (or within an associate's business). It also covers circumstances where the employer reasonably expects the employee to be redundant but has not yet been made redundant. If circumstances change, and the employee is not made redundant (eg because the employer restructures its business in such a way so as to retain the employee), the employer will still be entitled to the FBT exemption for any benefits provided during the period throughout which the redundancy test has been satisfied.

In addition, the employer must also be satisfied that the education or training is being undertaken for the primary purpose of enabling the employee to gain or produce salary or wages in respect of any employment to which the education or training relates. Education or training that is undertaken primarily for personal reasons will not satisfy this test. There must be a direct nexus between the education or training and the expected employment the employee is planning to pursue.

Some benefits are excluded from the FBT exemption including:

- benefits provided under a salary packaging arrangement

- benefits that are a payment or other amount covered by ITAA97 s 26-20(1) (such as student

  contributions towards a Commonwealth supported place at a higher education institution)

- benefits that involve primary and secondary school education courses, and

- benefits provided to relatives of certain employers (or certain individuals that are connected to the

  employer).

Retraining and reskilling benefits that are exempt from FBT are not included in an employer's FBT return or their employee's reportable fringe benefits amount.

Employers must keep a record of all training and education provided to redundant, or soon to be redundant, employees if they intend on claiming the exemption. Their business records should also show that they meet the requirements for claiming the exemption.

Applying all the above to Ruby Pty Ltd, the law applies to the 3 individuals as follows: Roger This benefit is covered by the FBT exemption for retraining and reskilling. Although Roger plans to use the skills and knowledge acquired to cultivate a vegetable patch at his home, this is not the main reason why he wishes to undertake this training. Accordingly, as Ruby is satisfied that this training is for the primary purpose of gaining future employment, the FBT exemption will be available. The fact that Roger ultimately secures new employment in retail does not prevent the FBT exemption from applying as at the time of the course, Roger was planning to become a horticulturalist.

If Roger had instead accepted a Commonwealth supported place for this degree, it would not have been covered by the FBT exemption for retraining and reskilling, meaning Ruby would be liable for FBT on any benefit provided to Roger in respect of this course.

Philip These benefits (both the tuition fees and textbooks) are covered by the FBT exemption for retraining and reskilling. Philip is made redundant in one part of Ruby's business but is able to be redeployed to another part of its business and hence meets the criteria for redundancy (FBTAA s 58ZE(2)).

Deidre These benefits (the tuition fees, as well as reasonable travel and accommodation expenses) are covered by the FBT exemption for retraining and reskilling. The change in circumstances which sees Deidre's redundancy being rescinded after the course does not affect the FBT exemption previously claimed.
