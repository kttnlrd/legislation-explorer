# 2-560 - Earnouts

Worked example: Earnouts Issue Diana sold all the shares in her business, Perseus Pty Ltd, to Persephone Pty Ltd in March 2023. The cost base of the shares was $600,000.

Under the sale arrangement, Diana received an upfront payment of $1 million at the time of sale and a right to 2 future payments of up to $100,000 in March 2024 and March 2025, provided the turnover of Perseus exceeded an agreed threshold during 2023 and 2024, respectively.

Perseus's turnover exceeded the agreed threshold in 2023 and so Persephone paid Diana a further $100,000 in March 2024.

Finally, in July 2024, Persephone decided it would prefer to end the arrangement immediately. It offered to pay Diana $50,000 if she would agree to forgo her right to further payments under the earnout agreement and Diana agreed to this offer. She was therefore paid $50,000 in July 2024.

Consider the tax consequences for Diana of the above transactions. What is her total capital gain and in which year is it recorded? Assuming she meets the basic conditions, is she entitled to the small business CGT concessions, on which amounts, and when must any claim be made?

Solution An earnout arrangement is where a business is sold for a set amount plus a percentage of profits in the future for a specified period.

Under the sale arrangement, Diana received an upfront payment of $1 million at the time of sale and a right to 2 future payments of up to $100,000 in March 2024 and March 2025, provided the turnover of Perseus exceeded an agreed threshold during 2023 and 2024, respectively. This right to future income is referred to as a look-through earnout right.

The CGT rules provide that capital gains and losses arising in respect of look-through earnout rights are disregarded and, instead, payments received or paid under the earnout arrangements affect the capital proceeds and cost base of the underlying asset or assets to which the earnout arrangement relates.

This tax treatment of earnout rights will result in the amount of the capital gain or loss made on the original disposal of the asset changing because of financial benefits provided or received in subsequent income years.

According to s 118-565 ITAA97, a look-through earnout right should satisfy all the following conditions:

- it is created as part of an arrangement for disposal of the business or its assets (ie the disposal must

  cause CGT event A1 to happen)

- it is a right to future financial benefits which are not reasonably ascertainable at the time the right is

  created

- the financial benefits under the right are contingent on, and reasonably related to, the future economic

  performance of the asset (or a related business) and the value of the benefits reasonably related to this

  performance

- it is created as part of arrangements entered into on an arm's length basis, and

- all the financial benefits that can be provided under the right must be provided over a period ending

  no later than 5 years after the end of the income year in which the relevant CGT event occurs. The

5-year requirement will be breached if the agreement includes an option for the parties to extend the period over which financial benefits are provided or to enter into a new agreement providing for the continuation of substantially similar financial benefits.

As the financial benefits may be provided up to 5 years after the end of the income year in which the CGT event occurred, the period of review for the income year in which the CGT event occurred may have passed before the taxpayer has received the financial benefits requiring the amendment. As a result, the period of review is extended for all of a taxpayer's tax-related liabilities that can be affected by the character of the look-through earnout right to the latter of:

- the period of review that would normally apply, and

- 4 years after the end of the final income year in which financial benefits can be provided.

This extension also applies to taxpayers' rights to object where they are dissatisfied with an assessment.

Diana's capital proceeds for the sale of the shares are $1 million — the total of the upfront payment she has received. Diana has also received the right to future payments which she would generally need to include in the capital proceeds, but as this is a look-through earnout right, the value of the right is disregarded when working out the capital proceeds.

As Diana has a cost base for the Perseus shares of $600,000, she has a capital gain of $400,000 (the capital proceeds of $1 million less the cost base of $600,000) because of the sale.

As a result of the first earnout payment of $100,000, the capital proceeds from the sale of the Perseus shares at the time of the original CGT event are now considered to be $1.1 million — made up of the $1 million initial payment and the $100,000 payment she received in March 2024. She has now made a capital gain of $500,000 (capital proceeds of $1.1 million less the cost base of $600,000) because of the sale.

The financial benefit provided to terminate the look-through earnout right is treated in the same way as a financial benefit provided under the right. Therefore, the payment of $50,000 received by Diana in July 2024 is also treated as additional capital proceeds.

As a result, Diana's total capital proceeds for the sale are $1.15 million, made up of the $1 million initial payment, the subsequent $100,000 payment under the earnout right and the $50,000 payment to end the earnout right.

Diana's final capital gain from the sale is $550,000 (capital proceeds of $1.15 million less the cost base of $600,000). Note that this is considered to have arisen in March 2023 at the time of the original CGT event.

This would necessitate going back and amending the return for the year ended 30 June 2023.

To the extent a taxpayer has accessed a concession (such as the small business CGT concessions, see below) for which they are ultimately not eligible due to these financial benefits, the taxpayer will be subject to a shortfall interest charge.

The CGT small business concessions effectively apply to a sale of assets involving a look-through earnout right by extending both the time to choose to apply the concessions and to acquire a replacement asset period. The time to make superannuation contributions pursuant to the CGT cap may also be extended, so that the contributions can be made up to 6 months after the latest possible financial benefit under the earnout right becomes due.

Note that while the receipt of financial benefits under a look-through earnout right may allow the taxpayer to make or remake choices, it does not entitle the taxpayer to undo the actions they have taken in that period.

For example, if Diana has made superannuation contributions in relation to one of the CGT concessions, she cannot choose to withdraw these contributions if the concessions turn out to be no longer available or they are only available for a reduced amount (for example, she might have assumed that she would receive $1.2 million, per the initial agreement, and she might have made superannuation contributions accordingly, but in the end she only received $1.15 million).

*Refs: s 118-565*
