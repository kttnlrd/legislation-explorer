# 2-370 - Water rights

Worked example: Water rights Issue Bernard Wilkie acquired vacant rural land on 12 September 1996. He ran a primary production business of growing wheat on the property for the whole of his period of ownership.

Upon purchase of the land, Bernard concurrently acquired water rights in relation to the river that runs through the property. The water entitlement was used exclusively to irrigate crops on Bernard's property for his farming business, apart from some periods where he temporarily transferred a small part of the water rights by selling a part of the annual water entitlement.

The farm was acquired for $1 million, including the water rights. A valuation of the property was undertaken at the time of purchase and the water rights were separately valued at $50,000.

The water access was previously governed by legislation, which has recently been replaced. With the introduction of the new legislation in November 2024, a new licence was granted and this now governs Bernard's water entitlements.

Bernard advises you that his entitlements under the new licence are essentially the same as under the previous licence.

Bernard satisfies the maximum net asset test for the purposes of the small business capital gains tax (CGT) concessions. Both the farm and the water rights are active assets, which Bernard has held for more than 15 years. Bernard is over 55 years old and is retiring from farming.

Explain to Bernard the CGT implications of the above transactions, in particular:

- whether Bernard is entitled to the statutory licence roll-over when the old water licence was replaced

  with substantially similar entitlements under the new legislation, and

- whether Bernard is able to use the small business 15-year exemption to shelter any gain that may

  arise on the ultimate disposal of the water rights.

Solution Tradeable water rights that are created or governed by statute are normally assets in their own right, distinct from the land to which they relate. Typically, they would be treated as capital assets unless the taxpayer was in the business of trading in them.

In accordance with the usual rules, CGT will not apply if the water rights are pre-CGT assets. Vendors may therefore need to trace back to identify when the water right was acquired.

The date of acquisition of the water right cannot be assumed to be the same as the date of acquisition of the land. However, the ATO has said that "if, at the time of separation, the land to which the water right relates is pre-CGT land, the water right will also be a pre-CGT asset. If the land was post-CGT at the time of the split, ITAA97 s 112-25 will apply to spread the cost base of the land across the water right and the land." (Minutes of NTLG Losses and CGT Subcommittee Meeting, 7 June 2006.) Where the right is post-CGT, the capital gain or loss rules will apply on the transfer. If the transfer is permanent, it will be treated in a similar way to a sale (CGT event A1). If the right is temporary or for a fixed term, it may be treated as similar to a lease (CGT event F1) or the creation of a right in another entity (CGT event D1) (former Interpretative Decisions ID 2004/53; ID 2004/54). The CGT discount normally applying to capital gains on assets held for at least 12 months does not apply to CGT events F1 or D1 (ITAA97 s 115-25).

CGT roll-over relief applies to capital gains or losses arising where a water entitlement ends and a replacement water right is issued (Subdiv 124-R). For these purposes, a water entitlement is a legal or equitable right that an entity owns that relates to water, including a right to receive water, take water from a water resource, have water delivered or deliver water.

Automatic roll-over relief applies where a taxpayer replaces a single water entitlement with one or more new water entitlements.

A separate roll-over applies where the water entitlement is a statutory licence (s 124-140). Section 124-140(3) sets out the meaning of "statutory licence" in this context. Subsections 124-140(1)(a) and (b) provide that there is a roll-over if a taxpayer's ownership of an original licence ends resulting in CGT event C2 happening and, as a result of that event, the taxpayer is issued with a new licence in place of the original licence. The new licence must authorise substantially similar activity as that authorised by the original licence.

As the CGT asset in this instance is a statutory licence, it satisfies the definition in s 124-140(3).

As a result of the coming into existence of Bernard's new water licence, his entitlements under the old licence expired. This expiry falls within s 104-25(1) as CGT event C2. Accordingly, the requirements under s 124-140(1)(a) and (b) have been satisfied. Further, for the purposes of s 124-140(1)(c), Bernard states that the new statutory water licence was granted in respect of the same underlying activity and on essentially similar terms as the previous licence.

As the conditions set out in s 124-140 have been satisfied, Bernard qualifies for CGT roll-over relief under Subdiv 124-A. The consequence is that any capital gain or loss arising from the ending of the original licence is disregarded and the cost base and acquisition date of the new licence will be the same as the old licence.

Bernard's water rights can be disposed of separately to the land and therefore the land and water rights are separate assets. The application of the small business concessions needs to be considered separately with regard to any sale of the land or water rights. Water rights would normally qualify as active assets for the purpose of the small business CGT concessions.

The water rights in this case were purchased after 1985 and Bernard has owned the asset for more than 15 years. The 15-year ownership period is not disturbed by the roll-over. For the purposes of the 15-year exemption from CGT, Bernard is treated as having acquired the asset when he acquired the previous asset.

Although Bernard has temporarily transferred a part of the annual water entitlement on irregular occasions, the water rights have been an active asset for at least 7½ years. Therefore, the water rights still qualify as small business active assets.

Accordingly, Bernard will qualify for the 15-year CGT exemption when he ultimately sells the water licence.
