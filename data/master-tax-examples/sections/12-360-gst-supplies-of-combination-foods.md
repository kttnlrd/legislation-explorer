# 12-360 - GST Supplies of combination foods

Worked example: GST: Supplies of combination foods Issue Alison Howard is the finance director of Murgle Foods Pty Ltd, which is about to launch a new product consisting of individual snack packs of hummus and a separate tub of savoury biscuits, packaged together by cardboard wrapping with the product label.

The design and marketing of the product is designed to emphasise that the contents of the 2 tubs will be mixed and consumed together. The biscuits are separately identifiable as they can be seen by the consumer at the point of sale.

Alison is of the view that GST is not chargeable on the new product as it is primarily a supply of hummus, which is not taxable. She comes to you to seek your guidance.

Advise Alison.

Solution Most food for human consumption is GST-free (s 38-2). To take a few common examples, GST typically does not apply to fruit, vegetables, meat products, fish, bread, cheese, eggs, milk, soup, sugar, tea and coffee.

However, there are some important exceptions to this. Food is not GST-free where it falls within any of the following categories:

- restaurant, catered or eat-in food

- hot takeaways

- prepared meals and other prepared food

- bakery products (but bread is GST-free)

- confectionery, snacks, ice-cream and biscuits

- alcohol, most soft drinks and certain other drinks

- certain food additives (s 38-3).

This raises the question, relevant to Alison, of what is the GST treatment of a product containing both GST- free foods and other ingredients which are not GST-free?

This was an issue considered by the AAT in Chobani Pty Ltd v Commissioner of Taxation 2023 ATC ¶10-669. In that case, Chobani sold a product comprising strawberry-flavoured yoghurt (which was GST- free) in the main compartment of a plastic tub and dry inclusions (ie a blend of cookie pieces and white chocolate chips) in a separate smaller compartment of the same plastic tub. The dry inclusions were intended to be "flipped" into the flavoured yoghurt by the customer.

The Commissioner advised the taxpayer that the ATO was changing its earlier view that supplies of the "flip" range of products with different flavours and dry inclusions were GST-free. The taxpayer objected to the Commissioner's view and the case came before the Tribunal, which had to determine whether the GST- free status of the supply of the product was excluded by the addition of (potentially taxable) "confectionery and biscuits" (s 38-3(1)(c) GSTA 1999). Broadly, this meant the taxpayer was required to prove that the product was not food that was a combination of one or more foods at least one of which was biscuit goods or confectionery or food of such a kind.

The Tribunal upheld the Commissioner's view that the flip yoghurt was indeed a combination product, and was subject to GST. The AAT found it "inescapable" that the product was a combination of flavoured yoghurt, cookie pieces and chocolate chips, within which the latter components were "not insignificant; remain readily identifiable; and are not subsumed into a separate product".

The principles of what is and what is not a combination product was later considered by the Commissioner in GSTD 2024/1 "Goods and services tax: supplies of combination food". This determination found that the following 3 principles apply when determining whether there is a supply of a combination food:

- There must be at least one separately identifiable taxable food.

- The separately identifiable taxable food must be sufficiently joined together with the other components

  of the overall product at the time of sale.

- The separately identifiable taxable food must not be so integrated into the overall product, or be so

  insignificant within that product, that it has no effect on its characterisation.

Note that Goods and Services Tax Ruling GSTR 2001/8 (Goods and services tax: Apportioning the consideration for a supply that includes taxable and non-taxable parts) deals with identifying supplies that include taxable and non-taxable parts. This includes how to distinguish mixed supplies from supplies that are in substance one thing – composite supplies. The Commissioner said that GSTR 2001/8 has no application to supplies of combination foods, as supplies of combination foods are wholly taxable and have no non- taxable parts.

The inclusion of hummus and biscuits in one package is therefore a combination food. Considering the factors stated in para 21 of the Determination, the biscuits are sufficiently joined together with the hummus at the time of sale. While the biscuits are not physically mixed with the hummus, the marketing, consumer experience and labelling of the product give the overall impression that the biscuits and hummus are supplied as a single item for consumption.

The biscuits are not so integrated into the overall product, nor so insignificant within that product, that they do not affect the product's characterisation. The biscuits are significant to the taste, texture and intended manner of consumption of the product, and form a key part of the marketing and design of the product. The overall impression of the product is that it is a combination of biscuits and hummus.

Accordingly, Alison must be advised that GST will be applied to the entire product.
