# 5-300 - Simplified trading stock rules

Worked example: Simplified trading stock rules Issue Max Edison runs a business as an importer of novelty keyrings. He satisfies the criteria to be a small business entity and uses the accruals accounting method. Max has 11,000 keyrings in stock as at 30 June 2024, which he valued for tax purposes at a cost of $1.50 per unit ($16,500).

During the year ended 30 June 2025, Max purchased 12,000 keyrings at a cost of $1.50 per unit. In addition, he also sold 14,000 units at a selling price of $4 per unit.

Max does not wish to carry out a stocktake at the end of the year. He believes that he maintains sufficient records to accurately reflect his stock at any given point in time and does not believe that it is necessary.

Is Max eligible to use the simplified trading stock rules in ITAA97 Subdiv 328-E? What would the application of these rules mean to Max?

Solution Subdivision 328-E, comprising ss 328-280 to 328-295, prescribes the simplified rules that small and medium business entities can choose to follow when accounting for trading stock.

Taxpayers can choose to apply the simplified rules in Subdiv 328-E for an income year if:

- they are a small business entity for that year, or would be if the aggregated turnover threshold were

  $50 million, and

- the difference between the value of trading stock on hand at the start of that income year and the

  reasonably estimated value at the end of the year is $5,000 or less.

Eligible taxpayers that choose to apply the simplified rules are not required to perform a stocktake or value each item of trading stock at the end of the year in accordance with ITAA97 s 70-45. The value of stock on hand at the end of the year is instead deemed to be the same as the opening value, and there is no adjustment to assessable income or deductions to account for the actual change.

These simplified trading stock rules are largely optional and come into effect only if the taxpayer chooses under s 328-285 not to account for changes in the value of trading stock less than $5,000.

If the choice is made, the closing value of the trading stock for tax purposes will be the same as the opening value for that year (ITAA97 s 328-295(2)). This will also be the opening value for the following year (ITAA97 s 70-40). Applying s 70-40, the value of Max's trading stock on hand at the start of 2024–25 is $16,500.

A reasonable estimate will approximate the value by the taxpayer that is reasonable in all of the circumstances. In some cases, the taxpayer maintains a relatively constant level of stock and will know the approximate value of stock on hand. Other taxpayers may maintain detailed stock records and may be able to provide a reasonable estimate from these records. Some taxpayers may maintain so little trading stock that a $5,000 change would be virtually impossible.

In making an estimate of stock on hand at the end of a year for the purposes of s 328-285, a taxpayer may apply the valuation methods and rules in Subdiv 70-C. For example, the taxpayer can elect to value trading stock at cost, market selling value or replacement value (s 70-45).

Where the difference between the value of trading stock on hand at the start of the year and the reasonably estimated value at the end of the year is greater than $5,000, the taxpayer must value each item of trading stock on hand at the end of the year and account for the change in value in the normal way under ITAA97 s 70-45.

Max estimates that his trading stock has declined in value by $3,000. This estimate is based on the difference between units purchased (12,000) and units sold (14,000) in the year, multiplied by the cost of the keyrings ($1.50). This estimate is reasonable because Max maintains accurate records of his trading stock.

Having estimated that the change in value of trading stock does not exceed $5,000, Max can therefore choose not to account for the change. He is not required to value each item of trading stock on hand at the end of the year for income tax purposes and a stocktake is unnecessary.

The value of trading stock on hand at the end of the 2024–25 year is deemed to equal the value of trading stock on hand at the start of the year (ie $16,500).

In calculating his taxable income for 2024–25, Max includes $56,000 in assessable income for sales (ie 14,000 × $4) and deducts $18,000 for purchases ($1.50 x 12,000).

If Max does decide to carry out an annual stocktake and accounts for the resulting changes in the value of trading stock, the normal trading stock rules in Div 70 will apply. This may be due to a preference by Max to adjust assessable income in annual increments rather than in one larger amount when the change in value of trading stock exceeds $5,000.
