# Aggregated turnover test ¶1-260

The second requirement for a small business entity is that it satisfies the "aggregated turnover" test.

Once aggregated turnover has been calculated (¶1-265) there are 3 alternate ways of satisfying the test (ITAA 1997 s 328-110). The first depends on the aggregated turnover of the previous income year, and the second and third tests depend on the estimated or actual aggregated turnover of the current income year.

First test: An entity satisfies the first test if its aggregated turnover for the previous income year was less than $10 million. This is so irrespective of the entity's likely or actual aggregated turnover for the current income year.

Illustration: Aggregate turnover test based on aggregated turnover for the previous income year A company continuing carrying on a business in the current income year has an aggregated turnover for previous income year of $9.9 million. The entity qualifies as a small business entity in current year even though it expects its aggregated turnover in the current year will increase to $10.5 million.

An entity that commences carrying on business part-way through an income year will need to satisfy either the second test or the third test, as there will be no previous year's aggregated turnover. To satisfy the second test, this entity will need to make a reasonable estimate of its likely aggregated turnover for the whole year, based on the situation as at the date it started to carry on the business.

Second test: An entity satisfies the second test if its aggregated turnover for the current income year, worked out as at the first day of that year, is likely to be less than $10 million. However, an entity cannot use this test if its aggregated turnover in each of the previous two income years was at least $10 million (this qualification is designed to prevent abuse and to simplify administration for the ATO).

Whether the current year aggregated turnover is likely to be less than $10 million is a matter of objective judgment. It is based on the balance of probabilities as at the start of the income year. Relevant factors would include:

- the aggregated turnover in previous income years

- the prospect of changes in sales patterns, reductions in staff numbers or operating hours

- plans to cease or wind down the business, and

- the prospect of other factors such as drought, declining industry prices or increased competition.

Illustration: Aggregate turnover in the current income year likely to exceed $10 million A company's aggregated turnover is $9.5 million in 2024–25 and $10.1 million in 2025–26. A severe industry downturn commences in June 2026. As at 1 July 2026, the company expects to shortly retrench half of its staff. Although it cannot satisfy the first turnover test in 2026–27 (because of the previous year's aggregated turnover is $10.1 million), it may be able to satisfy test 2, because:

- it is likely, as at 1 July 2026, that its 2026–27 aggregated turnover will be less than $10 million, and

- at least one of the 2 previous income years' aggregated turnovers was less than $10 million.

If instead the 2024–25 aggregated turnover had been $10.5 million. The second test could not be satisfied for 2026–27, as the aggregated turnover in each of the 2 previous years exceeded $10 million. In such a case, the company would need to rely on its actual 2026–27 aggregated turnover satisfying test 3 (see below).

Third test: An entity will satisfy the third test if its aggregated turnover for the current year, worked out as at the end of that year, is actually less than $10 million. This is so irrespective of the aggregated turnovers in previous income years.

Illustration: Actual aggregated turnover at the end of the year A company's aggregated turnover for the previous income year is $10.5 million (accordingly it cannot satisfy the first test for the current income year). As at 1 July of the current income year, it forecasts similar turnover to the previous year (accordingly the second test is not satisfied). In August of the current income year, operations are significantly disrupted by a fire that damages the business premises. The actual aggregated turnover for current year is $9.3 million. The company satisfies test 3 in the current year.

As this test requires eligibility to be worked out at the end of the income year, an entity that satisfies only this test, and not the others, will not be able to access GST concessions that are used throughout the year, ie accounting for GST on a cash basis (¶7-300), making an annual apportionment of input tax credits (¶5-020) or paying GST by quarterly instalments (¶8-037).

Where entity has more than one business The aggregated turnover test relates to the business income of the entity, not to each business conducted by the entity.

Illustration: Aggregation of business activities to determine aggregated turnover A sole trader carries on 2 separate businesses. The turnover of both businesses is considered together for the purposes of applying the aggregated turnover test.
