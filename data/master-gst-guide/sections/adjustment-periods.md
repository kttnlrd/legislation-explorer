# Adjustment periods ¶6-304

An adjustment for changes in planned use is made annually, usually in the tax period that ends on 30 June or the closest to 30 June — to align as closely as possible with the income tax year (s 129-20). This period is called the adjustment period. The first adjustment period must start at least 12 months after the end of the tax period in which the acquisition was made — to allow adequate time for the taxpayer to assess the use.

Illustration: Adjustment periods A business bought equipment on 1 December 2023, and was entitled to an input tax credit (ITC) in its GST remittance for the quarterly tax period ending 31 December 2023. The first adjustment period for the equipment is the tax period from 1 April 2025 to 30 June 2025.

If the business bought the equipment in January 2025, the first adjustment period is the tax period from 1 April 2026 to 30 June 2026.

The obligation to make these annual adjustments does not continue indefinitely. The number of adjustment periods ranges from one to 10, according to the type of acquisition and its GST-exclusive value.

In typical cases — for example, where the acquisition does not relate to making financial supplies — the limits are:

- if the GST-exclusive value is $1,000 or less, there is no requirement to make an adjustment

- if the GST-exclusive value is more than $1,000 but less than $5,000, adjustments for changes of use

  may be made in any of the first 2 adjustment periods after acquisition

- if the GST-exclusive value is more than $5,000 but less than $500,000, adjustments for changes of

  use may be made in any of the first 5 adjustment periods after acquisition

- if the GST-exclusive value is $500,000 or more, adjustments for changes of use may be made in any

  of the first 10 adjustment periods after acquisition.

Illustration: Calculating adjustments In December 2025, Alexander buys a commercial van to use in his courier business, with the intention that 25% of the van's use will be private. The GST-inclusive cost of the van is $66,000. Alexander is therefore entitled to an ITC of $4,500 (75% × $6,000).

At the end of the first annual adjustment period (30 June 2027), Alexander has in fact used the van 40% privately since purchase.

The ITC should therefore have been $3,600 (60% × $6,000) rather than $4,500. Alexander is liable for an increasing GST adjustment of $900.

At the end of the second annual adjustment period (30 June 2028), Alexander has in fact used the van 30% privately since acquisition. The ITC therefore should have been $4,200 (70% × $6,000) instead of $3,600. Alexander is entitled to a decreasing GST adjustment of $600.

As the GST-exclusive value of the van ($60,000) is between $5,000 and $500,000, adjustments may continue, if appropriate, for the next 3 adjustment periods.

If the acquisition related solely or partly to making financial supplies, and was not in any way of a private or domestic nature, the limits are:

- if the GST-exclusive value is $10,000 or less, there is no requirement to make an adjustment

- if the GST-exclusive value is more than $10,000 but less than $50,000, only one adjustment for a

  change of use is required. This adjustment is made in the first adjustment period that occurs at least

12 months after the tax period in which the acquisition was made. No subsequent adjustments are required

- if the GST-exclusive value is more than $50,000 but less than $500,000, adjustments for changes of

  use may be made in any of the first 5 adjustment periods after acquisition

- if the GST-exclusive value is $500,000 or more, adjustments for changes of use may be made in any

  of the first 10 adjustment periods after acquisition.

If the thing acquired is disposed of, lost, destroyed or stolen, or expires before the end of its last adjustment period, the last adjustment period for that acquisition will be the next tax period ending on 30 June or closest to it (s 129-25). However, this does not apply if there is a decreasing adjustment in respect of the disposal and a full ITC on acquisition (such as where the acquisition relates to financial supplies or private or domestic use) was not claimed (¶6-310). For special rules applying where a business owner dies, see ¶6-415.

A taxpayer cannot claim an adjustment in relation to events occurring after its registration is cancelled (GOL- HUT Pty Ltd v FC of T [2013] AATA 199).

*Refs: s 129-20), s 129-25)*
