# Amending an assessment ¶8-090

If a taxpayer lodges a return, then later lodges an amended return for the same tax period, the first return gives rise to a deemed assessment, and the second return is treated as an application for amendment of that assessment. Similarly, if the Commissioner makes a default assessment due to the taxpayer's failure to lodge a return, and the taxpayer later lodges a return for that period, that return will be treated as an application to amend the default assessment.

An assessment can be amended either unilaterally by the Commissioner or at the request of the taxpayer. If the request is made in the approved form (see ATO Amendments webpage), and the Commissioner makes the amendment in accordance with the request, a deemed notice of amended assessment arises. This is treated as being given to the taxpayer on the day when the Commissioner makes the appropriate change to the taxpayer's running balance account (RBA) (Administration Act Sch 1 ss 155-40, 155-80).

If the Commissioner makes an amendment that is not fully in accordance with the taxpayer's request, or the request was not in the approved form, an actual notice of amended assessment must be issued.

Period of review Under the self-assessment system, once there has been an assessment of the taxpayer's GST liabilities and entitlements, they remain payable without time limit. This means that they are able to be enforced at any time (¶8-100).

However, there is generally a 4-year period in which the assessment can be amended, either in favour of the Commissioner or the taxpayer (Administration Act Sch 1 s 155-35). This "period of review" is measured from the date on which notice of the assessment is given (or deemed to be given) to the taxpayer.

The 4-year period can be extended if there is a partly completed examination of the taxpayer's affairs — eg an audit, investigation or review — that is pending when the period expires. For this to apply, the Commissioner must obtain either the consent of the taxpayer or a Federal Court order. The request for consent, or the application to the court, must be made before the period expires. In deciding whether to grant an extension order, the court must be satisfied that the Commissioner's failure to complete the examination was attributable to actions taken by the taxpayer, or to the taxpayer's failure to take reasonable steps (s 155-35). This could apply, for example, where the taxpayer has been unreasonably obstructive or non- cooperative. An extended period of review may itself be extended.

As exceptions to the general rule, the Commissioner can amend an assessment at any time where:

- it is made in response to a request for amendment in the approved form made by the taxpayer within

  the period of review (Administration Act Sch 1 s 155-45)

- the amendment is necessary to give effect to a decision on review or appeal (Administration Act Sch 1

  s 155-60)

- it is made to give effect to a private ruling requested by the taxpayer during the period of review

  (Administration Act Sch 1 s 155-50)

- the Commissioner considers that there has been fraud or evasion (s 155-60)

- the Commissioner has made a declaration under the anti-avoidance rules (¶20-070) (Administration

  Act Sch 1 s 155-55).

In all but the first case, the Commissioner must issue an actual notice of amendment to the taxpayer.

Where an assessment is amended within the normal 4-year review period, there is another 4-year period in which that amendment may itself be reviewed (Administration Act Sch 1 ss 155-65, 155-70). This "refreshed" period runs from that date on which notice of the amended assessment was given to the taxpayer. This period cannot itself be extended. assessment Example: 4-year amendment period A taxpayer lodges a BAS for the September 2026 quarter on 28 October 2026. The Commissioner's notice of assessment is deemed to be given on the same day, so the period of review is until 28 October 2030. On 1 December 2029, within the period of review, the taxpayer applies for an amendment. The Commissioner allows the amendment on 15 December 2029, the "refreshed" 4- year period of review for that amendment starts from that date.

If amendments were made in relation to different particulars at different times during the normal 4-year period, a separate refreshed review period applies to each.

These rules about refreshed periods do not apply to those cases noted above where an amendment can be made at any time.

Making the estimate The estimate must be of an amount which the Commissioner thinks is reasonable (Administration Act, Sch 1, s 268-10). This could involve consideration of information already held about the taxpayer's business activities, industry benchmarks, third party information (such as land titles data) and patterns of past behaviour, including lodgment history and past claims (Practical Compliance Guideline PCG 2020/2).

Liability to pay The notice of the estimated amount is treated as having been given at the time the Commissioner leaves or posts it, and the taxpayer's liability to pay the estimated amount arises at that time (Administration Act, Sch 1, s 268-15; s 268-20; s 268-25). If the taxpayer fails to pay within seven days after the Commissioner has given the notice, interest (¶18-300) becomes payable on the unpaid amount from the date that the underlying liability arose (Administration Act, Sch 1, s 268-75).

The taxpayer's liability to pay the estimated amount is separate from its liability to pay the underlying GST.

However, if it pays the estimated amount, this also operates to reduce the underlying GST liability. determined on basis of Commissioner's estimate Example Enterprise has a history of GST evasion and fails to lodge a GST return. The Commissioner notifies it that it has an estimated liability of $50,000. Within 7 days, Enterprise pays $20,000 in part payment of the estimated liability.

Enterprise subsequently lodges a return, and is assessed as having an underlying GST liability of $55,000. The $20,000 part- payment is offset against that amount, leaving a reduced underlying liability of $35,000.

The same applies to payments of interest (Administration Act, Sch 1, s 268-80). Payment of an estimated amount does not relieve the taxpayer of the obligation to lodge a GST return.

Reducing or revoking the estimate The Commissioner has the power to retrospectively reduce or revoke the estimate at any time (Administration Act, Sch 1, s 268-35).

Although the Commissioner's decision on the estimate is not open to judicial review (¶18-600), a taxpayer can obtain a reduction or revocation of the estimate by lodging a sworn statement verifying:

- the taxpayer's net GST liability

- its taxable supplies, including taxable supplies of luxury cars, and creditable acquisitions

- its assessable dealings and wine equalisation tax credit entitlements, and

- the steps it took to comply with its GST obligations (Administration Act, Sch 1, s 268-40; s 268-90).

The statement must do more than making assertions without reference to primary facts or primary documents which must have existed (Transtar Linehaul Pty Limited v Deputy Commissioner of Taxation [2011] FCA 856; CLK Kitchens & Joinery Pty Ltd v Commissioner of Taxation [2019] FCA 1086).

Statutory declaration within seven-day period In general, the taxpayer's sworn statement must be in the form of a statutory declaration which is lodged within seven days after the Commissioner gave notice of the estimated amount, or within such longer period as the Commissioner allows. The declaration must be to the effect that the GST liability was less than the estimate (which should be reduced accordingly) or that the GST liability never existed (so that the estimate should be revoked).

It appears that in practice the Commissioner will normally allow 21 days, or longer where this can be justified in some cases. Even if a statement is lodged later than this, the Commissioner has indicated that it will still be considered and may be acted on (Practical Compliance Guideline PCG 2020/2).

Affidavit within 14-day period If court recovery proceedings have already commenced, the taxpayer's sworn statement must take the form of an affidavit. This must be filed, and a copy served on the Commissioner within 14 days from the time the taxpayer first took a procedural step in the proceedings (for example, by entering an appearance).

The affidavit must verify facts that are sufficient to prove that the GST liability was less than the estimate (which should be reduced accordingly) or that the GST liability never existed (so that the estimate should be revoked).

If the taxpayer that is the subject of the estimate notice is a company, the affidavit cannot be made by a director of the company. This is so even though the director may be subject to a Director Penalty Notice (¶8-105) which is based on non-payment of the estimated debt (Mandalinic v Stone (Liquidator) & Ors 2023 ATC ¶20-879).
