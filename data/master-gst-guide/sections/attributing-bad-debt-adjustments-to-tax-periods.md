# Attributing bad debt adjustments to tax periods ¶6-205

An adjustment is generally attributed to the tax period in which the taxpayer becomes aware of it (¶6-000).

- an adjustment for a bad debt is attributed to the tax period of the supplier in which it is written off, and

  to the tax period of the recipient in which the recipient becomes aware that the debt has been written

  off

- an adjustment for an overdue debt that has not been written off is attributed to the tax period of the

  supplier in which the supplier becomes aware that the debt has been overdue for 12 months, and to the

  tax period of the recipient in which the recipient becomes so aware (GST Ruling GSTR 2000/2).

As a practical matter, this means an increasing adjustment arises for a purchaser where a debt in respect of which input tax credits (ITCs) have been claimed remains unpaid for 12 months.

Illustrations: Adjustments for bad debts In the following illustrations both parties are registered for GST, remit GST on an accurals basis and have quarterly tax periods.

Bad debt. In January, Enterprises supplies goods on credit to a business customer for $110. The due date for payment of which is 15 March. Enterprises accounts for GST of $10 in its return for the tax period ending 31 March and the customer claims an ITC of the same amount in its return for the same period. The customer goes into liquidation without paying any of the debt, and Enterprises writes the debt off as bad in May (ie the next quarter). Enterprises will be entitled to a decreasing adjustment of $10 in its GST remittance for the tax period ending 30 June. Conversely, if the customer is aware that the debt has been written off, there will be a corresponding increasing adjustment for the customer of $10 in the corresponding tax period.

Where a debt arises and is written off as bad in the same tax period, the taxpayer is required to separately account for both the sale and the adjustment in their GST remittance for the tax period rather than omit them entirely on the basis that they "net off" (GST Ruling GSTR 2000/2).

If the customer had in fact paid $44 of the debt, only $66 would be bad, so Enterprises' decreasing adjustment and the customer's increasing adjustment would both be $6.

Partial credit. If customer only plans to use the goods 50% for business purposes it is only entitled to a 50% ITC. Enterprise remains entitled to a decreasing GST adjustment of $10 while the customer's ITC and subsequent increasing adjustment will both be reduced to $5.

Supply only partly taxable. If half the value of the supply by Enterprises was for GST-free goods, the GST and subsequent decreasing adjustment will be $5. The customer's ITC and subsequent increasing adjustment will also be $5. 12-month overdue debt. If the customer defaults and refuses to pay but Enterprises does not write off the debt, the debt becomes 12 months overdue on 15 March the following year. If the debt remains outstanding, Enterprises will be entitled to a decreasing adjustment of $10 in its March GST remittance the following year. The customer is required to make an increasing adjustment of $10 in its GST remittance for the same period.
