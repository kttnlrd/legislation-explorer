# Residential care, home care and disability services ¶13-340

Certain residential and home care, and disability services are GST-free. The exemptions are defined with reference to the aged care service list. The aged care service list is found in Pt 3 of the Aged Care Rules 2025 (ACR) made under s 602, in accordance with s 8 of the Aged Care Act 2024 (ACA). It is beyond the scope of this content to undertake an analysis of the aged care regime. It is suggested that providers of aged care services may be expected to be informed of the services they provide within the context of that legislation. Accordingly, this content focuses on the GST provisions that define the GST outcomes of the provision of services on the aged care list.

The Aged Care Act 2024 replaced the Aged Care Act 1997 effective 1 November 2025, under which the Quality of Care Principles 2014 were replaced by the Aged Care Rules 2025. This resulted in extensive consequential amendments to the GST legislation. While the regimes may appear equivalent, tax practitioners (and care providers) would be prudent to ensure that the GST treatment of supplies conforms with the new legislative regime rather than assume that the new regime embodies no substantive changes.

Similarly, until revised, existing ATO pronouncements, in the context of the previous regime, may only provide limited certainty.

Extra service fees only remain GST-free to the extent that the services were provided before 31 October 2026 and covered by Sch 1 to the Quality of Care Principles 2014 (s 38-25(5)).

Residential and home care, and disability services may be GST-free when provided to people in their own homes as well as those in nursing homes and serviced apartments in retirement villages.

Services are considered to be provided in a residential setting for the purpose of the exemption when they are provided to a resident of a serviced apartment in a retirement village (¶15-015) where the operator is contracted to provide daily meals and heavy laundry services to all the residents of the apartment (s 38-25(3A)). The ATO considers that this applies where the residents have a continuing need for those meals and services, that the operator has an obligation to provide them and that the operator does in fact make them available (GST Ruling GSTR 2012/3). The apartment must be part of a single complex accessible from a common corridor, there must be a responsible person on call to render emergency assistance, and there must be a communal dining facility available for use by residents. Independent living units do not satisfy these criteria.

Privately funded nursing homes and aged-care hostels that provide care to aged or disabled people are considered to be a residential setting if (GST Ruling GSTR 2012/3 para 8):

- the residents do not have a proprietary interest in the property

- residents can be moved at the operator's discretion under certain circumstances

- premises are marketed and held out to the public as a place for care and accommodation, rather than

  just accommodation, and

- residents have limited rights compared to residents who lease or own their premises.

Accommodation provided in the course of supplying any of the above GST-free services is also GST-free (s 38-25(4)). This may include, under the previous regime, the supply of a serviced apartment in a retirement village if at the time the resident requires daily living activities assistance or nursing services (GSTR 2012/3 paras 99–100). A separate exemption applies to supplies of retirement village accommodation by charities (¶15-015).

The supply of rights to receive GST-free care services is itself GST-free (s 9-30).

The ATO "GST and residential care" webpage includes a range of services provided in residential care situations and their GST treatment.

Amounts retained by the operator of a residential care facility from accommodation bonds paid by residents are treated as consideration for the GST-free supply. The same applies to non-refundable periodic payments of an accommodation bond (GST Determination GSTD 2011/4).

When she later leaves the facility, the amount retained by the provider from the lump sum is also treated as consideration for the GST-free services and accommodation.

Separate rules apply where accommodation is provided at an "over-55s" retirement village without residential care. This would normally be input taxed (¶11-310), except in certain situations where the village is operated by a charity (¶15-015).

By their nature, these care services cannot be supplied to a business entity. Where the services are provided to aged persons under a contract with a business entity that determines the type of care and pays for it, the business entity is actually the recipient of the supply. It follows that the supply by the care provider is not GST-free, rather the usual rules for remitting and claiming input tax credits apply. However, the position would be different if the business entity did not engage the care provider but merely made payment on behalf of the patient — in such a case, the exemption could apply.

Specialist aged care programs The supply of services under a specialist aged care program in respect of which a subsidy is payable under Ch 4 Pt 2 Div 5 ACA (Ch 7 Pt 9 ACR) is GST-free (s 38-35).

Specialist disability services and NDIS Specialist disability services including accommodation, community support, and community access and respite services are GST-free if the supplier receives funding under disability services legislation in respect of the services (s 38-40). The funding may be either "package" funding (directed at particular clients) or "block" funding (available for any clients) (Interpretative Decision ID 2003/993). However, it is apparently not sufficient that the patient or patient's family receives the funding and passes it on to the supplier.

In addition, effective until 30 June 2027, certain services and supports supplied under the National Disability Insurance Scheme (NDIS) are GST-free, even though the government funding is received by the disabled recipient or the funding manager instead of the supplier (s 38-38; A New Tax System (Goods and Services Tax) (GST-free Supply—National Disability Insurance Scheme Supports) Determination 2021 (F2025C00318 (C01))). To be exempt, the supply must be reasonable and necessary for implementation of the recipient's disability plan and must meet various substantiation requirements. This potentially applies to:

- assistance in coordinating or managing life stages, transitions and supports

- household tasks

- assistance with and training in travel/transport arrangements, excluding taxi fares

- interpreting and translation

- assistance to access and maintain education and employment

- assistive equipment for recreation

- early intervention supports for early childhood

- management of funding for supports in a participant's plan

- assistance with daily personal activities

- specialised assessment and development of daily living and life skills, including community

  participation

- assistive equipment for general tasks and leisure, including assistive technology specialist

  assessment, set up and training

- behavioural support and therapeutic supports

- home modifications.
