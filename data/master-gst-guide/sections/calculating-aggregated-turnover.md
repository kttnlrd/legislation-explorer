# Calculating aggregated turnover ¶1-265

An entity's aggregated turnover for an income year is the sum of its own "annual turnover" (see below) and the annual turnovers of other "relevant entities", namely:

- its affiliated entities (¶1-275), and

- its connected entities (¶1-280).

- derived by the entity from a dealing with a relevant entity, or

- derived by a relevant entity from a dealing with another relevant entity (ITAA 1997 s 328-115)

  because relevant entities are regarded as a single entity, rather than multiple entities.

Illustration: Related party dealings excluded from aggregated turnover Stefano is an affiliate of Iggie, who is accordingly a relevant entity. Ordinary income from Stefano's dealings with Iggie is not included in Stefano's aggregated turnover.

Assume that Stefano is also connected to X Corp, who is accordingly also a relevant entity. Ordinary income from Stefano's dealings with Iggie or with X Corp is not included in Stefano's aggregated turnover. Nor is ordinary income from dealings between Iggie and X Corp.

An entity's aggregated turnover includes the annual turnover of an entity that is a relevant entity at any time during the income year. However, the aggregated turnover does not include ordinary income derived by that entity during the period that it was not a relevant entity.

Illustration: Exclusion of certain income from aggregated turnover From 1 July to 31 December of the income year, Barbara's controlling shareholding in a company means that the company is a connected entity (and therefore a relevant entity). On 1 January, Barbara sells her shares. She and the company are no longer connected. Ordinary income derived by the company from 1 January is therefore not included in Barbara's aggregated turnover for income year.

When calculating the taxpayer's aggregated turnover, the taxpayer should calculate the annual turnovers of its connected entities and its affiliates for the relevant period that aligns with the taxpayer's income year, even if those entities have an accounting period different from the taxpayer's (Taxation Determination TD 2021/7).

Annual turnover An entity's annual turnover for an income year is the total ordinary income that the entity derives in the income year in the ordinary course of carrying on a business (ITAA 1997 s 328-120).

Ordinary income is income for tax purposes. Income is derived in the ordinary course of carrying on a business if:

- it is of a kind that is regularly or customarily derived by the entity in the course of carrying on its

  business, or

- it is a direct result of the normal activities of the business. Wages and salary are not business income

  and are therefore not included.

*Refs: s 328-115), s 328-120)*

- annual turnover does not include GST on taxable supplies made by the entity or any upwards GST

  adjustments

- if the entity carries on a business for only part of the income year, its annual turnover is calculated

  using a reasonable estimate of what it would have been if the business had been carried on for the full

  year. This applies even if the entity also carried on some other business for the whole of the income

  year (Interpretative Decision ID 2009/49).

Illustrations: Reasonable estimate of annual turnover If the turnover of an entity is $2.2 million for the 3 months of the year that the entity is carrying on business, a reasonable estimate of the full year's turnover may be $8.8 million (subject to other factors, such as seasonal demand).

An entity carries on 2 businesses. During year 1, one business has an annual turnover of $10.8 million, and the other has an annual turnover of $500,000. Halfway through year 2, the entity ceases the larger business, whose turnover for that half-year was $5.4 million. Assuming that all other factors have remained the same, the entity's annual turnover for year 2 would be reasonably estimated as $11.3 million ($10.8 million + $500,000). This applies even though its actual turnover for that year was $5.9 million ($5.4 million + $500,000)

- income derived from the sale of retail fuel is disregarded. Due to the low profit/volume ratios in this

  industry turnover is not considered a reliable indicator of size.

Dealings with associates If the entity has a non-arm's length dealing with an associate (¶17-500), the ordinary income is taken to include the amount that would have resulted if the dealing had been at arm's length. So, for example, if the income from the dealing was below market value, the market value would normally be substituted for the amount actually received. In determining the market value, however, normal discounts (eg for bulk purchases) may be taken into account. This arm's length rule does not apply if the associate is an "affiliate" or a "connected" entity. As explained above, dealings with these entities are excluded from aggregated turnover altogether.
