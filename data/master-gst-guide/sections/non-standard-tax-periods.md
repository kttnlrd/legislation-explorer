# Non-standard tax periods ¶7-105

In certain circumstances tax periods may be modified to accommodate commercial accounting practice. "Non-calendar" monthly tax periods The compulsory calendar month tax periods may be modified for businesses that do not use calendar months for commercial accounting purposes. Such entities that have GST turnovers of at least $20m, can apply to the ATO to have tax periods that reflect their management accounting practice (s 27-37). For example, this may be appropriate if the entity uses 13 four-weekly accounting periods, or 12 accounting periods, with some of four weeks and others of five weeks. Approval will be withdrawn if the entity subsequently fails to satisfy any of the preconditions (s 27-38).

Seven-day leeway for aligning tax periods Entities may change the day in each year on which a tax period would normally end to align with commercial accounting cut-off practice. However, the changed period must end no more than 7 days before or after the end of the original period. ATO consent is not required for this change (s 27-35).

If the day is changed in this way, the next period commences on the day after the changed period ends.

There is no change to the due date for lodgment of the BAS in respect of the altered tax period.

Illustration: Alignment of financial year and annual management reporting period A supermarket owner normally balances her accounts on Fridays. She reports GST quarterly. Assume the first period of the calendar year (ending 31 March) ends on a Tuesday. She opts to end her tax period on Friday, 27 March, so that she does not have to make a special balance on the Tuesday. Her next 3-month tax period starts on Saturday, 28 March, instead of 1 April. She must account for the tax period ending on 27 March by 28 April.

Transitional tax periods If a taxpayer has changed tax periods, the ATO can nominate a particular period as a tax period to ensure a continuous transition. The nominated period must be less than 3 months, and cannot overlap with any other tax period for which a GST return has already been lodged (s 27-30). This also applies at the time the taxpayer registers for GST.

Illustration: Changing GST reporting periods In August, a business reporting GST quarterly realises that its GST turnover will for the first time exceed $20m, requiring it to change to monthly GST tax periods from 1 September. Its previous tax period ended on 30 June. It may therefore be appropriate for the ATO to nominate a 2-month interim tax period from 1 July to 31 August.

*Refs: s 27-37), s 27-38), s 27-35), s 27-30)*
