# Recipient created tax invoices ¶5-140

A tax invoice is normally issued by the supplier. However, sometimes this will not be practicable, for example, where the recipient determines the value of the goods or services, rather than the supplier. In these cases, it may be more appropriate for the tax invoice to be issued by the recipient. The situations in which these recipient created tax invoices (RCTIs) can be issued are determined by the Commissioner (s 29-70(3)).

A New Tax System (Goods and Services Tax): Recipient Created Tax Invoice Determination 2023 (F2023L00785) applies to government-related entities, large business entities (broadly, those with turnovers of at least $20 million) and to business entities that determine the value of the taxable supply acquired from the supplier. These entities may issue an RCTI where the following requirements are satisfied:

- both the recipient and the supplier of the taxable supply are registered for GST when the RCTI is

  issued

- the recipient issues a document that complies with specified requirements for tax invoices

- the recipient issues the RCTI to the supplier within 28 days from when either the taxable supply is

  made by the supplier, or the value of the taxable supply is determined by the recipient

- the recipient retains the original or a copy of the RCTI for 5 years

- the recipient has a written agreement with the supplier, including one embedded within the RCTI, that

  meets certain requirements outlined in the instrument, and

- the recipient reasonably complies with its obligations under taxation laws.

The above legislative determination repealed numerous general and specific RCTI legislative instruments.

Dual purpose invoices A tax invoice issued by the recipient can also cover any supplies which the recipient makes back to the supplier. However, the Commissioner considers that the 2 supplies must be accounted for separately in the GST return of each party (GST Ruling GSTR 2013/1).

Illustration: Dual purposes tax invoices A cane grower supplies cane to a sugar mill, which in turn supplies testing services to the grower. Although the supplies are related they must still be treated as separate supplies, which must be accounted for separately in the parties' GST returns. The RCTI issued by the mill can, however, act as a tax invoice covering both supplies provided it treats them as separate.

The ATO considers that a RCTI can qualify as an "invoice", for the purpose of determining when liability arises under the accruals basis (¶7-205; GST Determination GSTD 2005/1).

Electronic purchasing systems Provided a summary document of taxable supplies received by the recipient together with any referenced document including a description of each each thing supplied together meet the legislative requirements for a RCTI the recipient will be entitled to attribute an ITC to a tax period (A New Tax System (Goods and Services Tax): Waiver of Tax Invoice Requirement (Acquisitions by Recipients Using Electronic Purchasing Systems) Determination 2023 [LI 2023/6]).

Foreign currency conversions If the consideration for the supply is expressed in foreign currency, the recipient issuing the invoice should hold a written advice from the supplier stating the supplier's particular exchange rate and the conversion day. However, if the recipient's computer system is set up in a way that does not enable the recipient to use the supplier's conversion information, the recipient can make a reasonable estimation (GST Ruling GSTR 2002/4; ¶4-200).
