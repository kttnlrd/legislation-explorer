# Standard method: time for lodgment ¶8-005

Under the standard method (¶8-002), a taxpayer with monthly tax periods must account for GST and pay any st net GST by the 21 day of the month following the end of the tax period (s 31-10). For example, the monthly return for March is due by 21 April.

Due dates for payment and lodgment for quarterly taxpayer using the standard method are:

*Refs: s 31-10)*

30 September                                   28 October 31 December                                    28 February 31 March                                       28 April 30 June                                        28 July These dates are adjusted where the due date falls on a weekend or public holiday (¶25-055).

Extensions The Commissioner has the power to extend the time for lodgment. Two-week extensions of the 28 October, 28 April and 28 July deadlines may be allowed for businesses lodging and paying their quarterly returns online using the ATO Online services for business (¶8-043). Extensions are also allowed where returns are prepared by tax agents. In individual cases, an extension may be appropriate where there are the usual delays in the settlement of affairs following a taxpayer's death. Applications for such individual extensions should be made before the due date to avoid the possibility of penalty.

People affected by major natural disasters, such as floods, droughts, bushfires or cyclones, may be eligible for concessional extensions of time in lodging returns or paying GST (¶18-305), and accelerated refunds where they are in necessitous circumstances. See ATO Natural disaster support webpage.

Adjusted monthly tax periods For taxpayers with monthly management accounting periods that fall within the first 7 days of the calendar month, there is a concession that allows alignment of the monthly GST reporting period with the monthly management accounting reporting period (¶7-105). This does not, however, change the lodgment date.

Illustration: Alignment of monthly GST reporting period with management accounting period A taxpayer has monthly management accounting periods that end on the 2nd day of the month. By aligning GST and management accounting periods, the taxpayer's monthly GST tax period for 30 September for example, will end on 2 October, rather than 30 September. There is no change to the lodgment date, the taxpayer would still be required to lodge and remit GST for the month ending 2 October (in lieu of 30 September) by 21 October.

BAS and FBT returns If a taxpayer has fringe benefits tax (FBT) instalments included on their BAS, the ATO recommends that the BAS for the period ending on 31 March should be lodged before the FBT return for the FBT year ending on that date to reduce the likelihood of delays in processing the FBT return due to FBT details not being up to date.
