# Overview: small businesses ¶1-250

An entity's eligibility to claim the following GST concessions normally depends on it qualifying as a "small business entity":

- cash accounting (¶7-300) or a simplified accounting method (¶7-000)

- annual apportionment of input tax credits (¶5-020)

- payment of GST by instalments (¶8-037).

(1) it must be carrying on a business (¶1-255), and (2) it must satisfy the aggregated turnover test (¶1-260). Broadly, this means that the income of the entity and associated entities must be less than $10 million (ITAA 1997 Subdiv 328-C). A higher $50 million ceiling applies in determining access to simplified accounting methods, with effect from 1 July 2021 (¶7-000).

For the limited range of entities that are carrying on an enterprise that does not constitute a business (eg some charities, trustees of superannuation funds and government bodies), the "aggregated turnover" test is replaced by a "GST turnover" test (¶3-030).These tests differ in various ways; for example, (1) the aggregated turnover test applies to the total ordinary income, whereas the GST turnover test excludes the value of input taxed and certain other supplies; (2) the aggregated turnover test takes into account the turnovers of affiliates and connected entities, as well as the turnover of the entity itself; and (3) GST turnover is determined as at the end of each month, whereas an entity's status as a small business entity is determined for each income year.
