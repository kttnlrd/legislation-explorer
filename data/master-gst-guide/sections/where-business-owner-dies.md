# Where business owner dies ¶6-415

When a GST-registered individual dies, their registration must be cancelled. This may give rise to an increasing adjustment on cessation of registration (¶6-410). However, those rules will not apply if the deceased's business continues to be carried on by a GST-registered estate trustee (s 138-17). Nor will they apply when a GST-registered estate beneficiary takes over the running of the business from the trustee.

However, a "planned use" (¶6-300) adjustment may arise. When applying these rules the business is treated as if it were still being carried on by the individual who died.

Illustration: Death of GST-registered individual — decreasing adjustment Lena is registered for GST and remits GST quarterly. In December, Lena buys a computer for the GST-inclusive price of $4,400.

She claims an ITC in her December quarter BAS for $300 based on 75% business and 25% private use. Lena dies unexpectedly the following January. David, the estate executor, continues to carry on the business and uses the computer. David's business use of the computer is however 90%. David, as executor, is entitled to a decreasing adjustment based on the change of business use from 75% to 90% from the time the estate commenced carrying the business of the deceased.

A different adjustment applies in the situation where the estate trustee distributes a deceased's business assets to a beneficiary who does not intend to continue carrying on the business (Div 139). In such a case, the trustee becomes liable for an increasing adjustment, to reflect the fact that the asset is no longer used for purposes of the business. This adjustment is calculated in a similar way to adjustments on cessation of registration (¶6-410).

Illustration: Death of GST-registered individual — increasing adjustment Continuing the previous example. David, the executor, also distributes another asset of Lena's business to Rebecca who intends to use it for private purposes. Lena had purchased this asset for $5,500 and claimed an input tax credit for this asset on the basis that it was used 100% for business purposes. When the asset is distributed to Rebecca, its GST-inclusive value is $3,300. The increasing adjustment of $300 is calculated as: 1/11 × 100% (Lena's actual business use) × $3,300 Special rules apply where a taxpayer with an annual tax period dies (¶8-040).
