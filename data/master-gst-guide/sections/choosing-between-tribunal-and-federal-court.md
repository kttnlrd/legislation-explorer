# Choosing between Tribunal and Federal Court ¶18-640

There are several matters that you should take into consideration when deciding whether to apply to the Tribunal for review or to appeal to the Federal Court against an objection decision.

Type of case If the dispute concerns the manner in which the Commissioner has exercised a discretion, the Tribunal would usually be the appropriate forum. Unlike a court, the Tribunal has the power to review the Commissioner's decision and substitute its own. If a contentious issue of law is likely to arise, a court hearing may be advisable.

Formality The Tribunal is not bound by the rules of evidence and its hearings are more informal than a court hearing.

Publicity Unlike most court proceedings, Tribunal hearings on tax matters are in private if the person applying for the review so requests. Where the Tribunal has heard a case in private, the reasons for its decision must be framed so as not to be likely to enable the identification of the person, unless an appeal has been lodged.

Costs In a Tribunal hearing the person applying for review and the Commissioner each bears their own costs. In a court hearing costs are usually awarded against the unsuccessful party. These may be substantial. Where the amount of tax in dispute is less than $5,000, the Tribunal will, in most cases, be a more suitable forum, largely because there is a smaller application fee.

Payment of disputed tax Liability for any GST or other amount (such as a penalty) is not suspended pending the outcome of an appeal or review. In other words, the Commissioner may enforce a debt for assessed tax against you even though your objection is still pending: see ¶18-600.

ART replaces former AAT The Administrative Review Tribunal (ART) replaced the former Administrative Appeals Tribunal (AAT) under legislation that came into force on 14 October 2024. This involved changes to the process for appointment of members, with the intention being to create a more transparent, merit-based system. There are also measures to promote speedier and more considered resolution of important or complex issues, and to increase coordination with court proceedings (Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024).

Taxpayers that have previously applied to the AAT for review of a decision do not need to submit a new application. All outstanding cases transitioned automatically to the ART.
