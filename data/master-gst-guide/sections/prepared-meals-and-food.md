# Prepared meals and food ¶13-140

GST applies to prepared food of a kind listed in Sch 1 items 1-7, namely (s 38-3(1)(c)):

- quiches

- sandwiches (using any type of bread or roll)

- pizzas, pizza subs, pizza pockets and similar food (but pizza rolls, which require the addition of fillings

  or toppings to make them ready to eat, are GST-free: Interpretative Decision ID 2008/132)

- platters, etc, of cheese, cold cuts, fruit or vegetables and other arrangements of food

- hamburgers, chicken burgers and similar food. However, hamburger patties sold separately (eg in

  packets at supermarkets) are GST-free

- hot dogs, and

- food marketed as a prepared meal, but not including soup.

These items are subject to GST irrespective of whether they are sold hot, cold or frozen, require any cooking, heating, thawing or chilling before consumption.

The ATO produces a Detailed Food List to assist taxpayers to correctly determine the application of GST to food. The detailed food list includes an appendix with the history of changes to the list.

Food of a kind marketed as a prepared meal Food of a kind marketed as a prepared meal may be subject to GST (s 38-3(1)(c) and Sch 1 item 4) irrespective of whether it is sold hot, cold or frozen, or limited further preparation, assembly or activity by the consumer, such as cooking, heating, thawing or chilling before consumption, is required. To be taxable as a prepared meal, the item must, in its unopened state, require refrigeration or freezing for its storage. This means, for example, a tin of baked beans is not taxable as a prepared meal because it does not require refrigeration until it is opened.

The ATO issued GST Determination GSTD 2025/1, that applies both before and after the date of release of 23 July 2025. The determination outlines the ATO view of how the decision in Simplot Australia Pty Ltd v FC of T [2023] FCA 115 applies to the determination of whether a food is of a kind marketed as a prepared meal. The Detailed Food List has been updated to reflect the finalised determination. In the "Method Statement" outlined in GSTD 2025/1 from para 111, the ATO encourages use of the detailed food list. The ATO GST Industry Issues, Food Industry partnership, Prepared food ruling has also been withdrawn. Some of the views outlined in GSTD 2025/1 deviate from ATO past practice and, public advices and guidance, specifically in relation to salad products (an extensive case study of which is provided in paras 67–74), and the determination includes a transitional compliance approach (para 181).

The ATO in GST Determination GSTD 2025/1 para 21, based on the decision in Simplot, considers whether item 4 applies to a food is single composite statutory question that requires:

- identification of "food of a kind" and whether the food in question is of that kind or genus, and

- whether the class or genus of food is marketed as a prepared meal.

GST Determination GSTD 2025/1 advocates the application of common sense and common experience to determine whether food is of a kind marketed as a prepared meal. Whether a food is a "prepared meal" requires an objective determination of the character of a food with reference to the ordinary meaning of "prepared meal" which is not defined in the legislation.

The test is an objective test informed by common sense and common experience — how reasonable people understand the attributes of the class or genus of foods, and that of the food in question. Accordingly, the test is not concerned with narrow perceptions or fringe views (para 26); nor will an isolated instance of marketing a food as a prepared meal result in that kind of food becoming food of a kind marketed as a prepared meal (para 42). The test applied in Simplot resulted in all the foods being taxable notwithstanding that they were novel or unique in nature with no direct competitors at the time of their launch.

The inclusion of the words "of a kind" in s 38-3(1)(c) is expansive not limiting (para 28) and means that the actual marketing of the food (or how the consumer uses the food) is not directly relevant to the application of the test, but merely as a component of the consideration of the marketing of food of the class or genus to which the food belongs (para 23).

Consideration of how food is "marketed" requires consideration of the activities of the sellers in communicating and conveying messages to the market. This includes labelling (including the name), packaging, display, product placement, promotion and advertising (GSTD 2025/1 para 35). The internal activities of the seller, nor the subjective intention or motivation of the seller do not convey any message to the market.

Whether food is a "prepared meal" includes consideration of quantity, composition, and presentation (para 43). No single factor is determinative, rather the evaluation of weight of each of these considerations with respect of a food is a question of fact and degree.
