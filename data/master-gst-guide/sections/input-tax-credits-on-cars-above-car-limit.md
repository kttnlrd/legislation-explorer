# Input tax credits on cars above car limit ¶12-110

The input tax credit (ITC) allowable on the creditable acquisition (¶5-010) of a car is generally limited if the GST- (and LCT-) inclusive price, exceeds the "car limit" in the financial year in which the taxpayer first used th the car for any purpose. The allowable ITC is limited to 1/11 of the car limit (s 69-10). The car limit in the year of purchase is $66,000. The ITC to which the business is entitled is 1/11 × $66,000 = $6,000. If the purchaser acquired the car for only 70% business use, the input tax credit would be further reduced to $4,200 (70% × $6,000).

In May of the same year (4 months after the purchase of the car), a tow bar is purchased and fitted to the car for $2,200. The car limit does not affect this transaction, which is treated as a separate transaction from the acquisition of the car, and the full ITC of $200 can be claimed. In contrast, if the tow bar had been included in the original purchase, it would have been treated as part of the acquisition cost of the car, to which the car limit applied to reduce the ITC entitlement (based on GST Advice GSTA TPP 074).

Both car limit and car are terms defined (s 195-1) with reference to the income tax legislation. A car is a motor vehicle (¶23-100, although a different tonnage requirement applies for LCT purposes) designed to carry a load of less than one tonne and fewer than 9 passengers. Motor cycles and similar vehicles are excluded. The ATO takes the position that the car limit is not restricted "luxury cars" for purposes of the LCT rules, but applies broadly (¶23-100; Item 1.3 Minutes of NTLG GST sub-group meeting, 12 September 2012).

The car limit does not apply if the purchaser is entitled to quote their ABN (¶23-250), ie if they intend to use the car solely for the following purposes:

- as trading stock, including holding the car for sale under a hire-purchase arrangement (GST Ruling

This does not alter the fact that the company is entitled to quote at the time of acquisition, and that it is therefore entitled to a full ITC (Interpretative Decision ID 2005/186).

Illustration: ITCs for leased cars exceeding the car limit Peta leases a luxury car for $2,200 a month. If the car is used entirely for business purposes, Peta can claim an ITC of $200 on each lease payment. The credit is not limited even though the market value of the car exceeds the car limit.

Effect on capital allowance claims Under the income tax law, capital allowance (formerly depreciation) does not apply to the extent that the cost (¶24-070) of the car exceeds the car limit (ITAA 1997 s 40-230). The cost of the car for capital allowance purposes is reduced by any ITC to which the purchaser is entitled.
