# Precious metals ¶16-210

The supply of precious metal is normally input taxed (s 40-100) and accordingly input tax credits (ITCs) cannot be claimed in respect of taxable acquisitions.

However, the first sale by a refiner of precious metal of refined precious metal to a dealer in precious metals is GST-free (s 38-385). As the prices of precious metals are a function of global prices, precious metal dealers are unable to pass on any GST they would be required to remit on sales. Making the initial supply after refining GST-free is intended to ensure that there is no GST embedded in the price of that sale.

The definition of a dealer in precious metals (s 195-1) requires the regular supply and acquisition of precious metals as a principal part of its business. A refiner of precious metals must regularly refine or convert precious metals as part of its business. Although it appears from Very Important Business Pty Ltd v FC of T 2019 ATC ¶10-497; [2019] AATA 1120 that refining is not regular unless the activity occurs with a constant or definite pattern, recurring at short intervals of time, with some frequency; a less strict interpretation was favoured in Very Important Business Pty Ltd v FC of T 2020 ATC ¶10-557; [2020] AATA 4698.

Meaning of precious metal

*Refs: s 40-100), s 38-385), s 195-1)*

- gold of at least 99.5% fineness

- silver of at least 99.9% fineness, or

- platinum of at least 99% fineness.

The metal must be in an investment form. The Commissioner considers that this means that it must be in a form that:

- is capable of being traded on the international bullion market

- bears an accepted mark or characteristic that guarantees its fineness and quality, and

- is usually traded at a price that is determined by reference to the spot price of the metal it contains.

On this basis, metals in bar or wafer form may qualify as precious metals, but not metals in granular form.

Precious metal does not include scrap gold (Cash World Gold Buyers Pty Ltd v FC of T 2020 ATC ¶10-533; [2020] AATA 1546), which is treated as a taxable supply in respect of which ITCs may be allowed where the conditions giving rise to entitlement are satisfied (ss 11-5, 29-10; ¶5-010). Whether precious metal material is in investment form is an objective test. Accordingly, silver in the form of jewellery, electrical nodes and rolled foil (Siltech PMR Pty Ltd v FC of T 2025 ATC ¶10-740; [2025] ARTA 26) is not precious metal. In the case of mixed supplies, care should be taken to ensure the identity of the precious and non-precious components (Very Important Business). "Bullion" coins whose value is closely related to the metal content, and which are traded by banks, bullion dealers and brokers, may be treated as precious metals. However, "proof" or numismatic coins which are marketed as collectibles and whose value is affected by factors such as novelty, rarity and condition are not precious metals and would be subject to GST in the normal way (GST Ruling GSTR 2003/10). The sale of gold and silver coin blanks for stamping into investment and collector's coins, to a mint is taxable (AGR Joint Venture v FC of T 2007 ATC 2692; [2007] AATA 1870).

Illustration: GST-free sale of precious metal by refiner to dealer A refiner supplies gold to a dealer in precious metals. As this supply is GST-free, the refiner is entitled to credits for GST on items it has acquired to use in the refinery process, but does not apply GST to the supply to the dealer. If the dealer then sells the gold, this sale will be input taxed, so the dealer will not be entitled to ITCs and GST does not apply on the sale.

ATO litigation The ATO has been an active litigant in cases where scrap has been "re-refined" to give rise to an second "first sale" to a dealer of precious metal (STNK v FC of T 2021 ATC ¶10-591; [2021] AATA 3399, FC of T v Complete Success Solutions 2023 ATC ¶20-852; [2023] FCAFC 19, Decision Impact Statement; ACN 154 520 199 Pty Ltd (in liq) v FC of T 2019 ATC ¶10-518; [2019] AATA 5981, ACN 154 520 199 Pty Ltd (in liq) v FC of T 2020 ATC ¶20-772; [2020] FCAFC 190, HNMF v FC of T 2023 ATC ¶10-700; [2023] AATA 4067, FC of T v ACN 154 520 199 Pty Ltd (in liq) 2025 ATC ¶20-977; [2025] FCAFC 146, CPG Group Pty Ltd v FC of T 2024 ATC ¶10-710; [2024] AATA 199). Refiner-taxpayers are then able to claim significant ITCs as the sale is GST-free rather than input taxed. In its litigation the ATO often includes the application of the general anti-avoidance provisions as alternative grounds on which to disallow the claim of ITCs.

Reverse charge rule From 1 April 2017, as a result of extensive ATO investigations into possible fraud and GST evasion in the gold bullion and precious metals industries as well as investigation of artificial arrangements involving refiners, bullion dealers, gold kiosks, dealers and buyers involved in gold recycling or carousel type arrangements by the Australian Federal Police's Serious Financial Crime Taskforce entities buying gold, silver or platinum that has been supplied as a taxable supply may be required to apply a "reverse charge" (Div 86). This means that if an entity buys gold, silver or platinum from another business, the entity will be responsible for reporting and paying the GST amount to the ATO when lodging their BAS.

The effect is that if an entity sells gold, silver or platinum to another business, it will:

- not need to remit any GST on the sale to the ATO

- need to clearly state that the sale is a reverse-charged sale on the tax invoice they provide. This also

  applies if an entity is a buyer and they use recipient-created tax invoices, and

- be responsible for reporting the sale when lodging their BAS.

This reverse charge rule does not apply if the market value of the goods exceeds the market value of any gold, silver or platinum included in the goods by 10% or more. In such a case, the vendor remains liable for the GST as normal. However, the parties may agree to voluntarily reverse charge the supply if they wish.

The ATO's method for determining "market value" is as follows (Goods and Services Tax Valuable Metals

(Weight of valuable metal in troy ounces) × (Spot price of the valuable metal on that date according to published rates) Where the GST on a supply is reverse charged, it is not included in the recipient's GST turnover (s 188-23; ¶3-030).

Gold nuggets, jewellery The sale of gold nuggets by a registered prospector is subject to GST. Naturally occurring gold nuggets are not of sufficient purity to be "precious metals" nor are they in investment form.

Jewellery fashioned from a precious metal is not itself treated as a precious metal (GST Ruling GSTR 2003/10) and would be subject to GST in the normal way.

The ATO accepts that there may be no taxable supply where, under specified types of bailment arrangements, a producer delivers gold bullion to a refiner for refining (ATO GST Industry Issues — Mining and Energy Industry Partnership: Precious metal refining).
