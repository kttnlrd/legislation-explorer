 Simplified Tax System (STS): accounting method - timing of deduction for discount expense on Commercial bills with a term of less than 12 months

Issue
Does an STS taxpayer pay, for the purposes of paragraph 328-105(1)(b) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 (ITAA 1997), the discount expense on a short term commercial bill it issues when the bill matures?
Decision
Yes. For the purposes of paragraph 328-105(1)(b) of the ITAA 1997, the discount expense on a short term commercial bill will be paid in the income year in which the bill matures and the STS taxpayer pays the face value of the commercial bill, at which point the STS taxpayer can claim the discount expense as an outgoing.
Facts
The taxpayer is part of the STS regime and accounts on a cash basis.
The taxpayer issued a commercial bill, with a maturity date of less than 12 months, for a sum less than the face value of the bill and used the proceeds in an income producing activity.
The commercial bill was issued in the current income year but will mature in the following income year.
On maturity of the bill the taxpayer will pay the holder the face value of the bill.
The difference between the face value of the bill and the amount it was issued for is an expense that is deductible, under section 8-1 of the ITAA 1997, to the taxpayer that issued the bill.
Reasons for Decision
Commercial bill discount expenses are generally an allowable deduction as an expense of raising finance provided that the proceeds of the commercial bill are used in an assessable income producing activity (see Coles Myer Finance Ltd v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation (1993) 176 CLR 640; 93 ATC 4214; 25 ATR 95). This expense is deductible, under section 8-1 of the ITAA 1997, on a straight line basis over the life of the bill for taxpayers who account on an accruals basis.
The STS accounting method, as set out in paragraph 328-105(1)(b) of the ITAA 1997, provides that an STS taxpayer can generally claim for an outgoing that would otherwise be deductible under section 8-1 of the ITAA 1997 in the income year in which it paid that expense.
The term 'paid' is not defined for the purposes of paragraph 328-105(1)(b) of the ITAA 1997 hence it is interpreted according to its ordinary meaning within the legislative context.
The Macquarie Dictionary (Revised Third Edition) includes as a definition of 'pay' or 'paid':

1.To discharge (a debt, obligation, etc) as by giving or doing something.
2.To give (money etc) as in discharge of debt or obligation.
3.To satisfy the claims of (a person etc) as by giving money due.

The ordinary meaning of the term 'paid' therefore includes situations where an entity either discharges a debt by giving money or it satisfies a claim by giving money due.
The commercial bill is paid when it matures and the holder is paid the full face value of the bill. At this point the drawer, an STS taxpayer, discharges their existing liability and satisfies the holder's claim regarding the payment of the face value of the commercial bill.

Example: The STS taxpayer draws a commercial bill, on 180 day terms, with a face value of $10,000 which is sold on 3 April 2003 to an arm's length purchaser for $9,280. At the end of the 180 day period, on 29 September 2003 when the bill matures, the drawer is required to pay the holder the full face value of the bill. The discount of $720 is ordinarily a deductible expense to the drawer of the commercial bill as it represents the cost of raising finance which is used in the assessable income producing activity of the drawer.

In a cash accounting system a business expense is deductible when it is paid. An expense is paid when the liability is discharged or the claim settled. In terms of paragraph 328-105(1)(b) of the ITAA 1997 the treatment of such an expense is quite straightforward. The paragraph states that an amount you can deduct under section 8-1 of the ITAA 1997, such as a commercial bill discount, is only incurred when it is paid. For a commercial bill this occurs when the holder is paid the full face value of the bill and the discount between the drawer's selling and re-purchasing price, of the bill, is incurred. The discount is therefore a deductible expense to an STS taxpayer in the income year in which the payment is made.
This situation is only applicable to STS taxpayers. Taxation Ruling TR 93/21 covers the deductibility of a commercial bill discount expense for taxpayers not in the simplified tax system.
