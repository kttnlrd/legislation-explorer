================================================================================
MANAGING SECTIONS ACROSS NEW COMPILATIONS: A PROVISION-TOKEN REGISTRY
Research consolidated 2026-09-22 | legislation-explorer corpus
================================================================================

WHAT THIS IS

Desk research into how to manage ~20,000 statute provisions (one markdown file per
provision, 9 Acts) so that a NEW COMPILATION of an Act produces an exact, auditable
add/change/delete list instead of a full re-ingest plus hand repairs. Four research
strands were run in parallel, each required to cite primary specifications rather
than commentary:

  1. How the legal-document markup standards identify a provision (Akoma Ntoso,
     ELI, USLM) and what they say happens on renumbering, moving and repeal.
  2. How two national legislation systems actually run point-in-time versions and
     amendment tracking in production (legislation.gov.uk; Australian Federal
     Register of Legislation), and what their public APIs expose.
  3. The engineering mechanics of a content-addressed, incrementally updatable
     store (git object model, canonicalise-before-hash, patience/histogram diff,
     three-way merge, identity vs label) plus verifiable prior art.
  4. Provenance and verification standards for derived datasets (PROV-O, SLSA,
     reproducible builds, W3C DWBP, FAIR, Unicode normalisation), and what
     evidence an auditor would need per provision.

Every citation is to a primary source; the numbered list is at the end. Items I
could not verify are marked so rather than smoothed over. Live checks I ran myself
are marked [live]. Claims taken from a research strand are cited to that strand's
source.

1. THE PROBLEM, PRECISELY (all figures live-verified)
================================================================================

The corpus cannot be reproduced from its own vendored sources:

  - Vendored sources: source/itaa-1997/C2026C00122VOL01-12.pdf.
  - The Federal Register says register ID C2026C00122 is ITAA 1997 **compilation
    263**, start 2026-04-01, end 2026-05-21 [live, 30].
  - Every section file, tree.json and rebuild.sh stamp compilation **266**.
  - rebuild.sh §1b reads the compilation number off the PDF (never the filename)
    and refuses the build: "STOP source is Compilation No. 263 but the build stamps
    266". The repo's own guard is correct; nothing reconciled the two.

Consequence: 26 provisions exist that the vendor's comp-263 PDFs never print (e.g.
40-291A, served live at /api/section/itaa-1997/40-291A), and 15 headed provisions
the PDFs do print are absent from the corpus (e.g. all of Subdivision 900-I,
26-95, 28-180, 290-95, 900-35/40/45). So a rebuild would gain 15 and lose 26.

The authoritative target is reachable and named:

  compilation 266  registerId C2026C00324  start 2026-07-01  end 2026-08-27
                   registered 2026-07-24  [live, 30-32]
  compilation 265  registerId C2026C00242
  compilation 264  registerId C2026C00218
  compilation 263  registerId C2026C00122   <- our vendored PDFs

and comp 266 carries a machine-readable change list: `reasons[]`, six entries, each
naming the amending Act and the provisions that changed [live, 30-32]:

  Amend  sch 1 (items 79-98)               by C2025A00057 Treasury Laws Amendment
                                            (Payday Superannuation) Act 2025
  Amend  sch 1 (items 1-6)                 by C2026A00058 Treasury Laws Amendment
                                            (Delivering an Efficient/Trusted Tax System)
  Amend  sch 4 (items 1-4)                 by C2026A00058
  Amend  sch 8 (items 38-50)               by C2025A00017 Veterans' Entitlements...
  Amend  sch 1 (items 1-51, 64-79A),       by C2026A00049 Treasury Laws Amendment
         sch 3 (items 1-3), sch 4 (items 1-16)   (Tax Reform No. 1) Act 2026
  Amend  sch 1 (items 54-59)               by C2026A00049

Also live: the version in force from 2026-08-27 onward reports
hasUnincorporatedAmendments = true, and comp 266's own record carries
publishComments recording that sections 40-340, 328-430 and 328-450 have been
modified under the Commissioner's Remedial Power [30]. In other words "current" is
not 266, and "the text as amended" is not the same as "the text as printed".

That is the single most important research finding: **the change list this project
needs is already published as structured data.** Nothing about the update workflow
has to be guessed from PDF diffs alone.

2. WHAT THE STANDARDS SAY ABOUT PROVISION IDENTITY
================================================================================

2.1 Akoma Ntoso / OASIS LegalDocML [1][2][3][4]

Three id attributes, each with a different job:

  - eId — "Expression-level identifier ... uniqueness of an element within a
    specific Expression ... it needs to be updated regularly whenever the
    structural role of the element changes in a new Expression (i.e., if the
    element is renumbered or changed in nature, e.g., from article to clause)."
  - wId — "The value of the wId attribute never changes; it must be the same values
    for the same elements in all the Expressions of a document." It exists only
    where the eId has diverged, anchored to a nominated Master Expression. The
    schema states the intent: "the wId is used to mark the identifier that the
    structure used to have in the original version, and is only needed when a
    renumbering occurred."
  - GUID — "an application-specific identifier ... required syntax none", no
    compliance impact.

The eId grammar [3] is `[prefix "__"] element_ref ["_" number]`, where the number is
the `<num>` text with "final punctuation, meaningless separations as well as
redundant characters" stripped. The governing principle that matters here is
**Persistency**: "the identifier of a part is persistent across versions ... if a
part is moved and renamed the identifier accompanies the part, and does not stay
with the number." The four divergence cases are enumerated, including "article 2 is
from now on known as article 15", with the operating rule: "Tracking is always based
on the wId, navigation is always based on the identifier that was the eId at the
moment, and transfer is always based on the eId." Divergences are recorded in
`<mappings>` (`@original` = first wId, `@current` = eId for a temporal period, plus
`@start`/`@end`), and `<previous href="...">` references the pre-modification
element "especially useful when renumbering occurs" [3][4].

Consolidation/versioning is expressed at three levels [1][2][4]: the root's
`@contains` attribute (`originalVersion` | `singleVersion` | `multipleVersions` —
where singleVersion requires that "amendment documents and the enactment dates of
the amendments ... be all mentioned in the <lifecycle> element"); `<lifecycle>` with
its mandatory `@source` agent and dated `<eventRef>`s; and `<proprietary>` (any
metadata, also with mandatory `@source`) as the sanctioned place for a compilation
number or vendor stamp. Version identity lives in the Expression IRI
`/{lang}@{version}`: an dangling `@` is the original, `@2004-07-21` is "as amended on
July 2004", and no version token means "the version in force at the moment of the
resolution of the IRI".

Correction to a widely repeated claim: **there is no date-based eId convention.**
The grammar has no date component; the Naming Convention was inspected and none
exists [3].

Silence worth knowing: **repeal is not specified as an identity question.** No clause
governs what happens to an eId/wId on repeal, nor whether a repealed number may be
reused. Repeal appears only in the modification layer (`<textualMod type="repeal">`,
`@status`, `@end` with no `@start` for "a fragment that was part of the original
document but has been repealed"). Treat identity-across-repeal as undocumented.

2.2 ELI (European Legislation Identifier) [5][6][7]

FRBR refinement: work = `eli:LegalResource`, expression = `eli:LegalExpression`,
manifestation = `eli:Format`, and "the item level is not part of ELI". Properties that
map onto this project's fields: `eli:id_local` ("The unique identifier used in a local
reference system to maintain backwards compatibility"); `eli:version_date` ("The
point-in-time at which the provided description of the legislation is valid"); and
`eli:consolidates` / `eli:consolidated_by` — "indicates that this consolidated legal
resource or expression (which is usually the product of an editorial process that
revises the legislation) takes into account another one ... the original version or
the previous consolidated version, and ... the legislations making the change".

URI components: `{point in time}` (YYYYMMDD or YYYY-MM-DD, "To retrieve the version of
the legislation valid at a given date") and `{version}` ("To distinguish between a
base act and a consolidated version"), with an incomplete ELI redirecting to "the
latest consolidated version or base act".

Where it helps this design: a consolidation may be modelled either as a separate
legal resource or as a new expression of the same resource — ELI explicitly declines
to pick. That means the choice is ours and must be written down, not assumed.

2.3 USLM (United States Legislative Markup) [8][9][10][11][12]

The most operationally explicit of the three, and the closest fit to a token:

  - `@id`: "should always be assigned an immutable (non-changing) value. If the item
    is subject to renaming or renumbering, then the @id attribute should not reflect
    any part of the changeable part ... prefixed with 'id' and followed by a GUID
    that is guaranteed to be globally unique across both time and space."
  - Lifecycle rules stated as MUSTs: "Whenever an element is moved, its @id attribute
    value must be preserved. When an element is copied, a new value for the @id
    attribute value must be generated for the new element created." And on
    repeal-plus-recreation: "If an item is deleted and later a similarly named item
    is created, then the new item should be assigned a newly generated identity as it
    is not related to the earlier item."
  - `@temporalId`: the human-readable, recomputable half — "may need to be recomputed
    based on the temporal state of a document", built as
    `[parentId + "_"] + [bigLevelPrefix] + num` with document/main levels omitted.
    Crucially, "the uniqueness of the @temporalId attribute is not enforced by the
    schema, and some ambiguity is possible."
  - Alternate versions of an item **exist alongside one another inside the same
    document**, each associated with the periods it was in force.

Taken together: three standards agree that identity and label are different things,
and USLM is the one that tells you exactly what to do when a label changes or a
provision is repealed and re-created.

3. HOW PRODUCTION SYSTEMS ACTUALLY DO IT
================================================================================

3.1 legislation.gov.uk [13]-[21]

An **effect** "is a record of the change that helps our editors track where (and
when) to apply amendments and commencements", and explicitly "does not contain the
amending text, and nor does it contain machine-readable instructions on how to apply
the amendment". The record and the payload are separate artefacts joined by a
provision-level citation — the editor reads the amending provision and applies it.

Effect payload fields [13][20]: type; affecting and affected provisions; **savings**
("provisions that qualify the meaning of the effect, usually to preserve the previous
meaning of the amended provisions in certain contexts"); in-force dates, which may be
split by extent or purpose; commencement authority; extent; and the boolean pair
applied / requires-applied (plus Welsh equivalents). The CLML `EffectType` carries a
generated `EffectId` (an MD5 hash), and each `InForce` "represents an amendment. An
effect has one amendment for each time its affecting provision comes into force in
full, in part or for specified extents or purposes", with a `Prospective` flag.

Textual vs non-textual is a first-class distinction: textual types change words
(inserted, substituted, repealed, words omitted); non-textual types record a legal
relationship without changing text (**applied**, **modified**, **excluded**). For tax
law the non-textual class is not an edge case — "applied (with modifications)" is
common, and our own corpus proves it (the comp-266 publishComments records
Commissioner's Remedial Power modifications to 40-340, 328-430, 328-450).

Point-in-time URIs: `/id/...` (identifier), document,
`.../{section}/{authority}/{extent}/{version}`, plus `/data.{ext}` for a
representation. "If no version is specified in a document URI, this is taken to refer
to the version of the legislation that is currently in force." Whole-item point-in-
time returns everything, with sections that "aren't valid at the date requested"
flagged `Match="false"` and `Status` of `Prospective`, `Repealed` or `Discarded`; a
repealed section's DocumentURI "will point to the version of the section that was in
force on the day before its repeal". Failures observed in testing: the documented
`/prospective` paths returned 404, and `/id/effect/{id}` returned 404 [caveat from
the research strand, not re-tested here].

Editorial reality, which is the part to copy: **unapplied effects are user-visible.**
A "Changes to Legislation" banner, a per-provision listing, All/Applied/Unapplied
filters on `/changes`, and `<ukm:UnappliedEffects>` in XML. The service guide is
blunt: "one of the limitations of this site is not all the changes we know about have
been applied to the content. It is therefore out of date." Instead of publishing a
backlog count, they publish service targets ("within a maximum of three months of
the coming into force date", after preparatory work that "typically take[s] from four
to eight weeks"), and the project wiki records that 2002 "was the last date at which
all primary legislation on the database was fully up to date".

The most directly reusable mechanism is the **Publication Log** [16]: one entry per
publication, republication or withdrawal, typed `legislation | draft |
associated-documents | changes` and `published | withdrawn`, with fields for
republished/new/newly-issued. Critically: "a publication of a set of changes may
delete some or all of the existing effects. This is still recorded as a 'published'
event." That is exactly the semantics an audited corpus needs for a compilation that
supersedes an earlier one.

Data endpoints (verified live at the time of research, except the 404s above):
`/ukpga/2007/3/data.xml`, `/ukpga/2007/3/2026-01-01/data.xml`,
`/ukpga/2007/3/section/11D/2026-01-01/data.xml`, `/data.akn`, `/data.xht`,
`/changes[/affected/...][/affecting/...]` with `/data.feed` and `/data.csv`,
`/update/data.feed`, and `/new/[yyyy-mm-dd]/data.feed`. Rate limit 3,000 requests /
5 minutes / IP with a mandatory user agent [14][15].

3.2 Australian Federal Register of Legislation [23]-[33]

The model is compilation-based and it is the model this corpus already claims to use.
A compilation "is a version of a law that shows the text of the law as amended at a
particular point in time"; the compilation number is "a unique alphanumeric
identifier assigned to a specific point-in-time version"; the compilation date "is
the date from which the text of the law shown in the compilation applies" [23]. OPC
must prepare and register compilations on amendment or disallowance, tied to Acts
"as soon as practicable after the amendments commence", with instruments lodged
"typically within two working days" [30][31].

**Authorised version** is a legal status, not a synonym for current: versions relied
on in court under ss 15ZA/15ZB are always PDF, stamped "Authorised version",
"Authoritative" or "Federal Register of Legislative Instruments" — and an unaltered
downloaded or printed copy retains that authority [22]. Exposed as `isAuthorised` in
the API.

**Composition of a compilation is inferable from its metadata, and the API exposes
the booleans that matter**: `hasUnincorporatedAmendments` on a Version ("the text
does not yet incorporate amendments that are already known") and
`hasCommencedUnincorporatedAmendments` on a Title. Plus, per version:
`reasons[]` — for each reason, `affect` (AsMade, Amend, Repeal, Cease, ChangeDate,
Disallow), a markdown citation, `affectedByTitle`, `amendedByTitle`, and
`dateChanged{from,to}`. Documents additionally carry `versionType`
(Rectification | Replacement | RetrospectiveCompilation), `rectificationVersionNumber`,
`rectified`, `rectificationReason`, `volumeNumber`, `format`, `pageCount`,
`sizeInBytes`, `isAuthorised`. The `$metadata` CSDL publishes all of it [32][33].

**Superseded compilations remain retrievable** — "a superseded version always has the
start date of the compilation in the URL" — and this was verified for comp 263, which
the API returns with `isCurrent:false, isLatest:false` and whose documents remain
`isAuthorised:true` [27][live].

**Editorial changes exist and are reported.** Division 3 of Part 2 of Chapter 2 of
the Legislation Act 2003 lets the First Parliamentary Counsel make editorial and
presentational changes, but "the editorial power cannot be used to change the effect
of the law, to resolve an ambiguity in the law or to rewrite legislation"; six-monthly
Editorial Changes Reports are published and the changes appear in compilation
endnotes [33]. This is the mechanism by which a compilation can differ from a pure
amendment-set — i.e. a text change with no amending Act behind it — which is exactly
the kind of silent difference that breaks a naive "diff against the previous
compilation" check.

4. ENGINEERING MECHANICS OF A TOKEN REGISTRY
================================================================================

4.1 Content addressing [34][35][36]

A git blob id is `sha1("blob " + byte-length + NUL + content)`, verified locally:
hashing `what is up, doc?` gave bd9dbf5a…, matching an independent
`sha1(b"blob 16\0" + content)`. The **filename is not in that hash** — content
identity and path identity are separate facts, which is what lets a provision keep
its identity while moving between parts.

A tree object is the Merkle node: "one or more entries, each of which is the SHA-1
hash of a blob or subtree with its associated mode, type, and filename". Two
consequences: **equality is a proof** (equal subtree ids mean identical names and
identical bytes below, with no file reads), and change detection prunes to O(changed
paths), not O(corpus). Measured on a synthetic 20,000-file corpus: `git add -A &&
git commit` 2.21s; a naive `find | sort | sha256sum` manifest 0.50s; after editing
three files, exactly three paths and three of nine Act-subtree ids changed.

4.2 Canonicalise before hashing [42]

Hashing raw bytes makes formatting differences look like content changes — and this
corpus has exactly that problem, since its fidelity gate already canonicalises
(dashes, quotes, curve/drawn glyphs) before comparing. RFC 8785 states the principle:
"cryptographic operations like hashing and signing need the data to be expressed in
an invariant format so that the operations are reliably repeatable." Measured: seven
byte-variants of `Section 6—Meaning of 'eligible entity'` (em dash vs ASCII, curly vs
straight quotes, CRLF vs LF, NBSP, non-breaking hyphen) collapsed from seven distinct
SHA-256s to **five** canonical hashes — the two non-collapsing pairs differ in how
much text is present (two hyphens vs one em dash; double vs single quoted phrase),
which is arguably a real difference and should not be folded.

Design conclusion: keep **two hashes per provision** — `raw_sha256` (bytes, and the
git blob) and `norm_sha256` (canonical text). Report a change only when `norm_sha256`
moves; label a raw-only difference `presentation_only` so it is visible rather than
swallowed.

4.3 Diffs and merges [37][38][39]

git's default is Myers, with `--patience` and `--histogram` available, and git-merge
concedes that a different algorithm "can help avoid mismerges that occur due to
unimportant matching lines" (ort defaults to histogram). Local reproduction on
repeated structural lines: Myers reported a duplicate numbered paragraph as deleted
here and added there; patience reported only the one heading that actually changed.
Both are minimal; patience's is the one a human can adjudicate.

Three-way merge, using a recorded base, is what distinguishes unchanged /
changed-upstream / changed-locally / both. Measured with GNU diff3 and
`git merge-file --diff3`: identical inputs merged unchanged; one-sided changes merged
cleanly; **independent edits on different lines merged cleanly with exit 0 and no
conflict markers**; conflicting edits on the same line produced the
`<<<<<<< ||||||| ======= >>>>>>>` block and exit 1.

That third case is the trap worth naming in the design: a clean merge can silently
combine two independent edits. Never accept a clean merge for a legal text — re-run
the fidelity gate on the merged bytes.

4.4 Identity vs label [3][40][41]

Akoma Ntoso formalises the split (wId immutable, eId evolving, `<mappings>` recording
divergences). Persistent-identifier practice treats stability as a service
commitment, not a property of the string: "persistence is neither inherent in an
object nor conferred on it by a particular naming syntax" [40]; W3C's position is
simply "a cool URI is one which does not change" [41]. The database analogue is a key
generated independently of the row's attributes.

Practical rule for this corpus: derive `provision_id` from the **original**
compilation's numbering, never the current display number, never reuse a retired id,
and keep a `mappings` table when the label changes.

4.5 Verifiable prior art [43]-[49]

  - **legalize-dev/legalize** (MIT), "Every law as a Markdown file. Every reform as a
    Git commit", 31 countries. Its SPEC.md v0.4 is the closest published design to
    what is proposed here: identifier = public name = file name, unique repo-wide;
    shard by `sha1(identifier)[:2]` because it "is always present and never changes"
    whereas "a key that can be corrected turns a metadata fix into a file rename — a
    change to the history that no legislature made"; commit trailers
    `Source-Id` / `Source-Date` / `Norm-Id` recording what amended what; per-file
    `git log -- <path>` as the version history; deterministic pipeline git identity so
    rebuilds reproduce commit hashes. It has **no** canonicalisation layer of its own
    and relies wholly on git blobs and trees.
  - **Laws.Africa Indigo** (GPL-3): identity is `Work.frbr_uri`; each version is a
    `Document` with an `expression_date`; `ArbitraryExpressionDate` marks a
    consolidation point; `Commencement` records which provisions commenced by
    provision id. Versions are compared **structurally** (`xmldiff` over rendered AKN
    HTML, with `eId` unset on ref/def/authorialNote first), not by text hash.
  - **NZ Legislation as Code** (`openfisca-aotearoa`, AGPL-3): parameters as YAML with
    date-keyed values. Rules, not documents — no provision-level identity registry,
    so not a model for corpus bookkeeping.
  - Could not verify: `laws-africa/limpopo` (HTTP 404, no claim made); `Laws.LK` (no
    repository found; nearest verifiable artefact is `nuuuwan/lk_legal_docs`, a
    document corpus rather than a structured store).

5. PROVENANCE AND VERIFICATION
================================================================================

5.1 PROV-O, and what it does not do [50][51]

PROV-O is a W3C Recommendation modelling Entity / Activity / Agent with
`prov:wasDerivedFrom`, `prov:wasGeneratedBy`, `prov:used`, `prov:wasAttributedTo`,
`prov:generatedAtTime`, `prov:wasRevisionOf`, `prov:specializationOf`,
`prov:alternateOf`. It also constrains vocabulary choice: the more specific
subproperties "should be used when applicable" — a provision transcribed from a PDF
is a quotation of a specialization of a compilation, and saying so signals verbatim.

Limits that matter: PROV-O contains no integrity vocabulary (no hash/checksum/
digest property); identity is asserted, not computed ("Two entities are equal if they
have the same identifier" — so `prov:wasDerivedFrom :compilation-266` against a
comp-263 PDF is a false triple PROV-O cannot falsify); and derivation carries no byte
semantics — it does not say which regions changed, nor require untouched regions to
stay identical. PROV-O describes provenance; it does not check it.

5.2 Reproducible vs verifiable [52][53][54][55][56]

"A build is **reproducible** if given the same source code, build environment and
build instructions, any party can recreate bit-by-bit identical copies"; "the
reproducibility of artifacts is **verified** by bit-by-bit comparison", usually by
hash. The manifest carries the claim; the verification is separate and someone
else's. A signed buildinfo "attests what their build result was. (This alone does not
indicate if it is reproducible...)".

Two rules transfer directly: build metadata must come from the source, not the build
("The date and time of the build itself is hardly of value as an old source code can
always be compiled long after it has been released"), and abbreviated VCS hashes are
non-deterministic because the abbreviation length "is dependent on the number of
objects in the Git repository" — so never stamp a build timestamp or short commit
hash into 20,000 files.

SLSA supplies the attestation shape: provenance "MUST unambiguously identify the
output package by cryptographic digest and describe how that package was produced",
with `resolvedDependencies[]` each carrying a digest map, plus `builder.id`,
`buildType`, `externalParameters` and run metadata; verification requires that the
attestation `subject` digest match the artefact [57][58][59]. Note the ceiling: even
SLSA's `SLSA_BUILD_REPRODUCED` is issued by a *third party* who reproduced the build
on independently operated platforms [60].

5.3 Dataset versioning and hashes as tamper checks [61][62][63][64][65]

W3C Data on the Web Best Practices require, in its own words: BP5 "Provide data
provenance information"; BP7 "Provide a version indicator" (because "explicit
versioning allows for repeatability in research"); BP8 "Provide a complete version
history that explains the changes made in each version"; BP9/BP10 persistent URIs
within datasets; BP11 "Assign URIs to individual versions of datasets as well as to
the overall series"; BP27 identifiers must survive removal. FAIR requires F1
(globally unique persistent identifier), F3 (metadata include the identifier), R1.2
(detailed provenance), with the caveat that FAIR "is not itself a standard or
specification".

Registry practice shows the field shape: npm's `integrity` is "a sha512 or sha1
Standard Subresource Integrity string for the artifact"; W3C SRI §3.1 defines
integrity metadata as algorithm + digest + options and §3.2 requires SHA-256/384/512,
refusing MD5/SHA-1; PyPI's JSON API publishes per-file `digests` (sha256,
blake2b_256), and its Integrity API returns provenance objects bundling attestations
"with the identity that produced them". The registry hash proves the bytes arrived
unchanged; the attestation ties those bytes to a build.

5.4 Normalise before comparing — but do not conflate the two checks [66][67]

UAX #15 §1.2: normalisation transforms each string into a Normalization Form so that
"a binary comparison of the transformed strings will then determine equivalence", and
§10 is explicit that a raw byte comparison is not an equivalence test, and that
"higher-level processes that transform or compare strings ... must respect canonical
equivalence or problems will result." Two consequences: record the normalisation form
**and** the Unicode version with any fingerprint (stability is promised only for
characters assigned in that version), and remember that normalisation is a comparison
device, not a storage format — UTS #39 warns that a confusable "skeleton" output
"should definitely not be used as a 'normalization' of identifiers".

So the fidelity gate needs two checks, not one: canonical-form equality for "this is
the same provision text", and an exact byte hash of the regions the pipeline never
touched for "nothing was silently altered".

================================================================================
6. THE DESIGN FOR THIS CORPUS: A PROVISION-TOKEN REGISTRY
================================================================================

6.1 Model choice (explicit, because ELI leaves it open)

A **compilation is a new Expression of the same Work**, not a new Work. Rationale:
the register treats a compilation as "a version of a law ... at a particular point in
time", the amending Acts are separate Works, and USLM keeps alternate versions of an
item inside one document. Consequence: identity is per provision, across
compilations; the compilation number is a *version*, never part of the identity.

6.2 The token (per provision, in frontmatter and in the manifest)

  id:            stable, opaque, never reused; derived from the ORIGINAL compilation's
                 numbering; a new suffix is minted for inserted provisions; a retired
                 id is retired forever (USLM: a later section with the same number is
                 "not the same item")
  act:           act slug
  label:         the CURRENT display number (e.g. "40-291A") — a label, not identity
  mappings:      [{original, current, start, end}] where the label has ever changed
                 (Akoma Ntoso <mappings>)

  Source pinning (per provision, not per repo):
  source_register_id / compilation_no / compilation_date
  source_pdf_path / source_pdf_sha256
  source_page / source_volume
  anchor_text:   first and last ~8 printed words, so a human can re-find it

  Fingerprints:
  raw_sha256          bytes as extracted, before any substitution
  norm_sha256         after documented canonicalisation
  norm_form:          e.g. NFC
  unicode_version
  untouched_sha256    the region the pipeline must not have written
  substitution_table_sha256   so the gate's tolerance is itself auditable

  Base bookkeeping:
  base_compilation_no / base_norm_sha256 / base_manifest_sha256

  Gate + build:
  gate_name / gate_version / gate_verdict ∈
      {pass, pass_with_substitutions, fail, no_source}
  source_reason: which `reasons[]` entry (affecting title + provisions) accounts for
      the current text, if any — including "non-textual: applied/modified", which is
      NOT a textual amendment and must never be recorded as one
  built_by: pipeline commit + extractor version + invocation id

A provision with no source is `no_source`, never `pass`.

6.3 The corpus manifest

`manifest.json`: provision_id → the fields above, plus per-Act subtree hashes and the
manifest's own SHA-256 as the corpus fingerprint. Then two independent proofs of
"nothing changed": a third party can re-hash files against the manifest, and can
compare git tree ids between compilations. Building it costs ~0.5s for 20,000 files,
measured.

6.4 The update workflow (new compilation N+1)

  1. RESOLVE. Ask the Register API for the title's versions: registerId,
     compilationNumber, start/end, isCurrent, hasUnincorporatedAmendments, reasons[].
     Refuse to proceed on a compilation whose register ID is not the one being
     vendored [our own bug: the filename said one thing, the PDF said another, and it
     took a guard to notice].
  2. VENDOR + PIN. Download the compilation, hash it, and record register ID +
     compilation number **read off the document** and cross-checked against the API
     record. Disagreement is a hard stop.
  3. CLASSIFY WITHOUT TEXT DIFFS. For each provision compare three hashes —
     base_norm_sha256 from the manifest, the file on disk, the new source — giving
     unchanged / changed-upstream / changed-locally / both in O(n) integer
     comparisons. Use patience/histogram diffs only to RENDER the changed ones.
  4. RECONCILE THE REASONS. Every changed provision must be attributable to a
     `reasons[]` entry, or to `hasUnincorporatedAmendments`, or to a recorded
     editorial change (Legislation Act 2003 Div 3 Part 2 Ch 2) — or it is unexplained
     and blocks. Conversely, every `reasons[]` entry naming provisions must show up
     as a change, or the sources are wrong.
  5. GATE. Run the fidelity gate on the merged bytes with the page evidence, and the
     byte-exact check on the untouched regions. Never accept a clean merge.
  6. APPLY. Block-applied, in reviewable units, with the provenance invariant (an
     insertion must be removable at its recorded offset to reproduce the prior bytes).
  7. RECORD. Publish a Publication-Log-style entry for the corpus: what was
     published, what was withdrawn, which effects were applied — "a publication of a
     set of changes may delete some or all of the existing effects ... still recorded
     as a published event".

6.5 Fail-closed rules

  - A provision present in the corpus but absent from its declared source is a BUILD
    FAILURE, not a finding. (This is the 26-and-15 situation: both directions must
    fail the build with the ids listed.)
  - A label change with no `mappings` row is a failure — a renumber must never
    silently retarget a citation.
  - A provision whose gate verdict is `no_source` may not be served as authoritative.
  - Non-textual effects (applied / modified / excluded) must be recorded as
    non-textual; the comp-266 publishComments show why (Commissioner's Remedial Power
    modifications to 40-340, 328-430, 328-450 alter the operation of provisions
    without amending their words).
  - "Not reproducible" is a first-class status. The UK service publishes its
    unapplied-effects state rather than implying completeness; this corpus should
    publish which compilations it has and has not reconciled.

7. WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE KNOWN DEFECTS
================================================================================

  - The 263/266 break becomes a build failure with a named cause (register ID
    mismatch) and a named fix (vendor C2026C00324), rather than a discovery months
    later. The repo's existing guard is right; it needs the API record as its other
    half.
  - The 26 "present but unprinted" and 15 "printed but missing" become non-zero exit
    codes listing provision ids, which is what should have happened when they were
    introduced.
  - Present-and-wrong operators and the font-encoded `×`/`´` class stop being a
    gate-versus-corpus argument: the substitution table is hashed into the manifest
    with the page evidence that justifies each entry, so the tolerance is auditable
    rather than implicit.
  - "Unchanged" becomes provable twice over (manifest re-hash; git subtree id), which
    is the property the corpus currently lacks entirely.
